# Year 9 Science: Biology teaching plan (9A, 9B, 9C)

Exploring Science International 9 | Built 28 October 2026 | Chinese text status: draft, needs native-speaker check

## 1. Overview

This plan covers the three Year 9 biology units of *Exploring Science International 9*, taught in this order: 9A Genetics and evolution, 9B Plant growth, 9C Threat from disease. Chemistry (9E Making materials) follows. Every lesson is built for a 40-minute period with the same shape as the school's science enrichment lessons: Do Now (4 min), Today (1 min: two "I can" goals and the key words), Hook (3), Learn (8 to 10, with a quick check), Do (12 to 16: worksheet or practical), Think and Reflect (4), Exit ticket (3) and a one-slide summary. Where the timetable gives a longer period, extend Do with the matching Workbook pages.

Each lesson has: a branded PowerPoint (English only, speaker notes on every slide, answers to every on-slide question in the notes); a standard worksheet; a worksheet with the same content plus the key vocabulary in Chinese at the top right and the command words glossed in Chinese (for students with lower English); and, in the unit Teacher Guide, the lesson plan, misconceptions, safety, equipment, slide-by-slide notes and a full answer key. Worksheets are designed to print in greyscale on A4.

The content follows the Student Book spread by spread, so students can read the matching pages before or after the lesson, and the Workbook pages are set as homework. Lesson structure, key learning points, keywords and misconceptions were cross-checked against the matching Oak National Academy KS3 lessons (Open Government Licence v3.0); the links are in each lesson's plan.

### 9A learning goals

**Lesson 1: Environmental variation**  
- I can describe abiotic and biotic environmental factors and give examples of environmental variation.
- I can tell continuous variation from discontinuous variation, and explain why environmental variation can make classification difficult.

**Lesson 2: Inherited variation**  
- I can give examples of inherited variation and explain how sexual reproduction causes it.
- I can sort inherited variation into continuous and discontinuous, and recognise a normal distribution.

**Lesson 3: Probability and statistics**  
- I can explain what probability is and write a probability as a percentage, a fraction and a decimal.
- I can calculate an experimental probability from results and compare it with a theoretical probability.

**Lesson 4: DNA, chromosomes and genes**  
- I can outline how the structure of DNA was discovered and say what each scientist did.
- I can describe how DNA, chromosomes, genes and genetic information are linked, and give the number of chromosomes in body cells and gametes.

**Lesson 5: Genetic counselling**  
- I can describe what a genetic test looks at and why babies and families may be tested.
- I can use probability and my knowledge of genes and chromosomes to explain a genetic test result simply, as a genetic counsellor would.

**Lesson 6: Genes, adaptation and extinction**  
- I can explain how changes in abiotic and biotic factors can make a species endangered or extinct, using the idea of adaptations.
- I can describe ways of preserving biodiversity, including nature reserves, breeding programmes and gene banks.

**Lesson 7: Natural selection and evolution**  
- I can explain how natural selection works on inherited variation when the environment changes, using the peppered moth and the Salton Sea tilapia.
- I can describe the theory of evolution by natural selection and explain how natural selection can produce a new species.

### 9B learning goals

**Lesson 1: Reactions in plants**  
- I can write the word and symbol equations for photosynthesis and aerobic respiration, and say where and when each happens in a plant.
- I can explain how limiting factors slow photosynthesis and why roots need oxygen.

**Lesson 2: Plant adaptations: roots, stems and leaves**  
- I can describe what plants need from the soil and the air, and how roots, root hair cells and xylem are adapted to supply water and mineral salts.
- I can explain how a leaf and its palisade cells are adapted for photosynthesis.

**Lesson 3: Gas exchange in leaves**  
- I can describe how stomata and guard cells control gas exchange in a leaf.
- I can explain how temperature and concentration difference affect diffusion, and why a plant loses more water on a hot day.

**Lesson 4: Plant products: starch, lipids and proteins**  
- I can describe how plants use glucose to make starch, cellulose, lipids and proteins, and explain why nitrates are needed.
- I can test a leaf for starch and explain what happens when a seed germinates.

**Lesson 5: Growing crops**  
- I can explain how fertilisers, pesticides, greenhouses and machines increase crop yield, and how pests and population growth change the food supply.
- I can describe how new varieties are made by cross-breeding and selective breeding, and interpret the results of a fertiliser investigation.

**Lesson 6: Farming problems and the carbon cycle**  
- I can explain how fertilisers, pesticides and clearing land can harm wild organisms, including eutrophication and bioaccumulation.
- I can use the carbon cycle to explain how farming and burning fuels change the carbon dioxide in the air, and suggest ways to farm more sustainably.

**Lesson 7: Bias, validity and organic farming**  
- I can identify bias in graphs and arguments, explain where accidental bias comes from, and say whether a conclusion is valid.
- I can compare organic and intensive farming and explain biological control.

### 9C learning goals

**Lesson 1: Threat from disease**  
- I can explain how hygiene, diet, immunisation and medicines have helped people live longer.
- I can give examples of the five types of disease and say which ones can be passed from person to person.

**Lesson 2: Immunity and vaccination**  
- I can describe how phagocytes and lymphocytes destroy microorganisms in the body.
- I can explain why you only get many infectious diseases once, and how vaccination protects people and communities.

**Lesson 3: Control systems: nerves and hormones**  
- I can describe how the nervous system detects a stimulus and produces a response.
- I can describe how hormones affect the body and compare the nervous and hormonal systems.

**Lesson 4: Treating diseases**  
- I can explain which medicines treat which types of disease, and why antibiotics do not work on viruses.
- I can explain how large amounts of human hormones are made quickly using genetically modified bacteria in a fermenter.

**Lesson 5: Medians and quartiles**  
- I can find the median, the lower and upper quartiles and the interquartile range of a simple data set.
- I can use quartiles to compare the variation in two groups and to interpret the results of a medicine trial.

**Lesson 6: Ecology: abundance and distribution**  
- I can choose suitable apparatus for measuring the abundance and distribution of organisms.
- I can use data from a sample to estimate a population size and say how accurate the estimate is.

**Lesson 7: In and out: surface area, diffusion and osmosis**  
- I can calculate a surface area : volume ratio and explain why it matters for cells and organisms.
- I can describe how osmosis happens through a partially permeable membrane and use it to explain what I see.

## Language access

- Slides are in English, with no more than about 30 English words per text block and a key words strip on every Learn slide.
- Two worksheets per lesson: *standard* (full English, key words with simple English meanings) and *CN* (the same questions; the key words box at the top right gives each term with its Chinese, and the command words used on the sheet are glossed in Chinese: Describe 描述, Explain 解释 and so on). Give the CN sheet to any student who would otherwise be stopped by the vocabulary; the science and the questions are identical, so both sheets are marked the same way.
- Students may answer Core questions in Chinese or English but should use the English key words. Stretch and Challenge ask for English with the sentence frames shown on the Think and Reflect slide; the reasoning may be in Chinese.
- One Chinese term per English term across all three units: see `source/glossary_cn.json`. All Chinese is marked draft, needs native-speaker check; change the glossary first, then rebuild.
- Routine: Say, show, check. Read the English goal, point to the key word, thumbs check before each task. Pair a weaker reader with a stronger one for the Do Now and Think and Reflect.

## Assessment and homework

- Every lesson ends with a three-question exit ticket (the strip at the foot of the worksheet). Collect it; the Teacher Guide gives the answers and says what a wrong answer means for the next lesson.
- The quick check slide (mini whiteboards) in every lesson gives a mid-lesson reading of the class.
- Core, Stretch and Challenge are tiers of the same worksheet, not ability groups: everyone starts at Core; Stretch and Challenge are for those who finish.
- Homework: the Workbook pages listed for each lesson.
- End of unit: the final lesson of each unit ends with a review summary; the Workbook's last page of each unit (9A Recreating animals p.14, 9B Organic farming p.26, 9C Combatting pandemics p.38) works as a revision sheet before the department's end-of-unit test.
