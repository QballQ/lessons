## 1. The six-strand rubric [Paste into Claude Design]

Create an A4 landscape, black-and-white-printable rubric poster for the Think Like a Scientist programme, using the Think Like a Scientist design system. No series colour: use the programme wordmark, so one version serves Years 7, 8 and 9. A grid of 6 rows (strands) by 4 columns (levels). Strand names in bold English with Chinese beneath in a lighter weight. Level headings in English and Chinese, with a simple greyscale level marker: one, two, three or four filled squares. Minimum 10 pt text in the cells (this is a teacher and display document). Footer note in English and Chinese.

**Title:** Think Like a Scientist Rubric / 像科学家一样思考 评价量规

**Levels:** Beginning 起步 (■□□□) | Developing 发展 (■■□□) | Secure 达标 (■■■□) | Excellent 优秀 (■■■■)

| Strand | Beginning 起步 | Developing 发展 | Secure 达标 | Excellent 优秀 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Questioning and predicting** 提问与预测 | Asks a question with help; makes a guess without a reason | Asks a question that can be tested; prediction has a simple reason | Writes a clear testable question naming what changes and what is measured; prediction uses science ideas | Sharpens the question; prediction uses science and states what result would show it is wrong |
| **2. Planning** 计划设计 | Lists some equipment and steps with support | Names the independent and dependent variables; method can mostly be followed | Identifies IV, DV and key controls; method is clear and repeatable, with repeats and safety | Justifies choices (range, repeats, sample size); anticipates problems; risk assessment is specific |
| **3. Doing and recording** 实施与记录 | Carries out steps with support; records some results | Follows the method safely; records results in a table with headings | Works carefully; table has units; repeats done; anomalies noticed | Adjusts the method sensibly when problems arise; records precisely; explains how anomalies were handled |
| **4. Analysing and concluding** 分析与结论 | Describes what happened | Draws a suitable chart with help; states a simple pattern | Chooses and draws the right graph accurately; conclusion uses data as evidence | Explains the pattern with science ideas; notes the limits of what the data can show |
| **5. Evaluating and critical thinking** 评价与批判性思维 | Says whether the experiment worked | Identifies one problem and a simple improvement | Explains how errors affected results; suggests specific improvements; judges how confident to be | Weighs the strength of evidence; considers other explanations; links to how scientists check each other's work |
| **6. Communicating** 表达与交流 | Shares findings using some key words, with frames and support | Presents findings clearly using key English vocabulary | Clear, organised presentation; uses vocabulary accurately; answers questions | Confident and well structured; adapts to the audience; responds to challenge with evidence |

**Footer:** We judge the science, not the English grammar. You may explain your thinking in Chinese; use key science words in English. 我们评价的是科学思维，而不是英语语法。你可以用中文解释想法，关键科学词汇请用英语。

### How to use the rubric (for the teacher)

- **Evidence:** strands 1 to 5 are judged mainly from the final product (poster, case file, fact-check report), with the logbook and Weeks 6 to 9 worksheets as supporting evidence. Strand 6 is judged from the Week 10 talk, including how the student answers a question.
- **Individuals, not groups.** The product is made by a group, but each student gets their own levels. Use what you saw in lessons, the student's logbook, who wrote which part, and how each student answers in the talk. If a student's own contribution to a strand is unclear, ask them one short question ("Why did you choose a bar chart?") before deciding.
- **Best fit.** Choose the level that describes most of the student's work in that strand. If the work sits between two levels, choose the lower one unless the student shows the higher level independently at least once.
- **Language.** A student who explains a strong idea in Chinese, with key words in English, can reach Secure or Excellent. Do not lower a level for grammar or accent.
- **Record** one letter per strand in the tracker: B, D, S or E (or N if a strand could not be judged).

## 2. What Secure looks like (for the teacher)

These are examples, not checklists. They show the Secure level in each year's final product so that teachers judge consistently. Data values in the examples are illustrative.

### Year 7 Ice Engineers: investigation poster and 2-minute Expo talk

| Strand | What Secure looks like |
|---|---|
| 1. Questioning and predicting 提问与预测 | Poster question: "Does the amount of sawdust change how much water an ice beam can hold before it snaps?" Prediction: "More sawdust will hold more water, because the fibres hold the ice together, like straw in mud bricks." |
| 2. Planning 计划设计 | Names the IV (amount of sawdust: 0, 5, 10, 15 g), the DV (volume of water in the bag when the beam snaps, in ml) and at least two controls (100 ml of water, same mould, same gap between the book stacks). Three beams of each type. Safety: gloves, catch tray, feet clear. |
| 3. Doing and recording 实施与记录 | Results table with headings and units (g, ml), all three repeats filled in, and a mean column. One beam that snapped early is circled as an anomaly, with a note ("it had a crack"). |
| 4. Analysing and concluding 分析与结论 | Line graph for amount of sawdust (or bar chart for type of fibre), axes labelled with units, means plotted accurately. Conclusion with data: "As the amount of sawdust increases, the water held increases, from a mean of 250 ml at 0 g to 600 ml at 15 g." |
| 5. Evaluating and critical thinking 评价与批判性思维 | "Some beams had air bubbles, so they snapped early and made our mean lower. Next time we would tap the moulds to remove bubbles and freeze every beam for the same time. We are fairly confident because our repeats were close, except one." |
| 6. Communicating 表达与交流 | The 2-minute talk follows the poster in order (question, method, results, conclusion). Uses "independent variable", "mean" and "anomaly" correctly. Answers one visitor question with a reason, using a sentence frame if needed. |

### Year 8 Science Detectives: case file and expert witness talk at the Court of Science

| Strand | What Secure looks like |
|---|---|
| 1. Questioning and predicting 提问与预测 | For the group's own test: "Which campus location matches the soil on the suspect's shoe?" Prediction: "The flower bed, because the shoe soil is dark and has small stones, like the flower bed soil." |
| 2. Planning 计划设计 | Collects a reference sample from every location. Names the IV (location of soil), what is observed or measured (layers and colour after settling) and controls (same mass of soil, same volume of water, identical jars, same settling time). Tests each sample twice or more. Goggles; hands washed. |
| 3. Doing and recording 实施与记录 | A results grid in which every sample is tested the same way, with units where there are measurements (for example, layer height in mm). Notes when a result looks wrong or a sample may be contaminated (for example, "dirty spoon used for sample C, repeated"). |
| 4. Analysing and concluding 分析与结论 | Matches scene evidence to references with a reason: "The scene ink made the same pattern of bands as pen B, so the note was written with a pen B. Two suspects carry pen B." Uses the footprint scatter graph to give a height estimate as a range ("about 150 to 170 cm"). |
| 5. Evaluating and critical thinking 评价与批判性思维 | Ranks each clue as strong, supporting or weak with a reason. Identifies the contaminated evidence and leaves it out. States a confidence level: "We are fairly confident it was Dr Huang, because she is the only suspect who matches all four reliable clues. No single clue points to her alone, and we left out the puddle because it was contaminated." |
| 6. Communicating 表达与交流 | The expert witness statement is in a clear order: what we tested, how, what it shows, how sure we are. Uses "reference sample", "evidence" and "contamination" correctly. Answers a jury question with evidence from the case file. |

### Year 9 Claim Busters: fact-check report (peer reviewed) and Conference talk

| Strand | What Secure looks like |
|---|---|
| 1. Questioning and predicting 提问与预测 | Claim: "Wet clothes make you lose heat faster in winter." Testable question: "Does a cup of warm water wrapped in wet cloth cool faster than one wrapped in dry cloth?" Prediction: "The wet cloth cup will cool faster, because water evaporating from the cloth takes heat away." |
| 2. Planning 计划设计 | Names the IV (wet or dry cloth), the DV (temperature drop in °C over 10 minutes) and controls (same cups, 200 ml of water, same starting temperature, same cloth, same place). Three cups of each type, and a sentence on why three. For human-participant claims: voluntary, anonymous, no pressure. Specific risk: warm water no hotter than about 60 °C, prepared by the teacher. |
| 3. Doing and recording 实施与记录 | A table of temperature (°C) against time (minutes) for every cup. Repeats done. A strange reading is noted and checked ("thermometer touching the cup wall; reading repeated"). |
| 4. Analysing and concluding 分析与结论 | Calculates mean drops and ranges. Line graph with both conditions, a key and labelled axes. Conclusion: "Wet-cloth cups dropped a mean of 14 °C; dry-cloth cups dropped 8 °C. The ranges did not overlap." Verdict on the three-point scale: Supported 有证据支持. |
| 5. Evaluating and critical thinking 评价与批判性思维 | Explains how one source of error could change the result, judges confidence ("fairly confident, but only three cups each, in one room"), and responds to at least one peer-review comment by changing the report. |
| 6. Communicating 表达与交流 | The Conference talk states the claim, the test, the data and the verdict with its uncertainty. Uses "sample size", "control" and "verdict" correctly. Answers a question from the floor with data rather than opinion. |

## 3. Award rules (for the teacher)

Every student who takes part receives a certificate. Award levels come from the six rubric strands. "Mostly" is defined exactly so that every teacher gets the same result from the same levels.

| Award | 中文 | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Gold** | 金奖 | At least 4 of the 6 strands at Excellent. |
| **Silver** | 银奖 | Not Gold, and at least 4 of the 6 strands at Secure or Excellent. |
| **Bronze** | 铜奖 | Not Gold or Silver, and at least 4 of the 6 strands at Developing, Secure or Excellent. |
| **Certificate of Participation** | 参与证书 | All other students who took part in the series. |

**Worked examples**

| Strands 1 to 6 | Count | Award |
|---|---|---|
| E, E, E, E, S, D | 4 Excellent | Gold |
| E, E, E, S, S, S | 3 Excellent (not Gold); 6 at Secure or above | Silver |
| S, S, S, D, D, E | 4 at Secure or above | Silver |
| S, S, D, D, D, B | 2 at Secure or above (not Silver); 5 at Developing or above | Bronze |
| D, D, B, B, B, D | 3 at Developing or above | Participation |

**Missing strands.** If a strand cannot be judged (for example, the student was absent for the Week 10 talk), record N. With 5 strands judged, the rule becomes "at least 4 of the 5". With 4 or fewer strands judged, use teacher judgement with the logbook, and write the reason in the tracker notes.

**Most Improved Scientist | 进步最大科学家奖** (one per class, in addition to the award level)
- Growth = Week 10 skills check score minus Week 1 skills check score.
- Eligible: students who sat both papers under normal conditions (not absent for either, no change in access arrangements between the two).
- The award goes to the eligible student with the highest growth. Growth must be at least +1.
- Tie-break 1: the higher share of the marks they could gain, that is growth ÷ (20 minus Week 1 score). For example, 8 → 14 (6 of 12 possible, 0.50) beats 2 → 8 (6 of 18 possible, 0.33).
- Tie-break 2: if still tied, give the award to both students.
- A student can receive Most Improved Scientist and any award level.

## 6. Peer feedback slip: two stars and a wish [Paste into Claude Design]

Create an A4 portrait, black-and-white-printable sheet of four peer feedback slips (A6 each, with dashed cut lines) for the Think Like a Scientist programme, using the Think Like a Scientist design system. No series colour (one version for all years). Each slip has two star icons and one wish icon (a simple outline star and a simple wand or speech bubble), drawn in line art for greyscale. Bilingual labels, English primary. Minimum 11 pt. Writing lines after each frame.

**Each slip**
- Title: Two stars and a wish / 两颗星和一个愿望
- Fields: For (group or name) 给 ________ | From 来自 ________
- ☆ Star 1 星星一: I liked ________ because ________. 我喜欢……，因为……。 (two lines)
- ☆ Star 2 星星二: The evidence was clear when ________. 证据很清楚的地方是……。 (two lines)
- ✦ Wish 愿望: Next time, you could ________. 下次你可以……。 (two lines)
- Small "ideas to look for" line in a lighter weight: question 问题 | fair test 公平测试 | graph 图表 | conclusion 结论 | evidence 证据 | how sure 把握程度

**How to use (for the teacher, not printed on the slip)**
- Week 10 (Year 7 Expo gallery walk, Year 8 Court of Science, Year 9 Conference), and Year 9 Week 9 peer review.
- Each student writes one slip for one other group. Model one good slip first ("I liked your graph because the axes have units").
- Feedback must be about the science and be kind and specific. Comments in Chinese are welcome; key words in English.
- Groups read their slips and may stick them on the back of the poster or into the case file or report. The slips are not marked, but they can support strand 5 evidence when a student's feedback shows strong critical thinking.

Print spec: 1 sheet per 4 students (6 sheets for a class of 24), plus spares.

## 7. Self-assessment slip [Paste into Claude Design]

Create an A5 portrait, black-and-white-printable self-assessment slip (two per A4 sheet, with a dashed cut line) for the Think Like a Scientist programme, using the Think Like a Scientist design system. No series colour (one version for all years). Six rows, one per rubric strand, each with four tick boxes. The four box headings use the official level names plus a student-friendly line. Bilingual, English primary. Minimum 11 pt. Include the "any language" thought-bubble icon beside the two writing prompts.

**Header**
- Title: How am I doing? / 我做得怎么样？
- Fields: Name 姓名 ________ | Class 班级 ____ | Date 日期 ____
- Instruction: Tick ONE box for each line. Be honest! 每行勾选一个。请诚实填写！

**Level headings**

| Beginning 起步 | Developing 发展 | Secure 达标 | Excellent 优秀 |
|---|---|---|---|
| I need help 我需要帮助 | I can do some of it 我能做到一部分 | I can do it 我能做到 | I can do it and explain why 我能做到并解释原因 |

**Statements**

| # | I can ... 我能…… |
|---|---|
| 1 | write a question I can test, and make a prediction with a reason. 提出可检验的问题，并有理由地预测。 |
| 2 | plan a fair test: what I change, what I measure, what I keep the same. 设计公平测试：改变什么、测量什么、保持什么不变。 |
| 3 | work carefully and record results in a table with units. 认真操作，并用带单位的表格记录结果。 |
| 4 | draw the right graph and use data in my conclusion. 画合适的图表，并用数据写结论。 |
| 5 | explain the problems in my test and how sure I am. 说明测试中的问题以及我有多大把握。 |
| 6 | present my findings clearly, using science words in English. 用英语科学词汇清楚地展示我的发现。 |

**Writing prompts** (two lines each, any language 可用中文)
- I am proud of ... 我为……感到自豪
- Next I want to get better at ... 接下来我想提高……

**How to use (for the teacher, not printed on the slip)**
- Students complete the slip in Week 9 (Think and Reflect) before the teacher decides rubric levels. It takes about 4 minutes.
- Compare the student's view with your judgement. A big gap (two levels or more) is worth a 1-minute conversation in Week 10.
- The two writing prompts can be copied into Section F of the skills report.
- Self-assessment does not change the rubric levels or the award.

Print spec: 1 slip per student (12 A4 sheets for a class of 24).
