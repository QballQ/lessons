## 1. Welcome and purpose

Think Like a Scientist is a ten-week enrichment programme for Years 7 to 9 in the bilingual domestic stream. Students' first language is Mandarin; English is their second language, with a wide range of confidence.

**What it is not.** It sits outside the national curriculum and has no exam focus. Nothing in it is "exam preparation", and there is no homework: all work, including the logbook, is done in class.

**What it is for.** The programme aims to:

- spark curiosity about how the world works;
- teach scientific thinking (observation, testable questions, variables, fair tests, data, conclusions);
- teach critical thinking (weighing evidence, spotting bias, judging how confident to be);
- build confidence in using scientific English, with Chinese as a support, not a barrier;
- give every student the experience of planning, carrying out and presenting a project of their own.

**The shape of every series.** Weeks 1 to 5 build thinking skills through short, hands-on tasks. Week 5 is a checkpoint. Weeks 6 to 10 put those skills to work on one project that students plan and own, ending with a public presentation in Week 10.

**Design principles you will notice in every lesson**

| Principle | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| Same lesson shape every week | Five phases, same order, same icons. Students know what comes next. |
| Low floor, high ceiling | Every task has a way in for students with less English (Core) and a way up (Stretch, Challenge). |
| Bilingual by design | Key words in English and Chinese; instructions in English with key words glossed; safety lines in full in both languages. |
| Kitchen-cupboard kit | Cheap, everyday materials. No specialist equipment. |
| Winter thread | Ice structures (Y7), a frozen-notebook mystery (Y8), winter claims (Y9). |
| Handover-ready | Every lesson has a one-page plan, a kit card, an answer key and notes for you. |

## 3. How a 40-minute lesson runs

### The five phases

| Phase | 中文 | Min | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| Do Now | 课前任务 | 4 | Retrieval of last week's words, or a puzzle. On the board as students arrive; they start straight away. |
| Hook | 引入 | 3 | A question, demo, story or thought experiment that creates curiosity. |
| Learn | 新知 | 7 | The key idea, with the bilingual vocabulary panel. |
| Do | 实践 | 18 | The practical or thinking task, with Core 基础 / Stretch 提高 / Challenge 挑战 tiers. |
| Think and Reflect | 思辨与反思 | 8 | A short critical-thinking discussion (pair talk in any language, report in English with a frame), then exit ticket and logbook line. |

The lesson plan in Section 1 of each lesson file gives minute-by-minute timings, slide numbers and an "If time is short" line. Use it.

### Week 1 (assessment) adjusted timings

| Year 7 | Min | Year 8 | Min | Year 9 | Min |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Skills check | 15 | Skills check (in place of Do Now) | 15 | Welcome, groups and roles | 2 |
| Interest survey | 3 | Interest survey | 3 | Skills check | 15 |
| Hook | 3 | Hook (case briefing, Song Ci) | 4 | Interest survey | 3 |
| Learn | 4 | Learn | 4 | Hook | 3 |
| Do (footprint puzzle) | 10 | Do (scene map) | 10 | Learn | 5 |
| Think and Reflect | 5 | Think and Reflect | 4 | Do (claim sort) | 8 |
| | | | | Think and Reflect | 4 |

Week 1 has one short activity only. Year 7's mystery cups move to the Week 2 Do Now. Start the skills check the moment students sit down.

### Week 5 (checkpoint) adjusted timings

| Year 7 | Min | Year 8 | Min | Year 9 | Min |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Do Now | 3 | Checkpoint (in place of Do Now) | 10 | Checkpoint (in place of Do Now) | 10 |
| Checkpoint | 10 | Hook | 2 | Hook (Tu Youyou) | 3 |
| Hook | 2 | Learn | 5 | Do part A: lucky pen trial | 7 |
| Learn | 5 | Do (indicator, cups ready) | 15 | Learn (debrief) | 6 |
| Do (graphing) | 14 | Think and Reflect | 8 | Do part B: results | 5 |
| Think and Reflect | 6 | | | Think and Reflect (ethics) | 9 |

Year 9 runs the trial before Learn on purpose, so students do not know about placebos until after the test.

### Week 10 (final assessment) outline timings

Skills check 15 minutes; talks about 2 minutes per group (about 15 minutes in total); survey 3 minutes and close 2 minutes. Posters, case files and reports go on display for a gallery walk at break or in a later session. Exact timings are in each Week 10 lesson file.

### Routines that make 40 minutes work

- **Kit trays ready before students arrive.** Set out one tray per group from the kit card (Section 5 of each lesson file). Anything slow (templates, indicator, ice, freezing mixtures, samples) is prepared in advance (see Section 8).
- **Fixed groups and tables.** Six groups of 3 or 4, seated together from the first minute of Week 1. Groups stay the same for Weeks 1 to 5. Project groups for Weeks 6 to 10 may be the same or re-formed in Week 6; record them in the tracker (column F).
- **Group roles, rotated weekly.** Role cards on every table.

| Role | 中文 | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Lead Scientist | 首席科学家 | Keeps the group on task and on time |
| Measurer | 测量员 | Takes the readings |
| Recorder | 记录员 | Writes results in the table |
| Safety Officer | 安全员 | Checks the kit card and safety rules |
| Reporter | 汇报员 | Shares the group's findings with the class |

In a group of 3, the Lead Scientist also reports (outline rule). In a group of 4 there are five roles for four people: let the Lead Scientist also report, or follow the group's existing arrangement in the handover note.

- **Scientist's Logbook 科学日志.** A slim A5 booklet, one page per week: What I did 我做了什么 / What I found 我发现了什么 / New words 新词汇 / A question I still have 我还想知道. Written in the last minutes of Think and Reflect, never as homework. Collect logbooks at the end of every lesson and keep them in the class box. Year 8: if time runs out, that week's evidence log row in the case file counts as the logbook entry. Year 9 starts the logbook in Week 2.
- **Exit tickets.** Every lesson ends with a short exit ticket (strip at the foot of the worksheet). Read them before the next lesson: they tell you who needs the Do Now recap and who is ready for Stretch.

## 4. Teaching second-language learners

### The bilingual approach

- **Vocabulary is bilingual; instructions are English with key words glossed.** Full translation would let students read only the Chinese, so most text stays in English.
- **Safety instructions are given in full in both languages**, on the safety slide and the kit card. Read them aloud.
- **6 to 10 key words per lesson**, shown English | 中文 in a vocabulary panel on every slide and worksheet, and recycled in the next Do Now.
- **"Think in any language, record key words in English."** Pair talk in Chinese is welcome in Think phases and wherever the lesson plan says so. Reporting back uses English sentence frames.
- **Mark the science, not the English.** Communication is judged on clarity of the science, not grammar or accent. A strong idea explained in Chinese with key words in English can reach Secure 达标 or Excellent 优秀.

### Tiers: Core 基础, Stretch 提高, Challenge 挑战

Tiers are **not** ability groups. Every student starts with Core and moves up when ready. A student with strong science and limited English can reach Challenge using frames and the My thinking box. Do not assign tiers by name.

| Tier | Typical tasks |
|---|---|
| Core 基础 | Cloze, gap fill with a word bank, matching, labelling, sorting, completing a table |
| Stretch 提高 | Explanations with sentence frames, short answers, completing a graph |
| Challenge 挑战 | Open questions, designing a test, predicting and justifying, critical-thinking prompts |

### Sentence frames

Each lesson plan lists its frames; slides show them in the Think phase. The core set:

| Year 7 | Year 8 | Year 9 |
|---|---|---|
| I observe ___. I infer ___ because ___. | The evidence shows ___. This supports / does not support ___ because ___. | The data suggests ___; however, ___. |
| I predict ___ because ___. | This sample matches ___ because ___. | To be more confident, we would need ___. |
| As the ___ increases, the ___ ___. | This evidence is strong / weak because ___. | This could also be explained by ___. |
| If I did it again, I would ___. | One problem with this evidence is ___. | Our verdict is ___ because ___. |

Point to the frame, say it once with the gap filled, then let students try. Leave frames on the board.

### The My thinking box (any language, 可用中文)

Every worksheet has a dashed "My thinking" box with one question. Students may answer in Chinese, English or both. Use it to see what students understand when their English cannot yet show it. It counts as evidence for the rubric. The fold-under word bank at the foot of the worksheet is a choice, not a crutch: students decide whether to look.

### Vocabulary traps

Each lesson plan lists likely traps (for example "mean" as average, "light" meaning not heavy, "range" as one number, "blind test"). Check the list before teaching; it takes one minute and saves ten.

### What to do with a silent student

1. Silence is normal in the first weeks of a second-language class. Do not single the student out in front of the class.
2. Give a role that needs action more than talk: Measurer or Recorder.
3. Allow rehearsal: pair talk in Chinese first, then the frame in English to a partner, then (later) to the class.
4. Accept non-verbal answers: thumbs, O/I gestures, vote cards, pointing to the word on the vocabulary panel.
5. Read the My thinking box and the exit ticket: the student may understand far more than they say.
6. For talks in Week 10, let the student present the part with numbers or a graph, with a cue card. Note in the tracker (column G, "Language support") and in the report comment what helped.

### How to stretch a fluent student

1. Move them to Challenge as soon as Core is secure; do not hold them on Core to "help others".
2. Ask for precision: "What exactly would show you were wrong?", "How confident are you, and why?", "What else could explain it?"
3. Give them a vocabulary job: add two new words to the class glossary with a student-friendly meaning.
4. Ask them to model a frame, then to answer without one.
5. In projects, ask for a second variable, a wider range, more repeats or an uncertainty estimate.
6. Keep them inside the group: as Reporter, they must explain the group's reasoning, not their own.

## 5. Assessment, step by step

### The components

| Component | When | Measures | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Final product (poster / case file / fact-check report) | Weeks 9 to 10 | Rubric strands 1 to 5 | Main judgement of scientific thinking |
| Final presentation (Expo talk / expert witness / conference talk) | Week 10 | Rubric strand 6 | Communication and confidence |
| Skills check (same 15-minute paper) | Weeks 1 and 10 | Core skills, out of 20 | Growth over ten weeks |
| Checkpoint (10 minutes) | Week 5 | Question types 1 to 6, out of 12 | Handover data |
| Scientist's Logbook | Weekly | Engagement, reflection, vocabulary | Supporting evidence; handover record |
| Interest survey (same in Weeks 1 and 10) | Weeks 1 and 10 | Interest and confidence | Programme impact |

Papers and mark schemes are in `assessment/`: `Skills_Check_Y7.md`, `Skills_Check_Y8.md`, `Skills_Check_Y9.md`, `Checkpoint_Y7.md` (and Y8, Y9), `Interest_Survey.md`, `Rubric_and_Reporting.md`, `Tracker_Spec.md`.

### Week 1: baseline

1. Print one skills check (year-specific) and one survey per student, in class order.
2. Skills check: 1 minute to give out and write names, 15 minutes working, 5-minute warning, collect at once.
3. You may read any question aloud in English once or twice and explain everyday words. Do **not** explain science words (variable, anomaly, mean, range, evidence, claim): knowing them is what the paper measures.
4. Survey straight after: 3 minutes. Read each statement aloud in English once. Say: "There are no right or wrong answers. This does not change your award." 没有对错之分，不影响你的奖项，请如实填写。
5. Mark with the mark scheme within the week. No half marks. Accept Chinese for explanations (Questions 4, 8, 9, 10). Ignore spelling and grammar if the science is clear.
6. Enter scores in the tracker (Section "Entering data" below).

> **Key point: do not go through or return the Week 1 skills check papers.** Do not hand them back, show answers or discuss questions before Week 10. The same paper is used again in Week 10; going through it destroys the growth measure. File the papers in the class assessment folder and record where in the handover note.

### Week 5: checkpoint

1. 10 minutes working plus 1 minute to give out and collect. Timing differs by year: Year 7 after the Do Now; Years 8 and 9 in place of the Do Now (Year 9 before the lucky pen trial).
2. Same language rules as the skills check. Accept Chinese for Question 4.
3. Mark out of 12 with the checkpoint mark scheme. Enter in tracker column Q before the handover note is written.
4. Unlike the skills check, you **may** go through the checkpoint answers. Week 6 is a good moment. The checkpoint is not reused.
5. The incoming teacher observes this lesson.

### Week 9: preparing to judge

1. Students complete the **self-assessment slip** (about 4 minutes, Think and Reflect) before you decide rubric levels. It does not change levels or awards; a gap of two levels or more is worth a 1-minute conversation in Week 10.
2. Year 9: peer review with the reviewer checklist. All years: peer feedback slips ("two stars and a wish") are used in Week 10 (and Year 9 Week 9).
3. Start noting evidence for strands 1 to 5 from drafts, Weeks 6 to 9 worksheets and logbooks.

### Week 10: final assessment

1. Skills check: the **same paper** as Week 1, fresh copies, same rules and timing.
2. Talks (Expo, Court of Science, Conference): about 2 minutes per group plus one question. Judge strand 6 here.
3. Survey: fresh copies, 3 minutes, before certificates.
4. After the lesson: mark skills checks, enter all Week 10 data, decide rubric levels, check awards, prepare reports and certificates. Only now may Week 1 and Week 10 papers be returned or discussed.

### Marking the skills checks and checkpoints

- Use the mark scheme table in each paper's Section 2: Answer | Accept | Do not accept | Marks.
- Mark the science, not the English. No half marks. If a student gives two answers and one is wrong, no mark (unless the question asks for more than one).
- Skills check maximum marks per question: Q1 2 | Q2 1 | Q3 3 | Q4 2 | Q5 2 | Q6 3 | Q7 1 | Q8 2 | Q9 2 | Q10 2 (total 20). Optionally enter question-by-question marks on the tracker's Question_analysis tab: any question below 50% in Week 1 is a skill to build in Weeks 2 to 9.
- Indicative skills check bands (for the report, not for awards): 0 to 5 Beginning 起步; 6 to 10 Developing 发展; 11 to 15 Secure 达标; 16 to 20 Excellent 优秀.

### Using the rubric

Six strands, four levels: **Beginning 起步 | Developing 发展 | Secure 达标 | Excellent 优秀.** Strands: 1 Questioning and predicting 提问与预测; 2 Planning 计划设计; 3 Doing and recording 实施与记录; 4 Analysing and concluding 分析与结论; 5 Evaluating and critical thinking 评价与批判性思维; 6 Communicating 表达与交流. Full descriptors and "What Secure looks like" for each year are in `assessment/Rubric_and_Reporting.md`, Sections 1 and 2.

- **Evidence.** Strands 1 to 5: mainly the final product, supported by the logbook and Weeks 6 to 9 worksheets. Strand 6: the Week 10 talk, including how the student answers a question.
- **Individuals, not groups.** Each student gets their own levels. Use what you saw, the logbook, who wrote which part and the talk. If unclear, ask one short question ("Why did you choose a bar chart?").
- **Best fit.** Choose the level that describes most of the work. If between two levels, choose the lower unless the student showed the higher level independently at least once.
- **Language.** Never lower a level for grammar or accent.
- **Record** one letter per strand: B, D, S, E, or N if it could not be judged.

### Award rules

| Award | 中文 | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Gold | 金奖 | At least 4 of the 6 strands at Excellent |
| Silver | 银奖 | Not Gold, and at least 4 of the 6 at Secure or Excellent |
| Bronze | 铜奖 | Not Gold or Silver, and at least 4 of the 6 at Developing or above |
| Certificate of Participation | 参与证书 | All other students who took part |

- **Missing strands.** With 5 strands judged, the threshold stays at 4 (of 5). With 4 or fewer judged, the tracker shows "Check": decide using the logbook, type the award over the formula and write the reason in Notes.
- **Most Improved Scientist 进步最大科学家奖** (one per class, in addition to the award level): highest growth (Week 10 minus Week 1 score), at least +1, among students who sat both papers under normal conditions with no change in access arrangements. Tie-break 1: the higher share of marks they could gain, that is growth divided by (20 minus Week 1 score). Tie-break 2: if still tied, both receive it. The tracker marks candidates "Y"; apply tie-breaks by hand.

### The interest survey

Six statements on a five-face scale, scored 1 (big frown, left) to 5 (big smile, right). Names are needed so Weeks 1 and 10 can be compared; results are only ever shared as class averages. Two faces ticked: enter the lower score. Blank: leave blank. The optional open question is not scored. A class change of +0.3 or more on a statement is worth reporting; report Statements 4 (science English confidence) and 5 (checking evidence) first.

### Reports and certificates

- **Skills report** (one page per student, bilingual; template in `Rubric_and_Reporting.md`, Section 5): strand levels, Week 1 / Week 5 / Week 10 scores, growth, project, award, teacher comment (about 50 words: one strength with an example, one next step, one sentence on attitude or growth; short plain sentences a parent can put through a translation app), optional student reflection copied from the self-assessment slip.

## 6. Practical work and safety

> **Key point: this guide does not replace the school's own risk assessment.** Every practical must be covered by the school's risk assessment process before it runs. The kit card in each lesson file lists hazards and controls in English and Chinese; read the safety slide aloud in both languages.

### General rules (every practical)

- Check the allergy list before any lesson with food materials (flour, cornflour, other powders, lemon, dough). Ask the school nurse or form tutors in Week 1.
- **No tasting, eating or drinking** anything in a practical, ever, including ice, water and food powders.
- **Hot water is teacher-only.** The teacher prepares warm water (about 50 °C for Y7 Plan B, no hotter than about 60 °C for Y9) and indicator before the lesson. Students never handle boiling water or a kettle.
- **Cold:** gloves for handling ice for more than a minute; ice and salt freezing mixtures get cold enough to hurt skin, so handle tubes, not the mixture.
- Goggles whenever the kit card says so (vinegar, iodine, indicator, freezing mixtures).
- Mop spills straight away; wet floors are the most likely injury.
- No standing on chairs or tables.
- Thermometers: use spirit-filled (red) or digital thermometers only, never mercury; broken glass is cleared by the teacher.
- Wash hands after every practical.
- The Safety Officer checks the kit card at the start of every Do phase.

## 7. Freezer: Plan A and Plan B

Freezer access is not confirmed. The programme does not depend on it: both plans are fully built, use the same skills, rubric and final products, and Plan B does not weaken the assessment.

### The Week 4 decision

- **Decide by Week 4 (w/c 2 November 2026)**, before any ice is needed. Check the canteen, boarding house and staff-room fridges.
- **What Plan A actually needs:** Y7 Week 6, one or two trays of ice cubes (about 24); Y7 Weeks 7 to 8, space for about 54 thin strips (5 mm thick) for one week, in one or two shallow trays (a domestic fridge's freezer compartment is enough); Y9 Week 6, about 2 to 3 kg of ice on the day (could come from the canteen).
- **What Plan B needs most:** 6 to 12 thermometers. Count them in the prep room in Week 1, whatever the freezer outcome.
- The current teacher records the decision (Plan A or Plan B, and the date) on each handover note.

### What changes in each affected lesson

| Lesson | Plan A (freezer) | Plan B (no freezer) | Plan B needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Y7 W6 | **Ice cube rescue:** wrap identical ice cubes in materials; measure meltwater after 30 minutes | **Keep it warm:** wrap cups of warm water (about 50 °C, teacher-prepared) in the same materials; measure the temperature drop over 15 minutes. Same fair-test skills, same snowman concept cartoon | 6 thermometers |
| Y7 W7 to 8 | **Pykrete:** fibre-reinforced thin ice strips; strength or melting test | **Straw-brick science:** fibre-reinforced dough strips (2 cups flour, 1 cup salt, about 1 cup water) with the same fibres, as the same 5 mm thin strips (an 18 g ball per strip), air-dried for a week. Strength test only, same water-bag method. Pykrete stays as the story hook, alongside straw in mud bricks | Flour, salt, a dry shelf for a week |
| Y9 W6 | **Mpemba test:** hot vs cold water in an ice and salt freezing mixture | **Cooling-curve test:** cups of water at about 60 °C and 35 °C; record temperature every minute; plot cooling curves. Year 8 does not need a freezer. The optional Week 10 frosty notebook prop can come from any staff fridge freezer compartment, or be skipped.

## 9. Year 8 case: keeping the Frozen Notebook consistent

**Where the Case Bible is:** `Y8_Science_Detectives/Y8_00_Case_Bible.md`. It is the single source of truth for the case. If a lesson file disagrees with it, the Case Bible wins (and tell the programme lead). It contains the solution: **keep it out of student hands**, never project it, never leave it on a desk.

### Rules for keeping the case consistent

- **Never reveal the solution early.** Do not confirm or deny any student's theory before Week 10. When groups name a suspect, ask: "How confident are you, and what evidence could change your mind?"
- Use the exact names, roles, heights, foot lengths, pens, powders and liquids in the Case Bible. Do not add a suspect. Say the names the same way every lesson.
- The scene footprint is 24 cm; map squares (E2, F2, F4, I2, I3, G5) never change.
- The mop bucket and "Wet floor" sign stay on every scene map.
- The **cleaning log card is not shown before Week 9**, and only after groups have built their first evidence board.
- Pen B never sits on a student table except as the labelled reference pen.
- Students who spot the mop bucket or "safe and cold" have found real clues: praise them, note their names, confirm nothing.
- The tone is kind: a missing notebook, nobody hurt. The ending has the culprit apologise and co-present.
- Weeks 7 and 8 student tests are designed **not** to change the solution (soil and paper towel match places everyone used; Ink 2 and Powder 2 confirm earlier results). Real results that look odd are handled as "contamination or error to evaluate", not as new story.
- The Case Bible's teacher prep sheet (Section 12) and science reference (Section 13) cover how to stage each clue.

### What students have found by the end of Week 5

Four suspects: Lily Chen (lab technician), Max Zhang (Year 12 student), Chef Zhou (head chef), Dr Ruby Huang (visiting scientist). Professor Frost owns the notebook.

| Week | Evidence | Result | Who still matches |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | Fingerprint on the glass | Loop (weak: about 2 in 3 people) | Lily, Max, Dr Huang (Chef Zhou has a whorl) |
| 3 | Note ink | Pen brand B | Max, Dr Huang |
| 4 | White powder | Baking soda | Chef Zhou, Dr Huang |
| 5 | Puddle | Alkali, about pH 9 | Lily (soap solution), Chef Zhou (baking soda solution) |

Tally after Week 5: Lily 2 of 4, Max 2 of 4, Chef Zhou 2 of 4, Dr Huang 3 of 4. Many groups were sure after Week 4; the liquid result in Week 5 deliberately does not fit, creating a conflict. Students' explanations are on the **Ideas Parking Board** (photographed in Week 5): keep them for Week 9. Evidence log rows 1 to 5 and the suspect tracker are filled in in each case file; rows for Weeks 6 to 10 are yours.

**What comes next:** Week 6 footprint (24 cm, height range about 150 to 170 cm) matches Lily and Dr Huang. Weeks 7 to 8 change nothing. Week 9 reveals the puddle was mop water, so it is set aside, and only Dr Huang matches every reliable clue. Week 10 reads the true ending after the jury's verdict. If the verdict is wrong, praise good reasoning and walk back through the evidence board together.

## 12. Troubleshooting and FAQ

| Problem | What to do |
|---|---|
| A practical fails (spinners will not spin, chromatography does not separate, nothing fizzes) | Use the backup in the lesson plan: Y8 W3 teacher's pre-test strips as "lab results"; spare spinner sheets; a teacher demo. Then turn the failure into an evaluation question: "What went wrong, and how would we fix it?" That is real science. |
| Data is poor or missing | Use the sample data set in the lesson file (for example Y7 W4 sample data card, also used in Y7 W5; Y9 W4 sample class data card; Y9 W2 sample data in the answer key). Groups using sample data are not behind; note it in the tracker. |
| A student is absent for part of the project | They join their group's product and do the parts they can; record "N" for any strand that cannot be judged (award threshold becomes 4 of 5; with 4 or fewer judged, decide with the logbook and note why). |
| A student is absent for the Week 1 or Week 10 skills check | Leave the score blank and write "ABS" in Notes. They are not eligible for Most Improved. Do not run a catch-up paper weeks later under different conditions. |
| A whole group is absent on test day (Week 8) | Other groups run an extra repeat of the missing group's sample, or the group uses pooled class data in Week 9. |
| The class runs out of time | Follow "If time is short" in the lesson plan. Never cut: assessment timing, safety briefings, Y8 secrecy, Y9 W5 reveal and withdrawal. Logbook can shrink to one line. |
| Week 10 is too full | Talks 2 minutes strictly; posters and reports displayed for a gallery walk at break or later; survey and skills check are fixed. |
| The freezer is unavailable or fails mid-project | Switch to Plan B (Section 7). If Y7 samples are lost after Week 7, run the Week 8 test as Plan B dough strips a week later or use the melting test on fresh ice cubes; record on the tracker notes and tell the lead. |
| Thermometers are short | Pool readings: one thermometer per two groups, or a teacher-read class set of readings. Ask the prep room early (Week 1). |
| Students only speak Chinese in group work | That is fine during thinking. Before reporting, give each group the frame on a card and 1 minute to rehearse in English. Ask the Reporter to use at least three key words from the vocabulary panel. Praise English key words used in Chinese talk. |
| A fluent student finishes early | Challenge tier first; then precision questions ("What would show you were wrong?"), extra repeats or a second variable, a glossary job, or explaining the group's reasoning as Reporter (Section 4). |
| A student will not be measured (Y8 W6, Y9 W4) | Measuring is voluntary. They take the Measurer or Recorder role; use sample data for their row. |
| A Y8 student asks "Is it Dr Huang?" (or anyone) | Neither confirm nor deny. "How confident are you? What evidence could change your mind?" |
| Someone suggests going through the Week 1 skills check | No. It is reused in Week 10. Only discuss it after Week 10. |
| An allergy is reported on the day | Remove the material for that student or group, give a non-food role, and tell the programme lead. Check the allergy list before every food-material lesson. |
| A Y9 group wants a claim not in the bank | Only bank claims. No health, medical, diet, traditional medicine, eating or drinking claims, or claims about named people or groups. |
| Chinese text on a slide looks wrong | All Chinese is draft until the native-speaker check. Note it and tell the programme lead before reprinting. |
