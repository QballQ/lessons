## 1. Lesson plan (one page, for the teacher)

- **Big idea:** Scientists separate what they can see (observations) from what they think it means (inferences), and they change their story when new evidence appears.
- **Learning goals:**
  - I can tell the difference between an observation and an inference.
  - I can change my inference when I get new evidence.
- **Key words:**

| English | 中文 | Student-friendly meaning |
|---|---|---|
| scientist | 科学家 | a person who finds things out by looking carefully and testing ideas |
| observation | 观察 | something you see, hear, touch, smell or measure |
| inference | 推断 | what you think is happening, using your observations |
| evidence | 证据 | the facts that help us decide what is true |
| clue | 线索 | a small piece of evidence |
| footprint | 脚印 | the mark a foot or paw makes in snow or mud |
| track | 足迹 | a line of footprints made by one animal |

- **Timeline (adjusted for Week 1 assessment):** the standard Do Now is replaced by the skills check and survey; Hook, Learn, Do and Think are shortened. Total 40 minutes.

| Phase | Min | Teacher does | Students do | Resource |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Assessment: skills check | 15 | Seats groups from the plan as students arrive; hands out the skills check; reads each question stem aloud in English; does not help with answers | Complete the baseline skills check alone, in silence | Slide 1 to 2; `assessment/Skills_Check_Y7.md` |
| Assessment: interest survey | 3 | Collects skills checks; hands out the survey; reads each statement aloud | Colour one face for each statement | Slide 3; `assessment/Interest_Survey.md` |
| Hook | 3 | Shows Stage 1 of the snow tracks: "Who walked here? What do scientists do?" Introduces the series in one sentence | Quick pair guesses (any language) | Slides 4 to 5 |
| Learn | 4 | Teaches observation vs inference with the melted snowman picture; models the two sentence frames | Say which statements are observations and which are inferences (thumbs up / thumbs down) | Slides 6 to 7; worksheet Core A |
| Do | 10 | Gives each group the Stage 1 card, then Stage 2 (after about 3 min), then Stage 3 (after about 6 min); circulates and pushes "How do you know?" | Footprint puzzle: write observations and inferences for each stage; change the story as evidence appears | Slides 8 to 9; footprint puzzle cards; worksheet Core B, Stretch, Challenge |
| Think and Reflect | 5 | Takes two stories from Reporters; asks "Can we be 100% sure?"; explains the logbook; collects exit tickets | Exit ticket; write the first logbook line | Slides 10 to 11; exit ticket; logbook |

- **Tiers:**
  - Core: sort six statements into observation or inference (word bank); complete the footprint table with frames for each stage.
  - Stretch: explain how your story changed, with the frame "At first I inferred ___. Now I infer ___ because ___."
  - Challenge: write two different stories that both fit all the evidence, and name one extra piece of evidence that would decide between them.
- **Language support:**
  - Frames: "I observe ___." "I infer ___ because ___." "At first I inferred ___. Now I infer ___ because ___."
  - Chinese talk welcome during the puzzle and the "Can we be sure?" discussion. Reporters share in English with frames.
  - Vocabulary traps: "observe" and "observation" both translate as 观察 (verb and noun); "infer" (推断) is often confused with "guess" (猜). An inference uses evidence; a guess does not. "Track" here means a line of footprints, not a running track or train track.
- **Misconceptions to watch for:**
  - "An inference is just a guess." No: an inference must be based on observations.
  - "If I see it, I know what happened." Observations are facts; stories about them are inferences.
  - "Changing your mind means you were wrong or bad at science." Changing an inference when new evidence arrives is exactly what scientists do.
- **Safety:** No practical hazards beyond normal classroom rules.
- **If time is short:** run the puzzle with Stage 1 and Stage 3 only (skip Stage 2); students write one observation and one inference per stage. If the logbook is squeezed, students write only "New words" today.
- **Assessment and handover links:**
  - Baseline skills check score (out of 20) entered in the progress tracker.
  - Interest survey responses entered in the tracker (Week 1 baseline for comparison in Week 10).
  - Exit ticket: one observation and one inference (quick check of the lesson goal).
  - Logbook started (first page).

## 4. Answer key (for the teacher)

**Slide 7 (thumbs):** 1 observation; 2 inference; 3 observation (a measurement is an observation); 4 inference.

**Core A:**
1. O
2. I (we did not see anyone build it)
3. O
4. I (the sun is an observation, but "because the sun came out" is an inference)
5. O
6. I (a snowman cannot feel; this is not even a good inference, a useful discussion point)

**Core B (model answers; accept any observation that can be seen on the card and any inference that is linked to an observation):**

| Stage | Observations (examples) | Inferences (examples) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | There are two kinds of footprints. The big prints have four toes and claw marks. The small prints have three thin toes. The small prints are in pairs. The big prints are about 30 cm apart. The small prints start next to a tree. | A big animal with paws (a dog or fox) is walking, because the prints are evenly spaced. A small bird is hopping, because its prints are in pairs and start next to a tree. |
| 2 | The big prints are further apart and deeper. The big prints come in groups of four. The small prints stop at some small dots (seeds). Around the seeds the small prints point in many directions. | The big animal started to run, because its prints are further apart. The bird was eating seeds, because its prints go in many directions around them. The big animal is going towards the bird. |
| 3 | The two tracks meet. The snow is messy where they meet. There is one feather. There are wing-shaped marks in the snow. No small prints leave. The big prints leave walking again. | Story A: the fox caught the bird, because there is a feather and no small prints leave. Story B: the bird flew away, because there are wing marks and birds do not need to walk away. |

**Stretch (Secure example):** "At first I inferred that a bird and a dog were just walking in the snow. Now I infer that a fox chased the bird because the fox's prints get further apart, and they meet at the messy snow."

**Challenge (Secure example):**
- Story 1: The fox ran at the bird while it was eating and caught it. The feather fell out and there are no small prints leaving.
- Story 2: The bird saw the fox and flew away just in time. It made wing marks in the snow when it took off and lost one feather. The fox sniffed around and walked away.
- Extra evidence (any one, with a reason): "Look for the bird's tracks somewhere else nearby, because if it flew away it might land and leave new tracks." / "Look for more feathers or blood, because a caught bird would lose more feathers." / "Look for a camera in the trees." / "Follow the fox's tracks to see if they lead to its den with the bird."
- Excellent level also notes that both stories fit, so we can only say which is more likely, and that the extra evidence must be something both stories predict differently.

**My thinking (guidance):** Good answers say we cannot be completely sure, but we can be more or less confident depending on the evidence; more evidence makes us more confident. Accept answers in Chinese. Note students who mention "testing" or "more evidence" for the handover note (ready for challenge).

**Exit ticket:**
- Observation (any one): The umbrella is wet. Water is dripping on the mat. The umbrella is next to the door.
- Inference (any one, with a linked reason): It is raining (or snowing) outside because the umbrella is wet. Someone has just come in because the umbrella is still dripping.
- Secure: one correct observation and one inference linked to an observation with "because".

### 6a. Footprint puzzle cards (three reveal stages)

Create a set of three A4 landscape puzzle cards for Year 7 Week 1, "The footprint puzzle | 脚印之谜", using the Think Like a Scientist design system, Ice Engineers series. Black-and-white printable line drawings. Print spec: 6 sets per class (18 cards), single-sided, ideally on card or laminated so they can be reused.

**The whole scene (for the illustrator).** A top-down (bird's-eye) view of a flat area of fresh, smooth snow, drawn as a white background with light grey shading for texture. The scene is one long picture divided into three equal vertical thirds: left, middle and right. Card 1 shows the left third only. Card 2 shows the left and middle thirds. Card 3 shows the whole scene. Each card uses the same scale so the parts line up exactly. A scale bar "10 cm" sits in the bottom-right corner of every card. No people. No blood or injured animals anywhere.

Two kinds of prints appear:
- **Large prints (fox):** each print is an oval heel pad with four oval toe pads in front and a small claw dot in front of each toe, about 5 cm long at the scene's scale. Drawn in dark grey.
- **Small prints (bird, e.g. a magpie):** each print has three thin toes pointing forward and one thin toe pointing back, like a small arrow, about 4 cm long. They always appear in side-by-side pairs, showing hopping. Drawn in dark grey, thinner lines.

**Card 1: Stage 1 (left third only)**
- Header: "Stage 1 | 第一阶段"
- A dark circle with a ring of snow around it at the top-left corner: the trunk of a tree seen from above, with a few thin branch shadows.
- Large prints enter from the left edge at mid-height and walk towards the right in a slightly zigzag line: 8 prints, evenly spaced (about 30 cm apart at scale), all the same depth.
- Small prints start right beside the tree trunk and hop down and to the right in a gentle curve: 6 pairs, evenly spaced (about 10 cm between pairs).
- At the right edge of the card both tracks are still separate: the large track is at mid-height, the small track is in the upper half.
- Prompt box at the foot of the card: "Observe: list 3 things you can see. Count and measure. | 观察 Infer: what do you think is happening? Why? | 推断"

**Card 2: Stage 2 (left and middle thirds)**
- Header: "Stage 2 | 第二阶段"
- Everything from Card 1, unchanged, in the left third.
- In the middle third, the large prints change: the gap between prints grows from about 30 cm to about 60 cm, and the prints now land in tight groups of four (a bounding pattern). Each print is drawn darker and slightly bigger, with small ridges of pushed-up snow around it, so it looks deeper. The track heads straight towards the right-hand edge of the middle third, slightly upwards.
- The small prints keep hopping and reach a small scattered patch of tiny dots (seeds and crumbs, about 15 cm across) close to the right edge of the middle third, in the upper half. Around the seed patch the small prints go in many different directions (about 8 pairs pointing different ways), showing the bird moving about while feeding.
- The large track has not yet reached the seed patch: it stops at the right edge of the card about 40 cm (at scale) from the patch.
- Prompt box: "What is new? 有什么新发现？ Does your story still fit? 你的故事还成立吗？ Change it if you need to."

**Card 3: Stage 3 (the whole scene)**
- Header: "Stage 3 | 第三阶段"
- Everything from Card 2, unchanged, in the left and middle thirds.
- At the seed patch (now fully visible where the middle and right thirds meet): a roughly round area of churned-up snow about 40 cm across, drawn with scribbly grey shading. Inside it, blurred partial prints of both sizes are mixed together.
- On the upper-right side of the churned area: a pair of fan-shaped wing marks pressed into the snow, one on each side of a small oval body mark, each fan made of 6 to 8 long feather lines spreading outwards (like a bird's take-off print). The small footprints end at these wing marks. No small prints appear anywhere after them.
- One small dark feather (about 6 cm long at scale) lies inside the churned area.
- The large prints leave the churned area towards the right. Close to the area they make one small loop (as if the animal sniffed around), then walk away in an even line (about 30 cm apart again, normal depth) and exit at the right edge.
- Prompt box: "Two stories? 两种故事？ Which clues support each one? 哪些线索支持哪个故事？ What extra evidence would help you decide? 还需要什么证据？"

**Teacher note printed small on the back of Card 3 (not for students):** "Both stories fit the evidence. Feather and no small prints leaving: the fox may have caught the bird. Wing marks: the bird may have flown away. Wildlife trackers see wing marks like these when birds escape."
