## 1. Lesson plan (one page, for the teacher)

- **Big idea:** Pooling everyone's results gives stronger evidence than one group alone; the right graph shows the pattern, and a Claim-Evidence-Reasoning conclusion and an honest evaluation say what the evidence means and how sure we can be.
- **Learning goals:**
  - I can choose the right graph for our data and write a conclusion with a claim, evidence (numbers) and reasoning (science).
  - I can explain one problem with our test, how it affected the results, and how sure we are.
- **Key words:**

| English | 中文 | Student-friendly meaning |
|---|---|---|
| pool (data) | 汇总（数据） | to put everyone's results together |
| class mean | 全班平均值 | the mean of all the groups' means for one sample type |
| bar chart | 条形图 | a graph with bars, used when the thing we change is a word or group |
| line graph | 折线图 | a graph with points and a line, used when the thing we change is a number |
| claim | 主张 | what you think the answer to the question is |
| evidence | 证据 | the numbers from the results that support the claim |
| reasoning | 推理 | the science that explains why the evidence supports the claim |
| evaluate | 评价 | to judge how good the test was and how sure we can be |
| improvement | 改进 | a change that would make the test better |
| confident | 有把握的 | how sure you are |

- **Timeline:**

| Phase | Min | Teacher does | Students do | Resource |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Do Now | 4 | Returns group folders (Week 8 results sheets); displays the class results table (prepared from the photo of the project board) | Calculate any group means not done in Week 8; check against the class table | Slide 1; worksheet Do Now box; Week 8 results sheets |
| Hook | 3 | "Snow Owls' plain samples: 250 ml. Ice Foxes': 267 ml. Who is right?" | Pair talk: why do they differ? Which do we trust more: one group or the whole class? | Slide 2 |
| Learn | 7 | Teaches pooling (class mean = mean of the group means); bar or line decision; models a CER conclusion and an evaluation sentence with the sample data | Bar or line? vote with a reason; complete Core A | Slides 3 to 6; worksheet Core A, B |
| Do | 18 | Assigns poster panels by role; circulates; checks graph axes, units, CER numbers; gives the sample class data to groups that need it | Each student completes their worksheet section, then writes their poster panel card; glue panels onto the A3 poster | Slides 7 to 9; worksheet Core C, Stretch, Challenge; pooling sheet (6a); poster template and panel cards (6b) |
| Think and Reflect | 8 | Self-assessment slip (4 min); then groups split the 2-minute talk using the talk frame; collects worksheets and posters | Self-assessment slip; choose who presents which panel next week; exit ticket | Slides 10 to 11; `assessment/Rubric_and_Reporting.md` Section 7; talk frame (Week 10 Section 6b); exit ticket |

- **Poster panels by role** (each student writes one panel card; groups of 3 share as shown):
  - Lead Scientist: Panel 1 Question and Panel 2 Prediction.
  - Safety Officer: Panel 3 Method in picture steps.
  - Measurer and Recorder: Panel 4 Results (class data table and graph).
  - Reporter: Panel 5 Conclusion (CER).
  - Everyone, led by the Lead Scientist: Panel 6 Evaluation and Panel 7 If we did it again (one sentence each from the Stretch and Challenge sections). Groups of 3: Lead Scientist also writes Panel 3; Reporter writes Panels 5 and 6.
- **Tiers:**
  - Core: copy the class means for our question; choose bar or line with a reason; complete the CER conclusion frame with numbers.
  - Stretch: evaluation frame: one problem, how it affected the results, one specific improvement.
  - Challenge: judge how confident we are, using the spread of the repeats and how many groups tested each sample; say what the data cannot tell us.
- **Language support:**
  - Frames: "Our claim is ___." "The evidence is: as the ___ increased from ___ to ___, the mean ___ increased from ___ to ___." "This is because the fibres ___." "One problem was ___. This made our results ___. Next time we would ___." "We are fairly / very / not very confident because ___."
  - Chinese talk welcome while deciding the claim and the evaluation; panels are written in English with key words. The "My thinking" box is for reasoning in Chinese.
  - Vocabulary traps: "claim" (主张) here is a scientific answer, not a claim to own something. "Pool" (汇总) is not a swimming pool. "Mean" is the average (平均值), as in Week 4.
- **Misconceptions to watch for:**
  - "Our group's result is the right answer and the others are wrong." Every group's result varies a little; the class mean is better evidence.
  - "A line graph is always better." Type of fibre is a word, so it needs a bar chart; amount of sawdust is a number, so it needs a line graph.
  - "The conclusion just says what we did." A conclusion answers the question, quotes numbers, and explains them with science.
  - "Saying what went wrong makes our poster worse." Honest evaluation is a strength, and it is a whole rubric strand.
- **Safety:** No practical hazards beyond normal classroom rules. Scissors and glue for the poster: carry scissors with the points down.
- **If time is short:** groups finish only Panels 1, 4 and 5 today (question, results, conclusion) and complete Panels 6 and 7 in the first minutes of Week 10 while the room is set up; the self-assessment slip moves to the start of Week 10 only if unavoidable (it should come before rubric levels are decided).
- **Assessment and handover links:**
  - The poster (main evidence for Rubric strands 1 to 5). Collect posters at the end; the teacher reads them before Week 10 and pencils in draft levels for strands 1 to 5 in the tracker.
  - Individual worksheets: who wrote what, for judging individuals (see "Individuals, not groups" in `assessment/Rubric_and_Reporting.md`).
  - Self-assessment slips: compare with your judgement; a gap of two levels or more is worth a 1-minute conversation in Week 10.
  - Exit ticket: each student's talk part for Week 10.

**Graphs for the sample data (for checking posters):**
- Plan A line graph: x-axis 0 to 15 g in steps of 5 (or 0 to 16 in steps of 2); y-axis 0 to 700 ml in steps of 100. Points (0, 250), (5, 350), (10, 467), (15, 600). Close to a straight line; a ruled line of best fit from about (0, 240) to (15, 600) is acceptable.

- Plan B bar chart: no fibre 400, sawdust 517, tissue 471, shredded paper 475.

**Core C (Secure examples):**
- Plan A, amount of sawdust: "Claim: adding more sawdust makes the ice beam stronger. Evidence: as the amount of sawdust increased from 0 g to 15 g, the mean water held increased from 250 ml to 600 ml. Reasoning: ice is brittle, so a crack runs straight through it. The sawdust fibres hold the ice together and stop cracks, like straw in mud bricks."
- Plan A, type of fibre: "Claim: all the fibres made the ice stronger, and sawdust was the best. Evidence: plain ice held a mean of 250 ml, sawdust 350 ml, shredded paper 333 ml and tissue 313 ml. Reasoning: fibres bridge cracks and hold the brittle ice together."
- Plan A, melting: "Claim: sawdust makes ice melt more slowly. Evidence: as the sawdust increased from 0 g to 15 g, the mean meltwater after 15 minutes decreased from 14.5 ml to 7 ml. Reasoning: when the surface melts, the sawdust is left on the outside as a furry layer. It is an insulator, so it slows heat moving from the air into the ice, like the materials in Week 6."
- Plan B, amount of sawdust: "Claim: more sawdust makes the dough bar stronger. Evidence: as the amount of sawdust increased from 0 g to 15 g, the mean water held increased from 400 ml to 721 ml. Reasoning: dry dough is brittle. The fibres hold it together and stop cracks spreading, like straw in mud bricks."
- Plan B, type of fibre: "Claim: all the fibres made the bars stronger, and sawdust was the best. Evidence: bars with no fibre held a mean of 400 ml, sawdust 517 ml, shredded paper 475 ml and tissue 471 ml. Reasoning: the fibres hold the brittle dough together across cracks."

**Stretch (Secure examples):**
- "One problem was that one 15 g beam slipped off the books. This made its result too low because the load pulled it sideways instead of snapping it. Next time we would mark 2 cm on each stack and line the beam up with the marks."
- "One problem was that some beams waited longer outside the cool box. This made their results too low because warmer ice is weaker. Next time we would take out one beam at a time and test it within one minute."
- "One problem was that some bars were still damp in the middle (Plan B). This made the results more spread out because damp bars bend instead of snapping. Next time we would dry the bars for two weeks and turn them every day."
- "One problem was that we poured faster on some tests. This made those results too low because a jolt is like an extra push. Next time the same person would pour every time, waiting 5 seconds between pours."
- Not Secure: "We should be more careful." (no specific problem, effect or change).

**Challenge (Secure and Excellent examples):**
- a) Secure: "Fairly confident, because the repeats for each sample were close (within 100 ml) and two groups found the same pattern for 15 g." Excellent: "We are very confident about the amount of sawdust, because every increase gave a higher mean in both groups. We are not very confident that sawdust is better than shredded paper, because the difference was only 17 ml, less than the spread of the repeats, and only one group tested shredded paper."
- b) Examples: "It cannot tell us what happens with 30 g of sawdust: the beam might crumble." / "It cannot tell us about cotton wool, because no group tested it." / "It cannot tell us whether a full-size ice ship would work."
- c) The sample might have snapped at the start of the last 50 ml pour, so the real load could be up to 50 ml less than we wrote. Excellent: this affects every result the same way, so the comparison between samples is still fair, but smaller pours (25 ml) would make each result more precise.

**My thinking (guidance):** he would be pleased that fibres made the material stronger (and, Plan A, slower to melt), matching his team's findings; he might ask us to test larger beams, colder ice, more sawdust (his team used about 14%, our 15 g in 100 ml is about 13%), or how long a beam lasts outdoors. Accept Chinese.

**Exit ticket:** check that every member of each group has a part, and that the order follows the poster. Note students who chose no part and talk to the group.
