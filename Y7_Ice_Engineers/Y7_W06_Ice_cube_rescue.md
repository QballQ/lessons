## 1. Lesson plan (one page, for the teacher)

- **Big idea:** A thermal insulator slows down heat transfer, so a coat on a snowman slows the heat moving from the warm air into the snow, and it melts more slowly.
- **Learning goals:**
  - I can plan and carry out a fair test to compare thermal insulators. 我能计划并进行一次公平测试，比较不同的隔热材料。
  - I can explain, using my results, why a coat slows down melting (or cooling). 我能用我的结果解释为什么外套能减慢熔化（或降温）。
- **Key words:**

| English | 中文 | Student-friendly meaning |
|---|---|---|
| thermal insulator | 隔热材料 | a material that slows down heat moving through it |
| insulate | 隔热 | to slow down heat moving in or out |
| heat transfer | 热传递 | heat moving from a warmer place to a colder place |
| melt | 熔化 | to change from solid to liquid, like ice to water |
| meltwater | 融化的水 | the water that comes from melted ice (Plan A) |
| temperature | 温度 | how hot or cold something is, measured in °C |
| thermometer | 温度计 | a tool for measuring temperature (Plan B) |
| material | 材料 | what something is made of, like paper, foil or wool |
| concept cartoon | 概念漫画 | a picture where characters give different ideas, and you decide who is right |

- **Timeline [Plan A] (adjusted: the ice is wrapped and started at the very beginning and measured in the last 8 minutes):** Do Now 4, Hook 3, Learn 7, Do part 1 14, Think and Reflect part 1 4, Do part 2 4, Think and Reflect part 2 4. Total 40 minutes. The ice melts for about 29 minutes.

- **Tiers:**
  - Core: choose a cartoon character and give a reason; record results in the table; gap-fill on insulation (word bank); complete the variables table.
  - Stretch: write a conclusion that quotes two results; explain why the unwrapped cup is needed (the control).
  - Challenge: say which character was most right and use the idea of heat transfer to explain why the others were wrong; spot one thing that was not fully controlled and say how it could change the results.
- **Language support:**
  - Frames: "I agree with ___ because ___." "The best insulator was ___ because it had the least meltwater / smallest temperature drop: ___." "A coat slows down heat moving from ___ to ___." "We kept ___ the same because ___."
  - Chinese talk welcome during the cartoon vote and the planning frame discussion.
  - Band 1 today: sits beside a language buddy, who explains each step in Chinese and never writes the answer. Starts with Core B (letters, times and numbers only, with bilingual headings), then Core A: the vote is made from the picture cue in each bubble and the 概要 gist line read by the buddy while the teacher reads the English. Core C is done on the Chinese version of the paragraph with the bilingual word bank (or the draw-a-line version); Core D is done by sorting the bilingual variable cards under the three gesture headings and copying them. Answers in Chinese count in every Core task; praise the English key word when it is used.
  - Vocabulary traps: "insulator" (隔热材料) here means heat, not electricity (绝缘体 is the electrical word, and students may have met it). "Warm" in "a coat keeps you warm" makes students think the coat makes heat: it does not. "Drop" here means the temperature goes down (下降), not dropping a spinner.
- **Misconceptions to watch for:**
  - "Coats make heat." A coat makes no heat. Your body makes the heat; the coat slows it escaping. A snowman makes no heat, so the coat just slows heat getting in.
  - "Cold comes in." Heat moves; cold does not flow. Ice melts because heat moves into it from the warmer air.
  - "Foil is shiny, so it must be the best." Foil is thin and lets heat through quickly by touch (conduction), though its shine helps a little. Thick materials that trap air (cotton wool, bubble wrap, fabric) usually work best.
  - "The unwrapped cup is wasted." It is the control: it shows what happens with no insulator, so we can see how much each material helps.
- **Safety:**
  - [Plan A] Gloves for handling ice for more than a minute; ice stays in sealed bags; mop spills straight away.
  - [Plan B] Only the teacher pours the warm water (about 50 °C, never hotter than 55 °C). Thermometers can break: tell the teacher at once. Mop spills straight away.
- **If time is short:**
  - [Plan A] Skip Challenge; take Think part 1 (planning frame) down to 2 minutes and finish page 1 in the Week 7 Do Now. Never cut the 4-minute measuring slot.
  - [Plan B] Test three cups (no wrap and two materials) instead of four; skip the logbook.
- **Assessment and handover links:**
  - Mini fair-test write-up on the worksheet (variables, prediction, results, conclusion): supporting evidence for Rubric strands 2 to 4.
  - Planning frame page 1 drafted: kept by the teacher in each group's project folder for Week 7.
  - Exit ticket: best insulator with a number from the results and a reason.
  - First project lesson for the incoming teacher: note who leads in each group and who needs language support.

## 4. Answer key (for the teacher)

**[Shared] Core A: the concept cartoon**
- **Owen is right.** The air is warmer than the snowman, so heat moves from the air into the snow. A coat is a thermal insulator: it slows that heat transfer, so the snowman melts more slowly.
- **Bo is partly right and makes a very good point.** A thick, dry coat that traps air insulates well. A thin coat insulates less. A wet coat insulates much less, because water lets heat through far more easily than trapped air. Excellent-level extra: in strong sunshine, a dark coat soaks up sunlight, which could warm its outside; but it still shields the snow, and most of the time the coat still slows melting.
- **Xin is wrong (the most common misconception).** A coat makes no heat. You feel warm in a coat because your body makes heat and the coat slows it escaping. A snowman makes no heat.
- **Lulu is right that a coat makes no heat or cold, but wrong that it makes no difference.** It slows heat moving in.
- Accept any choice before the test with a reason. After the test, Secure: "Now I agree with Owen because the wrapped ice had less meltwater (or the wrapped cups cooled less), so the materials slowed heat transfer."
