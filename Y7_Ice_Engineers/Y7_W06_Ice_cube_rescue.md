## 1. Lesson plan (one page, for the teacher)

- **Big idea:** A thermal insulator slows down heat transfer, so a coat on a snowman slows the heat moving from the warm air into the snow, and it melts more slowly.
- **Learning goals:**
  - I can plan and carry out a fair test to compare thermal insulators.
  - I can explain, using my results, why a coat slows down melting (or cooling).
- **Key words:**

| English | 中文 | Student-friendly meaning |
|---|---|---|
| thermal insulator | 隔热材料 | a material that slows down heat moving through it |
| insulate | 隔热 | to slow down heat moving in or out |
| heat transfer | 热传递 | heat moving from a warmer place to a colder place |
| melt | 熔化 | to change from solid to liquid, like ice to water |
| meltwater | 融化的水 | the water that comes from melted ice (Plan A) |
| temperature | 温度 | how hot or cold something is, measured in °C |
| thermometer | 温度计 | a tool for measuring temperature (Plan B) |
| material | 材料 | what something is made of, like paper, foil or wool |
| concept cartoon | 概念漫画 | a picture where characters give different ideas, and you decide who is right |

- **Tiers:**
  - Core: choose a cartoon character and give a reason; record results in the table; gap-fill on insulation (word bank); complete the variables table.
  - Stretch: write a conclusion that quotes two results; explain why the unwrapped cup is needed (the control).
  - Challenge: say which character was most right and use the idea of heat transfer to explain why the others were wrong; spot one thing that was not fully controlled and say how it could change the results.
- **Language support:**
  - Frames: "I agree with ___ because ___." "The best insulator was ___ because it had the least meltwater / smallest temperature drop: ___." "A coat slows down heat moving from ___ to ___." "We kept ___ the same because ___."
  - Chinese talk welcome during the cartoon vote and the planning frame discussion.
  - Vocabulary traps: "insulator" (隔热材料) here means heat, not electricity (绝缘体 is the electrical word, and students may have met it). "Warm" in "a coat keeps you warm" makes students think the coat makes heat: it does not. "Drop" here means the temperature goes down (下降), not dropping a spinner.
- **Misconceptions to watch for:**
  - "Coats make heat." A coat makes no heat. Your body makes the heat; the coat slows it escaping. A snowman makes no heat, so the coat just slows heat getting in.
  - "Cold comes in." Heat moves; cold does not flow. Ice melts because heat moves into it from the warmer air.
  - "Foil is shiny, so it must be the best." Foil is thin and lets heat through quickly by touch (conduction), though its shine helps a little. Thick materials that trap air (cotton wool, bubble wrap, fabric) usually work best.
  - "The unwrapped cup is wasted." It is the control: it shows what happens with no insulator, so we can see how much each material helps.
- **Safety:**
  - [Plan A] Gloves for handling ice for more than a minute; ice stays in sealed bags; mop spills straight away.
  - [Plan B] Only the teacher pours the warm water (about 50 °C, never hotter than 55 °C). Thermometers can break: tell the teacher at once. Mop spills straight away.
- **If time is short:**
  - [Plan A] Skip Challenge; take Think part 1 (planning frame) down to 2 minutes and finish page 1 in the Week 7 Do Now. Never cut the 4-minute measuring slot.
  - [Plan B] Test three cups (no wrap and two materials) instead of four; skip the logbook.
- **Assessment and handover links:**
  - Mini fair-test write-up on the worksheet (variables, prediction, results, conclusion): supporting evidence for Rubric strands 2 to 4.
  - Planning frame page 1 drafted: kept by the teacher in each group's project folder for Week 7.
  - Exit ticket: best insulator with a number from the results and a reason.
  - First project lesson for the incoming teacher: note who leads in each group and who needs language support.

## 4. Answer key (for the teacher)

**[Shared] Core A: the concept cartoon**
- **Owen is right.** The air is warmer than the snowman, so heat moves from the air into the snow. A coat is a thermal insulator: it slows that heat transfer, so the snowman melts more slowly.
- **Bo is partly right and makes a very good point.** A thick, dry coat that traps air insulates well. A thin coat insulates less. A wet coat insulates much less, because water lets heat through far more easily than trapped air. Excellent-level extra: in strong sunshine, a dark coat soaks up sunlight, which could warm its outside; but it still shields the snow, and most of the time the coat still slows melting.
- **Xin is wrong (the most common misconception).** A coat makes no heat. You feel warm in a coat because your body makes heat and the coat slows it escaping. A snowman makes no heat.
- **Lulu is right that a coat makes no heat or cold, but wrong that it makes no difference.** It slows heat moving in.
- Accept any choice before the test with a reason. After the test, Secure: "Now I agree with Owen because the wrapped ice had less meltwater (or the wrapped cups cooled less), so the materials slowed heat transfer."

**[Plan A] Core B: expected results** (30 minutes, cubes about 20 g in sealed bags, room about 20 to 22 °C; real values vary)

| Material | Typical meltwater (ml) |
|---|---|
| no wrap (control) | 8 to 10 |
| foil | 6 to 8 |
| newspaper | 5 to 6 |
| fabric | 4 to 5 |
| bubble wrap | 3 to 4 |
| cotton wool | 2 to 4 |

Check: units in the heading only; the bare cup has the most meltwater; rank 1 is the least meltwater. If a wrapped cube has more meltwater than the bare one, look for a reason (bag leaked, cube was smaller or larger, someone held it).

**[Plan B] Core B: expected results** (150 ml of water, start about 46 to 49 °C, 15 minutes)

| Material | Typical temperature drop (°C) |
|---|---|
| no wrap (control) | 9 to 12 |
| foil | 7 to 9 |
| newspaper | 6 to 8 |
| fabric | 5 to 7 |
| bubble wrap | 4 to 6 |
| cotton wool | 4 to 6 |

Check the subtraction in every row (start take away end). Note: Cup A has no lid as well as no jacket, so it loses heat from the top too; this is a good Challenge discussion point.

**[Shared] Core C:** warmer, colder, no, insulator, slows, heat.

**[Shared] Core D:** I change: the material (type of wrapping). I measure: [Plan A] the volume of meltwater (ml) after about 30 minutes / [Plan B] the temperature drop (°C) after 15 minutes. I keep the same (any three): [Plan A] size of ice cube (same tray); same type of bag; same cup; same time; same place in the room; one layer of wrapping; [Plan B] same cup; 150 ml of water; same starting temperature (same jug); same time (15 minutes); same place; one layer; same thermometer and same reading order.
Prediction (Secure): "I predict cotton wool will be the best insulator because it is thick and traps a lot of air, and trapped air slows heat transfer."

**Stretch:**
- a) [Plan A] Secure example: "The best insulator was cotton wool because it had only 3 ml of meltwater. The worst was foil with 7 ml." [Plan B] Secure example: "The best insulator was bubble wrap because the temperature dropped by only 5 °C. The worst was foil with a drop of 8 °C." Check values match their table. The worst material is a wrapping, not the control.
- b) "We need the control cup because it shows what happens with no insulator, so we can see how much each material slows the melting (or cooling)."

**Challenge:**
- a) Secure example: "Owen was most right. Heat moves from the warm air into the cold snowman. A coat is a thermal insulator, so it slows the heat transfer and the snowman melts more slowly. Xin is wrong because a coat does not make heat; it only slows heat moving. Lulu is right that it makes no heat, but wrong that it makes no difference. Bo is partly right: a thin or wet coat would slow the heat less." Excellent: uses our results as evidence and notes that wrapping works both ways (keeps ice cold and keeps warm water warm).
- b) [Plan A] Examples: "The cubes were not exactly the same size, so a bigger cube might melt more slowly. Control: weigh each cube and choose cubes with the same mass." / "We wrapped some tighter than others, so there was more trapped air in some. Control: use the same size square and one elastic band." / "Some cups were near the window, so they got warmer. Control: put all cups in the middle of the table."
- b) [Plan B] Examples: "The cups did not all start at the same temperature, because the teacher poured them one after another. Control: measure the drop, not the end temperature, or use a flask." / "Cup A had no lid, so it lost heat from the top. Control: give cup A a plastic lid, or test lids separately." / "The thermometer was moved between cups and took time to settle. Control: use one thermometer per cup."

**My thinking (guidance):** a thick robe is an insulator, so it slows heat moving from the very hot desert air and sunshine into the body. It works the same way as the snowman's coat. Accept Chinese. Note students who make this link: they are ready for Challenge work.

**Exit ticket:** Secure: names a material, gives one correct number with its unit (ml or °C), and links the reason to less meltwater / a smaller drop (and ideally to trapping air or slowing heat transfer).

### Kit card [Plan B]: Keep it warm

**Per group (6 groups)**
- Identical plastic cups (about 250 ml), labelled A, B, C, D, each with a line marked at 150 ml: 4
- Pre-cut material, one of each: a 25 cm x 10 cm jacket strip and a 10 cm x 10 cm lid square with a 1 cm slit in the middle. Set A (groups 1, 3, 5): foil, newspaper, cotton wool sheet; Set B (groups 2, 4, 6): fabric, bubble wrap, newspaper
- Elastic bands: 6
- Thermometer (alcohol or digital, not mercury): 1
- Stopwatch: 1
- Tray and paper towels: 1 tray, 6 towels
- Planning frame (Week 7 Section 6a): 1 per group, plus a group folder

**Per class**
- Large jug (at least 2 litres) and a second jug or flask; kettle (teacher use only)
- 1 spare thermometer for checking the jug
- Mop or a roll of paper towels

**Teacher prep before the lesson (about 20 minutes)**
- Check all 7 thermometers: stand them in one cup of room-temperature water; they should agree within 1 °C. Replace any that do not (5 min).
- Mark the 150 ml line on every cup with a measuring cylinder and a marker (5 min).
- Cut the jackets and lids; set out the trays by Set A and Set B (5 min).
- During the Do Now: mix kettle water and cold tap water in the jug until it reads about 50 °C (never above 55 °C). You need about 3.6 litres for 24 cups (5 min).

**Hazards and controls**
- Only the teacher pours the warm water. It is about 50 °C, never hotter than 55 °C.
  只有老师可以倒温水。水温约50 °C，绝不超过55 °C。
- Do not move the cups once they are full.
  杯子装水后不要移动。
- Thermometers can break. Lay them flat. If one breaks, do not touch it: tell the teacher.
  温度计易碎，请平放。如有破损，不要触碰，立即告诉老师。
- Mop up spills straight away so no one slips.
  洒出的水请立即擦干，以免滑倒。

**Clean-up**
- Teacher collects and counts thermometers first.
- Water poured into the sink; wet newspaper and cotton wool in the bin; foil, fabric and bubble wrap dried for reuse; tables wiped.
