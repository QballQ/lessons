## 1. Lesson plan (one page, for the teacher)

- **Big idea:** A graph turns a table of numbers into a picture, so we can spot the pattern and write a conclusion that uses the data as evidence.
- **Learning goals:**
  - I can choose between a bar chart and a line graph and plot my results accurately.
  - I can write a conclusion using "As the ___ increases, the ___ ___."
- **Key words:**

| English | 中文 | Student-friendly meaning |
|---|---|---|
| graph | 图表 | a picture that shows numbers |
| bar chart | 条形图 | a graph with bars, used when the thing we change is a word or group |
| line graph | 折线图 | a graph with points and a line, used when the thing we change is a number |
| axis (axes) | 坐标轴 | the two lines at the side and bottom of a graph |
| plot | 描点 | to mark a point on a graph with a small cross |
| line of best fit | 最佳拟合线 | one smooth line that goes as close as possible to all the points |
| pattern | 规律 | something that happens again and again in the results |
| conclusion | 结论 | what we found out, using our results as evidence |
| increase | 增加 | to get bigger |

- **Timeline (adjusted for the Week 5 checkpoint):** Do Now 3, Checkpoint 10, Hook 2, Learn 5, Do 14, Think and Reflect 6. Total 40 minutes.

| Phase | Min | Teacher does | Students do | Resource |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Do Now | 3 | Shows a quick mean-and-units retrieval question; hands out group envelopes (results tables from Week 4) | Calculate one mean; add the missing unit | Slide 1; worksheet Do Now box |
| Checkpoint | 10 | Hands out the checkpoint; reads each question stem aloud in English; does not help with answers; collects papers at exactly 10 minutes | Complete the checkpoint alone, in silence | Slide 2; `assessment/Checkpoint_Y7.md` |
| Hook | 2 | Shows the same spinner means as a bar chart and as a line graph: "Which tells the story better?" | Vote; give a reason | Slide 3 |
| Learn | 5 | Teaches the rule (word or group: bar chart; number: line graph); IV on the x-axis, DV on the y-axis; plotting with crosses; line of best fit | Say "bar" or "line" for quick examples; watch the plotting demo | Slides 4 to 6 |
| Do | 14 | Circulates; checks axis labels, units, crosses and the line; gives sample data card to groups with poor or missing data | Copy means into the table; plot the line graph; draw a line of best fit; write a conclusion with the frame | Slides 7 to 8; worksheet Core, Stretch, Challenge; sample data card |
| Think and Reflect | 6 | Runs "Bar or line?" with four cards; collects exit tickets | Vote and justify; exit ticket; logbook line | Slides 9 to 11; Bar or line? cards; exit ticket |

- **Tiers:**
  - Core: copy means into the table; complete the axis labels from the word bank; plot three points on a pre-scaled grid; complete the conclusion cloze.
  - Stretch: explain the choice of a line graph with a frame; write a conclusion that quotes two mean values from the data.
  - Challenge: explain the pattern using air resistance; use the line of best fit to predict the fall time for 10 cm wings and say how confident you are.
- **Language support:**
  - Frames: "As the ___ increases, the ___ ___." "We drew a ___ because the independent variable is a ___." "When the wings were ___ cm, the mean fall time was ___ s." "I predict ___ because ___."
  - Chinese talk welcome during the Hook vote and "Bar or line?".
  - Vocabulary traps: "plot" here means mark a point (描点), not the story of a book. "Axis" singular, "axes" plural. "Increase" and "decrease" are easy to mix up: use arrow gestures (up and down). "Line graph" (折线图) is not the same as "line of best fit".
- **Misconceptions to watch for:**
  - "Join the dots with a ruler, dot to dot, is always right." For a line of best fit, draw one smooth line close to all the points; do not force it through an anomaly.
  - "The x-axis can go either way." The independent variable (what we change) goes on the x-axis (horizontal); the dependent variable (what we measure) goes on the y-axis (vertical).
  - "Longer wings make it heavier, so it falls faster." The extra card is tiny compared with the paper clip; longer wings mean more air resistance, so a longer fall time.
  - "The graph proves it for any length." Our data only covers 4 to 8 cm; predicting outside that range is less certain.
- **Safety:** No practical hazards beyond normal classroom rules.
- **If time is short:** Core students plot and write the conclusion cloze only; skip the line of best fit for Core and do it as a class demo. Run "Bar or line?" with two cards instead of four.
- **Assessment and handover links:**
  - Checkpoint score (questions 1 to 6 of the skills check structure, marked with the scheme in `assessment/Checkpoint_Y7.md`) entered in the progress tracker.
  - Graph and conclusion on the worksheet (Rubric strand 4, Analysing and concluding).
  - Exit ticket: choose bar or line with a reason.
  - This is the handover lesson: the incoming teacher observes or co-teaches. The current teacher completes the handover note after this lesson (checkpoint summary, groups and roles, students needing language support or more challenge, freezer decision from Week 4).

## 4. Answer key (for the teacher)

**Do Now:** 1.40 + 1.46 + 1.52 = 4.38 ÷ 3 = 1.46 s. Unit of wing length: cm.

**Core A:** class data, or sample data: 4 cm, 1.31 s; 6 cm, 1.68 s; 8 cm, 2.08 s.

**Core B:**
- x-axis label: Wing length (cm). y-axis label: Mean fall time (s).
- Points (sample data): (4, 1.31), (6, 1.68), (8, 2.08). On the grid, 1.31 is just over 26 minor squares up (1.30 = 26 minor squares); 1.68 is just under 34 minor squares (1.70); 2.08 is just under 42 minor squares (2.10). Accept points within one minor square.
- Line of best fit: a straight ruled line passing close to all three points, roughly from (4, 1.30) to (8, 2.07). The line should not be forced through an anomaly if a group plotted one.
- Title (if given): "How wing length affects the fall time of a spinner" or similar.

**Core C:** As the **wing length** increases, the **fall time** **increases**.

**Stretch:**
- a) number (accept "number that can have any value" or "continuous").
- b) Secure example (sample data): "As the wing length increases, the fall time increases. When the wings were 4 cm, the mean fall time was 1.31 s. When the wings were 8 cm, the mean fall time was 2.08 s." Class data: check values match their table.

**Challenge:**
- a) Secure example: "Longer wings have a bigger area, so they push against more air. This makes more air resistance pushing up on the spinner, so it falls more slowly and takes longer to reach the floor." Excellent: notes that the extra card adds very little mass compared with the paper clip, so the air resistance effect is much bigger.
- b) Sample data: about 2.4 to 2.5 s (the straight line through the points rises about 0.19 s for every 1 cm of wing). Accept any value that matches the student's own extended line. Reason: "because the line goes up by about 0.4 s for every 2 cm, so 10 cm should be about 0.4 s more than 8 cm."
- c) Secure example: "I am fairly confident because the three points are close to a straight line, but 10 cm is outside the range we tested. Longer wings might bend or not spin properly, so the pattern might change. We would need to test 10 cm wings to check." Excellent: also mentions reaction-time error and only three repeats, and suggests testing more wing lengths (5 cm, 7 cm, 10 cm).

**Bar or line? cards (Section 6a):** A bar chart (type of hat: groups). B line graph (time in minutes: a number). C bar chart (type of wrapping material: groups). D line graph (mass of salt in g: a number).

**My thinking (guidance):** a graph shows the pattern at a glance: you can see if the line goes up or down, spot anomalies that sit away from the line, and read values between the points. Accept Chinese.

**Exit ticket:** bar chart, because the independent variable (type of material) is a word or group, not a number. Secure: correct choice plus the reason linked to the independent variable.

**Checkpoint:** mark using the mark scheme in `assessment/Checkpoint_Y7.md` and enter scores in the progress tracker before completing the handover note.
