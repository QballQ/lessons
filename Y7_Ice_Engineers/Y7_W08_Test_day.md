## 1. Lesson plan (one page, for the teacher)

- **Big idea:** A test is only as good as the way it is carried out: every sample is tested in exactly the same way, every result is recorded, and repeats show us how much results vary.
- **Learning goals:**
  - I can carry out a strength (or melting) test carefully and safely, in exactly the same way for every sample. 我能安全、仔细地进行强度（或熔化）测试，并对每个样品用完全相同的方法。
  - I can record repeat results with units and notice anything unusual. 我能带单位记录重复测试的结果，并注意到异常之处。
- **Key words:**

| English | 中文 | Student-friendly meaning |
|---|---|---|
| strength | 强度 | how much force something can take before it breaks |
| load | 负载 | the weight that pushes or pulls on something |
| snap | 断裂 | to break suddenly into pieces |
| volume | 体积 | how much space a liquid takes up, measured in ml |
| millilitre (ml) | 毫升 | the unit of volume; 1 ml of water has a mass of about 1 g |
| mass | 质量 | how much matter is in something, measured in g |
| repeat | 重复 | to do the same test again with a new sample |
| anomaly | 异常值 | a result that does not fit the pattern |
| catch tray | 接水盘 | a tray or bucket under a test to catch what falls |

- **Timeline (adjusted for test day: shorter Do Now, Hook and Learn so the Do phase has 22 minutes):** Do Now 3, Hook 2, Learn 6, Do 22, Think and Reflect 7. Total 40 minutes.

| Phase | Min | Teacher does | Students do | Resource |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Do Now | 3 | Hands each group its tray of samples [Plan A: from the cool boxes; Plan B: from the drying shelf] and its folder; checks role cards; shows the Today slide and reads the two goals in English while the trays go out (a student reads the Chinese) | Safety Officer reads the kit card hazards aloud; Recorder copies sample codes into the results table; others build the rigs | Slides 1 to 2 (Today slide); kit tray; worksheet Core A |
| Hook | 2 | Holds up a plain sample and a fibre sample: "Which will hold more water? How will it break?" | Quick prediction, hands up | Slide 3 (Plan A) or Slide 4 (Plan B) |
| Learn | 6 | Demonstrates one rig step by step (no sample snapped); explains the recording rule and parallel roles; safety slide. [Plan A: melting groups start their test now] | Watch; Measurers check their gap with a ruler | Slides 5 to 10; rig diagram (6a) |
| Do | 22 | Circulates with a towel; checks spans (10 cm), counting of pours and notes; times the melting groups (Plan A); pushes for 3 repeats, accepts 2 | Two rigs in parallel; test every sample; record every result; write notes on how each sample broke; calculate means if time | Slides 11 to 12; worksheet Core, Stretch, Challenge |
| Think and Reflect | 7 | Recorders write their results on the class project board; teacher picks one odd result: "Anomaly or not?"; collects exit tickets | Discuss (any language), share with a frame; exit ticket; tidy | Slides 13 to 14; class project board; exit ticket |

- **Roles for parallel testing (groups of 4 or 5):**
  - Rig 1: Lead Scientist pours; Measurer watches the sample at eye level and calls "Snap!".
  - Rig 2: Safety Officer pours; Reporter watches and calls.
  - Recorder: hands out the next sample, counts pours for both rigs (a tally for each), writes every result and note.
  - Groups of 3: run one rig (pour, watch, record) and aim for 2 repeats; or run two rigs with the Recorder also watching Rig 2.
  - [Plan A] Melting groups: Measurer and Recorder run the 15-minute test for all samples at once; the others help peel, time and read the measuring cylinder.
- **Tiers:**
  - Core: record each result in the results table (headings given); tick the safety checklist; describe how one sample broke.
  - Stretch: calculate the mean for each sample type; circle any anomaly and write a reason.
  - Challenge: compare how plain and fibre samples broke and explain the difference using *fibre* and *brittle*; name one thing that was hard to keep the same and how it could change the results.
- **Language support:**
  - Frames: "It snapped at ___ ml." "The plain sample broke ___, but the ___ sample ___." "The anomaly is ___ because ___." "It was hard to keep ___ the same because ___."
  - Chinese talk welcome while testing; results and notes are written in English with key words. Under the language access policy a note written in Chinese also counts: mark the science, and praise the English key word when it appears.
  - Vocabulary traps: "load" (负载) is not "loud". "Snap" here means break suddenly, not take a photo or click your fingers. "Volume" (体积) here is an amount of liquid, not loudness.
  - Band 1 today: sit the student beside their language buddy and give them a doing job first (pourer or watcher on Rig 1), where the result is a number, not a sentence. The rig is built from the bilingual 6a diagram on the table, not from the slide. For Core B they read the Chinese headings and the Chinese rule; the Our test line is copied from the Week 7 planning frame or circled from the two bilingual options; the Notes column may be filled in Chinese or by copying one bilingual Core C phrase. Core C is ticked from the bilingual phrases. The exit ticket may be answered in Chinese.
- **Misconceptions to watch for:**
  - "We should press on the sample to help it break." Never: every sample must get only the water load, added the same way.
  - "If our first result is good, we do not need repeats." One sample can have a hidden crack or bubble; repeats show whether a result is typical.
  - "A sample that snaps early is a failed test, so we can forget it." Record it with a note. It might be an anomaly, or it might be the real result.
  - [Plan A] "The melting test is a race to finish first." Every sample melts for exactly the same time (15 minutes) in the same place.
- **Safety:**
  - Catch tray (bucket or deep bowl) under every hanging bag; feet and hands clear of the bag; the pourer kneels to the side, never under the bag.
  - Mop spills at once; towels on every table; water from the bags goes back into the supply jug, not on the floor.
  - [Plan A] Gloves for handling ice for more than a minute; samples peeled with gloves on.
  - [Plan B] Never eat the dough; flour allergy students do not handle the strips; wash hands after.
- **If time is short:** two repeats per sample type (the minimum); means are calculated in the Week 9 Do Now. Never skip writing notes on broken samples.
- **Assessment and handover links:**
  - Raw results table with repeats and notes (main evidence for Rubric strand 3, Doing and recording).
  - Class project board: each group's results copied up for Week 9 pooling. Photograph it after the lesson.
  - Exit ticket: one result with its unit and one thing that went well or badly.
  - Note any group with missing or unusable results: they use the sample class data in Week 9.

## 4. Answer key (for the teacher)

**[Shared] Core A:** all boxes ticked before testing starts. Do not let a group test without a bucket under the bag.

**[Shared] Core B: what good results look like.** Headings complete (sample, repeats with ml, mean, notes); every value a multiple of 25 ml (the recording rule); at least 2 repeats per sample, ideally 3; notes on every broken sample. Expected values depend on the class trial (Week 7 kit card): plain strips about 150 to 500 ml, fibre strips higher. Thin plain ice varies a lot because of bubbles and cracks.

**Worked example (made-up group "Frost Hares", Plan A, amount of sawdust, strength):**

| Sample | Repeat 1 (ml) | Repeat 2 (ml) | Repeat 3 (ml) | Mean (ml) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 g (plain ice) | 275 | 300 | 250 | 275 | All snapped suddenly into two clean pieces |
| 0.3 g sawdust | 375 | 350 | 400 | 375 | Cracked, then the halves dropped |
| 0.9 g sawdust | 550 | 600 | 575 | 575 | Cracked slowly; fibres held the halves together |

Means: 825 ÷ 3 = 275 ml; 1125 ÷ 3 = 375 ml; 1725 ÷ 3 = 575 ml.

**[Plan A] Melting worked example (made-up group "Arctic Terns", amount of sawdust, 15 minutes):**

| Sample | Repeat 1 (ml) | Repeat 2 (ml) | Repeat 3 (ml) | Mean (ml) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 g | 6.5 | 6.5 | 9.5 | 6.5 | Repeat 3 was next to the window in the sun: anomaly, left out |
| 0.3 g sawdust | 5.5 | 5.0 | 5.5 | 5.3 | Fibres left on top |
| 0.9 g sawdust | 3.5 | 3.0 | 3.5 | 3.3 | Thick furry layer of sawdust on the surface |

Means: (14 + 16) ÷ 2 = 15 ml; 33 ÷ 3 = 11 ml; 21 ÷ 3 = 7 ml.

**[Plan B] note:** dough strip results look similar in pattern; expect a little more variation because strips dry unevenly. A strip that bends and sags without snapping is probably still damp inside: record "sagged" and the volume when it slipped off or touched the bucket, and flag it as a possible anomaly.

**Core C:** any accurate description. Typical: plain samples snap suddenly into two clean pieces (brittle); fibre samples crack more slowly, and the pieces often stay joined by fibres.

**Stretch:** means as above for their own data; Secure: divides by the number of values used (2 if an anomaly is left out). Anomaly: e.g. "The anomaly is 50 ml because the strip had a crack before we tested it." / "... because it was out of the box for 5 minutes and started to melt."

**Challenge:**
- a) Secure: "The plain ice was brittle: it snapped suddenly into two pieces. The sawdust strip cracked, but the fibres held the pieces together, so it held more water before it broke." Excellent: links to crack-stopping (fibres bridge the crack, so the crack cannot run straight through).
- b) [Plan A] Examples: "Some strips were out of the box longer, so they were warmer and weaker, which would make their results lower." / "Some strips had air bubbles, which make weak spots, so they snapped early." / "Some pours were faster, which jolts the strip." [Plan B] Examples: "Some strips were damper in the middle, so they bent instead of snapping." / "Some strips were thicker than 1 cm, so they were stronger." / "Some pours were faster."

**Slide 13 (anomaly or not?):** 50 ml is an anomaly: it is far below 275 and 325 ml, and the strip had a crack before the test. Leave it out of the mean ((275 + 325) ÷ 2 = 300 ml), circle it, write the reason, and test a spare if there is one.

**My thinking (guidance):** testing to breaking point tells engineers the largest load a material can take, so they can design buildings, bridges and ships that stay well below it (a safety margin); breaking things also shows how they fail (suddenly or slowly). Accept Chinese.

**Exit ticket:** Secure: a result with the correct unit (ml), and a sensible control that was hard to keep the same.
