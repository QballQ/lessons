## 1. Lesson plan (one page, for the teacher)

- **Big idea:** Measurements always vary a little, so scientists repeat them, work out a mean and look carefully at any result that does not fit.
- **Learning goals:**
  - I can record repeat measurements with the correct units and calculate a mean.
  - I can spot an anomaly and explain why I leave it out of the mean.
- **Key words:**

| English | 中文 | Student-friendly meaning |
|---|---|---|
| measure | 测量 | to find out how big, long or fast something is, using a tool |
| unit | 单位 | what a number is counted in, like cm or s |
| second (s) | 秒 | the unit of time |
| repeat | 重复 | to do the same test again |
| mean | 平均值 | add up the results, then divide by how many there are |
| anomaly | 异常值 | a result that does not fit the pattern |
| results table | 结果表格 | a table for writing results neatly, with headings and units |
| reaction time | 反应时间 | how long it takes you to react, for example to press a stopwatch |

- **Timeline:**

| Phase | Min | Teacher does | Students do | Resource |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Do Now | 4 | Shows the retrieval task: name the variables in a salt-and-ice test; "Two people timed the same drop: 1.62 s and 1.85 s. Why?" | Write IV, DV, one control; one reason the times differ | Slide 1; worksheet Do Now box |
| Hook | 3 | Drops one L spinner once from 2 m; three volunteers time the same drop; writes the three times on the board | Watch; suggest why one drop gives three different times | Slide 2 |
| Learn | 7 | Teaches units, repeats, mean = total ÷ number of values, anomaly; models a worked example with an anomaly | Follow the worked example; try one mean in Core C | Slides 3 to 6; worksheet Core A to C |
| Do | 18 | Returns group spinner envelopes (spares ready); circulates; checks units, decimal points and anomaly reasons. If a group's data is poor or spinners fail, gives the sample data card | Three repeats for each wing length (9 drops); record; circle any anomaly with a reason; calculate means | Slides 7 to 8; results table; sample data card; worksheet Stretch, Challenge |
| Think and Reflect | 8 | Puts one anomaly on the board: "Include it or leave it out?" Takes reasons; collects exit tickets | Pair talk (any language), share in English with a frame; exit ticket; logbook line | Slides 9 to 11; exit ticket; logbook |

- **Tiers:**
  - Core: match quantities to units; gap-fill on repeats and the mean (word bank); calculate one mean with a step-by-step box.
  - Stretch: find the anomaly in a table and explain it with frames; calculate the mean without it.
  - Challenge: explain why timings differ and suggest how to reduce each cause; judge whether ten repeats would be better than three.
- **Language support:**
  - Frames: "The anomaly is ___ because ___." "I will leave it out of the mean because ___." "The times are different because ___." "To make it more accurate, we could ___."
  - Chinese talk welcome during "Include it or leave it out?".
  - Vocabulary traps: "mean" here is the average (平均值), not "unkind" or "to mean something". "Second" is a unit of time (秒), not "second place". "Repeat" in a table heading means the test number (Repeat 1 = 第1次).
- **Misconceptions to watch for:**
  - "The anomaly is a mistake, so I can rub it out." Never rub out a result: circle it, write why, and leave it out of the mean.
  - "Any result I do not like is an anomaly." An anomaly does not fit the pattern of the other repeats, and there is usually a reason (it hit the table, it did not spin).
  - "The mean is the middle number." The mean is total ÷ number of values; the middle number is a different average (the median, not needed this year).
  - "More decimal places means more accurate." A stopwatch shows 0.01 s, but our reaction time is about 0.2 s, so the last digit is not really reliable.
- **Safety:**
  - No standing on chairs or tables; the Dropper stands flat on the floor.
  - Keep the floor under the drop marks clear of bags.
- **If time is short:** two repeats per wing length (the minimum), or test only 4 cm and 8 cm with three repeats each; use the sample data card for the means practice.
- **Assessment and handover links:**
  - Completed results table with means (output for Week 5 graphing; groups keep it in their envelope).
  - Exit ticket: calculate a mean and exclude an anomaly (checkpoint question 6 skill).
  - Note groups whose data is unusable: they use the sample data in Week 5.

```
Slide 2 | Phase tag: Hook 引入

```
Slide 3 | Phase tag: Learn 新知
Title: Units matter / 单位很重要

Speaker notes: A number without a unit means nothing: 2 what? 2 seconds? 2 minutes? In a results table we write the unit once, in brackets, in the heading. Then we write just the numbers in the boxes.
```

```
Slide 4 | Phase tag: Learn 新知
Title: Repeat and find the mean / 重复测量，求平均值
On-slide text: Repeat each test 3 times. Mean = total ÷ number of values.
Vocabulary panel: repeat | 重复; mean | 平均值
Visual: Three stopwatch readings (1.31, 1.25, 1.37) flowing into a plus sign, a total box "3.93", then "÷ 3", then a mean box "1.31 s". Clear step arrows.
Speaker notes: One measurement could be unlucky. Repeating and taking the mean gives a better value. Model it: 1.31 + 1.25 + 1.37 = 3.93. 3.93 ÷ 3 = 1.31 s. Round the mean to 2 decimal places, the same as the readings.
```

```
Slide 5 | Phase tag: Learn 新知
Title: Spot the anomaly / 找出异常值
On-slide text: An anomaly does not fit the pattern. Circle it. Write why. Leave it out of the mean. Never rub it out!
Vocabulary panel: anomaly | 异常值; mean | 平均值; repeat | 重复
Visual: A results table row for 8 cm wings: 2.05, 1.12, 2.11. The value 1.12 is circled. A note beside it: "hit the table, did not spin". An eraser crossed out.
Speaker notes: 1.12 s is much shorter than the other two. The group wrote down why: the spinner hit the table and did not spin. So we leave it out: (2.05 + 2.11) ÷ 2 = 4.16 ÷ 2 = 2.08 s. Notice we divide by 2, because now there are only two values.
```

```
Slide 6 | Phase tag: Learn 新知
Title: What if we left it in? / 如果保留异常值会怎样？
On-slide text: With the anomaly: mean 1.76 s. Without it: mean 2.08 s. Which one shows the real pattern?
Vocabulary panel: anomaly | 异常值; mean | 平均值
Visual: Two simple bar pairs side by side for 8 cm wings: one bar 1.76 labelled "anomaly left in", one bar 2.08 labelled "anomaly left out". A small reference bar for 6 cm wings at 1.68.
Speaker notes: Left in, the mean is 5.28 ÷ 3 = 1.76 s, almost the same as the 6 cm wings at 1.68 s, so the pattern nearly disappears. Left out, 2.08 s fits the pattern: longer wings, longer fall time. That is why we leave out anomalies, but only with a reason.

## 4. Answer key (for the teacher)

**Do Now:** IV: salt or no salt (or the amount of salt). DV: the time for the ice to melt. Control (any one): size of ice cube; room temperature; type of plate or saucer; starting temperature of the ice. Bonus: each person has a different reaction time, so they press the stopwatch at slightly different moments.

**Core A:** wing length: cm; fall time: s; drop height: m.

**Core B:** repeat, mean, total, number, anomaly, reason.

**Core C:** total = 3.93 s; mean = 3.93 ÷ 3 = 1.31 s.

**Core D (class results):** will vary. Check: units are in the headings only; all values have 2 decimal places; means are calculated as total ÷ number of values used; anomalies are circled, not rubbed out, with a reason in Notes. If a group's results do not follow the pattern or are incomplete, give them the sample data card (Section 6a) for Week 5.

**Stretch:**
- a) The anomaly is 1.12 s because it is much shorter than the other two times for 8 cm wings (2.05 s and 2.11 s), and shorter even than the 6 cm times.
- b) I will leave it out because it does not fit the pattern, and something probably went wrong (for example the spinner hit the table or did not spin).
- c) 2.05 + 2.11 = 4.16 ÷ 2 = 2.08 s.
- d) (1.68 + 1.74 + 1.62) ÷ 3 = 5.04 ÷ 3 = 1.68 s.

**Challenge (Secure examples):**
- a) Reaction time: the Measurer starts and stops the watch late or early. Better: the same person times every drop, or drop from higher so the fall takes longer and the reaction time error is a smaller part of it. Release: the Dropper lets go at slightly different heights or pushes the spinner. Better: line the paper clip up with the tape mark and open the fingers gently. Spinning: the spinner sometimes falls a little way before it starts to spin. Better: fold the wings to the same angle each time. Moving air: windows, fans or people walking past. Better: close windows, switch off fans, stand still.
- b) Agree or disagree, with both sides. Advantage: more repeats make the mean more reliable and make anomalies easier to spot. Disadvantage: it takes much longer, and the spinner may get bent after many drops (which changes it). Secure: "3 is enough for a lesson, but 5 would be better if we had time." Excellent: notes that more repeats reduce the effect of random errors (reaction time) but do not fix a mistake that happens every time (for example always dropping from too low).
- c) No. Our reaction time is about 0.2 s, so the real time could be about 0.2 s more or less than 2.05 s. The last digit (0.05) is not reliable. Excellent: suggests rounding to 0.1 s (2.1 s) or using the mean of repeats.

**Include it or leave it out? (slide 9):** 2.40 s is the anomaly: it is much longer than 1.70 s and 1.66 s. Possible causes: the Measurer stopped late; air from a fan or open window held it up; it was dropped from higher. If the group has no reason, the best thing is to repeat that drop.

**My thinking (guidance):** No. A result is evidence; rubbing it out hides what really happened. We circle an anomaly, explain it, and leave it out of the mean, so other people can still see it. Scientists who hide results they do not like are being dishonest. Accept Chinese.

**Exit ticket:** 1. The anomaly is 2.40 s. 2. (1.70 + 1.66) ÷ 2 = 3.36 ÷ 2 = 1.68 s. Secure: correct anomaly, correct working, divides by 2 (not 3), unit given.
