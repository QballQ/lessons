## 1. Lesson plan (one page, for the teacher)

- **Big idea:** Scientists share what they found, show their evidence, answer questions honestly and listen to feedback; that is how science gets checked.
- **Learning goals:**
  - I can present my part of our investigation clearly in English, using key science words and numbers from our results. 我能用英语清楚地展示我负责的探究部分，使用关键科学词汇和我们结果中的数字。
  - I can ask or answer a question about evidence, and give kind, specific feedback. 我能提出或回答一个关于证据的问题，并给出友善、具体的反馈。
- **Key words:**

| English | 中文 | Student-friendly meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Expo | 成果展 | an event where people show what they made and found |
| presentation | 展示 | a short talk to show and explain your work |
| audience | 观众 | the people who listen to a talk |
| evidence | 证据 | the numbers from the results that support the claim |
| conclusion | 结论 | what we found out, using our results as evidence |
| feedback | 反馈 | comments that help someone see what went well and what to improve |
| confident | 有把握的 | how sure you are |
| scientist | 科学家 | a person who finds things out by looking carefully and testing ideas |

- **Timeline (adjusted for the final skills check and survey):** Skills check 15, Hook 1, Do (Expo talks) 15, Think and Reflect 4, Survey and certificates 5. Total 40 minutes. There is no separate Learn phase this week. The gallery walk happens at break or in a later session. The Today slide (Slide 2) is shown during the Hook minute, while the skills check papers are collected; the skills check itself is never shortened.

- **Order of talks and who asks (fixed before the lesson):** write the six group names in order on Slide 3. Each group is asked a question by the group that speaks next; the first group asks the last group. Each student writes their peer feedback slip for the same group they ask.
- **Tiers:** (during the Expo)
  - Core: present your part using the talk frame; write one key number from each other group in the Expo notes.
  - Stretch: ask or answer a question with evidence; write a peer feedback slip that names something specific (a number, a graph, a step).
  - Challenge: compare two groups' conclusions: do they agree? If not, why might that be?
- **Language support:**
  - Frames on the talk frame card, one per part. Answering: "That is a good question. Our evidence shows ___, but it does not show ___." Asking: read the question card aloud. Big question: "Scientists find things out by ___."
  - Students may glance at or read from their talk frame card. A student who freezes may give one sentence in Chinese and then the key words in English.
  - Band 1 today: the student sits beside their language buddy. Before Talk 1 the buddy reads the Chinese frame for the student's part on the talk frame card (6b) and the Chinese line of their question card (6c), and the student rehearses once in Chinese; the student may read from the card, present the results part with the numbers and the graph (as planned in Week 9), and say one sentence in Chinese and then the English key words. Start with Core: the Expo notes table has bilingual headings and pre-printed choices to circle, so for each talk the student circles what the group changed and what they measured (the poster's Question panel carries the same bilingual labels) and copies one number with its unit from the poster's Results panel; the listen-for strip above the table shows which three sentences of each talk give the answers. Chinese answers count for every Core task; the exit ticket may be answered in Chinese by pointing to a week icon and using the starter 科学家___.
  - Strand 6 is judged on the clarity of the science, not on grammar or accent (`assessment/Rubric_and_Reporting.md`, Section 1).
- **Misconceptions to watch for:**
  - "A good talk means our results were perfect." A good talk explains what went wrong too.
  - "If another group got a different answer, one of us is wrong." Different results are worth discussing: what did they do differently?
  - "Feedback means saying what is bad." Two stars and a wish: two specific good things and one helpful idea.
- **Safety:** No practical hazards beyond normal classroom rules. Posters are on walls or display boards: keep walkways clear of bags.
- **If time is short:** the skills check is never shortened (it must match Week 1). Cut talks to 90 seconds; ask only three questions in total (after talks 2, 4 and 6). Move peer feedback slips to the gallery walk. Never skip the survey.
- **Assessment and handover links:**
  - Final skills check (score into tracker column S, "W10 skills check").
  - Interest survey (tracker columns U to Z, W10 S1 to S6).
  - Strand 6 from the talk record (6d); strands 1 to 5 confirmed from the posters (draft levels pencilled in after Week 9).
  - Peer feedback slips stuck on the back of each poster (can support strand 5).
  - Then awards, certificates and skills reports from `assessment/Rubric_and_Reporting.md`. Close-out tasks are in Section 7.

## 4. Answer key (for the teacher)

**Skills check:** mark with the mark scheme in `assessment/Skills_Check_Y7.md`, Section 2. Enter the total in tracker column S. Growth = Week 10 score minus Week 1 score (the tracker calculates it).

**Survey:** score with `assessment/Interest_Survey.md`, Section 2; enter W10 S1 to S6.

**Core (Expo notes):** any accurate entry, for example "Frost Hares | amount of sawdust | water held when the strip snapped | 0.9 g held 575 ml". Check units.

**Stretch:** a) any question card copied. b) Secure: a fair judgement with one thing the group said.

**Challenge (Secure examples):**
- "Frost Hares and Polar Bears both found that 0.9 g of sawdust made the strips much stronger than plain ice (575 ml each, compared with about 275 to 300 ml), so they agree."
- "Snow Owls and Ice Foxes both found tissue held a mean of 375 ml, but both groups only had two good repeats for tissue, and Ice Foxes' repeats were 50 ml apart, so we cannot be very sure."
- Excellent: names a specific difference in method (strips out of the box for different times; strips dried for different times; pouring speed) and explains how it could change the result.

**My thinking (guidance):** good answers link a method to an example: "Scientists find things out by doing fair tests: we changed only the amount of sawdust and kept 12 ml of water in every strip the same." / "... by repeating tests and taking the mean." / "... by being honest about anomalies." / "... by checking each other's work, like the plan check and today's questions." Accept Chinese. Copy strong answers (anonymised) into the programme summary.

**Exit ticket:** any thoughtful answer; use them for the teacher comment on the skills report and for the programme summary.

**Rubric judgement:** strands 1 to 5 from the poster (confirm the draft levels made after Week 9, using the worksheets from Weeks 6 to 9 and the logbook to judge individuals); strand 6 from the talk record. Use "What Secure looks like: Year 7" in `assessment/Rubric_and_Reporting.md`, Section 2. Note: the Secure examples there use illustrative values; your class values will differ.
