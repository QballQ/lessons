## 1. Lesson plan (one page, for the teacher)

- **Big idea:** When two things change together (correlation), it does not prove that one causes the other (causation): a hidden third factor, or chance, may explain it, and graphs can be drawn to mislead.
- **Learning goals:**
  - I can plot a scatter graph and describe the correlation it shows.
  - I can explain why a correlation does not prove causation, name a possible confounding variable, and fix a misleading graph.

**Key words (9)**

| English | 中文 | Student-friendly meaning |
|---|---|---|
| correlation | 相关性 | When two things change together. |
| positive correlation | 正相关 | As one goes up, the other goes up too. |
| negative correlation | 负相关 | As one goes up, the other goes down. |
| causation | 因果关系 | When one thing really makes another thing happen. |
| confounding variable | 混杂变量 | A hidden third factor that changes both things. |
| scatter graph | 散点图 | A graph of dots that shows if two measurements are linked. |
| anomaly | 异常值 | A result that does not fit the pattern. |
| axis | 坐标轴 | The number line along the side or bottom of a graph. |
| misleading | 误导性的 | Making people believe something that is not true. |

**Tiers**
- **Core:** record the card matches; plot the class (or sample) data on the pre-scaled grid; describe the correlation with the word bank.
- **Stretch:** explain one card using "confounding variable"; describe the pattern with "As ___ increases, ___"; spot the anomaly.
- **Challenge:** draw the line "arm span = height" and use it to give a verdict on the claim "your arm span equals your height"; explain why a scatter graph axis may start at 140 cm but a bar chart axis must start at 0.

**Language support**
- Frames: "As ___ increases, ___ increases / decreases." / "This could also be explained by ___." / "A correlation does not prove ___ because ___." / "The graph is misleading because ___."
- Chinese talk is welcome while matching cards and while discussing the graph. Measurements are called out in English ("one hundred and sixty-five centimetres").
- Vocabulary traps: "correlation" and "cause" are often used as if they mean the same thing; 相关 and 因果 are different. "Confounding" is a new word for most: link it to "confusing the picture". "Axis" plural is "axes".

**Misconceptions to watch for**
- "If two things rise together, one must cause the other."
- "A strong correlation proves causation." Even strong correlations can be caused by a third factor.
- "A bar chart that looks very different means a big difference." Always check where the axis starts.
- "One odd point ruins the pattern." An anomaly should be checked, not ignored or allowed to decide the verdict.

**Safety:** Low hazard. Tape measures: retract slowly and keep the metal end away from faces. Measuring bodies can feel personal: measuring is voluntary, no names on the board, and no comments about anyone's size. Students who prefer not to be measured take the Measurer or Recorder role; their row uses the sample data.

**If time is short:** skip measuring and give every group the sample data card; or cut the matching to 4 pairs (P1, P2, P4, P7). Fix the graph can become a 2-minute whole-class discussion with only questions 1 and 2.

**Assessment and handover links**
- Output: scatter graph (Core) and corrected bar chart (Fix the graph sheet). Collect the Fix the graph sheets to check who can explain a misleading axis.
- Exit ticket: confounding variable and correlation type (links to skills check question 10, correlation or cause).
- Logbook line: New words 新词汇: correlation, causation, confounding variable, with one example of each.

## 4. Answer key (for the teacher)

**Do Now:** mean = (0.18 + 0.15 + 0.20 + 0.19) ÷ 4 = 0.72 ÷ 4 = 0.18 s; range = 0.20 minus 0.15 = 0.05 s; Supported 有证据支持, Not supported 证据不支持, Not enough evidence yet 证据尚不足.

**Core 1 (matching cards):** P1 S · P2 Q · P3 W · P4 U · P5 R · P6 V · P7 T. Card **P7** could really show cause, because it comes from a fair test where only the light was changed.
Accept sensible alternative hidden factors students propose themselves (for example P6: "traffic jams on rainy days").

**Core 2 (graph):** points plotted accurately to within one small square (2 cm). With the sample data, 24 points rise from about (152, 151) to (183, 188), with one anomaly at (165, 145).

**Core 3:** positive correlation. "As height increases, arm span increases."

**Stretch 1 (models):**
- P1: "Card P1 shows a correlation between hot drink sales and colds. This could also be explained by cold winter weather, which is a confounding variable because it makes people buy hot drinks and also keeps people indoors together, so colds spread."
- P4: "...between foot size and reading. This could also be explained by age, which is a confounding variable because older children have bigger feet and have had more years of reading practice."
- P5: "...between the number of firefighters and fire damage. This could also be explained by the size of the fire: big fires cause more damage and need more firefighters."

**Stretch 2 (sample data):** "The point (165 cm, 145 cm) looks like an anomaly. A possible reason is a measuring or recording mistake, for example 165 written as 145, or the arms were not fully stretched." Excellent adds: "We should measure that person again, not just delete the point." With class data, accept any point clearly off the pattern, with a reason.

Counts from the table: above the line (positive difference) **10**; exactly on the line (0) **5**; below the line (negative difference) **9**; within 2 cm (difference from -2 to +2) **14**.
Model verdict (Secure): "Our verdict is not supported as an exact rule, but supported as an approximate rule: arm span is usually within about 5 cm of height, but only 5 out of 24 people were exactly equal." Excellent: "If the claim means 'roughly equal', the evidence supports it; if it means 'exactly equal', it does not. The claim needs an operational definition (Week 6 word)." Accept "Not enough evidence yet" if justified by the small sample or measuring uncertainty of about 1 to 2 cm.

**Challenge 2 (model):** "On a scatter graph we read the position of each point, so starting at 140 cm just zooms in, and the zigzag shows the gap. On a bar chart, people compare the length of the bars, so if the axis does not start at zero, small differences look huge, which is misleading."

**My thinking (model):** "Not necessarily. Schools with more money can afford bigger libraries and also more teachers, equipment and extra classes. Money (school funding) could be a confounding variable. The correlation alone is not evidence that the library causes the higher results; we would need a fair test." Accept any reasonable third factor (school size, families who read a lot at home).

**Exit ticket:** 1. Hot weather or summer (accept holidays). 2. Negative correlation. 3. Whether the axis starts at zero (also accept: sample size, who made the graph, units, labels).

**Fix the graph sheet** (see 6d):
1. Brand Q's bar looks **4 times** as tall as Brand P's bar (Q rises 0.8 hours above the axis start, P rises 0.2 hours).
2. Brand P 9.6 hours; Brand Q 10.2 hours; difference **0.6 hours = 36 minutes**; 0.6 ÷ 9.6 × 100 = **about 6%** longer.
3. Redrawn chart: vertical axis from 0 to 12 hours, gridlines every 1 hour; bar P to 9.6 and bar Q to 10.2; the bars look almost the same height. Axis labelled "Battery life (hours)", bars labelled.
4. Other problems (any two): only 1 battery of each brand was tested (sample size too small); the advert was made by Brand Q (not an independent source); no information about how the test was done (same torch? same temperature?); no repeats or range shown.
5. Verdict on "Brand Q batteries last much longer": **Not enough evidence yet** (accept Not supported for "much longer", since the measured difference is only about 6%).
