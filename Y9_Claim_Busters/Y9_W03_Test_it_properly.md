## 1. Lesson plan (one page, for the teacher)

- **Big idea:** One result can fool us, so we repeat, calculate the mean and range, and ask whether a difference is bigger than the normal variation.
- **Learning goals:**
  - I can carry out a fair ruler-drop test with five repeats per hand and calculate the mean and range.
  - I can write a conclusion that says whether a difference is big enough to matter, mentioning uncertainty.

**Key words (10)**

| English | 中文 | Student-friendly meaning |
|---|---|---|
| reaction time | 反应时间 | How long it takes you to respond to something. |
| dominant hand | 惯用手 | The hand you write with. |
| independent variable | 自变量 | The thing we change on purpose. |
| dependent variable | 因变量 | The thing we measure. |
| control variable | 控制变量 | Things we keep the same so the test is fair. |
| repeat | 重复 | Do the same test again to check. |
| mean | 平均值 | Add up all the values, then divide by how many there are. |
| range | 极差 | The biggest value minus the smallest value. |
| uncertainty | 不确定性 | How unsure we are about a result, because measurements vary. |
| variation | 波动（变化） | Results changing a little each time, even in the same test. |

**Timeline**

| Phase | Min | Teacher does | Students do | Resource |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Do Now | 4 | Shows two retrieval questions (a bias, a mean). Takes answers. | Answer on the back of the worksheet. | Slides 1 to 2 |
| Hook | 3 | Drops the 15 cm "PRIZE" strip between a volunteer's fingers (held at the middle). Repeats with 2 or 3 volunteers. Then asks "Is your dominant hand faster?" | Watch; vote Yes / No / No difference. | Slides 3 to 4, prize strip |
| Learn | 7 | Names the variables. Demonstrates the ruler drop with a student, using the picture steps. Shows how to use the conversion table and how to calculate mean and range. | Say the variables back; spot the controls in the demo. | Slides 5 to 8, method card, conversion table |
| Do | 18 | Reads the safety slide aloud in both languages. Circulates: checks the 0 cm mark is level with the top of the thumb, that droppers wait a random time, and that hands alternate. | In pairs: 5 drops per hand per person, alternating hands; convert each distance to time; calculate mean and range; Stretch and Challenge. | Slides 9 to 10, rulers, method card, conversion table, worksheet |
| Think and Reflect | 8 | Shows the overlap diagram. Quick class count: whose dominant hand was faster? Asks "Is that enough to give a verdict?" Collects exit tickets. | Decide whether their own difference is bigger than their range; share with a frame; exit ticket and logbook line. | Slides 11 to 12, worksheet |

**Tiers**
- **Core:** complete the results table; calculate the mean and range for each hand with the guided steps.
- **Stretch:** compare the two hands using means and ranges; write a conclusion with an uncertainty sentence.
- **Challenge:** check a table value with t = √(2d ÷ g); explain why hands were alternated; judge whether the class count is enough to support the claim.

**Language support**
- Frames: "My dominant hand mean was ___ s and my other hand mean was ___ s." / "The data suggests ___; however, ___." / "To be more confident, we would need ___." / "Our verdict is ___ because ___."
- Chinese talk is welcome in pairs during the test. Readings are called out in English numbers ("fourteen centimetres").
- Vocabulary traps: "range" in everyday English means a choice of things; in maths it is one number (biggest minus smallest). 极差 is not 范围 here. "Mean" is not "means" (method). Students may say "reflex": a reflex is automatic; catching a ruler is a reaction.

**Misconceptions to watch for**
- "A bigger distance means faster." No: the ruler falls further when you are slower.
- "Any difference in the means proves the claim." Small differences can be normal variation.
- "The range tells you the average." Range tells you how spread out the results are.
- "Five repeats makes it certain." Repeats make us more confident, never certain.

**Safety:** Low hazard. Rulers can hit faces or fingers if flicked or swung. Students sit for every test, the dropper holds the ruler still and upright, and the catcher keeps their face back from the ruler. See the safety slide and kit card for the bilingual lines.

**If time is short:** do 3 repeats per hand (the minimum) instead of 5; skip the Challenge formula check; run the class count with hands up instead of collecting numbers.

**Assessment and handover links**
- Output: results table with mean and range, and a conclusion that mentions uncertainty (Stretch). Spot-check 6 worksheets for correct range calculation; range errors are common and feed into the Week 5 checkpoint (question 6: mean and anomaly).
- Exit ticket: mean and range calculation plus one verdict.
- Logbook line: What I found 我发现了什么: "My dominant hand was ___ by ___ s, which is / is not bigger than my range."

## 4. Answer key (for the teacher)

**Do Now:** 1. Confirmation bias. 2. 12 + 15 + 11 + 14 = 52; 52 ÷ 4 = 13 cm.

**Core 1:** I change **which** hand I use: the **independent** variable. I measure catch distance, converted to **reaction time**: the **dependent** variable. I keep the same: the same ruler, the same dropper, the same starting position (also accept: same gap between finger and thumb, arm resting on the table, no warning, random wait, same chair): **control** variables.

**Core 2 and 3: sample results** (one student; class results will vary, typically 0.13 to 0.22 s)

| Try | Dominant: distance (cm) | Dominant: time (s) | Other: distance (cm) | Other: time (s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 14 | 0.17 | 16 | 0.18 |
| 2 | 12 | 0.16 | 13 | 0.16 |
| 3 | 15 | 0.17 | 18 | 0.19 |
| 4 | 11 | 0.15 | 15 | 0.17 |
| 5 | 13 | 0.16 | 17 | 0.19 |
| Mean | | 0.81 ÷ 5 = 0.162, so **0.16 s** | | 0.89 ÷ 5 = 0.178, so **0.18 s** |
| Range | | 0.17 minus 0.15 = **0.02 s** | | 0.19 minus 0.16 = **0.03 s** |

Note: students should convert each try first, then take the mean of the times. Taking the mean distance and converting gives almost the same answer here, but not always, because the conversion is not a straight line. Accept either at Core; praise the correct order.

**Stretch 1 (sample):** "My dominant hand was faster by 0.02 s." (0.178 minus 0.162 = 0.016 s, which rounds to 0.02 s.)

**Stretch 2 (sample):** Dominant bar from 0.15 to 0.17 s; other bar from 0.16 to 0.19 s. They overlap (0.16 to 0.17 s).

**Stretch 3 (Secure model):** "The data suggests my dominant hand is slightly faster, by about 0.02 s; however, the ranges overlap, so this difference could be normal variation between tries. To be more confident, we would need more repeats and results from more people." Excellent adds a verdict: "Our verdict for me is not enough evidence yet."

**Challenge 1:** d = 20 cm = 0.20 m. t = √(2 × 0.20 ÷ 9.8) = √(0.40 ÷ 9.8) = √0.0408 = 0.202 s, which is about 0.20 s.

**Challenge 2 (model):** "We might get better with practice, or get tired. If we did all the dominant-hand tries first, the other hand would get more practice (or more tiredness), so the test would not be fair. Alternating shares the practice equally, so it is a control." Excellent: names practice as a possible confounding factor (preview of Week 4).

**Challenge 3 (model):** "15 out of 24 is more than half, but if hands were equal we would expect about 12, and 15 could happen by chance. I would want to know how big the differences were, whether they were bigger than each student's range, and whether a bigger sample gives the same pattern. Verdict: not enough evidence yet." Accept "Supported, but weakly" only if the student explains the uncertainty.

**My thinking (model):** "No. Results vary each time because of attention, tiredness and small changes in how the ruler is dropped. Scientists repeat tests and use the mean and range so that one lucky or unlucky result does not decide the verdict."

**Exit ticket:**
1. Mean = (0.19 + 0.16 + 0.21 + 0.18 + 0.16) ÷ 5 = 0.90 ÷ 5 = **0.18 s**
2. Range = 0.21 minus 0.16 = **0.05 s**
3. **Not supported** is the best answer: the other hand's mean (0.17 s) is actually slightly smaller, so the data does not support "dominant is faster". Also accept **Not enough evidence yet** if the reason mentions that the difference (0.01 s) is much smaller than the ranges (0.05 s and 0.04 s), so the ranges overlap. "Supported" is incorrect.

## 7. Notes for the incoming teacher

- The most common error is the range: students write "0.15 to 0.17" instead of subtracting. Insist on one number.
- Watch for droppers who drop in a rhythm; catchers learn the rhythm and "catch" impossibly fast (under 5 cm). Those tries should be repeated.
- What students found hard in trial: (leave blank for now)
- The method card and table are reused if a group chooses claim 12 ("You react faster in the morning than after lunch") in Week 7. Next week moves from comparing two groups to looking for patterns between two variables.
