## 1. Lesson plan (one page, for the teacher)

- **Big idea:** To find out whether something really works, scientists run fair trials with a control group, a placebo and blinding, and they treat the people in their trials honestly and with respect.
- **Learning goals:**
  - I can explain why a fair trial needs a control group, a placebo, blinding and a big enough sample.
  - I can compare two groups' results using means and ranges and give an honest verdict, and discuss what makes research with people ethical.

**Key words (10)**

| English | 中文 | Student-friendly meaning |
|---|---|---|
| fair trial | 公平试验 | A test that compares groups fairly, changing only one thing. |
| control group | 对照组 | The group that does not get the thing being tested, for comparison. |
| placebo | 安慰剂 | A fake treatment that looks real but does nothing. |
| placebo effect | 安慰剂效应 | When believing something works changes the result. |
| blind test | 盲测 | The people in the test do not know which group they are in. |
| double-blind | 双盲 | Neither the people in the test nor the testers know who is in which group. |
| random | 随机 | Decided by chance, not by choice. |
| sample size | 样本量 | How many people or things are tested. |
| ethics | 伦理 | Rules about what is right and fair, especially when people take part. |
| informed consent | 知情同意 | Agreeing to take part after being told what will happen. |

**Adjusted timeline (Week 5 checkpoint).** The 10-minute checkpoint replaces the Do Now. The trial runs before Learn, so students do not know about placebos until after the test. The deception is mild (being told an ordinary pen might help memory), lasts about 7 minutes, and is fully explained in the same lesson.

| Phase | Min | Teacher does | Students do | Resource |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Checkpoint (replaces Do Now) | 10 | Gives out the checkpoint, reads the instructions, keeps time. Students use their own pens. Incoming teacher observes. | Complete the checkpoint alone and in silence. | Slides 1 to 2, `assessment/Checkpoint_Y9.md` |
| Hook | 3 | Tells the Tu Youyou story: an idea from a text more than 1,600 years old, tested carefully, leading to artemisinin and a Nobel Prize. | Answer: "Should we believe an idea because it is old? How would you find out?" | Slides 3 to 4 |
| Do (Part A: the trial) | 7 | Follows the lucky pen script exactly. Hands out a pen and a folded card to each student (alternating versions). Times the study, distraction and recall. Shows the list for marking. | Read their card silently; study the word list for 60 seconds; count backwards; recall for 2 minutes; swap and mark by code. | Slides 5 to 8, pens, cards, word lists, recall sheets |
| Learn (debrief) | 6 | Keeps the promise: reveals both cards and that all pens are identical. Teaches control group, placebo, placebo effect, blinding, double-blind, random allocation, sample size. | Hold up their card when their code is called; repeat key words. | Slides 9 to 10 |
| Do (Part B: results) | 5 | Collects group results on the board by code only; calculates class means with the class. | Recorders complete the group results table; everyone completes Core and Stretch. | Slide 11, group results table, worksheet |
| Think and Reflect | 9 | Leads the ethics debrief (5 min): offers everyone the chance to withdraw their score. Collects exit tickets (2 min). Logbook line (2 min). | Discuss ethics questions in pairs (any language), share with frames; exit ticket and logbook line. | Slides 12 to 13, ethics debrief card, worksheet |

**Tiers**
- **Core:** record class results; gap fill on control group, placebo, blind test and sample size.
- **Stretch:** compare the two means and ranges and give a verdict; explain why every pen had to be identical.
- **Challenge:** redesign the trial to be double-blind and randomised with a bigger sample; explain what it would mean if the "lucky" group had scored higher; explain why Tu Youyou's team tested so many extracts.

**Language support**
- Frames: "The control group was ___ because ___." / "The data suggests ___; however, ___." / "To be more confident, we would need ___." / "I think it was / was not OK because ___."
- Chinese talk is welcome during the ethics debrief: feelings are easier to discuss in a first language. Report back in English with a frame.
- Recall may be written in English or Chinese; both count, and spelling does not matter. This is the same rule for both groups, so the test stays fair.
- Vocabulary traps: "blind" here does not mean unable to see. "Trial" in English also means a court case. 安慰剂 is a fake medicine; the idea also applies to fake "lucky" objects.

**Misconceptions to watch for**
- "The placebo effect means the person is stupid or fooled." Expectations affect everyone, including scientists.
- "If the lucky group scored higher, the pen works." The pens were identical, so any real difference must come from belief (placebo effect) or chance.
- "A small difference in means proves something." Compare with the ranges and the sample size.
- "Tu Youyou proved old remedies are true." Her team tested many old ideas and most did not work; careful testing found the one that did.

**Safety and ethics:** No practical hazards beyond normal classroom rules. Ethics controls for the trial: taking part is voluntary (a student may watch instead); the deception is mild, harmless and short; it is explained in the same lesson, straight after the test; scores are anonymous (codes only, never names) and any student may withdraw their score after the debrief. Never use scores to compare individual students.

**If time is short:** the checkpoint and the debrief are fixed. Cut the recall time to 90 seconds; skip Part B calculations and give the class means from the board; cut Challenge. Never cut the reveal or the chance to withdraw scores.

**Assessment and handover links**
- Checkpoint scores (out of 12) go into the tracker today (column "W5 checkpoint"); they feed the handover note (end of Week 5).
- Output: trial results (group results table and worksheet Stretch).
- Exit ticket: placebo, control group and blind test definitions.
- Logbook line: What I found 我发现了什么: "A fair trial needs ___ because ___."
- The incoming teacher observes this lesson. Use the last 2 minutes to introduce them to the class.

## 4. Answer key (for the teacher)

**Checkpoint:** use the mark scheme in `assessment/Checkpoint_Y9.md` (6 questions, 12 marks, based on skills check questions 1 to 6). Enter scores in the tracker before the handover note is written.

**Core 1:** class values vary. Sample class results (use if the class data is lost or too small, labelled "Sample data from another class"):

| | Code 41 ("Focus Pen") | Code 52 (control) |
|---|---|---|
| Scores | 9, 11, 8, 12, 10, 7, 13, 10, 9, 11, 10, 12 | 10, 9, 12, 8, 11, 9, 10, 7, 12, 10, 9, 11 |
| Number of people | 12 | 12 |
| Total | 122 | 118 |
| Mean score | 122 ÷ 12 = 10.17, about **10.2** | 118 ÷ 12 = 9.83, about **9.8** |
| Range | 13 minus 7 = **6** | 12 minus 7 = **5** |

**Core 2:** a) control b) placebo c) blind d) random e) sample size

**Stretch 1 (Secure model, sample data):** "The Code 41 mean was 10.2 and the Code 52 mean was 9.8. The difference was about 0.3 words. The ranges were 6 and 5. The data suggests the Focus Pen group remembered slightly more; however, the difference is much smaller than the ranges and there were only 12 people in each group, so it could be chance. Our verdict is not enough evidence yet (accept not supported) because the difference is tiny compared with the normal variation." Excellent adds: "I was not blind as the tester, and we only did one test each."

**Stretch 2 (model):** "If the pens were different, a better pen (for example, one that writes more smoothly) could change the scores. Then we would not know whether it was the pen or the belief. Identical pens mean the only difference between the groups was what the card said."

**Challenge 1 (model):** "A helper who is not in the room shuffles the cards and keeps the code list, so neither the students nor the teacher running the test know who is in which group (double-blind). The marker also does not know the codes. Use a much bigger sample, for example every Year 9 class, with the same words, timing and instructions for everyone. Repeat the test with a different word list." Accept any answer that includes double-blind and bigger sample; Excellent also mentions random allocation and informed consent.

**Challenge 2 (model):** "1. The placebo effect: believing the pen helps might make people concentrate harder or feel more confident. 2. Chance: with small groups, one group can score higher by luck. (Also accept: the tester treated the groups differently, since the test was not double-blind.)"

**Challenge 3 (model):** "An old idea is a claim, not evidence. Most old remedies they tested did not work, so if they had trusted the texts they would have chosen wrongly. Testing many extracts carefully, and comparing them fairly, showed which one really worked."

**My thinking (model):** "Sometimes, if telling people would spoil the test, but only if it is harmless, short, approved by an ethics committee, explained straight afterwards, and people can withdraw their data. In real medical trials, people give informed consent: they agree that they might get a placebo, even though they are not told which group they are in."

**Exit ticket:** 1. A fake treatment that looks real but does nothing (accept "something with no real effect, used for comparison"). 2. To compare with the group that got the treatment, so we know whether any difference was caused by the treatment and not by something else. 3. **b)**.

### 6a. Lucky pen trial: teacher script

Create an A4 teacher script card for Year 9 Week 5, Claim Busters series, using the Think Like a Scientist design system. Print spec: A4, 1 copy for the teacher (and 1 for the incoming teacher).

**Before you start.** Every pen is identical. Code 41 cards carry the "Focus Pen" message; Code 52 cards are the control. The cards are shuffled, so chance decides each student's group. Treat every student in exactly the same way during the test.

**1. Hand out (1 minute). Say:**
"Now we are doing a short memory test. Everyone gets a pen and a card. Read your card silently. Do not show anyone your card and do not talk about it. I promise I will explain everything straight after the test. Write the code from your card in the box at the top of your recall sheet. If you would rather not do the memory test, that is completely fine: you can watch instead."

**2. Study (1 minute 15 seconds). Say:**
"Turn over the word list. You have 60 seconds to read the words. Read only; do not write anything." Start the timer. At 60 seconds: "Stop. Turn the list face down." Safety Officers collect the lists.

**3. Distraction (30 seconds). Say:**
"In your head, count backwards in threes from 100: 100, 97, 94, and so on. Keep going until I say stop." At 30 seconds: "Stop."

**4. Recall (2 minutes). Say:**
"Use your new pen. Write down as many words from the list as you can remember, in any order. You can write them in English or in Chinese. Spelling does not matter. You have two minutes." At 2 minutes: "Pens down."

**5. Mark (1 minute 30 seconds). Say:**
"Swap your recall sheet with your neighbour. Look at the list on the screen. Give one point for each word that is on the list, in English or Chinese. A word written twice only scores once. Write the score out of 20 in the score box, then give the sheet back, face down."

**6. Reveal: go straight into the debrief (Learn). Say:**
"Now I keep my promise. There were two different cards. Code 41, please hold up your card. Your card said your pen was a special Focus Pen that helps memory. Code 52, hold up yours. Your card just said 'use this pen'. In fact, every pen in this room is exactly the same, ordinary pen. I told half of you something that was not true. I am sorry for the trick. It was harmless, and it was the only way to find out whether believing something works can change the result. Nobody did anything wrong, and nobody's individual score will be shared."

**Do not:** praise or encourage one group more than the other; look at codes while students are working; use the trial to compare individual students.
