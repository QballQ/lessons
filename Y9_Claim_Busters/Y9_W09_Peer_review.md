## 1. Lesson plan (one page, for the teacher)

- **Big idea:** Scientists do not just publish what they think: other scientists check their work first (peer review), and good scientists welcome criticism and change their work because of it.
- **Learning goals:**
  - I can write a fact-check report that gives a verdict on the three-point scale with a confidence level, using our data as evidence.
  - I can review another group's report like a journal reviewer, with specific and helpful comments, and revise our own report in response to a review.

**Key words (10)**

| English | 中文 | Student-friendly meaning |
|---|---|---|
| peer review | 同行评审 | Other scientists check a piece of work before it is published. |
| reviewer | 审稿人 | The person who checks the work and writes comments. |
| fact-check report | 事实核查报告 | A report that tests a claim and gives an honest verdict. |
| verdict | 结论（裁定） | Our final decision about a claim. |
| confidence | 把握程度 | How sure we are about our verdict: low, medium or high. |
| limitation | 局限性 | Something that stops our evidence from showing everything. |
| major issue | 主要问题 | A problem that could change the verdict. |
| minor issue | 次要问题 | A small problem that makes the report less clear but does not change the verdict. |
| revise | 修改 | Change and improve your work after feedback. |
| feedback | 反馈 | Comments that help someone improve their work. |

**Adjusted timeline (Week 9).** The report must be drafted, reviewed and revised in one lesson, and the self-assessment slip is completed in Think and Reflect, so the standard timings change: Do Now 3, Hook 2, Learn 4, Do 20 (draft 12, review 8), Think and Reflect 11 (revise 5, self-assessment 4, talk parts and exit ticket 2). Reports are collected at the end of the lesson; there is no homework.

**Section owners for the draft (groups of 4; in groups of 3, the Lead Scientist also takes the Reporter's sections)**
- Lead Scientist: page 1 (claim, question, prediction, one-sentence summary).
- Measurer: page 2 (method, variables, operational definition, sample size).
- Recorder: page 3 results table (means and ranges, checked with a calculator).
- Reporter: page 3 graph and "What the graph shows".
- Safety Officer: ethics and safety (page 2) and limitations (page 4).
- Everyone, last 3 minutes: CER analysis, verdict and confidence (page 4).

**Tiers**
- **Core:** write your section of the report with the frames; find problems in the sample extract; use the reviewer checklist with Yes / Partly / No.
- **Stretch:** write the CER analysis with numbers from the data; rewrite the sample extract's verdict honestly with a confidence level; write comments that say where, why it matters and how to fix it.
- **Challenge:** weigh other explanations and the limits of the data in the limitations; reply to a reviewer comment you disagree with, using evidence; explain why journals use reviewers who did not do the work.

**Language support**
- Frames for authors: "We tested ___ by ___." / "The mean ___ was ___ compared with ___." / "This could also be explained by ___." / "Our verdict is ___ with ___ confidence because ___." / "To be more confident, we would need ___."
- Frames for reviewers: "In section ___, ___. This matters because ___. We suggest ___." / "The strongest part of this report is ___ because ___."
- Chinese talk is welcome during drafting and reviewing; reports are written in English with key words in English. Comments may be written in Chinese if needed, with key science words in English.
- Vocabulary traps: "review" here is not "revise for a test" (复习); it is checking someone's work (评审). "Revise" here means improve the work (修改), not study. "Confidence" is about the evidence, not about feeling brave. "Major" and "minor" are about effect on the verdict, not about how long the comment is.

**Misconceptions to watch for**
- "A good review only says nice things." A good review is kind and honest: it finds real problems and suggests fixes.
- "If the reviewers find problems, our project failed." Every real paper gets comments; responding well is part of strand 5.
- "Not enough evidence yet is a weak verdict." It is often the most honest one.
- "High confidence means we did a lot of work." Confidence depends on the evidence: size of difference, overlap of ranges, sample size and fairness.

**Safety:** No practical hazards beyond normal classroom rules. Reviews comment on the science, never on people or their English.

**If time is short:** the draft and the review are fixed. Reviewers write a minimum of 2 comment slips (at least one major or the strongest minor). Revisions focus on major issues only. If the self-assessment slip cannot be done today, do it in the first 2 minutes of the Week 10 conference set-up only if time allows; otherwise skip it (it does not change levels).

**Assessment and handover links**
- Output: a revised fact-check report per group, with the reviewer checklist, comment slips and revision log attached. Collect them all at the end of the lesson.
- Before Week 10: judge rubric strands 1 to 5 for each student from the report, the plan, the data sheet, the revision log, the logbook and your Week 8 run-sheet notes (`assessment/Rubric_and_Reporting.md`, Sections 1 and 2). Prepare certificates as described in the Week 10 lesson plan.
- Self-assessment slips (`assessment/Rubric_and_Reporting.md`, Section 7): compare with your judgement; a gap of two levels or more is worth a 1-minute chat in Week 10.
- Reviewer checklists and comment slips are supporting evidence for strand 5 (evaluating and critical thinking), including for the reviewers.
- Exit ticket: one change made after review, verdict with confidence, and each student's part in the Week 10 talk.
- Logbook line: if time allows, What I found 我发现了什么: "A reviewer made us change ___."

## 4. Answer key (for the teacher)

**Core 1: problems in the sample extract (any three)**

| Problem | Major or minor | Section |
|---|---|---|
| The question is not testable ("good" is not defined; nothing measured is named). | Major | Question |
| Only one cup with a lid and one without: too small a sample, could be chance. | Major | Method |
| No fixed time (how long did they wait?) and no controls listed (same cup, same volume, same place). | Major | Method |
| No units in the table headings (°C). | Minor | Results |
| The bar chart axis starts at 36, so a 3 °C difference looks huge: misleading. | Major (accept minor if the student says the numbers are still readable) | Results |
| No limitations: every test has some. | Major | Limitations |
| The verdict overclaims: "proves", "always", "100% sure" from one cup each. | Major | Verdict |
| No operational definition of "warm for longer". | Major | Method |

**Core 2 (model):** "In section 2 (method), you do not say how long you waited before measuring the end temperature. This matters because if the lid cup was measured sooner, it would be warmer for that reason alone. We suggest you state the time (for example 10 minutes) and measure both cups at the same time."

**Stretch 1 (model):** "Our verdict is Supported with low confidence because the lid cup was 3 °C warmer at the end, but we only tested one cup of each and did not record the time. To be more confident, we would need three or more cups of each, measured at the same fixed time." Also accept "Not enough evidence yet" with a sensible reason. "Proves" and "100%" must be gone.

**Stretch 2 (model):** "The strongest part of this report is that both cups started at the same temperature (50), because that makes the comparison fairer."

**Challenge 1 (model):** "Thank the reviewer and agree. Add the small sample size to the limitations, explain that the difference could be chance, and lower our confidence (for example from high to medium or low). We could suggest a bigger test as a next step." Excellent: "If our ranges did not overlap at all, we could explain that in the reply, but still lower the confidence."

**Challenge 2 (model):** "The authors are too close to their own work and see what they expect (confirmation bias). Reviewers who did not do the work notice problems more easily. Hiding names helps reviewers judge the science, not the person, and lets them be honest without worrying about friendship or fame."

**My thinking (model):** any thoughtful answer. "Receiving can be harder because it feels personal, but scientists need feedback to catch mistakes; giving good feedback is hard because it must be honest and kind at the same time."

**Exit ticket:** 1 any specific change ("we added units", "we changed High to Medium confidence", "we redrew the bar chart from 0"). 2 should match their report. 3 every student ticks at least one part.

**What a Secure and an Excellent report look like:** use `assessment/Rubric_and_Reporting.md`, Section 2, "Year 9 Claim Busters" (the claim 4 example). Do not rewrite it for students.

**Typical reviewer comments and good author responses**

| Reviewer comment | Good response |
|---|---|
| "No units in your table." | Agree: add units to every heading. (Minor.) |
| "Your ranges overlap, but your confidence is High." | Agree: lower to Medium or Low, and explain why in the verdict. (Major.) |
| "You used a line graph for brands A, B and C." | Agree: redraw as a bar chart starting at 0. (Major if it changes how the data reads.) |
| "Your evidence has no numbers." | Agree: add the means and the difference. (Major.) |
| "You should have tested 20 people." | Partly agree: we had 18 minutes; add sample size to limitations and lower confidence. |
| "Your verdict should be Supported." | Disagree with a reason if the data does not show it: "Our ranges overlap, so the difference could be chance; we keep Not enough evidence yet." |

Header: "Reviewer report 审稿意见 | Report from Group ___ | Claim ___ | Reviewer code R___ (no names 不写姓名)".

Rules box (bordered, top): "Comment on the science, not the people or their English. Be specific: say where. Say why it matters. Suggest a fix. Be kind and honest. 只评论科学内容；具体指出位置；说明原因；提出建议；友善而诚实。"

**Part A. Summary 摘要.** "The authors tested ___ by ___. They found ___. Their verdict is ___ with ___ confidence." (3 lines)

**Part B. Major checks: could change the verdict 主要检查：可能改变结论.** Table with 5 columns and 9 rows. Headings: Check | Yes 是 | Partly 部分 | No 否 | Where? (page or section)
1. The question is testable and matches the claim.
2. The test is fair: one thing changed, key controls kept the same.
3. The sample size and repeats are enough, and the authors give a reason.
4. The table has units, means and ranges. We checked one mean with a calculator and it is correct.
5. The graph is the right type, with labelled axes, units and an honest scale.
6. The verdict follows from the data: the evidence has numbers and the reasoning makes sense.
7. The limitations are honest and the confidence level matches the evidence.
8. People: ethics followed (voluntary, codes not names), or no human participants.

**Part C. Minor checks 次要检查.** Table with 4 columns and 5 rows (same Yes / Partly / No headings, no "Where" column):
1. Units are given everywhere.
2. Key science words are used correctly.
3. The graph and table have titles.
4. The report is easy to follow in order.

**Part D. Strengths 优点.** "The strongest part of this report is ___ because ___." (2 lines)

**Part E. Recommendation 建议** (tick one):
☐ Accept as it is 直接接受 ☐ Accept after minor changes 小修后接受 ☐ Major changes needed, then check again 大修后再审

Footer: "We wrote ___ comment slips (3 to 5). At least one is about a strength."
