## 1. Lesson plan (one page, for the teacher)

- **Big idea:** Before we believe a claim, we ask what kind of claim it is and whether evidence could ever check it.
- **Learning goals:**
  - I can sort claims into fact, opinion, testable and not testable, and explain my choice.
  - I can use Claim, Evidence, Reasoning and the three-point verdict scale to judge a claim.

**Key words (8)**

| English | 中文 | Student-friendly meaning |
|---|---|---|
| claim | 说法（主张） | A sentence that says something is true. |
| fact | 事实 | A claim that has been checked many times and is accepted as true. |
| opinion | 观点 | What someone thinks or likes. Evidence cannot prove it right or wrong. |
| testable | 可检验的 | We could collect evidence that might show the claim is wrong. |
| not testable | 不可检验的 | No possible evidence could ever check it. |
| evidence | 证据 | Information (measurements, observations, data) that helps us decide. |
| reasoning | 推理 | Explaining how the evidence links to the claim. |
| verdict | 结论（裁定） | Our final decision about a claim. |

**Adjusted timeline (Week 1 assessment week).** The baseline skills check (15 minutes) and interest survey (3 minutes) replace the usual Do Now and shorten Learn and Do. There is one short activity only: the claim sort.

| Phase | Min | Teacher does | Students do | Resource |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Welcome (replaces Do Now) | 2 | Seats groups of 4 at fixed tables, gives out role cards, explains "no exams, lots of thinking". | Find their table, take a role card. | Slides 1 to 2, role cards |
| Baseline skills check | 15 | Gives out the paper, reads the instructions aloud, keeps time. Reassures: "This is to show your growth, not a test score." | Complete the Year 9 skills check alone and in silence. | Slide 3, `assessment/Skills_Check_Y9.md` |
| Interest survey | 3 | Gives out the survey, reads each statement aloud in English. | Circle one face per statement. | Slide 4, `assessment/Interest_Survey.md` |
| Hook | 3 | "Can you see the Great Wall from the Moon with your eyes alone?" Hands-up vote: Yes / No / Not sure. Does not reveal. | Vote, then tell a partner why (any language). | Slide 5 |
| Learn | 5 | Teaches the four kinds of claim with the flowchart, then CER and the verdict scale. | Repeat the key words; answer quick "fact or opinion?" questions with fingers (1 to 4). | Slides 6 to 8 |
| Do | 8 | Gives each group a card set and an A3 sorting mat. Circulates; challenges groups with "Could any evidence show that is wrong?" | Claim sort in groups (16 cards, 4 boxes). Record the sort on the worksheet (Core), then Stretch / Challenge. | Slide 9, claim cards, sorting mat, worksheet |
| Think and Reflect | 4 | Returns to the Great Wall: shows the evidence, models a CER and asks for a verdict. Collects exit tickets. | Agree a verdict with a partner, share one sentence with a frame. Exit ticket. | Slides 10 to 11, worksheet exit ticket |

**Tiers**
- **Core:** record the group sort; gap fill on the four kinds of claim using the word bank.
- **Stretch:** explain why card J is testable but card E is not; rewrite a vague claim (card P) so it can be tested.
- **Challenge:** write a full CER with a verdict for the Great Wall claim using the evidence box; explain why card N can never be tested.

**Language support**
- Frames: "This claim is testable because we could measure ___." / "This is an opinion because ___." / "Our verdict is ___ because ___."
- Chinese talk is welcome during the sort and the Hook pair talk. Groups report back in English with a frame.
- Vocabulary traps: "fact" in everyday speech means "anything I am sure about"; here it means "checked many times". 结论 is also used for "conclusion", so stress that "verdict" is the final decision on a claim. "Testable" does not mean "true": a testable claim can be false.

**Misconceptions to watch for**
- "If it is not testable, it must be false." No: not testable means evidence cannot decide, so scientists cannot say either way.
- "Opinions can never be studied." A claim about opinions ("Most of our class prefers winter") is testable with a survey. Card J is planted for this.
- "A fact is just something a teacher or a book says." A fact is a testable claim that has passed many tests.
- "Not enough evidence yet" sounds like failure. It is an honest and respectable scientific answer.

**Safety:** No practical hazards beyond normal classroom rules.

**If time is short:** the skills check and survey are fixed. Cut the Challenge section and run the Great Wall verdict as a 1-minute teacher model. If the sort is not finished, groups sort only cards A to L.

**Assessment and handover links**
- Baseline skills check papers (marked out of 20) go into the progress tracker; interest survey responses go into the tracker survey tab.
- Exit ticket: shows whether each student can tell testable from not testable (quick check for Week 2 grouping).
- Logbook: no logbook line this week because of the assessment time. Students start the logbook in Week 2.

## 4. Answer key (for the teacher)

**Claim sort (best fit, with defensible alternatives)**

| Card | Best fit | Notes and acceptable alternatives |
|---|---|---|
| A. Water freezes at 0 °C at normal air pressure. | Fact | Testable is also correct (every fact is testable). Accept if the reason says "it can be checked". |
| B. Sound travels faster through water than through air. | Fact | True: about 1,500 m/s in water, about 340 m/s in air. Many students will say testable; accept with the note that it has already been tested. |
| C. The Moon shines because it reflects light from the Sun. | Fact | Accept testable. |
| D. The Earth takes about 365 days to travel once around the Sun. | Fact | Accept testable. |
| E. Winter is the best season of the year. | Opinion | Not testable is also defensible: no measurement can decide "best". |
| F. Basketball is more exciting to watch than football. | Opinion | Push Stretch students: "Most people in our school find basketball more exciting" would be testable. |
| G. Pandas are the cutest animals in the world. | Opinion | |
| H. Rainy days are boring. | Opinion | |
| I. A paper aeroplane with bigger wings flies further. | Testable | Good fair-test discussion: what else must stay the same? |
| J. Most students in our class prefer winter to summer. | Testable | The planted trap. It is about opinions but it is a claim about the world: a survey can check it. Not "opinion". |
| K. A dropped coin lands heads up more often than tails up. | Testable | Toss a coin many times and count. A fair coin gives about 50:50, so this is likely to be not supported. |
| L. The Great Wall of China can be seen from the Moon with your eyes alone. | Testable | It has already been tested and is not supported, so it is not a fact. Students who put it under Fact have accepted a popular myth: use it in Think and Reflect. |
| M. There is an invisible, silent dragon in the school car park that leaves no trace at all. | Not testable | The claim is built so no evidence could count against it. |
| N. Last night everything in the universe doubled in size, including every ruler. | Not testable | Every measuring tool doubled too, so every measurement would look the same. |
| O. If dinosaurs had not died out, they would be using smartphones today. | Not testable | We cannot rerun history; no observation could check it. |
| P. My lucky number makes good things happen to me. | Not testable (as written) | Too vague: "good things" is not defined. It becomes testable if rewritten (see Stretch 2). Accept testable only with a clear measure. |

**Core 2:** a) claim b) opinion c) testable d) fact e) verdict

**Stretch 1 (model):** "Card J is testable because we could do a survey of everyone in our class and count how many prefer winter. Card E is not testable because 'best' depends on what each person likes, so no measurement can show it is right or wrong."

**Stretch 2 (models, accept any with a clear measure and comparison):**
- "Students sitting in seat number 7 get higher scores on a 10-question quiz than students in other seats." Measure: quiz scores; compare seat 7 with other seats.
- "On days when I wear my lucky number shirt, I score more goals in football practice." Measure: goals per practice; compare lucky-shirt days with other days.

**Challenge 1 (Secure-level model)**
- Evidence: The wall is less than 10 m wide, but the Moon is about 384,000 km away. Astronauts on the Moon could not see any human-made structures, and even at 400 km it is very hard to see.
- Reasoning: Something only a few metres wide is far too small to see from 384,000 km; it is like trying to see one hair from 4 km away. If people who were actually there could not see it, the claim does not match the evidence.
- Verdict: Not supported 证据不支持.
- Excellent adds: the claim is testable and has been tested, so it was never a fact even though it is popular; "many people repeat it" is not evidence.

**Challenge 2 (model):** "If everything doubled, including every ruler, our measurements would give the same numbers as before. Nothing we could observe would be different, so no evidence could ever show the claim is true or false."

**My thinking (model):** No. "False" means the evidence shows it is wrong. "Not testable" means evidence cannot decide either way, so a scientist cannot say it is true or false. (Excellent: scientists usually set untestable claims aside because there is no way to check them.)

**Exit ticket:** 1 and 2: any claim about the school that names something to measure, count or survey, with a matching method (for example "The canteen queue is longer on Mondays than on Fridays: time the queue at 12:00 every day for two weeks."). 3: opinion (accept not testable).
