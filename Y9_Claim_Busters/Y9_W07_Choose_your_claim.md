## 1. Lesson plan (one page, for the teacher)

- **Big idea:** A good investigation is planned before anyone touches the kit: a testable question, a fair comparison, a sample size we can justify, and care for safety and for the people who take part.
- **Learning goals:**
  - I can turn a claim into a testable question and plan a fair test with variables, controls and a justified sample size.
  - I can plan ethically for human participants (voluntary, no pressure, anonymised) and write a specific risk assessment.

**Key words (10)**

| English | 中文 | Student-friendly meaning |
|---|---|---|
| plan | 计划 | What we will do, step by step, before we start. |
| testable question | 可检验的问题 | A question we can answer by measuring or counting. |
| sample size | 样本量 | How many people or things we test. |
| justify | 说明理由 | Give a reason for a choice. |
| control variable | 控制变量 | Something we keep the same so the test is fair. |
| participant | 参与者 | A person who takes part in a test. |
| voluntary | 自愿的 | Done by choice; anyone can say no. |
| anonymised | 匿名的 | Names are removed; codes are used instead. |
| hazard | 危险源 | Something that could cause harm. |
| risk assessment | 风险评估 | A plan of what could go wrong and how we will make it safer. |

**Planning roles (12 minutes, all writing at once on one A3 frame, or on the two A4 halves)**
- Lead Scientist: claim, testable question and prediction (boxes 1 and 2); keeps time.
- Measurer: variables and operational definition (boxes 3 and 4); method steps (box 7).
- Recorder: results table (box 10) and kit list (box 11).
- Safety Officer: ethics (box 8) and risk table (box 9).
- Reporter: sample size and its justification (box 5) and controls or comparison (box 6). In groups of three, the Lead Scientist also does the Reporter's boxes.

**Tiers**
- **Core:** complete the planning frame using the claim card's method hint and the frames; write the testable question on the worksheet.
- **Stretch:** justify the sample size; name at least three control variables, each with a reason; write an operational definition; find the problems in a weak plan.
- **Challenge:** name a bias that could affect the investigation and plan a protection (blind testing, counterbalancing, deciding the definition first); write a risk assessment with controls specific to the claim; judge which ethics scenarios are not acceptable and how to fix them.

**Language support**
- Frames: "Does ___ change ___?" / "We will change ___, measure ___ in ___ (units), and keep ___ the same." / "We will test ___ for each condition, because ___." / "A hazard is ___. It could ___. We will ___." / "Taking part is voluntary, so we will ___."
- Chinese talk is welcome while choosing and planning; boxes are written with key words in English. The planning frame accepts short notes, not full sentences.
- Vocabulary traps: "control" can mean a control variable (kept the same) or a control group or condition (for comparison); point out both. "Hazard" is the thing; "risk" is what could happen and how likely. "Anonymous" (nobody knows who) and "anonymised" (names replaced by codes) are close enough at this level. 计划 is the plan; 方法 is the method.

**Misconceptions to watch for**
- "More is always better, so we will test 100 cups." Sample size must fit the time and kit: 3 per condition is the target for objects, 2 the minimum.
- "We will see what happens and decide what to measure afterwards." The DV and the operational definition are decided now, before the data (Week 6 lesson).
- "Ethics only matters for medical tests." Any test with people needs consent, no pressure and codes, even a puzzle or a ruler drop.
- "Our friends will take part, so we do not need to ask." Friends can feel pressure most of all; ask everyone the same way.

**Safety:** No practical hazards this week. Plans must include the bilingual safety lines from the kit card that apply to the claim (ice and salt, warm water poured by the teacher, no eating or drinking, human-participant ethics). Do not approve any plan that involves eating, drinking or tasting, health or diet claims, or anything about named people.

**If time is short:** allocate claims yourself from the ranked top threes during the Hook (collect slips as students arrive). Cut the ethics quick-fire to two scenarios. Plans not approved by the end of the lesson: collect them, approve or annotate them within two days, and return the slips so kit can be prepared. Never let a group start Week 8 without a signed slip.

**Assessment and handover links**
- Output: an approved plan (planning frame plus signed approval slip) for every group. This is the main evidence for rubric strand 2 (Planning) and supports strand 1 (Questioning).
- Exit ticket: testable question, one control with a reason, one ethics rule.
- Logbook line: What I did 我做了什么: "Our claim is ___. We will test it by ___."
- Teacher action after the lesson: complete the Week 8 kit list from the approval slips (bottom stub). In Plan A, order ice for Week 8 for any group doing claims 1, 2, 5 or 6.

## 4. Answer key (for the teacher)

**Do Now:** 1. Saying exactly what we will measure or observe, so everyone counts the same thing. 2. Any question that names the change and a measurement, for example "Does putting a lid on a cup of warm water change the temperature drop in 10 minutes?"

**Core 1 (models for each claim; accept any version that names what changes and what is measured)**

| Claim | Model testable question |
|---|---|
| 1 | Does water that starts at about 55 °C become fully solid sooner than water at about 20 °C in an ice and salt bath? |
| 2 | Does an ice cube take longer to melt completely in fresh water or in salt water at the same temperature? |
| 3 | Does covering the top of a bottle of warm water with fabric reduce its temperature drop in 15 minutes more than covering the same area of its side? |
| 4 | Does a cup of warm water wrapped in wet cloth have a bigger temperature drop in 10 minutes than one wrapped in dry cloth? |
| 5 | Does an ice cube with salt sprinkled on it lose more mass in 10 minutes than one without salt? |
| 6 | Does 50 g of crushed ice lower the temperature of 200 ml of water more in 3 minutes than 50 g of ice cubes? |
| 7 | Do people recall more words from a 20-word list when they study it with music than in silence? |
| 8 | Does the time to finish the number hunt puzzle go down over five tries? |
| 9 | As height increases, does foot length increase? (or) How close is each person's arm span to their height? |
| 10 | Does a bottle of fizzy drink with a spoon in its neck lose less mass in 20 minutes than one without? |
| 11 | Which brand of paper towel (A, B or C) holds the most water per 10 cm square? |
| 12 | Are ruler-drop reaction times shorter in the morning than after lunch? |

**Core 2:** 1 C · 2 D · 3 E · 4 F · 5 A · 6 B

**Stretch 1 (any three, with fixes):**
- The papers are in different places (windowsill and desk), so one may get more sun: put both side by side in the same place.
- Only one sheet of each: use three of each.
- "After a while" is not a fixed time: measure after exactly 10 minutes, or every 2 minutes.
- Touching is not a measurement: use a thermometer under or on each sheet.
- Friends' opinions could be biased (they may know which is "supposed" to be warmer): measure instead, or make it blind.
- No operational definition of "warms up faster": decide it first, for example "bigger temperature rise in 10 minutes".

**Stretch 2 (models):** "We will test 3 cups for each condition, because three lets us see whether the results vary and spot an anomaly, and all six cups can cool at the same time, so it fits in 18 minutes." / "We will test 8 people in both conditions, because people vary a lot, and testing each person twice means we compare the same person with music and without."

**Challenge 1 (models):**
- "Bias could affect our results if the timer knows which paper towel is 'Brand X' and expects it to win. To protect against it, the teacher labels the brands A, B and C and keeps the key until we finish." (claim 11)
- "Bias could affect our results if everyone does the silent test first and gets better with practice. To protect against it, half our participants do music first and half do silence first (counterbalancing)." (claim 7)
- "Bias could affect our results if we choose the definition of 'melted' after we see the results. To protect against it, we write the definition in box 4 now." (any ice claim)

**Challenge 2 (model):** "Problems: testing only friends may put pressure on them to take part, and it is not a fair sample; posting names breaks anonymity, and slow people could be embarrassed. Better plan: ask volunteers from the whole class in the same polite way, tell them they can say no or stop at any time, record results by code (P1, P2 ...), and share only the means. Test each person at both times, half of them in the morning first if possible."

**My thinking (model):** "Only when telling people everything would spoil the test, and only if it is harmless, short, approved first, and explained straight afterwards, with people able to withdraw their results. Otherwise people should know what they are agreeing to."

**Exit ticket:** 1 as Core 1. 2 Any genuine control for their claim, with a reason about fairness ("so the only thing that changes is ___"). 3 Any of: voluntary, can say no or stop, no pressure, codes not names, can withdraw results, nothing to eat or drink.

**Ethics quick-fire (slide 11):** A not OK (pressure). B not OK (not anonymised; possible embarrassment). C OK. D not OK (no eating or drinking; also a health-type claim).

### Model completed plan for claim 4 (Secure level, teacher reference)

| Box | Secure-level entry |
|---|---|
| 1. Claim | 4: Wet clothes make you lose heat faster in winter. |
| 2. Question and prediction | Does a cup of warm water wrapped in wet cloth have a bigger temperature drop in 10 minutes than one wrapped in dry cloth? Prediction: the wet cloth cup will drop more, because water evaporating from the cloth takes heat away. |
| 3. Variables | IV: wet or dry cloth. DV: temperature drop in 10 minutes (°C), measured with a thermometer. |
| 4. Operational definition | "Lose heat faster" = bigger temperature drop between minute 0 and minute 10. |
| 5. Sample size | 3 cups wet, 3 cups dry, because three shows whether results vary, and all six cool at the same time so it fits in the time. |
| 6. Controls and comparison | Same cups, 200 ml of water, same start temperature (about 50 °C, poured by the teacher), same type and size of cloth, cloth wetted with room-temperature water and squeezed the same way, same place on the bench. Comparison: dry cloth cups. |
| 7. Method | 1. Wrap 6 cups in cloth (3 wet, 3 dry) with rubber bands. 2. Teacher pours 200 ml warm water into each. 3. Thermometers in; read all six (minute 0). 4. Read again at 5 and 10 minutes. 5. Work out each drop. |
| 8. Ethics | No human participants. |
| 9. Risk | Warm water: scald if spilled; teacher pours, cups stay on the tray. Glass thermometer: breaks; never stir with it, tell the teacher. Spills: slipping; wipe up at once. |
| 10. Results table | Cup, condition, start temperature (°C), temperature at 5 min (°C), temperature at 10 min (°C), drop (°C); 6 rows plus mean and range for each condition. |
| 11. Kit | 6 identical cups, 6 cloth pieces (same size), 6 rubber bands, cup of room-temperature water, 2 to 6 thermometers, stopwatch, tray, paper towels. |

Excellent adds: a reason for 10 minutes (long enough for a clear drop); a plan for an anomaly ("if one cup is very different, we check the thermometer and repeat that cup"); a note that the cloth must be wetted with room-temperature water, or cold water would be a confounding variable.

**Common "Not yet" problems and what to say**
- Question does not match the claim: "Which word in your question is the claim's main idea?"
- No units for the DV: "What will you write after the number?"
- Fewer than three controls: "What could be different between your two conditions by accident?"
- Sample size with no reason, or too big to fit 18 minutes: "How long does one test take? How many can you do in 18 minutes?"
- "Be careful" in the risk table: "Careful how, exactly?"
- Claim 12 with no plan for a second time of day: see the claim 12 card and agree an arrangement (see Section 7).

### 6e. Model plan sheet: "A lid keeps a cup of water warm for longer"

Create an A4 portrait model plan sheet for Year 9 Week 7, Claim Busters series, using the Think Like a Scientist design system. Print spec: A4, greyscale, 6 per class (one per group); also used on slides 4 to 8. A banner across the top: "MODEL PLAN: not in the claim bank 示例计划（不在说法库中）". Lay it out as a filled-in miniature of the planning frame, with the same box numbers and headings.

1. Claim: A lid keeps a cup of water warm for longer.
2. Question: Does putting a lid on a cup of warm water change the temperature drop in 10 minutes? Prediction: the cup with a lid will drop less, because the lid stops warm water evaporating and warm air rising away.
3. Change: lid or no lid (card lid with a hole for the thermometer). Measure: temperature drop in 10 minutes (°C), with a thermometer. Keep the same: identical cups, 150 ml of water, start temperature (about 50 °C, from the teacher), same place, same thermometers.
4. Operational definition: "warm for longer" = a smaller temperature drop from minute 0 to minute 10.
5. Sample size: 3 cups with lids and 3 without, because three shows whether results vary and helps us spot an anomaly, and all six cups cool at the same time, so it fits in 18 minutes.
6. Comparison: cups with no lid. To stop bias: we read all six thermometers at exactly the same times.
7. Method: 1. Label 6 cups L1 to L3 and N1 to N3. 2. Teacher pours 150 ml of warm water into each. 3. Lids on L cups, thermometers through the holes; thermometers into N cups. 4. Read all six at 0, 5 and 10 minutes. 5. Work out each drop, then the mean and range for each condition.
8. Ethics: ☑ No human participants.
9. Risk: Warm water | scald if spilled | the teacher pours, cups stay on the tray, we never carry them. Glass thermometer | breaks, sharp glass | never stir with it; tell the teacher if it breaks. Spilled water | slipping | wipe up at once.
10. Results table: Cup | Lid? | Temperature at 0 min (°C) | Temperature at 5 min (°C) | Temperature at 10 min (°C) | Drop (°C). Rows: L1, L2, L3, N1, N2, N3, Mean (lid), Mean (no lid), Range (lid), Range (no lid).
11. Kit: 6 identical cups, 3 card lids with holes, 6 thermometers (or 3, read in turn), stopwatch, tray, paper towels, marker.
