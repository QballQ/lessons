## 1. Lesson plan (one page, for the teacher)

- **Big idea:** Scientists share their verdicts in public, say honestly how sure they are, and answer questions with evidence; and after ten weeks we can see how much our own thinking has grown.
- **Learning goals:**
  - I can present our claim, test, data and verdict clearly in a 2-minute talk, with a confidence level and its reason.
  - I can answer or ask a question from the floor using evidence, not opinion.

**Key words (8)**

| English | 中文 | Student-friendly meaning |
|---|---|---|
| conference | 大会 | A meeting where scientists share their results. |
| talk | 演讲 | A short spoken presentation. |
| audience | 观众 | The people listening. |
| question from the floor | 现场提问 | A question asked by someone in the audience. |
| verdict | 结论（裁定） | Our final decision about a claim. |
| confidence | 把握程度 | How sure we are about our verdict: low, medium or high. |
| uncertainty | 不确定性 | How unsure we are about a result, because measurements vary. |
| evidence | 证据 | Information (measurements, observations, data) that helps us decide. |

**Adjusted timeline (Week 10 assessment week).** The final skills check (15 minutes) comes first, while students are fresh, then the conference talks (about 15 minutes: 2 minutes per group plus one question), then the interest survey and certificates (5 minutes). There is no separate Learn phase. Reports are displayed for a gallery walk at break or in a later session.

**Running order rota (6 groups)**
- Groups present in order 1 to 6. The group before you asks your question and writes your feedback (Group 6 asks and gives feedback to Group 1; Group 1 to Group 2; and so on).
- Each student speaks one part of the talk frame. In groups of four, one student does two short parts; in groups of three, two students do two parts each.

**Tiers**
- **Core:** speak your part of the talk using the frame; tick each group's verdict and confidence on the listener sheet; write one feedback slip.
- **Stretch:** answer the question from the floor with data; on the listener sheet, say which talk had the strongest evidence and why.
- **Challenge:** ask a question that tests another group's verdict (another explanation, a limitation, what would change their mind); explain which verdict you would challenge and what evidence would change your mind.

**Language support**
- Talk frame (Other printables 6a) on every table and on slide 5. Students may hold the frame and read from it; the rubric judges the clarity of the science, not the English.
- Answer frames: "Good question. Our data shows ___." / "We did not test that, but we think ___ because ___." / "That could also be explained by ___." / "To find out, we would need ___."
- Chinese talk is welcome while groups prepare in the opening 2 minutes and while writing feedback slips. Talks and questions are in English with key words; a student who is stuck may say one sentence in Chinese and then the key word in English.
- Vocabulary traps: "conference" is not a lesson; "the floor" means the audience, not the ground. "Confidence" means how strong the evidence is, not how confident the speaker feels.

**Misconceptions to watch for**
- "Not enough evidence yet means our project failed." Say clearly in the opening: it is an honest and respected verdict, and many real conference talks end with it.
- "A question from the floor is an attack." It is how scientists check each other's work, as in Week 9.
- "Confidence and verdict are the same thing." A group can say "Supported, low confidence".

**Safety:** No practical hazards beyond normal classroom rules. Groups show graphs and photos, not practical kit: no ice, warm water or fizzy drinks at the conference.

**If time is short:** the skills check (15 minutes) and the survey (3 minutes) are fixed, because they measure growth. Cut talks to 90 seconds with no question for the last groups, and take their questions during the gallery walk. Certificates can be presented at the start of the next session or an assembly (see "Certificates" below).

**Certificates in this lesson.** Award levels need all six rubric strands, and strand 6 comes from today's talk. Before the lesson, work out each student's provisional award from strands 1 to 5 (judged from the collected Week 9 reports, plans, data sheets, revision logs and logbooks) using the award rules in `assessment/Rubric_and_Reporting.md` Section 3. For most students the talk cannot change the award; for students on a boundary, print both possible certificates and hand over the right one after the talks. If this is not practical, present the certificates at the next opportunity instead. **Most Improved Scientist** cannot be decided until today's skills checks are marked: present it later.

**Assessment and handover links**
- Final skills check papers (marked out of 20 with the mark scheme in `assessment/Skills_Check_Y9.md` Section 2) go into the tracker, column S; growth (column T) is calculated automatically.
- Interest survey responses go into the tracker, columns U to Z (scoring: `assessment/Interest_Survey.md` Section 2).
- Strand 6 (Communicating) from the conference marking grid; strands 1 to 5 from the report and supporting evidence; all six go into tracker columns AC to AH.
- Two stars and a wish slips stay with the groups (they may stick them on the back of the report); they are not marked but can support strand 5.
- No exit ticket or logbook line is required this week; if there is a spare minute, use the worksheet's final strip. Collect logbooks for reporting.

## 4. Answer key (for the teacher)

**Final skills check:** mark with the mark scheme in `assessment/Skills_Check_Y9.md` Section 2 (20 marks). Use the same marker and the same strictness as Week 1 where possible. None of the skills check items use claim-bank claims, so project work does not give students the answers directly.

**Interest survey:** score with `assessment/Interest_Survey.md` Section 2.

**Core 1:** matches what each group said. Use the class answers to spot listening: if most students record a different verdict from the one a group intended, the talk was unclear (useful strand 6 evidence).

**Stretch 1 (model):** "The talk with the strongest evidence was Group 3 because they tested three cups of each, their ranges did not overlap, and they gave the means (12.3 °C and 7.0 °C)."

**Stretch 2 (model):** "The question was 'What else could explain your result?' Our answer was 'The wet cloth was colder at the start; to check, next time we would wet it with room-temperature water and measure its temperature first.'"

**Challenge 1 (model):** "I would challenge Group 5's verdict 'Supported, high confidence' for the music claim, because they tested only five people and their ranges overlapped. I would ask how many words different the means were. I would change my mind if a bigger, counterbalanced test showed the same difference with ranges that did not overlap."

**Challenge 2 (model):** "Yes, both can be right, because they tested different questions under different conditions. 'Losing heat faster' for a wet cup is a different operational definition from 'most heat lost through the head'. Each verdict is only true for what was actually measured."

**My thinking (model):** "I will ask what the evidence is, whether it was tested fairly with a control and enough people, whether a correlation is being sold as a cause, who made the claim, and how sure we can be. If there is not enough evidence, I will say 'not enough evidence yet'."

**Awards and certificates:** use `assessment/Rubric_and_Reporting.md` Section 3 (award rules, including missing strands and Most Improved Scientist) and Section 4 (certificate wording). Strand 6 guidance: Section 1, strand 6 row, and Section 2, "Year 9 Claim Busters", strand 6 example.
