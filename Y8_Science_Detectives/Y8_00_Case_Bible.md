# Y8 Case Bible | The Frozen Notebook | 冰封笔记本之谜

Series: Science Detectives 科学侦探 | Weeks 1 to 10 | Teacher reference (not for students)
Chinese text status: draft, needs native-speaker check

**What this file is.** The single source of truth for the Year 8 case. Every lesson file, printable and answer key must agree with this document. If a lesson file and this bible disagree, this bible wins and the lesson file should be corrected. Keep it out of student hands: it contains the solution.

**Tone rules.** This is a mystery about a missing notebook, not a crime against a person. Nobody is hurt, threatened or frightened. All characters are fictional. No real staff, students, schools or companies are used. The ending is kind: the notebook is found safe and the person who took it apologises.

---

## 1. The case at a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Title | The Frozen Notebook \| 冰封笔记本之谜 |
| Place | Lab 3, the Winter Science Lab, at fictional Snowfield School (雪原学校) |
| When | A snowy Thursday evening in December, the night before the Winter Science Fair (冬季科学展) |
| What is missing | Professor Frost's blue research notebook. It holds her method for growing perfect snowflake crystals, which she plans to show at the Fair |
| Window of time | Notebook last seen at 6:50 pm (by Max Zhang). Missing when Professor Frost returns at 7:30 pm |
| Evidence left behind | 1 a handwritten note; 2 a fingerprint on a drinking glass; 3 a spilled white powder; 4 a small puddle of liquid; 5 a white powdery footprint |
| Culprit | **Dr Ruby Huang** |
| Where the notebook is | In the lab freezer, inside a sealed freezer bag, behind the ice-cube trays. Cold, but completely safe. That is why it is "the frozen notebook" |
| The twist (Week 9) | The puddle (evidence 4) was soapy mop water left by the cleaning team at 6:30 pm. It has nothing to do with the missing notebook |

**Solution in two lines.** Dr Ruby Huang is the only suspect who matches all four reliable clues: a loop fingerprint, brand B ink, baking soda powder and a 24 cm footprint. The puddle that seemed to point to Lily Chen and Chef Zhou was contaminated mop water, so it is set aside.

## 2. The story (read-aloud version for Week 1)

It is the night before the Winter Science Fair at Snowfield School. Outside, snow is falling. Inside Lab 3, everyone is busy getting ready.

At 5:00 pm, Professor Frost finishes writing in her blue research notebook. Inside is her secret method for growing perfect snowflake crystals. She leaves the notebook on her desk and goes to a staff meeting.

Over the next two hours, four people come into the lab. A cleaning team mops the floor by the sink.

At 7:30 pm, Professor Frost comes back. Her notebook is gone. On her desk is a short note. There is a fingerprint on her drinking glass, white powder on the floor, a small puddle near the sink and one white footprint.

The Fair starts tomorrow. Professor Frost needs her notebook, and she needs detectives who think like scientists. That means you.

**The note (exact text, written by hand in black pen B, with a small snowflake doodle after "Sorry"):**

> Do not worry.
> Your notebook is safe and cold.
> Back before the Fair.
> Sorry!

(The words "safe and cold" are a fair clue to the freezer. Do not point this out. Praise any student who infers it, and ask: "What evidence would you need to check that?")

## 5. Scene evidence: what the real tests will show

| No. | Evidence 证据 | Where found (scene map square) | What it really is | Week tested | Result students should get |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Handwritten note 纸条 | On the desk, E2 | Written by the teacher with pen B | 3 | Chromatogram of note ink matches pen B |
| 2 | Fingerprint on a drinking glass 玻璃杯上的指纹 | On the desk, F2 | Stylised printed loop, smudged so only the pattern type shows | 2 | Loop 箕形纹 |
| 3 | White powder 白色粉末 | Floor beside the desk, F4 | Baking soda | 4 | Fizzes with vinegar; no colour change with iodine; partly dissolves |
| 4 | Puddle of liquid 一小摊液体 | Floor by the sink, I2 (next to the mop bucket at I3) | Soapy mop water (staged as soap solution mixed with baking soda solution) | 5 | Blue-green with red cabbage indicator: alkali, about pH 9 |
| 5 | White powdery footprint 白色粉末脚印 | Floor, G5, toes pointing towards the freezer | A shoe print 24 cm long, made with baking soda | 6 | Length 24 cm; estimated height about 150 to 170 cm |

**Important limit, stated honestly to students.** Our school lab can classify a fingerprint pattern (loop, whorl, arch) but cannot compare the fine ridge details that real fingerprint experts use. The scene print is smudged, so only its type can be seen. This keeps the fingerprint clue weak, which is the point.

**Running tally of matches**

| After week | Lily Chen | Max Zhang | Chef Zhou | Dr Huang | What students are likely to think |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 1 of 1 | 1 of 1 | 0 of 1 | 1 of 1 | "Not Chef Zhou, but it could be anyone else" |

**Fairness check (built in on purpose).**
- No single test identifies the culprit. Every physical clue matches at least two suspects.
- Only the combination of four clues points to one person: loop AND brand B AND baking soda AND 24 cm footprint is true only for Dr Huang.
- By Week 4 some groups will have reached Dr Huang. That is fine. Do not confirm or deny. Ask "How confident are you, and what evidence could change your mind?" Week 5 then gives them a clue that does not fit, which is exactly what the Week 9 lesson on tunnel vision needs.
- The twist is planted from Week 1: the mop bucket and "Wet floor" sign are on the scene map next to the puddle, and the cleaning team is on the sign-in sheet. Observant students can find it themselves. Reward them.

## 8. Weeks 7 and 8: student-designed tests (story hooks that do not change the solution)

The outline offers four options. Each has a story hook so it feels part of the case, but each result is deliberately weak or confirmatory, because real student results cannot be predicted.

| Option | Story hook | What it shows | Effect on case |
|---|---|---|---|
| Soil | A little soil was found in the tread of the footprint. Which campus location does it match? | It matches the flowerbed by the science block entrance | Weak: every suspect walked past that flowerbed to reach the lab. Rules nobody in or out |
| Paper | A crumpled paper towel was found later, in the corridor bin just outside the lab door. Which paper towel is it? | It matches the lab's own paper towels | Weak: everyone in the lab could use them |
| Ink 2 | Is water the best solvent to separate our inks? (water, salt water, vinegar) | Confirms the note still matches pen B with a second solvent | Makes the ink match more reliable; still matches 2 suspects |
| Powder 2 | Could the scene powder be a mixture of two powders? | Students test known mixtures. The scene powder (baking soda) gives the same results as pure baking soda | Confirms the Week 4 result is not a mixture |

## 9. Week 9: the contamination twist

**What is revealed.** The teacher hands out a new evidence card: the **Cleaning log** (清洁记录).

> **Snowfield School Cleaning Log | Lab 3 | Thursday**
> 6:30 pm. Mopped floor by the sink with soapy floor cleaner. Left bucket and "Wet floor" sign by the sink to dry. Some water left on the floor. Signed: Cleaning team.

**What it means.**
- The puddle at I2 is beside the mop bucket and the "Wet floor" sign (both on the scene map since Week 1).
- Soapy floor cleaner is alkaline, which explains the blue-green indicator result.
- The puddle was there before the notebook went missing (it was last seen at 6:50 pm).
- So the puddle is contaminated evidence (污染的证据): it got there for an innocent reason that has nothing to do with the notebook. It cannot be used to rule anyone in or out.
- Nobody did anything wrong. The cleaning team was doing their job. This is the most common kind of contamination in real forensic work: ordinary life leaves traces at a scene.

**Tunnel vision discussion.** Two kinds of tunnel vision are likely in the class:
1. Groups that decided "Dr Huang" after Week 4 and ignored the liquid result without a reason. They were right, but for a weak reason: a scientist must explain evidence that does not fit, not ignore it.
2. Groups that switched to "Lily" after Week 5 because the newest clue felt most important, ignoring the three clues that rule her out.
The scientific approach: keep all the evidence, notice the conflict, look for an explanation, then check it (the cleaning log).

**Evidence strength after the twist (model answer for the Week 9 evidence board)**

| Evidence | Matches | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Fingerprint: loop | Lily, Max, Dr Huang | Weak (common pattern) |
| Ink: brand B | Max, Dr Huang | Supporting |
| Powder: baking soda | Chef Zhou, Dr Huang | Supporting |
| Footprint: 24 cm | Lily, Dr Huang | Supporting |
| Liquid: alkali | (Lily, Chef Zhou) | Contaminated: set aside |
| Sign-in sheet and alibis | Dr Huang and Lily were nearby between 6:50 and 7:30 pm; Max was in the lab only until 7:00 pm | Supporting testimony (can be checked, can be wrong) |
| **All reliable physical evidence together** | **Dr Huang only** | **Strong** (no single clue is strong; the combination is) |

Suggested confidence statement: "We are highly confident, but not certain, that Dr Huang took the notebook, because she is the only suspect who matches all four reliable clues."

## 10. Week 10: the true ending (read-aloud reveal)

After the class jury gives its verdict, read this aloud (slide and printed card):

> The next morning, Professor Frost opened the lab freezer to get ice for the Fair. Behind the ice-cube trays was a freezer bag. Inside was her blue notebook: cold, a little frosty, but every page safe.
>
> Dr Ruby Huang came to see her before the Fair opened. "I am so sorry," she said. "My demonstration was going wrong, and I wanted to read your snowflake method. I heard a noise, I panicked, and I hid the notebook. I wrote the note so you would not worry. I spilled my baking soda on the way to the freezer."
>
> Professor Frost was quiet for a moment. Then she smiled. "You could have just asked," she said. "Science works better when we share. Why don't we present the snowflake demonstration together?"
>
> That afternoon, two scientists stood side by side at the Winter Science Fair, and the snowflakes were perfect.
>
> Case closed. 结案。

**If the class verdict is wrong.** Praise the reasoning that was good, then walk back through the evidence board together: "Which clue did we trust too much? Which clue did we ignore?" The reveal is a learning moment, not a test.

## 11. Teacher procedure: making the ink evidence match real results

The chromatography result must match the story exactly, so the teacher controls which real pen wrote the note.

1. **Buy** four black washable (water-based) felt-tip pens from four different brands, ideally two of each (one spare). Do not use permanent markers: their ink will not move with water.
2. **Pre-test** (two weeks before Week 3, about 20 minutes): cut 2 cm by 10 cm strips from white coffee filters. Draw a pencil line 2 cm from the bottom. Put a small, dark dot of one pen on each strip. Stand each strip in a cup with water 1 cm deep (below the dot). Wait 6 to 8 minutes.
3. **Check** that all four patterns look clearly different (different colours, or the same colours at clearly different heights). If two look too similar, replace one brand and test again.
4. **Assign letters.** Choose the pen with the most striking pattern (for example, blue, purple and yellow bands) as **pen B**. Label the others A, C and D. Cover any brand name on every pen with masking tape and write the letter on the tape.
5. **Record** the real results on the Teacher Pre-test Record (Week 3 file, Section 6) and keep the dried pre-test strips as the answer key.
6. **Write the note** for display: on a plain white card, write the exact note text (Section 2) with pen B. Photograph it for the Week 1 slides.
7. **Make the note ink samples** for Week 3 (10 minutes, any time before the lesson): on coffee-filter strips (2 cm by 10 cm), draw a pencil line 2 cm from the bottom and write "N" in pencil at the top. With pen B, put a small dot on the line, let it dry for a few seconds, and dot again on the same spot three times. Make one per group plus two spares. Story: "The forensic lab took a sample of ink from the note."
8. **Never** let pen B sit on a student table except as a labelled reference pen.

## 13. Science reference for the teacher

### Powders (Week 4)

| Powder | Under the magnifier | Water | Vinegar | Iodine (optional) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Salt 食盐 | Small, clear, cube-shaped crystals | Dissolves, clear | No fizz | No change (stays orange-brown) |
| Sugar 白糖 | Larger, glassy crystals, uneven chunky shapes, not neat cubes | Dissolves, clear | No fizz | No change |
| Baking soda 小苏打 | Very fine white powder, no crystals visible | Partly dissolves, slightly cloudy | **Fizzes strongly** | No change (may look slightly paler) |
| Cornflour 玉米淀粉 | Very fine, bright white, squeaky when pressed | Does not dissolve; milky, settles | No fizz | **Blue-black** |
| Flour 面粉 | Fine, creamy off-white, slightly clumpy | Does not dissolve; cloudy, lumpy | No fizz | **Blue-black** (sometimes dark purple-black) |

Telling cornflour from flour: cornflour is brighter white, finer and squeaks when rubbed; a thick paste of cornflour and water goes hard when pressed and runny when left (oobleck). Flour is creamier in colour and makes a sticky dough. Telling salt from sugar without tasting: look at the crystal shape under the magnifier (salt makes small cubes).

### Liquids and red cabbage indicator (Week 5)

| Liquid | Acid, neutral or alkali | Approximate pH | Cabbage indicator colour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lemon juice 柠檬汁 | Acid | 2 | Red to pink |
| White vinegar 白醋 | Acid | 3 | Red to pink |
| Water (bottled or purified) 纯净水 | Neutral | 7 | Purple |
| Baking soda solution 小苏打溶液 | Alkali | 8 to 9 | Blue to blue-green |
| Soap solution 肥皂水 | Alkali | 9 to 10 | Blue-green to green |
| Scene liquid (soap plus baking soda solution) | Alkali | About 9 | Blue-green |
| (Strong alkali, not used) | Strong alkali | 12 to 14 | Green-yellow to yellow |

**Making the indicator (teacher only, the day before, about 25 minutes).** Chop a quarter of a red cabbage (紫甘蓝) into small pieces. Put it in a heatproof jug. Pour on about 500 ml of hot water from the kettle. Stir and leave for 15 minutes until the water is deep purple. Strain through a sieve into a clean bottle and let it cool. Label it "Red cabbage indicator: do not drink". Keep it in the fridge; use within 3 days. This makes enough for one class (about 60 ml per group, as six cups of 10 ml, plus spare and the Hook demo).

**Use bottled or purified water** for the indicator and the "water" reference. Some tap water is slightly alkaline and turns the indicator blue-purple, which confuses the neutral reference.

**Soap solution.** Grate about 1 teaspoon of plain bar soap (not washing-up liquid, which is often close to neutral) into 200 ml of warm water and stir until cloudy. Test it: it must turn the indicator blue-green or green. If it only goes blue-purple, add a pinch of baking soda.

### Fingerprints (Week 2)

| Type | 中文 | How to recognise it | How common |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loop | 箕形纹 | Ridges come in from one side, curve round and go back out the same side | Most common, about 60 to 65% of fingerprints |
| Whorl | 斗形纹 | Ridges make circles or a spiral in the middle | About 30 to 35% |
| Arch | 弓形纹 | Ridges come in one side, rise in a gentle hill and leave on the other side | Rare, about 5% |

### Chromatography (Week 3)

Black washable ink is a mixture of coloured dyes. Water is the solvent. As water soaks up the filter paper, each dye dissolves and is carried up at a different speed, so the dyes separate into bands. The same ink always gives the same pattern under the same conditions, so a pattern can be matched to a reference sample. Rf = distance moved by spot ÷ distance moved by solvent. Rf has no units and is always less than 1.

### Footprint and height (Week 6)

Foot length is roughly 15% of height (height is about 6.5 times foot length), with a lot of variation between people. A 24 cm foot suggests a height of about 160 cm; using a class scatter graph, students should give a range of about 150 to 170 cm rather than one number. For simplicity, the scene print is treated as the foot length.

## 14. Continuity checklist (for anyone writing Weeks 6 to 10)

- Use these exact names, roles, heights, foot lengths, pens, powders and liquids. Do not add a fifth suspect.
- The scene footprint is 24 cm. The scene map square references (E2, F2, F4, I2, I3, G5) do not change.
- Professor Frost's pen is D and her fingerprint is an arch.
- The cleaning log is not shown to students before Week 9.
- The mop bucket and "Wet floor" sign stay on every version of the scene map.
- The ending is kind. Dr Huang apologises and co-presents with Professor Frost.
- Evidence log rows for Weeks 1 to 5 are filled in by students in the Week 1 to 5 lessons; rows for Weeks 6 to 10 are left for the incoming teacher.
