## 1. Lesson plan (one page, for the teacher)

- **Big idea:** Testing every sample in the same way, in the same order, and recording the results in a grid lets us identify an unknown by comparing it with known reference samples; a results grid can be turned into an identification key.
- **Learning goals:**
  - I can test five known powders and the scene powder systematically and record the results in a grid. 我能系统地检测五种已知粉末和现场粉末，并把结果记录在结果记录表中。
  - I can use my results to identify the scene powder and turn the grid into a flowchart key. 我能用我的结果识别现场粉末，并把结果记录表变成流程图检索表。
- **Key words:**

| English | 中文 | Student-friendly meaning |
|---|---|---|
| systematic | 系统的 | Doing every test the same way, in the same order |
| soluble | 可溶的 | Can dissolve in water |
| insoluble | 不溶的 | Cannot dissolve in water; it stays cloudy or sinks |
| fizz | 冒气泡 | Make lots of small bubbles |
| react | 反应 | Change into new substances (bubbles can be a sign) |
| starch | 淀粉 | A substance in flour and cornflour |
| iodine solution | 碘液 | An orange-brown liquid that turns blue-black with starch |
| crystal | 晶体 | A solid with a regular shape, like a tiny cube |
| results grid | 结果记录表 | A table that shows every test on every sample |
| identification key | 检索表 | Yes / no questions that lead to the name of something |

- **Timeline:**

| Phase | Min | Teacher does | Students do | Resource |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Do Now 课前任务 | 4 | Shows a chromatography recap; hands back dry chromatograms; then shows the Today slide (goals and key words, about 1 minute) | Answer 3 recap questions; stick chromatograms in the Week 3 worksheet box | Slides 2 to 3 |
| Hook 引入 | 3 | Holds up the evidence bag: "White. So are five things in the canteen cupboard. Which one?" | Vote: which powder do you think it is? (opinion, to be tested) | Slide 4 |
| Learn 新知 | 7 | Teaches the three tests and what a positive result looks like; models one powder; safety slide; goggles on | Copy "what to look for" into the grid header; Safety Officer checks goggles | Slides 5 to 7 |
| Do 实践 | 18 | Circulates; controls the iodine bottle (or gives it to Safety Officers); at minute 12 calls "tests finished, start your key" | Test 5 knowns and the scene powder with magnifier, water, vinegar, iodine (12 min); complete the flowchart key (6 min) | Slides 8 to 10; worksheet; kit tray |
| Think and Reflect 思辨与反思 | 8 | Asks "Which suspects are linked to baking soda?" then "Some of you think you know who did it. How sure are you?"; collects exit tickets | Identify the powder with a frame; update evidence log row 4 and suspect tracker; confidence vote; exit ticket | Slides 11 to 12; evidence log |

- **Tiers:**
  - Core: complete the results grid; identify the scene powder with a frame; name the linked suspects.
  - Stretch: complete the flowchart key from the results grid.
  - Challenge: design a key that works without iodine; predict the results for a mixture of salt and cornflour.
- **Language support:** Frames: "The scene powder matches ___ because it ___ ." "When we added vinegar, it ___ ." "This evidence is strong / weak because ___ ." Chinese welcome during testing and pair talk. Traps: "soluble" vs "solvent" (last week); "fizz" is new; "dissolve" is a verb, "soluble" an adjective; "starch" is unfamiliar.
  - Band 1 today: sit the student beside a Band 3 language buddy, who reads the Chinese line of each task and explains it in Chinese but never writes the answer. Give the student a doing job first (Measurer on the water cups), where the result is seen, not read; the practical follows the five picture steps on Slide 8 and the bilingual lab card 6.1 on the table. Start with Core a during the testing: the grid headings, row labels and result words are bilingual, and the picture key above the grid lets the student record with a tick, a cross or a copied picture instead of a word. Then Core b: fill the Chinese frame with a result word from the bilingual word bank; the reason may be written in Chinese. Then Core c: use the sorting strip under the question (four suspect names with their powder, EN 中文) and circle the linked names and cross out the ruled-out names, so the Week 1 suspect cards need not be read. Before the Do task, run Say, show, check on Slide 8: teacher reads the English, a student reads the Chinese, thumbs check. The My thinking box and the exit ticket may be answered in Chinese using the English key words.
- **Misconceptions to watch for:** "Dissolved means it disappeared" (it is still there, spread through the water). "Cloudy means dissolved." "All white powders are the same." Deciding by look or smell instead of tests. Using one spoon for all powders (contamination: a useful link to Week 9).
- **Safety:** Goggles for vinegar and iodine. No tasting or eating any powder or liquid. Allergy check (wheat flour, cornflour). Iodine stains skin and clothes: optional, teacher-controlled, droppers only. Avoid making flour dust clouds. Wash hands at the end. (Kit card, Section 5.)
- **If time is short:** Skip iodine (use the water test to spot the starches) and do the magnifier check only for salt and sugar. Stretch key can be finished as the Week 5 Do Now if needed.
- **Assessment and handover links:** Results grid and key on the worksheet; evidence log row 4; suspect tracker column "Powder"; confidence row. Exit ticket shows whether students can justify an identification with a test result. Scientist's Logbook: if there is time at the end of Think and Reflect, students add one line under "What I found"; otherwise this week's evidence log row counts as the logbook entry.

## 4. Answer key (for the teacher)

**Core a)**

| Powder | Looks like | Water | Vinegar | Iodine |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Salt | Small clear cube crystals | Dissolves, clear | No fizz | No change (orange-brown) |
| Sugar | Larger glassy crystals, uneven shapes | Dissolves, clear | No fizz | No change |
| Baking soda | Very fine powder, no crystals seen | Partly dissolves, slightly cloudy | **Fizzes** | No change |
| Cornflour | Very fine, bright white, squeaky | Does not dissolve: milky, settles | No fizz | **Blue-black** |
| Flour | Fine, creamy white, clumpy | Does not dissolve: cloudy, lumpy | No fizz | **Blue-black** |
| Scene powder | Very fine powder, no crystals seen | Partly dissolves, slightly cloudy | **Fizzes** | No change |

Accept "dissolves" for baking soda if students used plenty of water. Accept "dark purple" or "black" for the iodine result.

**Core b)** "The scene powder matches baking soda because it fizzes with vinegar (and does not turn blue-black with iodine)."

**Core c)** Linked: Chef Zhou and Dr Ruby Huang. Ruled out: Lily Chen (salt) and Max Zhang (sugar).

**Stretch key**
Blank 1: baking soda | Blank 2: cornflour | Blank 3: flour | Blank 4: salt | Blank 5: sugar.
Accept other correct key orders (for example, starting with the iodine question) as long as every powder ends in its own box and every question is yes / no.

**Challenge a)** Replace Diamond 2 with "Does it dissolve in water?" No: cornflour or flour (then Diamond 3). Yes: salt or sugar (then Diamond 4). Secure answers check that baking soda has already been removed by the vinegar question, since it also partly dissolves.

**Challenge b) Secure-level example**
"Water: the salt dissolves but the cornflour makes it cloudy. Vinegar: no fizz, because neither salt nor cornflour fizzes. Iodine: blue-black, because of the starch in cornflour. It would not match the scene powder, because the scene powder fizzed and did not turn blue-black."
