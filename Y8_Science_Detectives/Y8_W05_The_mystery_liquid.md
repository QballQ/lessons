## 1. Lesson plan (one page, for the teacher)

- **Big idea:** An indicator changes colour to show whether a liquid is an acid, neutral or an alkali; comparing an unknown with reference samples on the same indicator places it on the pH scale, but an indicator cannot tell us exactly which substance it is.
- **Learning goals:**
  - I can use red cabbage indicator and reference samples to decide whether the scene liquid is an acid, neutral or an alkali, and estimate its pH.
  - I can notice when a new piece of evidence does not fit the others, and suggest explanations that could be checked.
- **Key words:**

| English | 中文 | Student-friendly meaning |
|---|---|---|
| indicator | 指示剂 | A liquid that changes colour in acids and alkalis |
| acid | 酸性 | Sour-type liquids like lemon juice; turn cabbage indicator red or pink |
| alkali | 碱性 | Opposite of an acid, like soap; turns cabbage indicator blue, green or yellow |
| neutral | 中性 | Not an acid and not an alkali, like pure water; stays purple |
| pH scale | pH值（酸碱度） | Numbers from 1 to 14: low = acid, 7 = neutral, high = alkali |
| colour chart | 比色卡 | A chart that matches each colour to a pH number |
| reference sample | 参照样本 | A known liquid we compare the unknown with |
| consistent | 一致的 | Fits with the other evidence |

- **Timeline (adjusted for the Week 5 checkpoint):**

| Phase | Min | Teacher does | Students do | Resource |

- **Tiers:**
  - Core: results table (colour, acid / neutral / alkali, pH); identify the scene liquid type and the matching suspects.
  - Stretch: update the suspect tracker; explain which suspect fits most evidence now and which clue does not fit; explain why the indicator cannot separate soap solution from baking soda solution.
  - Challenge: suggest two explanations for the clue that does not fit and the evidence that would check each one.
- **Language support:** Frames: "The scene liquid turned ___ , so it is ___ with a pH of about ___ ." "This sample matches ___ because ___ ." "The liquid evidence does not fit ___ because ___ ." "One problem with this evidence is ___ ." Chinese welcome during pair talk and on sticky notes. Traps: "acid" (noun) vs "acidic" (adjective): both are fine; "alkali" spelling; "neutral" is not "natural".
- **Misconceptions to watch for:** "Acids are always dangerous; alkalis are safe" (both can be harmful when strong). "The indicator tells you exactly what the liquid is" (only acid, neutral or alkali and roughly how strong). "Evidence that does not fit means we were wrong about everything" or the opposite, "just ignore it".
- **Safety:** Goggles for vinegar, lemon juice, soap solution and indicator. No tasting. The teacher makes the indicator in advance with hot water; students never handle hot water. Wash hands afterwards. (Kit card, Section 5.)
- **If time is short:** Test only lemon juice, water, soap solution and the scene liquid. Drop Stretch c) and Challenge; take sticky-note ideas orally.
- **Assessment and handover links:** Checkpoint score into the progress tracker (Week 5 column). Results table; evidence log row 5; suspect tracker column "Liquid". **Ideas Parking Board** sticky notes are photographed and passed to the incoming teacher for Week 9. The handover note is completed after this lesson (outline Section 10). Scientist's Logbook: if there is time at the end of Think and Reflect, students add one line under "What I found"; otherwise this week's evidence log row counts as the logbook entry.

## 4. Answer key (for the teacher)

**Core a)**

| Liquid | Colour | Type | pH (about) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lemon juice | Red or pink | Acid | 2 |
| Vinegar | Red or pink | Acid | 3 |
| Water | Purple | Neutral | 7 |
| Soap solution | Blue-green or green | Alkali | 9 to 10 |
| Baking soda solution | Blue or blue-green | Alkali | 8 to 9 |
| Scene liquid | Blue-green | Alkali | About 9 |

Accept pH values within about 1 of these. If water came out blue-purple, the water was slightly alkaline (tap water); accept "neutral or very slightly alkali" and discuss why bottled water is used.

**Core b)** "The scene liquid turned blue-green, so it is an alkali with a pH of about 9."

**Core c)** Soap solution and baking soda solution.

**Core d)** Linked: Lily Chen (soap solution) and Chef Zhou (baking soda solution). Ruled out: Max Zhang (lemon juice, acid) and Dr Ruby Huang (vinegar, acid).

**Stretch a)**

| Suspect | Fingerprint | Ink | Powder | Liquid | Total ✓ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lily Chen | ✓ | ✗ | ✗ | ✓ | 2 |
| Max Zhang | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ | ✗ | 2 |
| Chef Zhou | ✗ | ✗ | ✓ | ✓ | 2 |
| Dr Ruby Huang | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ | 3 |

**Stretch b) Secure-level example**
"The suspect who fits the most evidence is Dr Huang. The liquid evidence does not fit her because her liquid is vinegar, which is an acid, but the scene liquid is an alkali."

**Stretch c)** Both are alkalis with similar pH values, so they give similar blue to blue-green colours. An indicator shows acid, neutral or alkali and roughly how strong, not which substance it is.

**Challenge a) Acceptable explanations (any two)**
- The puddle was there before the notebook was taken, for a different reason (for example, the mop bucket next to it, or the sink).
- The puddle was contaminated or mixed with something else (for example, some baking soda from the spill dissolved in it).
- Two different people left different clues.
- The person carried a liquid we do not know about.
- A test result was wrong (for example, a dirty dropper).
Do **not** confirm which is right. The true answer (soapy mop water, from the cleaning log) is revealed in Week 9.

**Challenge b) Secure-level example**
"The puddle is next to the mop bucket and the 'Wet floor' sign at I3, and the sign-in sheet says the cleaning team mopped at 6:30 pm. I would ask the cleaning team what they used and test their mop water with the indicator. If it is the same colour, the puddle might not be evidence about the notebook at all." This is an Excellent-level answer: praise it, record the student's name for the incoming teacher, and do not confirm.

**My thinking: Secure-level ideas**
Never ignore evidence just because it does not fit your favourite suspect (that is tunnel vision). It is right to set evidence aside only when you can show it is unreliable or unrelated, for example if it was contaminated.

1 Alkali | 2 About 9 (accept 8 to 10) | 3 Dr Huang (3 matches out of 4).

## 5. Kit card [Paste into Claude Design]

Create a half-A4 kit card for Year 8 Week 5 using the Think Like a Scientist kit card template.

- **Per group (4 students):**
  - Goggles, 4
  - Small clear plastic cups, each with about 10 ml (2 cm) of red cabbage indicator, ready filled and labelled, 6
  - Labelled cups of reference liquids, about 10 ml each: lemon juice, white vinegar, water (bottled or purified), soap solution, baking soda solution, 5
  - Labelled cup "Evidence 4: liquid, floor by sink (I2)" (scene liquid), about 10 ml, 1
  - Droppers, 6 (or 2 droppers and a cup of rinse water)
  - Sheet of white paper to stand cups on, 1
  - pH colour chart (laminated, printed in colour if possible), 1
  - Paper towels
- **Per class:** checkpoint papers (1 per student), sticky notes, Ideas Parking Board space on the wall.
- **Teacher prep:** The day before (about 25 minutes): make the red cabbage indicator with hot water from the kettle, strain, cool and refrigerate (Case Bible, Section 13). Before the lesson (about 15 minutes): make soap solution and baking soda solution; mix equal amounts of the two to make the scene liquid; fill and label all cups; test the scene liquid yourself (it must turn blue-green); put checkpoint papers on desks.
- **Hazards and controls:**
  - Wear goggles when using indicator, vinegar, lemon juice and soap solution. 使用指示剂、白醋、柠檬汁和肥皂水时请佩戴护目镜。
  - Never taste or drink any liquid. 不要品尝或饮用任何液体。
  - Only the teacher uses hot water. The indicator is made before the lesson. 只有老师可以使用热水。指示剂在课前已制好。
  - If liquid gets in your eyes, rinse with plenty of water and tell the teacher. 如有液体进入眼睛，请用大量清水冲洗并告诉老师。
  - Wipe up spills straight away. 洒出的液体要立即擦干。
  - Wash your hands after the practical. 实验后请洗手。
- **Clean-up:** Pour all liquids down the sink with plenty of water. Rinse cups if reusing, or recycle. Goggles back in the box. Wipe tables. Everyone washes hands. Leftover indicator: label and keep in the fridge for up to 3 days, then discard.
