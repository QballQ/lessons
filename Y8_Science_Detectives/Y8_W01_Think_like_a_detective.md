# Y8 W01 | Think like a detective | 像侦探一样思考

Series: Science Detectives 科学侦探 | Week 1 of 10 | 40 minutes | Phase: Skills
Chinese text status: draft, needs native-speaker check

## 1. Lesson plan (one page, for the teacher)

- **Big idea:** Detectives and scientists separate what they can see (observations) from what they think it means (inferences), and they trust evidence more than opinion.
- **Learning goals:**
  - I can sort statements into observations and inferences. 我能把句子分成观察和推断。
  - I can write an observation and an inference about the scene, and say what evidence would check the inference. 我能写出关于现场的一个观察和一个推断，并说出用什么证据来检验推断。
- **Key words:**

| English | 中文 | Student-friendly meaning |
|---|---|---|
| forensic science | 法庭科学 | Using science to find out what happened |
| detective | 侦探 | A person who solves a mystery using clues |
| evidence | 证据 | Things we can see, measure or test that help us decide what is true |
| crime scene | 案发现场 | The place where something wrong happened; here, Lab 3 |
| suspect | 嫌疑人 | A person who might have done it |
| observation | 观察 | What you can see, hear, touch or measure. No guessing |
| inference | 推断 | What you think the observation means |
| opinion | 观点 | What someone thinks or feels; it cannot be tested |
| alibi | 不在场证明 | A person's story of where they were at the time |

- **Timeline (adjusted for Week 1 assessment):**

| Phase | Min | Teacher does | Students do | Resource |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Do Now 课前任务 (Skills check) | 15 | Seats groups at fixed tables as they arrive; hands out the skills check; reads instructions once; times 15 minutes | Complete the baseline skills check alone | Slide 2; `Skills_Check_Y8.md` |
| Do Now 课前任务 (Interest survey) | 3 | Collects skills checks; hands out the survey; shows the Today slide (Slide 4) while the surveys are collected, one minute inside that transition, never from the skills check time | Complete the 6-question smiley survey | Slide 3, then Slide 4 (Today); `Interest_Survey.md` |
| Hook 引入 | 4 | Plays the "case alert": reads the briefing aloud over slides 5 and 6; tells the Song Ci story in one minute | Listen; look at the note; open the case file | Slides 5 to 6; case file cover and briefing |
| Learn 新知 | 4 | Teaches observation vs inference with the glass example; hand gesture: eye = observe, head-tap = infer | Call out O or I for 3 quick statements with the gesture | Slides 7 to 8 |
| Do 实践 | 10 | Circulates; pushes "What exactly can you see? Which square?"; prompts Stretch and Challenge | Study the scene map in groups; complete worksheet Core, then Stretch, then Challenge | Slides 9 to 10; scene map; worksheet |
| Think and Reflect 思辨与反思 | 4 | Introduces the four suspects in one slide; asks "Opinion or evidence?" about one statement; collects exit tickets | Hear the suspects; vote opinion or evidence; complete exit ticket; put worksheet in case file | Slides 11 to 12; exit ticket |

- **Tiers:**
  - Core: tick observation or inference for 8 scene statements.
  - Stretch: write 3 observations (with map squares) and 3 inferences using frames.
  - Challenge: choose one inference and describe the evidence or test that would check it; sort two statements about suspects into opinion or evidence.
- **Language support:** Frames: "I observe ___ at square ___." "I infer ___ because ___." "This is an opinion because ___." Pair talk in Chinese is welcome while studying the map; record key words in English. Traps: "evidence" is uncountable (not "evidences"); "infer" vs "inference"; "suspect" as a noun (嫌疑人) and verb (怀疑).
  - Band 1 today: seat the student beside a Band 3 language buddy; the buddy explains each task in Chinese and never writes the answer. Start with Core: the student reads the Chinese line under each of the eight sentences, finds the square (F2, F4, G5) on the scene map, where the prop labels (salt, vinegar, baking soda, lemon tea) are bilingual, and ticks O or I; the eye and thought-bubble icons over the O and I columns match Slide 7, and the teacher models sentences 1 and 2 with the gestures before Core starts. Run Say, show, check on Slide 9 before groups begin: teacher reads the English, a student reads the Chinese, thumbs check. For the exit ticket the student points to a square on the A3 wall map, says the observation in Chinese to the buddy, then writes O and I in Chinese using the two frames printed bilingual on the strip, and copies the English key words from the fold-under word bank. Stretch and Challenge are glossed, not required; a translation app on the teacher's phone is a fair access tool for the Challenge stems.
- **Misconceptions to watch for:** Treating any sensible guess as an observation ("The powder is sugar"). Thinking an inference is wrong or bad (it is useful; it just needs testing). Deciding the culprit from personality ("the chef looks friendly").
- **Safety:** No practical hazards beyond normal classroom rules. If you set up the optional scene table: students look but do not touch, taste or smell the items.
- **If time is short:** Skip the Song Ci story here and tell it at the start of Week 2. Drop Challenge. Suspect slide can move to the Week 2 Do Now (students read the suspect cards then).
- **Assessment and handover links:** Baseline skills check scores and survey responses go into the progress tracker (Week 1 column). Exit ticket shows who can separate observation from inference. Case file opened; evidence log row 1 completed in Week 2 Do Now if not done today. Scientist's Logbook: if there is time at the end of Think and Reflect, students add one line under "What I found"; otherwise this week's evidence log row counts as the logbook entry.

## 4. Answer key (for the teacher)

**Core**
1 O | 2 I | 3 O | 4 I | 5 O | 6 I | 7 O | 8 I
Common error: 4 and 8 marked O. Ask "Can you see that it is sugar, or are you guessing?" and "Can you see the person walking?"

**Stretch: acceptable observations (any 3)**
- There is a glass with a fingerprint on the desk at F2.
- There is a note on the desk at E2.
- There is white powder on the floor at F4.
- There is one white footprint at G5. The toes point towards the freezer.
- There is a small puddle on the floor at I2, near the sink.
- There is a mop bucket and a "Wet floor" sign at I3.
- There is an empty space on the desk where the notebook was.
- The window is closed and locked.
- The clock shows 7:30.
- There is a volcano model, a vinegar bottle and a tub of baking soda on bench B4 to C4.
- There are jars of crystals on bench B5 to C5; there is a tray of buns on C6.
- The pen pot on the desk has one pen, labelled D.

**Stretch: acceptable inferences (any 3, each with a "because")**
- The person who took the notebook drank from the glass, because there is a fingerprint on it.
- The person spilled powder, because it is on the floor beside the desk.
- The person walked to the back of the lab, because the footprint points that way.
- Nobody came in through the window, because it is locked.
- The notebook is somewhere cold, because the note says "safe and cold". (Praise this; do not confirm.)
- The puddle might be from the mop, because the mop bucket is next to it. (Praise this strongly; it is the Week 9 twist. Do not confirm.)
- The person who wrote the note did not want Professor Frost to worry, because it says "Do not worry".

**Challenge a) Secure-level example**
"I infer the person who took the notebook drank from the glass. To check my inference, I would compare the fingerprint on the glass with the suspects' fingerprints. If it is true, I expect the fingerprint to match one of the suspects." Accept any sensible test: test the powder, compare the footprint size with suspects' shoe sizes, test the ink, look in the freezer.

**Challenge b)**
- Evidence (E): the sign-in sheet is a written record that can be checked. (Excellent answers note it could still be wrong if someone signed in falsely.)
- Opinion (O): "looks too serious" is a feeling about a person; it cannot be tested and does not show what happened.

**My thinking (any language): Secure-level ideas**
Looking yourself means you do not rely on rumours or guesses; you can record exactly what is there; other people's stories can be wrong; evidence can be checked by others.

**Exit ticket**
Any correct pair, for example O: "There is white powder at F4." I: "Someone spilled the powder when they took the notebook." Mark as secure if the O contains no interpretation and the I goes beyond what is seen.
