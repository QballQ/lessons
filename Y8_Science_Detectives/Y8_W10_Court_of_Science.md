## 1. Lesson plan (one page, for the teacher)

- **Big idea:** Scientists communicate and defend their conclusions with evidence, answer hard questions honestly (including what their evidence does not show), and accept the result of checking, even if it proves them wrong.
- **Learning goals:**
  - I can explain one piece of evidence to the Court of Science clearly: what we tested, how, what it shows and how sure we are. 我能向科学法庭清楚地解释一项证据：我们检测了什么、怎样检测、结果说明了什么、我们有多大把握。
  - I can answer or ask a question about evidence, and reach a verdict based on the strength of all the evidence. 我能回答或提出一个关于证据的问题，并根据全部证据的强度作出裁决。
- **Key words:**

| English | 中文 | Student-friendly meaning |
|---|---|---|
| court | 法庭 | The place where evidence is heard and a decision is made |
| jury | 陪审团 | The group of people who listen to the evidence and decide |
| verdict | 裁决 | The jury's decision |
| expert witness | 专家证人 | A scientist who explains one piece of evidence to the court |
| testimony | 证词 | What a witness says |
| evidence | 证据 | Things we can see, measure or test that help us decide what is true |
| confidence | 把握 | How sure we are, and why |
| case closed | 结案 | The mystery is solved |

There is no separate Learn phase this week.

- **Order of witnesses (fixed, so the story builds):** 1 Fingerprint (Evidence 2) | 2 Ink (Evidence 1) | 3 Powder (Evidence 3) | 4 Liquid and contamination (Evidence 4) | 5 Footprint (Evidence 5) | 6 Soil and paper (Evidence 6 and 7). **Who asks:** the group that speaks next asks the jury question; the Fingerprint group asks the last group (Soil and paper).
- **Tiers:** (during the Court)
  - Core: give your part of the talk using the statement frame; fill in jury notes with key words. Band 1 students circle the strength in each row and copy the result and the suspect names from the expert result card (6.6).
  - Stretch: answer or ask a jury question with evidence; name the strongest evidence and why.
  - Challenge: say what your evidence does not show; judge which expert answered best and why.
- **Language support:** Frames on the stand: "We tested ___ . We compared it with ___ . The result was ___ . This matches ___ . This evidence is strong / supporting / weak because ___ ." Answering: "That is a good question. Our evidence shows ___ , but it does not show ___ ." Asking: read the card aloud. Verdict: "My verdict is ___ because ___ ." Students may glance at their frame; reading it aloud is fine. Strand 6 is judged on clarity of the science, not grammar or accent. A student may give one sentence in Chinese and then the key words in English if they freeze.
  - Band 1 today: the student sits beside their language buddy, who reads each paired Chinese line on the slides, the jury notes sheet and the question cards and explains the task in Chinese but never writes the answer. On the stand (Core speaking), the student's part is on the Week 9 statement frame, which carries the Chinese frame beneath each English frame with the same gaps: the buddy reads the Chinese frame once before the Court opens, the student may read from the frame, and may say one sentence in Chinese and then the English key words (the tip is printed on the expert result card, 6.6; say it aloud before witness 1). For the jury notes (Core), start with the strength column: each expert group holds up its bilingual result card (6.6) while it speaks, so the student circles the strength printed in the row and copies the result key word and the suspect names from the card or the fold-under word bank instead of decoding the spoken English; the buddy points to the row. For the jury question (Stretch), the group reads the Chinese line of the card together and the student may read the English aloud with the buddy. On the exit ticket the student circles a suspect name (or 证据不足) and writes the reason in Chinese with the frame 我的裁决是___，因为___。 After the teacher reads the true ending in English, a confident Chinese reader reads the four 概要 lines on the reveal card, so the student hears who took the notebook and why. Chinese answers count for every Core task.
- **Misconceptions to watch for:** "A verdict means certain." "If the jury disagrees with us, we were wrong" (only the evidence decides). "Weak evidence was a waste of time." "The expert with the most confident voice is the most reliable."
- **Safety:** No practical hazards beyond normal classroom rules. If you bring out the notebook prop from the freezer, it is cold and damp: pass it round in its bag, not out of it.
- **If time is short:** The skills check is never shortened (it must match Week 1). Cut talks to 75 seconds; ask only three jury questions in total (after talks 2, 4 and 6). Move peer feedback slips and certificates to the next session or form time. Never skip the verdict and the true ending.
- **Assessment and handover links:** Final skills check (score into tracker column "W10 skills check"); interest survey (W10 S1 to S6); strand 6 from the talk record; final case file (strands 1 to 5, checked with the final case file checklist, Section 6.4); peer feedback slips into case files. Then award rules, certificates and skills reports from `../assessment/Rubric_and_Reporting.md`. Evidence log row 10 (verdict). Close-out tasks are listed in Section 7.

**Stretch (Secure):** "The strongest evidence was the combination of the four reliable clues, because only Dr Huang matches all of them." Accept a single supporting clue with a correct reason (for example "the ink, because it matches only two suspects").

**Challenge (Secure):** names an expert and says the answer used evidence (a result, a number of suspects, a reference sample) and was honest about limits.

**My thinking: Secure-level ideas** Test, do not guess; compare with reference samples; repeat; rank evidence by how many possibilities it fits; check evidence that does not fit instead of ignoring it; be confident but not certain; change your mind when the evidence changes.

**Exit ticket (verdict):** Dr Ruby Huang, high confidence (not certain), because she is the only suspect who matches all four reliable clues. Mark the reasoning, not only the name.

### Model expert witness talks (Secure level, about 90 seconds each)

1. **Fingerprint:** "We are the fingerprint experts. We tested Evidence 2, the fingerprint on the glass at F2. We classified it as a loop, an arch or a whorl, and compared it with the suspects' reference prints. The scene print is a loop. Lily, Max and Dr Huang have loops; Chef Zhou has a whorl, so he is ruled out. This evidence is weak, because loops are very common: about 60 to 65% of people have them. It does not show who touched the glass, because our lab can only see the pattern type, not the fine details."
2. **Ink:** "We are the ink experts. We tested Evidence 1, the ink on the note, with paper chromatography. We compared it with reference pens A, B, C and D, using the same paper, the same solvent depth and the same time. The note matches pen B, and in Week 8 it still matched pen B in salt water and vinegar. Max and Dr Huang carry brand B; Lily, Chef Zhou and Professor Frost are ruled out. This is supporting evidence: it matches two suspects, and a pen can be borrowed."
3. **Powder:** "We are the powder experts. We tested Evidence 3, the white powder at F4, with water, vinegar and iodine, beside five reference powders. It fizzed with vinegar and did not turn blue-black, so it is baking soda. In Week 8 we checked it was not a mixture. Chef Zhou and Dr Huang are linked to baking soda; Lily and Max are ruled out. This is supporting evidence, because it matches two suspects."
4. **Liquid:** "We are the liquid experts. We tested Evidence 4, the puddle at I2, with red cabbage indicator and reference liquids. It turned blue-green: an alkali, about pH 9, like soap or baking soda solution. At first it seemed to point to Lily and Chef Zhou. But the cleaning log shows the floor was mopped at 6:30 pm with soapy cleaner, before the notebook went missing. So the puddle is contaminated evidence and we set it aside. Nobody did anything wrong; it was just ordinary cleaning."
5. **Footprint:** "We are the footprint experts. We tested Evidence 5, the 24 cm footprint at G5. We measured the foot length and height of our class, plotted a scatter graph and drew a line of best fit. A 24 cm foot suggests a height of about 160 cm, and our range is about 150 to 170 cm. Lily (158 cm) and Dr Huang (165 cm) fit; Max and Chef Zhou are too tall. This is supporting evidence: it gives a range and matches two suspects, and our data come from students, not adults."
6. **Soil and paper:** "We are the soil and paper experts. We tested Evidence 6, soil from the footprint, with settling bottles against four campus soils, and Evidence 7, the paper towel from the bin, against four papers. The soil matches the flowerbed by the science block entrance, and the paper towel matches the lab's own towels. But every suspect walked past that flowerbed and could use those towels, so this evidence is weak: it rules nobody in or out. It is still useful, because it tells the jury what the evidence cannot show."

### Good answers to likely jury questions

- "How many suspects does your evidence match?" Give the number and names; say what that means for strength.
- "Could your evidence be contaminated?" Name how you prevented it (clean spoons, fresh cups, same-way testing) or, for the liquid, explain the cleaning log.
- "What does your evidence not show?" For example: the fingerprint type does not show whose finger it was; a pen can be borrowed; baking soda was used by two people; the height is a range.
- "How confident are you?" "Highly confident, but not certain, because ..."
- "Pens can be borrowed. Does the ink prove who wrote the note?" "No. It shows a brand B pen was used, which fits two suspects. It is one piece of a combination."
