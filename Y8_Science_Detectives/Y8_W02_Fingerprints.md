## 1. Lesson plan (one page, for the teacher)

- **Big idea:** Scientists sort things into groups (classify) and count how common each group is; how common a feature is decides how strong it is as evidence.
- **Learning goals:**
  - I can take my own fingerprint and classify it as a loop, whorl or arch. 我能采集自己的指纹，并把它分类为箕形纹、斗形纹或弓形纹。
  - I can use class data in a bar chart to explain why a common fingerprint type is weak evidence. 我能用条形图中的全班数据解释为什么常见的指纹纹型是薄弱证据。
- **Key words:**

| English | 中文 | Student-friendly meaning |
|---|---|---|
| fingerprint | 指纹 | The pattern of lines (ridges) on the end of your finger |
| pattern | 纹型 | The shape the lines make |
| loop | 箕形纹 | Lines come in from one side, turn round and go back out the same side |
| whorl | 斗形纹 | Lines make circles or a spiral in the middle |
| arch | 弓形纹 | Lines go in one side, make a small hill and go out the other side |
| classify | 分类 | Put things into groups by what they look like |
| tally | 划记计数 | Counting with marks: four lines and one line across means 5 (like writing 正 in Chinese) |
| bar chart | 条形图 | A chart with bars to compare groups |
| common / rare | 常见的／罕见的 | Many people have it / very few people have it |

- **Timeline:**

| Phase | Min | Teacher does | Students do | Resource |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Do Now 课前任务 | 4 | Shows 4 quick questions about the suspect cards; checks roles are given out; then shows the Today slide (goals and key words, about 1 minute) | Answer from the suspect cards in the case file; fill evidence log row 1 if not done | Slides 2 to 3; suspect cards; evidence log |
| Hook 引入 | 3 | Shows fingerprint facts and an ancient Chinese contract signed with finger marks | Look at their own fingertips with a magnifying glass | Slide 4 |
| Learn 新知 | 7 | Teaches the three patterns with hand-drawn examples; demonstrates the pencil-and-tape method under the visualiser | Practise naming patterns on slide 6; watch the method | Slides 5 to 7 |
| Do 实践 | 18 | Circulates; at minute 8 collects tallies on the board (one per student, right index finger); at minute 14 hands out the Fingerprint File | Take prints (5 min), classify (3 min), tally and bar chart (6 min), classify the scene print and suspect prints (4 min) | Slides 8 to 10; fingerprint record card; worksheet; Fingerprint File |
| Think and Reflect 思辨与反思 | 8 | Runs "Always, sometimes, never"; asks "How strong is the fingerprint evidence?"; collects exit tickets | Pair talk (any language), share with a frame; update evidence log and suspect tracker; exit ticket | Slides 11 to 12; evidence log |

- **Tiers:**
  - Core: label the three patterns; classify own print; complete the tally table and bar chart.
  - Stretch: work out the percentage of the class with each type and compare with the world figures; say which suspects match the scene print.
  - Challenge: explain why a loop at the scene is weak evidence, and what scene print would have been stronger evidence and why.
- **Language support:** Frames: "My fingerprint is a ___ because the lines ___." "This sample matches ___ because ___." "This evidence is strong / weak because ___." Chinese talk welcome while classifying and in the Think pair talk. Traps: "loop" and "arch" are everyday words with new meanings here; "tally" is new for most; say "fingerprint type" (纹型), not "the same fingerprint".
  - Band 1 today: sit the student beside a Band 3 language buddy, who reads the Chinese line of each task and explains it in Chinese but never writes the answer. Do Now: the buddy reads the four Chinese questions on Slide 2; the answers are names and numbers copied from the suspect cards, so no English sentence is needed. Learn: the three gestures on Slide 5 need no English; the student copies them and says 箕形纹 / 斗形纹 / 弓形纹. Before the prints are taken, run Say, show, check on Slide 8: teacher reads each English step, a student reads the Chinese, thumbs check. Worksheet order: Core b first (take the right index print on the bilingual record card, tick 箕形纹 / 斗形纹 / 弓形纹 under the box, then copy the ticked word into the Chinese frame; a Chinese answer counts), then Core a (match each drawing to the three labelled drawings and the gesture icons from Slide 5, printed beside the answer lines; the Chinese pattern names are accepted as answers), then Core c (copy the numbers from the board table, which carries the same bilingual row labels as the Slide 9 visual, so each row is matched by its Chinese label, not by position), then Core d (write a title in Chinese or English and draw the bars from the table). On Slide 10 the student classifies the six prints by shape with the Fingerprint File and points to who matches. The exit ticket may be answered in Chinese using 箕形纹 and 被排除 from the fold-under word bank and the frame 这个证据有力／薄弱，因为___。 from Slide 11.
- **Misconceptions to watch for:** "Same type means same fingerprint" (no: every fingerprint is unique, but there are only three main types). "A matching fingerprint type proves who did it." "Rare things are always more important" (rare is stronger evidence only if it matches).
- **Safety:** Graphite on fingers: wash hands after. No pencils or tape in mouths. Normal classroom rules otherwise.
- **If time is short:** Each student takes only the right index print. Teacher draws the class bar chart on the board while students copy it. Drop Challenge.
- **Assessment and handover links:** Fingerprint record card (in case file). Class tally and bar chart on worksheet. Evidence log row 2 and suspect tracker column "Fingerprint". Exit ticket shows who understands common vs rare as evidence. Scientist's Logbook: if there is time at the end of Think and Reflect, students add one line under "What I found"; otherwise this week's evidence log row counts as the logbook entry.

## 4. Answer key (for the teacher)

**Core a)** 1 arch | 2 loop | 3 whorl

**Core b)** Any type that matches the student's actual print. Check a sample of cards. Smudged prints: ask the student to retake one finger.

**Core c) and d)** Depends on the class. Typical class of 24: about 15 loops, 8 whorls, 1 arch (a class may have 0 arches; that is normal). Bar chart marking: title, both axes labelled, bars of equal width with gaps, heights correct to the nearest unit. Secure title example: "Fingerprint types of our class (right index finger)".

**Stretch a)** Example with 15 / 8 / 1 out of 24: loop 63%, whorl 33%, arch 4%. Accept answers rounded to whole numbers. Class figures will not match the world exactly because the sample is small.

**Stretch b)** Depends on data; mark the logic of the comparison.

**Stretch c)**

| Print | Type |
|---|---|
| Scene print (glass) | Loop |
| Lily Chen | Loop |
| Max Zhang | Loop |
| Chef Zhou | Whorl |
| Dr Ruby Huang | Loop |
| Professor Frost | Arch |

Matches: Lily Chen, Max Zhang, Dr Ruby Huang. Ruled out: Chef Zhou (and Professor Frost: the print on her own glass is not hers).

**Challenge a) Secure-level example**
"A loop is weak evidence because loops are very common: about 60 to 65% of people have them. Three of the four suspects have loops, so the fingerprint does not tell us which one touched the glass. It only rules out Chef Zhou."

**Challenge b) Secure-level example**
"A whorl would be stronger, because only Chef Zhou has a whorl, so it would match just one suspect. But it still would not prove he took the notebook, because about a third of all people have whorls, he might have touched the glass at a different time, and the print only shows that someone touched the glass, not that they took the notebook." Excellent answers mention that an arch would be even rarer (about 5%), and that real experts compare ridge details to make a print strong evidence.

**My thinking: Secure-level ideas**
Compare the fine ridge details; get a clearer print; check whether the glass was washed before the evening; find more prints on the desk or notebook shelf; combine it with other evidence.

**Exit ticket**
1 loop | 2 Chef Zhou | 3 Weak, because loops are common / three suspects match.

**Evidence log row 2 (model)**
Evidence: 2 Fingerprint on glass | Test: classify the pattern | Result: loop | IN: Lily, Max, Dr Huang | OUT: Chef Zhou | Strength: weak.

### 6.2 Fingerprint File (scene print and references)

Create an A4 portrait "Fingerprint File" page for the Think Like a Scientist Science Detectives series, black-and-white printable, styled like a forensic report. Print: 1 per group (6 per class), plus 1 for display.

- Header: "Fingerprint File 指纹比对档案 | Case: The Frozen Notebook 案件：冰封笔记本之谜 | Evidence 2 证据2" with a "LAB REPORT 实验报告" stamp.
- Top section: one large box (8 cm by 10 cm) labelled "Scene print: lifted from the drinking glass, desk F2 现场指纹：取自办公桌F2处的玻璃杯". Draw a stylised fingerprint, black ridges on white, clearly a **loop**: ridges enter from the left edge, curve round in a tight U and leave again on the left; one small triangular meeting point (delta) on the right side. Over the centre and lower third, draw a soft grey smudge that blurs fine detail, but leaves the overall loop shape easy to see. Caption: "Smudged: only the pattern type can be seen. 指纹模糊：只能看出纹型。"
- Lower section: five smaller boxes (each 4.5 cm by 5.5 cm) in a 3 then 2 layout, each with a clear, unsmudged stylised print drawn in the same style, and a label strip underneath. Each print must be drawn differently in detail (different ridge counts and spacing) so no two look identical, but the pattern types are:
  - "Lily Chen 陈莉": **loop**, opening to the right.
  - "Max Zhang 张铭轩": **loop**, opening to the left, wider U.
  - "Chef Zhou 周师傅": **whorl**, concentric circles in the centre, two deltas.
  - "Dr Ruby Huang 黄若琳": **loop**, opening to the left, narrower U.
  - "Professor Frost 弗罗斯特教授": **arch**, smooth hill shape, no delta.
- Under each small box: "Type 纹型: ________".
- Footer note: "Our lab can classify the pattern type only. We cannot compare fine ridge details. 我们的实验室只能分辨纹型，无法比对细节特征。"

Do not put the answers (pattern names) on this sheet: students classify them.
