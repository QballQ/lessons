# Y8 W03 | Who wrote the note? | 纸条是谁写的？

Series: Science Detectives 科学侦探 | Week 3 of 10 | 40 minutes | Phase: Skills
Chinese text status: draft, needs native-speaker check

Case reference: all story details follow `Y8_00_Case_Bible.md`. Scene evidence tested today: evidence 1, the ink on the note (result: matches pen B). **Before this lesson the teacher must complete the pen pre-test and make the note ink strips (Case Bible, Section 11).**

## 1. Lesson plan (one page, for the teacher)

- **Big idea:** Black ink is a mixture of dyes; paper chromatography separates them into a pattern that can be matched against reference samples, which act as controls for a fair comparison.
- **Learning goals:**
  - I can run a paper chromatogram and use reference samples to match the ink on the note to a pen.
  - I can explain what the match shows and what it does not show.
- **Key words:**

| English | 中文 | Student-friendly meaning |
|---|---|---|
| chromatography | 色谱法 | A way to separate the colours (dyes) in a mixture |
| chromatogram | 色谱图 | The paper strip with the separated colours on it |
| mixture | 混合物 | Two or more things mixed together but not joined |
| dye | 染料 | A colour that dissolves |
| solvent | 溶剂 | The liquid that dissolves things; today it is water |
| dissolve | 溶解 | Mix into a liquid so you cannot see the pieces |
| reference sample | 参照样本 | A known sample we compare the unknown with |
| baseline | 基线 | The pencil start line near the bottom |
| solvent front | 溶剂前沿 | How far the water climbed up the paper |
| Rf value (Stretch) | 比移值（Rf值） | Distance moved by spot ÷ distance moved by solvent |

- **Timeline:**

| Phase | Min | Teacher does | Students do | Resource |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Do Now 课前任务 | 4 | Shows three fingerprint drawings and one question | Name the patterns; answer "Which suspect did the fingerprint rule out?" | Slide 2 |
| Hook 引入 | 3 | Holds up the note; asks "Is black ink just black?"; shows one dried pre-test strip from the pen pre-test | Predict, then see black split into colours | Slide 3; one pre-test strip |
| Learn 新知 | 7 | Explains how chromatography works and why we need reference samples; models setting up one strip | Complete the method cloze on the slide together | Slides 4 to 6 |
| Do 实践 | 18 | Times the stages; circulates checking water is below the baseline; at minute 12 calls "strips out" | Set up 5 strips (5 min); while they run, do worksheet Core a (7 min); remove strips, mark solvent front, compare, complete results and match tables; Stretch: Rf | Slides 7 to 9; worksheet; kit tray |
| Think and Reflect 思辨与反思 | 8 | Asks "Does the match prove who wrote the note?"; collects exit tickets | Pair talk (any language), share with a frame; update evidence log row 3 and suspect tracker; exit ticket | Slides 10 to 11; evidence log |

- **Tiers:**
  - Core: method cloze with word bank; results table (colours seen); match table.
  - Stretch: calculate Rf values for a worked example and for the main spot on each of their own strips.
  - Challenge: explain why the reference samples matter, predict what goes wrong if the baseline is drawn in pen or sits under the water, and explain why a match does not prove who wrote the note.
- **Language support:** Frames: "The note matches pen ___ because the colours ___." "This sample matches ___ because ___." "This supports / does not support ___ because ___." Chinese welcome during pair talk and while waiting for strips. Traps: "solvent" vs "solution"; "dye" vs "die"; "match" as a verb.
- **Misconceptions to watch for:** "Black ink is one colour." "Water makes the colours" (no: the dyes were already in the ink). "The pen matches, so that person wrote it" (no: two suspects carry pen B, and pens can be borrowed). "Rf can be more than 1" (no: the spot cannot travel further than the solvent).
- **Safety:** Low hazard. Do not drink the water; wipe spills straight away; keep pen caps on (ink stains clothes).
- **If time is short:** Run only N and B plus one other pen. Skip Stretch on own strips and use the worked example only. If strips fail, use the teacher's pre-test strips as "lab results".
- **Assessment and handover links:** Chromatograms stuck in the case file; results and match tables on the worksheet; evidence log row 3; suspect tracker column "Ink". Exit ticket shows understanding of reference samples. Scientist's Logbook: if there is time at the end of Think and Reflect, students add one line under "What I found"; otherwise this week's evidence log row counts as the logbook entry.

## 4. Answer key (for the teacher)

**Core a)** mixture | solvent | dissolve | separate | pencil | reference

**Core b)** Colours depend on the brands you bought: copy them from your Teacher Pre-test Record (Section 6.2). Mark for: colours listed bottom to top, and N marked as the same as B only. Typical washable black inks separate into combinations of blue, purple or violet, pink or red, yellow and brown.

**Core c)**

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Which pen matches N? | Pen B |
| Which suspects carry it? | Max Zhang and Dr Ruby Huang |
| Ruled out by the ink? | Lily Chen (pen A) and Chef Zhou (pen C) |
| Did Professor Frost write it? | No: her pen D does not match |

**Stretch a)** Purple: 3.2 ÷ 8.0 = 0.40. Yellow: 7.2 ÷ 8.0 = 0.90.

**Stretch b) and c)** Values depend on the pens. Check: Rf always less than 1; no units; the main spot in N has the same Rf as pen B to within about 0.05. Secure c): "The Rf value of the main spot in N is closest to pen B, which supports our match because the same dye travels the same fraction of the way up the paper under the same conditions."

**Challenge a)** A pattern on its own means nothing; we need known samples (references) tested the same way to compare it with.

**Challenge b)** (i) The felt-tip baseline would dissolve and run up the paper too, mixing extra colours into the results. (ii) The ink would dissolve into the water in the cup instead of climbing the paper, so there would be little or no pattern.

**Challenge c) Secure-level example**
"The match only shows the note was written with a brand B pen. Two suspects, Max and Dr Huang, both carry brand B, and anyone could have borrowed one of their pens. Handwriting comparison, or the footprint or powder evidence, would help decide between them." Excellent answers add that brand B came free in every Fair welcome pack, so other exhibitors may have one too.

**My thinking: Secure-level ideas**
Different companies use their own recipes (different dyes, different prices, different colours of black); this is what makes each pattern useful as evidence.

**Exit ticket**
1 Pen B | 2 Lily Chen and Chef Zhou | 3 So we can compare the unknown ink with known inks tested in exactly the same way.

### 6.1 Chromatography lab card (table top)

Create an A5 landscape laminated lab card for the Think Like a Scientist Science Detectives series, black-and-white printable. Print: 6 (one per group).

- Header: "Chromatography method 色谱法步骤 | Evidence 1: the note".
- Five numbered picture steps (same drawings as slide 7) with one short line each:
  1. Draw a pencil baseline 2 cm from the bottom. Write the letter at the top in pencil. 用铅笔在离底部2厘米处画基线。
  2. Put a small, dark dot of the pen on the baseline. Dot 3 times. 在基线上点一个小而深的墨点，点3次。
  3. Fold the top over a pencil and tape it. Hang the strip in the cup. Water 1 cm deep, below the dot. 把纸条挂在杯中，水深1厘米，低于墨点。
  4. Wait about 7 minutes. Do not move the cup. 等待约7分钟，不要移动杯子。
  5. Take the strip out. Mark the solvent front in pencil. Dry flat. 取出纸条，用铅笔标出溶剂前沿，平放晾干。
- Side box: "Strip N is ready made from the note ink. Treat it exactly like the others. N号纸条已用纸条墨水制好，操作方法相同。"
