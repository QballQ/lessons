## 1. Lesson plan (one page, for the teacher)

- **Big idea:** No single clue proves who took the notebook; scientists rank each piece of evidence by how many suspects it fits, set aside evidence that is contaminated (with a checked reason, not because it is inconvenient), and judge the whole combination with an honest level of confidence.
- **Learning goals:**
  - I can rank each piece of evidence as strong, supporting or weak, and explain why the puddle must be set aside as contaminated evidence.
  - I can write a Claim-Evidence-Reasoning conclusion with a confidence level, and explain how tunnel vision could have misled us.
- **Key words:**

| English | 中文 | Student-friendly meaning |
|---|---|---|
| strong evidence | 有力证据 | Points to only one suspect |
| supporting evidence | 支持性证据 | Matches some suspects (here, two); it helps, but does not decide |
| weak evidence | 薄弱证据 | Matches most or all suspects, or something everyone used |
| contamination | 污染 | When something gets into the evidence for another reason, so it no longer tells us what happened |
| tunnel vision | 隧道视野 | Looking only at one idea and not seeing the rest of the evidence |
| confirmation bias | 确认偏误 | Noticing only the evidence that agrees with what we already think |
| claim | 主张 | What we say happened |
| reasoning | 推理 | Explaining how the evidence supports the claim |
| confidence | 把握 | How sure we are, and why: low, medium or high |
| expert witness | 专家证人 | A scientist who explains one piece of evidence to a court |

- **Timeline (adjusted: the evidence board, twist, conclusion, witness preparation and the self-assessment slip all happen this week):**

| Phase | Min | Teacher does | Students do | Resource |

- **Tiers:**
  - Core: complete the ranking table (matches and strength); explain in one sentence why the puddle is set aside; write a CER conclusion using the frame.
  - Stretch: add a confidence statement with a reason ("highly confident, but not certain, because ..."); give three facts that show the puddle is contaminated.
  - Challenge: explain the two kinds of tunnel vision this case could cause; explain why the combination is strong when no single clue is; say what new evidence could change your mind.
- **Language support:** Frames: "This evidence is strong / supporting / weak because it matches ___ suspects." "Evidence 4 is contaminated because ___ ." "We claim that ___ took the notebook. The evidence is ___ . This shows ___ because ___ ." "We are ___ confident, but not certain, because ___ ." "One problem with this evidence is ___ ." Chinese welcome for board building and the tunnel vision talk; written conclusions use English key words. Traps: "contamination" (noun), "contaminated" (adjective); "evidence" is uncountable ("three pieces of evidence", not "three evidences"); "bias" here is about how we think, not about being unfair to a person; "confident" vs "confidence".
- **Misconceptions to watch for:** "We can ignore any clue that does not fit" (only with a checked reason). "The newest clue is the most important." "More clues for one person means proof." "Contamination means someone did something wrong" (the cleaning team was doing its job). "High confidence means certain."
- **Safety:** No practical hazards beyond normal classroom rules. (Soil groups: settling bottles stay sealed on the windowsill; look, do not open.)
- **If time is short:** Pre-fill the "Matches" lines on the evidence cards. Take the tunnel vision discussion as a 1-minute show of hands. Move the self-assessment slip to the first 4 minutes of break or form time (it must be done before you decide rubric levels). Do not cut the twist or the CER conclusion.
- **Assessment and handover links:** CER conclusion and evidence board (photograph it) are the main rubric evidence for strands 4 and 5 and go into the case file. Evidence log row 9. Self-assessment slips (compare with your judgement before Week 10). **Collect case files at the end of this lesson** and draft rubric levels for strands 1 to 5 before Week 10 (Week 10 has no time for this); strand 6 is judged from the Week 10 talk. ## 4. Answer key (for the teacher)

**Do Now:** Lily 3 of 5 | Max 2 of 5 | Chef Zhou 2 of 5 | Dr Huang 4 of 5 (does not match the liquid).

**Core a) Model evidence board (before the twist)**

| Evidence | Matches | Strength (first board) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Ink: pen B | Max, Dr Huang | Supporting |
| 2 Fingerprint: loop | Lily, Max, Dr Huang | Weak (common pattern) |
| 3 Powder: baking soda | Chef Zhou, Dr Huang | Supporting |
| 4 Liquid: alkali | Lily, Chef Zhou | Supporting (most groups, before the twist) |
| 5 Footprint: 24 cm | Lily, Dr Huang | Supporting |
| 6 Soil: flowerbed | All four (everyone walked past it) | Weak |
| 7 Paper towel: lab's own | All four (everyone could use them) | Weak |
| Sign-in sheet and alibis | Dr Huang (7:00 to 7:20) and Lily (prep room until 7:30) were nearby between 6:50 and 7:30 pm; Max was in the lab until 7:00 pm | Supporting testimony (can be checked, can be wrong) |

Notes on testimony: Max was in the lab until 7:00, after he saw the notebook at 6:50, so testimony alone does not rule him out; Chef Zhou (left 6:20, seen in the canteen) and the cleaning team (6:30 to 6:40) left before the notebook was last seen. Accept "supporting" or "weak" for testimony with a reason; it is not physical evidence.

**Model evidence board after the twist (Case Bible, Section 9)**

| Evidence | Matches | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Fingerprint: loop | Lily, Max, Dr Huang | Weak (common pattern) |
| Ink: brand B | Max, Dr Huang | Supporting |
| Powder: baking soda | Chef Zhou, Dr Huang | Supporting |
| Footprint: 24 cm | Lily, Dr Huang | Supporting |
| Liquid: alkali | (Lily, Chef Zhou) | Contaminated: set aside |
| Sign-in sheet and alibis | Dr Huang and Lily were nearby between 6:50 and 7:30 pm; Max until 7:00 pm | Supporting testimony |
| Soil and paper towel | Everyone | Weak |
| **All reliable physical evidence together** | **Dr Huang only** | **Strong** (no single clue is strong; the combination is) |

Ink 2 and Powder 2 results (if your class did them) make the ink and powder matches more reliable; they stay supporting.

**Core b) Secure-level example**
"Evidence 4 is contaminated evidence because the cleaning team mopped the floor by the sink at 6:30 pm with soapy floor cleaner and left water on the floor, before the notebook was last seen at 6:50 pm. The puddle got there for an innocent reason, so it tells us nothing about who took the notebook."

**Core c)** Lily 2 | Max 2 | Chef Zhou 1 | Dr Huang 4.

**Core d) Secure-level example**
"I claim that Dr Ruby Huang took the notebook. The evidence is: the fingerprint on the glass is a loop, like hers; the note was written with a brand B pen, like hers; the powder is baking soda, which she had for her volcano; and the footprint shows a person about 150 to 170 cm tall, and she is 165 cm. This shows it was probably her because she is the only suspect who matches all four reliable clues. The liquid did not match her, but it was mop water, so we set it aside."
Accept a different claim only if it is argued from evidence and deals with the clues that do not fit (it will usually fall down on Core c). Do **not** confirm the answer this week; the reveal is in Week 10.

**Stretch a) Secure-level example (Case Bible wording)**
"We are highly confident, but not certain, that Dr Huang took the notebook, because she is the only suspect who matches all four reliable clues." Accept "medium" with a good reason (for example "each clue matches other people too, and pens can be borrowed").

**Stretch b) Three facts (any three)**
- The puddle at I2 is next to the mop bucket and "Wet floor" sign at I3 (on the map since Week 1).
- The cleaning log says the floor by the sink was mopped at 6:30 pm and water was left on the floor.
- Soapy floor cleaner is alkaline, which explains the blue-green indicator result.
- The puddle was there before the notebook was last seen (6:50 pm), so it cannot be from whoever took it.
- The sign-in sheet shows the cleaning team in Lab 3 from 6:30 to 6:40 pm.

**Challenge a) Secure-level example (Case Bible, Section 9)**
"Some detectives decided it was Dr Huang after Week 4 and ignored the liquid without a reason. They were right, but for a weak reason: a scientist must explain evidence that does not fit, not ignore it. Other detectives switched to Lily after Week 5 because the newest clue felt most important, and ignored the three clues that rule her out (ink, powder, and the combination)." Excellent answers name confirmation bias (only noticing evidence that agrees with what you already think) and describe the scientific approach: keep all the evidence, notice the conflict, look for an explanation, then check it (the cleaning log).

**Challenge b)** Each clue matches several people, but the chance that one person matches all four by accident is much smaller. Only Dr Huang is in all four groups: loop AND brand B AND baking soda AND a 24 cm footprint.

**Challenge c)** Examples: Dr Huang's brand B pen is missing and someone else had it; a second baking soda spill from Chef Zhou's visit; someone else in the building fits all four clues; Lily's report of a noise at 7:10 turns out to be someone else; finding the notebook and testing it for fingerprints.

**My thinking: Secure-level ideas**
Yes, but only when you can show with checked evidence that it is contaminated or unrelated. First notice that it does not fit, then find a possible reason, then check that reason. Never set evidence aside just because it does not agree with your favourite suspect.

**Tunnel vision sort (Ideas Parking Board)**
- Checked and supported: any note about the mop, the cleaning team, the sink, "the puddle was there before", or "it is not evidence". Name and praise these students publicly (they found the twist in Week 5).
- Checked and not supported: "two people took it" (nothing needs a second person); "Dr Huang brought a different liquid".
- Cannot check yet: "the test was wrong" (we could repeat it, but the cleaning log explains the result anyway).
