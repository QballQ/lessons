This lesson closes the case in one period. It replaces Weeks 7 to 10 for now: the student-designed test (Weeks 7 and 8) and the Court of Science (Week 10) are held back for later, because one period a week and the classes' current level of English make them hard to run well. ## 1. Lesson plan

- **Big idea:** No single clue solves the case. Putting all the reliable evidence together, and explaining the clue that does not fit, does.
- **Learning goals:**
  - I can put all the evidence together to decide which suspect matches. 我能把所有证据放在一起，判断哪名嫌疑人相符。
  - I can explain why one piece of evidence must be set aside. 我能解释为什么有一条证据必须搁置。
  - I can give a verdict and say how sure I am. 我能作出裁决，并说出有多大把握。
- **Key words:** evidence 证据 | matches 相符 | contaminated 被污染的 | set aside 搁置 | verdict 裁决 | case closed 结案
- **Shape of the lesson:** a two-minute start, then hands-on work with the evidence kit all the way through. Teacher talk is kept to short prompts between steps.

| Phase | Min | Teacher does | Students do | Resource |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Do Now | 3 | Recaps the five clues; shows the kit | Open Envelope 1; lay the suspect cards along the top of the board and the lab reports down the side | Slides 1 to 4 |
| Step 1: match | 8 | Models one box, then circulates | Write ✓ or ✗ in all 20 boxes, from the cards, not from opinions | Slide 5; evidence board |
| Step 2: count | 4 | Takes counts from groups; asks "Does anyone match all 5?" | Count the ✓ for each suspect out of 5; notice that nobody has 5 | Slide 6 |
| Step 3: new evidence | 4 | Says "Open Envelope 2" | Read the cleaning log, look at the map close-up, complete three lines | Slide 7; cleaning log sheet |
| Step 4: set aside | 3 | Explains contaminated evidence in two sentences | Cross out clue 4; count again out of 4 | Slide 8 |
| Step 5: check the time | 3 | Points to the time window | Use the sign-in sheet: who was in the lab between 6:50 and 7:30 pm? | Slide 9; sign-in sheet |
| Step 6: verdict | 5 | Runs the vote | Fill in the verdict slip; hold up the chosen suspect card | Slide 10; verdict slip |
| Step 7: find the notebook | 5 | Says "Open Envelope 3"; sends a student to the freezer | Use the note and the footprint to work out where the notebook is; a student opens the freezer and finds it | Slides 11 to 12; Envelope 3; the frozen notebook |
| Reveal and close | 5 | Reads the ending; asks two questions | Listen; a student reads the Chinese summary lines; say one thing they will remember | Slides 13 to 14; ending story |

- **Language support:** Chinese talk is welcome in every step. Every card, frame and instruction is in English with Chinese. Students need only three English frames: "___ matches ___ of 4 clues." "We think ___ took the notebook." "The notebook is in the ___." Sit students who are new to English next to a language buddy who explains each step in Chinese and does not write the answer.
- **If time is short:** skip Step 5 (the sign-in sheet) and take the verdict as a show of cards without the slip. Never cut the freezer.
- **If a group finishes early:** "Which clue is the weakest, and why?" (the fingerprint: three of the four suspects have a loop).

## 2. Answer key (teacher only)

| Evidence and result | Lily Chen | Max Zhang | Chef Zhou | Dr Ruby Huang |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Note: ink brand B | ✗ (brand A) | ✓ | ✗ (brand C) | ✓ |
| 2 Fingerprint: loop | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ (whorl) | ✓ |
| 3 Powder: baking soda | ✗ (salt) | ✗ (sugar) | ✓ | ✓ |
| 4 Puddle: alkali | ✓ (soap solution) | ✗ (lemon juice) | ✓ (baking soda solution) | ✗ (vinegar) |
| 5 Footprint: height 150 to 170 cm | ✓ (158 cm) | ✗ (182 cm) | ✗ (176 cm) | ✓ (165 cm) |
| Step 2: out of 5 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 4 |
| Step 4: without the puddle, out of 4 | 2 | 2 | 1 | **4 of 4** |

- **The twist:** the cleaning team mopped the floor by the sink with soapy cleaner at 6:30 pm. The notebook was still on the desk at 6:50 pm, so the puddle was there before it was taken. It is contaminated evidence and is set aside. Soap is an alkali, which explains the indicator result.
- **The culprit:** Dr Ruby Huang, the only suspect who matches all four reliable clues. She was in the lab from 7:00 to 7:20 pm.
- **The notebook:** in the lab freezer, in a freezer bag, behind the ice-cube trays. The note says "safe and cold"; the footprint at G5 points towards the freezer.
- **Students' own results:** the lab report cards give the confirmed results, so every group works from the same evidence even if one of their own tests did not work.

## 3. Kit and prep

**Each group (6 groups):** A3 evidence board; Envelope 1 (4 suspect cards, 5 lab report cards, sign-in sheet); Envelope 2, sealed (cleaning log); Envelope 3, sealed (Where is the notebook?); 1 verdict slip; pencils. Students may also use their case files and evidence logs.

**Per class:** the frozen notebook in the freezer; the ending story (teacher copy); the answer key (teacher copy).

**To buy or find:** 18 envelopes per class (3 per group; C5 or DL), 1 blue notebook or exercise book, 1 zip-lock freezer bag, glue stick. A cool box and ice packs if there is no freezer near the room.

**Prep, about 40 minutes for the first class, 10 minutes for each class after that:**

1. Print the kit (the first page of the A4 PDF lists the copies). Cut the suspect cards, lab report cards and verdict slips. (15 min)
2. Fill and label the envelopes; seal Envelopes 2 and 3. (15 min)
3. The day before: glue the cover on the blue notebook, seal it in the freezer bag with the "Evidence" label, and put it in the lab freezer behind the ice-cube trays, at least 2 hours before the lesson so the bag is frosty. (10 min)
4. Between classes: put the notebook back in the freezer and re-seal Envelopes 2 and 3 with tape.

**Hazards and controls:** no chemicals or glassware. Only one student opens the freezer, with the teacher beside them; nothing else is taken out.

## 5. Later follow-up

When there is time, Weeks 7 and 8 (design and run your own forensic test) can be taught as a stand-alone practical, and Week 10 (Court of Science) as a speaking activity. The students will already know the answer, so frame them as "How would we prove it to a court?"
