Scene evidence tested today: evidence 5, the white powdery footprint at G5 (result: 24 cm long; estimated height about 150 to 170 cm; matches Lily Chen and Dr Ruby Huang). **First lesson led by the incoming teacher.** Print the scene footprint at 100% scale and check it measures exactly 24 cm.

## 1. Lesson plan (one page, for the teacher)

- **Big idea:** When two measurements are linked (a correlation), a scatter graph and a line of best fit let us estimate one from the other, but because people vary, an honest estimate is a range, not a single number.
- **Learning goals:**
  - I can measure foot length and height carefully, plot them on a scatter graph and draw a line of best fit.
  - I can use the graph to estimate the height of the person who left the scene footprint as a range, and say which suspects fit.
- **Key words:**

| English | 中文 | Student-friendly meaning |
|---|---|---|
| foot length | 脚长 | How long your foot is, from the back of the heel to the tip of the longest toe |
| height | 身高 | How tall a person is, from the floor to the top of the head |
| data | 数据 | Numbers or facts we collect |
| scatter graph | 散点图 | A graph with one dot (or cross) for each person, showing two measurements |
| correlation | 相关性 | A link between two measurements: when one goes up, the other usually goes up (or down) too |
| line of best fit | 最佳拟合线 | One straight line drawn through the middle of the points, showing the pattern |
| estimate | 估计 | A careful guess using data |
| range | 范围 | From the smallest likely value to the biggest likely value |

- **Timeline:**

| Phase | Min | Teacher does | Students do | Resource |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Do Now 课前任务 | 4 | Introduces self as the new case leader; shows 3 recap questions on the Week 5 liquid | Answer from the evidence log; check row 5 is filled in | Slide 2; evidence log |
| Hook 引入 | 3 | Holds up the true-size scene footprint next to a metre stick: "Can a footprint tell us how tall someone is?" | Predict with a frame; vote: short, medium or tall person? | Slide 3; scene footprint |
| Learn 新知 | 7 | Teaches scatter graph, correlation and line of best fit with 6 example points; shows how to read off a value and give a range; models measuring safely and kindly | Copy the reading-off method; practise reading one value on slide 5 | Slides 4 to 6 |
| Do 实践 | 18 | Times the stages; types class data into the projected table; at minute 7 calls "plot now"; at minute 15 calls "line and estimate" | Measure in pairs (4 min); Recorder reads group data to the teacher (3 min); plot the scatter graph (8 min); draw line of best fit and estimate (3 min) | Slides 7 to 9; worksheet; scene footprint; sample data card |
| Think and Reflect 思辨与反思 | 8 | Asks "Which suspects fit your range?"; runs "Always, sometimes, never"; collects exit tickets (with option vote for Week 7) | Compare suspects with the range; update evidence log row 6, tracker and confidence; pair talk; exit ticket | Slides 10 to 12; evidence log |

- **Tiers:**
  - Core: record data; plot the scatter graph; draw a line of best fit; read off the height for a 24 cm foot; name the suspects who fit.
  - Stretch: give the estimate as a range using the points near 24 cm; describe the correlation with a frame; explain why a range is more honest than one number.
  - Challenge: explain two limits of this estimate (our data are from 12 to 13 year olds, the suspects are adults; a shoe print is not exactly a foot); compare the scene print directly with the suspects' foot lengths and say whether this agrees.
- **Language support:** Frames: "As foot length increases, height ___ ." "The line of best fit shows a height of about ___ cm for a 24 cm foot." "The person was probably between ___ cm and ___ cm tall." "This sample matches ___ because ___ ." "This evidence is strong / weak because ___ ." Chinese welcome during measuring and pair talk. Traps: "high" vs "height" vs "tall" (say "height", not "high"); "estimate" (noun and verb); "range" here means "from ... to ...", not a cooking range or a mountain range; "line of best fit" does not have to go through every point or through zero.
- **Misconceptions to watch for:** "The line must join every dot" (dot-to-dot). "The line must start at zero." "Correlation means a rule: everyone with a 24 cm foot is 160 cm tall." "One answer is more scientific than a range" (the opposite is true here). "Big feet cause tallness" (both come from growing; there is a link, not a cause).
- **Safety:** Shoes off for measuring (socks on): shoes go under the desk, not in walkways. No standing on chairs or tables to read a metre stick. Be kind: heights and feet are personal. No one's measurements are read out by name; any student may use the sample data instead of measuring. (Kit card, Section 5.)
- **If time is short:** Skip measuring and use the sample data card for everyone (saves about 7 minutes). Plot 12 points instead of 24. Drop Challenge.
- **Assessment and handover links:** Scatter graph and height estimate on the worksheet (rubric strand 4 evidence for the final case file). Evidence log row 6; suspect tracker column "Footprint"; confidence row W6. Exit ticket includes each student's Week 7 option choice, which you need **before Week 7** to plan groups and kit. Scientist's Logbook: if there is time at the end of Think and Reflect, students add one line under "What I found"; otherwise this week's evidence log row counts as the logbook entry.

## 4. Answer key (for the teacher)

Answers below use the **sample data set** (Section 6.2). With class data, mark the method and check the values are sensible.

**Sample data facts (checked)**
- 24 students; foot length 21.5 to 26.5 cm; height 143 to 174 cm.
- Mean foot length 23.8 cm; mean height 158 cm. Height is about 6.6 times foot length on average (6.4 to 6.9 for individuals).
- Calculated best-fit line: height ≈ 6.3 × foot length + 8. A good hand-drawn line passes close to (22 cm, 147 cm), (23.8 cm, 158 cm) and (26 cm, 172 cm).
- Strong positive correlation (correlation coefficient about 0.93, for your information only).

**Core d)** Marking: title (for example "Foot length and height of 24 Year 8 students"), both axes labelled with units, 24 crosses plotted to within half a small square. Zigzag breaks are not required from students but credit them.

**Core e)** About 160 cm. Accept 157 to 163 cm for a sensible hand-drawn line. Common error: joining the dots, or drawing the line from the origin; ask "Are there about as many crosses above your line as below?"

**Core f)** Close: Lily Chen (158 cm) and Dr Ruby Huang (165 cm). Far: Max Zhang (182 cm) and Chef Zhou (176 cm).

**Stretch a)** Crosses with foot length 23.5 to 24.5 cm (students S02, S03, S07, S10, S11, S14, S19, S21, S24) have heights from 150 cm to 168 cm. Accept "about 150 to 170 cm". Secure answers say "between 150 cm and 168 cm" or round to "about 150 to 170 cm".

**Stretch b)** Lily ✓ | Max ✗ | Chef Zhou ✗ | Dr Huang ✓.

**Stretch c)** "As foot length increases, height increases (usually). This is a positive correlation."

**Stretch d)** People with the same foot length are not all the same height (on the sample data, nine people with feet of 23.5 to 24.5 cm range from 150 to 168 cm), so one number would sound more certain than the evidence really is.

**Challenge a) Acceptable reasons (any two)**
- Our data come from 12 to 13 year olds; the suspects are adults, whose body proportions can be a little different.
- A shoe print is usually longer than the foot inside the shoe (often by 1 to 2 cm), so the foot may be shorter than 24 cm. (The Case Bible treats the print as the foot length for simplicity; this is an honest limit to discuss.)
- The print may be smudged or stretched if the person was moving, so 24 cm may not be exact.
- Our sample is small (24 people) and from one class.
- Measuring errors in the class data (posture, shoes, rounding).

**Challenge b) Secure-level example**
"The print is 24 cm. Lily's foot is 23.5 cm and Dr Huang's is 24.5 cm, both within 0.5 cm. Chef Zhou's (26.5 cm) and Max's (27.5 cm) are too long. So the foot lengths agree with the height range: the same two suspects fit." Excellent answers add that this is not a separate clue: it is the same footprint checked a second way.

**Challenge c)** Harder: 30 cm is outside our data (the graph stops at 28 cm and no student has feet that long), so we would have to extend the line beyond the data, which is less reliable.

**My thinking: Secure-level ideas**
Supporting evidence: it matches two suspects, not one, and the estimate is a range because people vary.

**Exit ticket**
1 About 150 to 170 cm (accept a range that includes 160 cm and is between 10 and 25 cm wide) | 2 Lily Chen and Dr Ruby Huang | 3 Any option (record the choice for group planning).

**Always, sometimes, never:** Sometimes. There is a positive correlation, but people with the same foot length can differ in height by 15 cm or more.
