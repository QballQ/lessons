## 1. Lesson plan (one page, for the teacher)

- **Big idea:** A good forensic test starts with a clear question, compares the scene sample with known reference samples tested in exactly the same way, changes only one thing, and is planned to be safe before anyone touches the kit.
- **Learning goals:**
  - I can write a testable question for my group's forensic test and name what we change, what we observe or measure, and what we keep the same.
  - I can plan a safe method with reference samples, repeats, a risk table and a results table.
- **Key words:**

| English | 中文 | Student-friendly meaning |
|---|---|---|
| testable question | 可检验的问题 | A question we can answer by doing a test and collecting data |
| independent variable | 自变量 | The one thing we change (here, usually which sample we test) |
| dependent variable | 因变量 | What we observe or measure to see the result |
| control variable | 控制变量 | Things we keep the same so the test is fair |
| fair test | 公平测试 | A test where only one thing changes |
| reference sample | 参照样本 | A known sample we compare the unknown with |
| method | 实验步骤 | The steps of the test, in order, clear enough for someone else to follow |
| prediction | 预测 | What we think will happen, and why |
| hazard | 危险源 | Something that could cause harm |
| risk | 风险 | How likely the harm is, and how bad it could be |

- **Timeline:**

| Phase | Min | Teacher does | Students do | Resource |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Do Now 课前任务 | 4 | Shows 3 recap questions (footprint graph, contamination) | Answer on mini whiteboards or in the logbook | Slide 2 |
| Hook 引入 | 3 | "New evidence from the forensic team": reveals Evidence 6 (soil) and Evidence 7 (paper towel) in labelled bags, plus two questions from the lab; hands each group its option card | Read the story hook on the option card; Reporter reads the question aloud | Slides 3 to 4; option cards |
| Learn 新知 | 7 | Teaches the six planning steps; models variables and reference samples with the Week 4 powder test; shows one worked risk-table row | Copy the frame "We change ___ . We observe ___ . We keep ___ the same." | Slides 5 to 7 |
| Do 实践 | 18 | Circulates; **checkpoint 1 at minute 6** (question and reference samples signed off); **checkpoint 2 at minute 12** (method and risk table signed off); collects kit requests | Groups plan on the plan sheet using the option card: question, references, variables, method (by minute 12); risk table, results table, kit request | Slides 8 to 9; worksheet (plan sheet); option cards |
| Think and Reflect 思辨与反思 | 8 | Runs "Would you rather test?"; organises a 2-minute plan swap with the next group; collects exit tickets and plan sheets | Choose the better design and justify; check another group's plan against the checklist; exit ticket | Slides 10 to 12 |

- **Tiers:**
  - Core: write the question, list reference samples, name the variables with frames, put method steps in order and add amounts, complete the risk table from the option card, write results table headings with units.
  - Stretch: explain why each control variable matters; justify the number of repeats; add one specific risk of your own with a control.
  - Challenge: make a prediction with a reason and state what result would show it is wrong; explain how your result could (or could not) change the case; anticipate one problem and plan a fix.
- **Language support:** Frames: "Our question is: Which ___ matches ___ ?" "We change ___ . We observe / measure ___ . We keep ___ the same." "We will repeat each test ___ times because ___ ." "The hazard is ___ . To stay safe we will ___ ." "I predict ___ because ___ ." Chinese welcome for group planning talk; the plan sheet is written with English key words. Traps: "hazard" vs "risk" (the hazard is the thing, the risk is the chance and size of harm); "control" means two things here (a control variable, and a reference sample used as a control); "method" is a list of steps, not the kit list.
- **Misconceptions to watch for:** "Fair means everyone gets a turn." Changing two things at once (for example a different amount of soil and a different location). Forgetting that the scene sample must be tested exactly like the references. "One go is enough." Writing a kit list instead of a method. Thinking a result will "prove" who did it.
- **Safety:** No practical work today; students only look at sealed evidence bags. The safety planning in the risk table is checked by the teacher before Week 8 (checkpoint 2, then again when you read the plan sheets).
- **If time is short:** Skip the plan swap. Stretch and Challenge can be finished at the start of Week 8 while kit is handed out. The teacher can sign off checkpoint 2 after the lesson when reading plan sheets.
- **Assessment and handover links:** The plan sheet is the main rubric evidence for strands 1 (questioning and predicting) and 2 (planning); it goes into the case file. Evidence log row 7 (see answer key). Kit requests tell you exactly what to prepare for Week 8. Exit ticket shows who can name the IV, DV and a control. Scientist's Logbook: if there is time at the end of Think and Reflect, students add one line under "What I found"; otherwise this week's evidence log row counts as the logbook entry.

## 4. Answer key (for the teacher)

**Do Now:** 1 about 147 cm (sample data; accept 144 to 150) | 2 Lily Chen and Dr Huang | 3 To stop contamination: a dirty spoon could carry one powder into another and give a false result.

### Model plans (Secure level) for each option

**Option: Soil**
- Question: "Which campus location matches the soil from the footprint tread (Evidence 6)?"
- Samples: four references (A flowerbed by the science block entrance; B sports field edge; C sandpit; D tree bed by the gate) and the scene sample (Evidence 6).
- Variables: change: which soil sample. Observe or measure: colour on white paper; what we see under the magnifier (stones, sand, roots); layers in the settling bottle after 15 minutes (number of layers, height of each layer in mm, how cloudy the water is, what floats). Keep the same: 1 level tablespoon of soil; 150 ml of water; identical clear plastic bottles; shake for 30 seconds; same settling time; same place on the bench.
- Method: 1 Label five bottles A, B, C, D, Scene. 2 Put 150 ml of water in each. 3 Add 1 level tablespoon of soil to each (clean spoon each time). 4 Screw lids on tightly and shake each for 30 seconds. 5 Stand on the bench; do not move them. 6 Observe and draw at 1, 5, 10 and 15 minutes; measure layer heights in mm at 15 minutes. 7 While waiting, put a pinch of each soil on white paper and look with the magnifier. 8 Repeat with a second set of bottles (the other pair).
- Risks: soil may contain germs or sharp bits: gloves for anyone with cuts, do not touch your face, wash hands after; bottle could leak when shaken: check lids are tight, goggles while shaking; water spills: wipe up at once.
- Results table headings: Sample | Colour on white paper | What we see under the magnifier | Layers at 15 min (draw and label) | Height of bottom layer (mm) | Water cloudy or clear? | Floating bits?
- Repeats: two bottles per sample (minimum), because one bottle might be shaken differently.
- Prediction (Challenge): "I predict the scene soil will match the flowerbed, because it is dark with small bits of plant, like flowerbed soil. If it matches the sandpit (mostly sand, water clears fast), my prediction is wrong."
- Effect on the case (Challenge): it can show where the soil came from, but every suspect walked past the flowerbed by the science block entrance to reach the lab, so it cannot rule anyone in or out.

**Option: Paper**
- Question: "Which paper towel matches the crumpled paper towel found in the corridor bin outside the lab (Evidence 7)?"
- Samples: four references (P1 the lab's own paper towels from the sink dispenser; P2 canteen napkin; P3 tissue; P4 kitchen roll from a shop) and the scene paper (Evidence 7).
- Variables: change: type of paper. Observe or measure: number of layers (ply); pattern pressed into the paper (under the magnifier); height the coloured water climbs in 60 seconds (mm). Keep the same: strip size 2 cm by 12 cm; strips cut in the same direction (along the arrow); water 1 cm deep with one drop of food colouring; same time (60 s); same cups.
- Method: 1 Cut 3 strips of each paper, 2 cm by 12 cm, along the direction arrow. 2 Draw a pencil line 1 cm from the bottom of each strip. 3 Fold the top over a pencil and tape. 4 Lower the strip until the bottom just touches the coloured water (the water reaches the pencil line); start the timer. 5 At 60 seconds lift out and mark how high the water reached. 6 Measure from the pencil line in mm. 7 Peel a corner to count the layers; look at the pattern with the magnifier. 8 Repeat 3 times for each paper, scene paper last.
- Risks: food colouring stains skin and clothes: one drop only, wipe spills; scissors: cut away from fingers, pass handle first; water on the floor: wipe up at once.
- Results table headings: Paper | Layers (ply) | Pattern | Height after 60 s, strip 1 (mm) | Strip 2 (mm) | Strip 3 (mm) | Mean (mm)
- Repeats: 3 strips each, because paper is not perfectly even and one strip could touch the side of the cup.
- Effect on the case: it can show which paper towel it is, but if it is the lab's own towel, anyone in the lab could have used one, so it rules nobody in or out.

**Option: Ink 2**
- Question: "Which solvent separates our black inks best: water, salt water or vinegar? Does the note ink (N) still match pen B?"
- Samples: references: pens A, B, C and D (at least B and one other); scene sample: note ink strips N.
- Variables: change: the solvent. Observe or measure: number of colour bands; distance between the lowest and highest band (mm); whether N and B look the same. Keep the same: coffee-filter strips 2 cm by 10 cm; pencil baseline 2 cm from the bottom; small dot, dotted 3 times; solvent 1 cm deep; 7 minutes; same cups.
- Method: 1 Make salt water: 1 level teaspoon of salt in 100 ml of water, stir until dissolved. 2 Pour 1 cm of water, salt water or vinegar into labelled cups. 3 Draw pencil baselines and labels on strips. 4 Dot pen B (and pen A) on their strips; N strips are ready made. 5 Hang N and B side by side in each solvent, not touching. 6 Wait 7 minutes; remove; mark the solvent front. 7 Count bands and measure the spread. 8 The second pair repeats the whole set.
- Risks: vinegar could splash in eyes and sting: goggles, small amounts, do not move cups once filled; salt water and vinegar must not be tasted: never put anything in your mouth; ink stains: caps on.
- Results table headings: Solvent | Strip | Number of bands | Colours, bottom to top | Spread of bands (mm) | Same pattern as N? (Y / N)
- Repeats: two full sets (one per pair).
- Effect on the case: if N matches B in all three solvents, the ink match is more reliable, but it still matches two suspects (Max Zhang and Dr Huang).

**Option: Powder 2**
- Question: "Can we identify a mixture of two powders, and is the scene powder (Evidence 3) pure baking soda or a mixture?"
- Samples: references: pure baking soda; known mixture K1 (baking soda + cornflour); known mixture K2 (salt + flour); our Week 4 results grid. Unknowns: Mystery mixture X; scene powder (Evidence 3).
- Variables: change: which sample. Observe: magnifier (crystals or fine powder?), water (dissolves, cloudy, settles?), vinegar (fizz?), iodine (blue-black?). Keep the same: a pea-sized spoonful; one clean spoon per sample; a third of a cup of water; 5 drops of vinegar; 1 drop of iodine; same tile.
- Method: 1 Put a pinch of each sample on black card and look with the magnifier. 2 Add a pea-sized spoonful of each to a cup of water and stir 10 times. 3 Put a pea-sized spoonful in a tile well and add 5 drops of vinegar; watch for 10 seconds. 4 Put a pea-sized spoonful in another well and add 1 drop of iodine (Safety Officer only). 5 Record each result straight away. 6 The second pair repeats every test.
- Risks: vinegar and iodine could splash in eyes: goggles; iodine stains: one drop, dropper only, Safety Officer only; food allergy (wheat, corn): allergic students do not handle flour or cornflour; no tasting.
- Results table headings: Sample | Magnifier | Water | Vinegar | Iodine | What we think it contains
- Effect on the case: if the scene powder gives exactly the same results as pure baking soda (and none of the signs of a second powder), the Week 4 result is confirmed. It still matches two suspects (Chef Zhou and Dr Huang).

### Marking guidance

- **Core a)** Secure: names the scene sample and the references, and could be answered by testing. Developing: "What is the soil?" (no comparison).
- **Core c)** Most groups' independent variable is "which sample we test" (for Ink 2 it is "the solvent"). Accept "type of soil / paper / powder". Do not accept "the soil" alone for the dependent variable: ask "What will you look at or measure?"
- **Core d)** Secure: amounts and times stated, steps in order, scene sample treated the same way, repeats included.
- **Core e)** Each hazard has a matching control. "Be careful" alone is not a control.
- **Core f)** Units where there are measurements (mm, s, ml); one row per sample, scene sample included.
- **Stretch a)** Example: "We must keep the volume of water the same because more water would spread the soil out and make the layers thinner, so it would not be fair."
- **Stretch b)** Example: "Two or three times, because one result might be a mistake; if the repeats agree we can trust the result more."
- **Challenge a)** Excellent answers state a result that would show the prediction is wrong.
- **Challenge b)** Excellent answers realise that Soil and Paper cannot rule anyone in or out (everyone used the flowerbed path and the lab towels), and that Ink 2 and Powder 2 can only make existing results more reliable.
- **My thinking:** The scene sample on its own means nothing; it only becomes evidence when compared with known samples tested in the same way.

**Exit ticket:** answers matching the group's option, as in the model plans.
