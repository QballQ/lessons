# Language access policy | 语言支持政策

Think Like a Scientist | Years 7 and 8 (bilingual domestic stream) | Version 1.0, October 2026
Chinese text status: draft, needs native-speaker check

---

## 0. Why this policy exists

Feedback from the Year 7 and Year 8 classes: students could not tell what a lesson was for or what a task was asking, because learning goals, instructions and questions were in English only. The classes range from nearly no English to conversational English. The original rule ("key words bilingual, instructions in English with key words glossed, no whole-paragraph translation") set the bar too high for the bottom of that range and gave no way in for the learning goals.

This policy replaces that rule for Years 7 and 8. Year 9 is unchanged for now. The ten-week plan, the 40-minute lesson shape, the tiers and the assessment are unchanged. What changes is the language in which the frame of every lesson is presented.

## 1. Five principles

1. **Understand first, in whichever language.** A student must be able to find out what the lesson is for, what to do, what to answer and how to stay safe without reading English. These things are fully bilingual.
2. **Translate the frame, not the content.** The science content itself (explanations on Learn slides, reading texts such as witness statements) stays in English and gets a one-line Chinese key idea or gist, not a sentence-by-sentence translation. Students still read and hear English science every lesson.
3. **Capable students are not slowed down.** English stays first and larger; Chinese sits beneath in a lighter weight and can be ignored. Stretch and Challenge stay English-rich, with a Chinese gloss on the question only.
4. **Answers in any language count.** We judge the science, not the English. Key science words in English are taught, modelled and praised, never demanded as a condition of answering.
5. **One Chinese term per English term.** Every key word uses the Chinese in `00_Language_Support/Glossary_Y7_Y8.md`, in every file, so students meet the same pairing all series.

## 2. Student language bands (for planning, never for labelling)

| Band | What the student can do in English | What they need from the materials |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A few words; cannot follow a spoken instruction or read a sentence | Chinese for every goal, instruction and question; pictures; bilingual word bank; permission to answer in Chinese; a language buddy |
| 2 | Short sentences with support; reads simple English slowly | Chinese beneath English to confirm meaning; sentence frames with a Chinese gloss; word bank |
| 3 | Conversational; reads the English first | English primary; Chinese as a check only; Stretch and Challenge in English with frames |

Record the band (1, 2 or 3) in the tracker, column G "Language support", after Weeks 1 to 2. Bands are for planning seating, buddies and which tier a student starts on. They are not told to students and are not ability groups. A student in Band 1 can reach Challenge and Excellent 优秀 by thinking in Chinese and using English key words.

## 3. The translation matrix

"Full" means the complete English text is followed by its complete Chinese. "Gist" means one short Chinese line (about 25 characters at most) that gives the meaning. "English" means no Chinese is added.

| Type of text | Rule | Example | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Learning goals ("I can" statements) | Full | I can name the independent variable. 我能说出自变量。 | Lesson plan; new Today slide; worksheet header; exit ticket self-check |
| B. Task instructions (anything that tells a student what to do) | Full, every sentence | Draw a line from each picture to the right box. 把每张图片和正确的框连线。 | Do Now, Do and Think slides; every worksheet task; every student printable; clean-up lines |
| C. Questions students answer | Full stem, including sub-questions and short scenario text in the stem | Which test is not fair? Why? 哪个测试不公平？为什么？ | Exit tickets; Think prompts; Core, Stretch and Challenge stems; My thinking question; Do Now question |
| D. Sentence frames | English frame, then the frame in Chinese with the same gaps | We must keep ___ the same because ___. 我们必须保持___不变，因为___。 | Everywhere a frame appears |
| E. Headings, labels, table headings, field labels, tier names, button-like words | Full | Fall time (s) 下落时间（秒） | Worksheets, printables, slides |
| F. Word banks (inline and fold-under) | Full, every item | length 长度 ǀ independent 自变量 | Every worksheet |
| G. Safety lines | Full (unchanged) | | Safety slide, kit card |
| H. Learn content: explanations, definitions, facts | English (30 words or fewer) plus one Chinese Key idea 核心概念 line | Key idea 核心概念：公平测试只改变一个量。 | Learn slides; Hook slides that state an idea |
| I. Reading texts: story, witness statements, briefings, lab reports, scenario cards | English text kept; one Chinese gist line per paragraph or statement. Exception: the Year 8 Week 1 case story ("What happened") is Full, because the whole series depends on understanding it | 概要：她说自己7点到7点20分在准备火山实验。 | Printables, case file, puzzle cards |
| J. Student answers | Core: Chinese or English, using the English key words from the word bank. Stretch and Challenge: English with frames encouraged; reasoning may be in Chinese | | Worksheet template footers (fixed text, not repeated in each file) |
| K. Teacher-only text: lesson plan body, speaker notes, answer keys, kit lists, prep, notes for the incoming teacher, mark schemes | English | | Sections 1, 4, 5 (except hazards and clean-up) and 7 |
| L. Numbers, units, data, names, letters (S, M, L, A to D), symbols | No translation | 2 m, 4 cm, Lin, Kai | Everywhere |

What does **not** need translation, and why: speaker notes (the teacher speaks), answer keys, kit lists and prep (teacher and technician), the body of Learn explanations beyond the key idea (students hear the teacher explain, see the picture and have the vocabulary panel), reading texts beyond the gist (reading English is part of the Year 8 detective work; the gist lets Band 1 students take part), Stretch and Challenge reasoning (the question is glossed; the thinking is the student's), vocabulary panels (already bilingual).

## 4. Rules for slides (Section 2 of every lesson file)

1. **Today slide.** Every lesson gains one slide, placed immediately after the last Do Now slide and before the first Hook slide. It carries the phase tag Do Now 课前任务 and the layout name "Today 今天". Content: "Today I can ... 今天我能……", the lesson's two learning goals (full bilingual), and "Key words today 今天的关键词" listing the lesson's key words EN 中文. Speaker notes: read each goal in English, a student reads the Chinese; point to the key words; one minute, inside the Do Now's 4 minutes. In Weeks 1 and 5, where the Do Now is an assessment, the Today slide is shown while the papers are collected and takes its minute from that transition, never from the assessment time. All later slides are renumbered, and every "Slide n" reference in the file (timeline, worksheet, answer key, printables, notes) is updated.
2. **Paired lines.** On Do Now, Hook, Do and Think and Reflect slides, every sentence of on-slide text that instructs or asks is written as a pair: English sentence, one space, Chinese sentence, one pair per line. Write "On-slide text:" on its own line, then the paired lines beneath it.
3. **Key idea line.** On Learn slides, and on Hook slides that state an idea, the English stays as it is (30 words or fewer) and one line is added: "Key idea 核心概念: [Chinese only]". Do not translate the English sentence by sentence.
4. **Frames** on slides follow rule D.
5. **Vocabulary panel, titles, phase tags and safety slides** are unchanged (already bilingual).
6. **Speaker notes** stay in English. Where the teacher should have a student read the Chinese aloud, say so.
7. Keep the English at 30 words or fewer per slide. Chinese does not count, but do not add more English to make room for it.

## 5. Rules for worksheets (Section 3)

1. **Header** gains a line "Today I can 今天我能:" followed by the two goals, bilingual.
2. **Every task instruction and every question stem** is paired (English, then Chinese) on the same line, including sub-questions (a, b, c), scenario sentences inside a question, and "circle one" type instructions.
3. **Table headings, column labels and field labels** are bilingual, with units kept as symbols: "Fall time (s) 下落时间（秒）".
4. **Word banks** (inline and fold-under) list every item EN 中文.
5. **Frames** follow rule D.
6. **My thinking** question is paired. Its label is "My thinking 我的想法 (any language 可用中文)".
7. **Exit ticket** questions are paired, and the strip gains one self-check line: "Today I can 今天我能: Goal 1 目标1 ☐  Goal 2 目标2 ☐".
8. **Core must be doable by a Band 1 student** using the Chinese instruction, the pictures and the bilingual word bank. If a Core task depends on reading an English paragraph, add a gist line to that paragraph.
9. **Stretch and Challenge** stems are paired; the expected answer length and the lines stay as they are.
10. Fixed footers under tier headings are supplied by the worksheet template, not written into each file: Core: "Answer in English or Chinese. Use the English key words. 可用英语或中文作答，请使用英语关键词。" Stretch and Challenge: "Try English with the frames. Reasons may be in Chinese. 尽量用句型框架写英语，理由可以用中文。"

## 6. Rules for kit cards and other printables (Sections 5 and 6)

1. Kit card: hazards already bilingual; **clean-up lines** become bilingual (students do the clean-up). Item lists and teacher prep stay English.
2. Student-facing printables: **instructions, prompts, labels, headings and questions** are paired (rule B, C, E). Role cards and logbook pages are fully bilingual.
3. **Reading texts** (statements, briefings, reports, puzzle prompts that tell a story) follow rule I: English kept, one gist line per paragraph or statement, written as "概要：……" in the lighter weight.
4. Teacher-only cards (reveal cards, notes printed "not for students") stay English.

## 7. Rules for assessment

1. **Skills checks (Weeks 1 and 10).** The questions are not changed. The same printed paper must be used in Week 10 as in Week 1; if Week 1 has already been sat, do not reprint. Access is given by the teacher reading the English and the Chinese gloss aloud, by a reader where recorded in the tracker, and by accepting Chinese for every written answer.
2. **Checkpoints (Week 5).** Every stem, sub-stem and scenario sentence is paired. Instruction words are paired. Chinese accepted for every written answer.
3. **Mark schemes** stay English (teacher only), with the rule "mark the science, not the English" kept.
4. **Interest survey, rubric footer, certificates, skills report, self-assessment and peer feedback slips** are already bilingual and unchanged.

## 9. Teacher routines that make the translation work

1. **Say, show, check.** Read the English line. A student reads the Chinese line. Thumbs up, sideways or down: "Do you know what to do?" Start the task only when most thumbs are up; go to the sideways and down thumbs first.
2. **Language buddies.** Pair Band 1 with Band 3 for pair talk and group work. The buddy may explain the task in Chinese; the buddy does not write the answer. Rotate the buddy every three weeks or so.
3. **Rehearse before reporting.** Think in Chinese, say it to a partner with the English frame, then say it to the class. The frame card is on the table.
4. **Praise the English key word, accept the Chinese sentence.** "Good: 'independent variable'. Now say it with the frame."
5. **Translation app or Chinese-speaking colleague.** For a Band 1 student stuck on a Stretch or Challenge question, a translation app on the teacher's phone or a Chinese-speaking colleague is a fair access tool. Note it in the tracker notes if it was used in an assessment.
6. **Point to the dictionary.** When a key word comes up, point to its page in My Science Dictionary. Students write their own example in the dictionary in the last minute of Think and Reflect when there is time.

## 10. Writing conventions for anyone editing the files

- Pair format: English text, one space, Chinese text, on the same line. On slides, one pair per line under "On-slide text:".
- Chinese uses full-width punctuation: 。？！：、（）. Gaps in frames are "___" in both languages.
- Chinese sits beneath the English in a lighter weight in the designed output; in the content files it simply follows the English.
- Do not translate names, numbers, units, letters or symbols. Do not translate the English key word inside a Chinese sentence: write 自变量, not "independent variable 自变量" again, unless the line is a word bank item.
- Names: students' names in scenarios and assessment questions (Lin, Kai, Mia, Leo, Ruby and so on) stay in Latin letters inside Chinese lines. The Year 8 story characters and places are the one exception: they keep the Chinese forms printed on the suspect cards and locked in the glossary (弗罗斯特教授, 陈莉, 张铭轩, 周师傅, 黄若琳, 雪原学校, 3号实验室).
- Use the glossary. If a term is not in the glossary, add it to the "Added during the language pass" list at the end of the glossary with the file where it was first used.
- No em dashes, no en dashes anywhere. British English.
- Keep "Chinese text status: draft, needs native-speaker check" in every file header.
- Do not change the science, the data, the story facts, the timings, the tiers or the structure of the seven sections. Headings that contain "[Paste into Claude Design]" must keep that exact phrase.

## 11. Native-speaker check: priority order

The volume of Chinese has roughly doubled, so the check should run in this order so that the most used text is checked first: (1) Today slides and learning goals, (2) instructions on slides and worksheets, (3) question stems and exit tickets, (4) word banks and the dictionary, (5) frames, (6) key idea and gist lines, (7) printables. Any term the checker changes must be changed in the glossary too, then found and replaced in every file.
