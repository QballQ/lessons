# Reading books and materials for the assessment table

The Early Reading Screen has its own graded passages, so the assessment can run with no books at all. A small set of **real levelled books** is still worth having: some children read more confidently from a real book; the assessor can check a result with an unseen text; and the same books tell the homeroom teacher exactly where to start the child's reading. This list says which books to buy, which titles to use for a running record at each level, and what to use with older students who read at a low level.

**Checked October 2026** on Oxford University Press (Oxford Owl, OUP China) and UK school supplier pages. Pack contents and ISBNs change: check the ISBN with the supplier before ordering. ISBNs marked * were seen only in retailer search listings.

**Oxford's end-of-year expectations** for fluent English readers (a guide only): end of Reception Level 3; end of Year 1 Level 6; end of Year 2 Level 10; end of Year 3 Level 13; end of Year 4 Level 15. So a fluent English reader **starting** Year 1 reads at about Yellow, starting Year 2 at about Orange, and starting Year 3 at about White. Most children joining from Chinese schools read below this at entry. That is expected, and is what the English band (A to E) takes into account.

**TreeTops** (Oxford Reading Tree for juniors) starts at Level 8, with a band of its own (Brown at Levels 8 to 11, Grey from 12). The books look older while the text stays easy, which suits students in Year 3 and above who read at a low level.

**Stories with familiar settings.** Some titles have Chinese or East Asian settings, which can help a child talk about the story: *The Dragon Dance* (Level 4 More Stories B), *The Magic Paintbrush* (Level 5 Traditional Tales), *The New Year Race* (Level 6 Decode and Develop), *Holiday in Japan* (Level 7 Decode and Develop), *Mulan* (Level 9 Traditional Tales). Familiar content makes comprehension easier, so use them for talk rather than for a first running record.

**Do not use** these titles, which are in old packs but no longer in the current ones (some were withdrawn by the publisher): *The Hole in the Sand* (Level 2); *Chinese Adventure*, *The Jigsaw Puzzle*, *Australian Adventure* (Level 7); *The Flying Carpet*, *The Kidnappers*, *Egyptian Adventure* (Level 8); *Survival Adventure*, *Rescue!*, *The Blue Eye* (Level 9). Buy the current Level 7 to 9 packs listed above, not older six-book packs.

**Old stock.** Books labelled "Stage" (rather than "Level"), or with the old names Owls, Magpies or Wrens, come from before 2011. Stages 1 to 9 in the 2011 edition match Levels 1 to 9, but older series names do not. Home editions ("Read with Oxford", Stages 1 to 6) use a different numbering altogether. Level each book from Oxford's reading record list, not from the cover: Oxford Owl, "Pupil reading record: Oxford Levels 1 to 20" (oxfordowl.co.uk/contents/57667).

## 5. Free and online options

- **Oxford Owl eBook library** (home.oxfordowl.co.uk/books/free-ebooks): free levelled eBooks for ages 3 to 11, including Oxford Reading Tree, Read Write Inc. and Songbirds, searchable by Oxford Level and book band. Free registration is needed. Best on a computer or tablet. Useful for families to read at home at the level the assessment found.
- **Oxford Owl for School**: free teacher registration gives extra eBooks (30 Oxford Primary titles and 50 Read Write Inc. titles) through class logins, plus teaching notes for every pack, and the reading record list of every Oxford Reading Tree title by level.
- Check that these sites load reliably from the school network in China before relying on them on the day.

## 6. Buying in China

- **Imported Oxford Reading Tree** "校园版" (school edition) sets are sold on JD.com, including some sold by JD itself, and labelled 牛津大学出版社. Check that what arrives matches the pack contents above.
- **Chinese editions**: FLTRP (外研社, the 丽声 brand) publishes phonics and non-fiction series based on Oxford Reading Tree, and The Commercial Press (商务印书馆) publishes 牛津阅读树进阶读物 and TreeTops (树冠英语) sets. These are useful for classrooms but are not the same books as the UK packs, so level them carefully.
- **Familiar books.** Oxford Reading Tree is very popular in China, so children from bilingual schools may already know the Biff, Chip and Kipper stories well. This is another reason to run assessments with Decode and Develop, inFact or Collins Big Cat.
- The OUP China (Hong Kong) online shop delivers only to Hong Kong addresses. UK school suppliers (for example Madeleine Lindley) and international school suppliers ship to schools abroad; order by ISBN.

## 7. Running a running record with a real book

1. Choose a book at the level the child reached on the Early Reading Screen passages (or one level below to start).
2. Mark a section of about 100 words (Levels 1+ to 4: the whole book, often 30 to 80 words). Count the words.
3. Use the running record codes and the accuracy table on the Early Reading Screen record: 95% or more accuracy is easy (independent), 90 to 94% is right for teaching (instructional), below 90% is too hard.
4. Ask three or four questions: one "find it in the book", one "why do you think", one word meaning, and one "what might happen next" or "what did you think of it".
5. Record the title, level, accuracy and comprehension on the Early Reading Screen record.
