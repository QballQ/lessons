# Interviewer guide: Years 1 and 2

**This guide is reusable.** Nothing is written on it: keep one in the room, in a folder or laminated. Ratings and notes for each child go on the **Interview sheet** (`forms/Interview_Sheet_Y1_Y2`).

**About 6 minutes, 1:1, play-based.** Most of what you need about a 5 or 6 year old comes from watching them during the whole visit, not from questions. This short chat, at the end of the visit, is **in English**. It has three jobs: to hear the child's spoken English, to get to know the child (likes, family, friends, **what they want to be**), and to judge their readiness for the international programme. Use the list in Section 3 for the whole visit, and tick it on the Interview sheet. The sentence reading is not part of this chat: in Years 1 to 3 the assessor runs the Graded Sentence Reading Screen (`shared/Sentence_Reading_Guide`) after the Early Reading Screen and writes the points on the Reading front sheet.

**How to do it.** Sit on the floor or at a low table beside the child. Offer paper and crayons: "Can you draw you, doing something you love?" Talk about the drawing. Ask the main question first; if the child does not answer, use the **easier** prompt (a choice, a yes or no, or "show me"). Wait. Praise effort. Never press a child who is upset; stop and let them go back to their parent.

**Pace of speech.** Speak slowly and clearly, about 120 words a minute: short phrases, with a pause after each. Use the same pace for every candidate in the year group. Do not rush: wait at least five seconds before rephrasing.

**Safeguarding.** A young child is never alone with one adult behind a closed door. If a child says something that worries you, write down their words and pass it to the Designated Safeguarding Lead the same day.

## 1. Questions

On the Interview sheet, write short notes under "Key things the student said" (their own words, and how they said it), and a line about the drawing.

| Topic | Main question | Easier |
|---|---|---|
| **Me** | What is your name? How old are you? | Show me with your fingers. |
| | Tell me about your picture. | Is this you? What are you doing? |
| **Family** | Who lives at your home? | Do you have a brother? A sister? A pet? |
| **Likes** | What do you like to play? | Do you like to run? Draw? Play with blocks? |
| | What is your favourite food? Favourite animal? | Do you like noodles? Do you like cats? |
| **School** | Tell me about your school (or kindergarten). | Do you like it? What do you do there? |
| | Who are your friends? | Do you play with friends? |
| **Feelings** | What makes you happy? | Are you happy today? (point to a happy or sad face) |
| **When I grow up** | What do you want to be when you grow up? | A teacher? A doctor? A footballer? |
| **Here** | Would you like to come to this school? What would you like to do here? | Do you want to play here? Learn English here? |

## 2. Speaking band

Choose the band that fits best, for a child of this age, and tick it on the Interview sheet. Rate **listening in conversation** with the same letters.

| Band | What you hear |
|---|---|
| **A** New to English | No English yet, or one or two words. Uses Chinese, pointing or silence. May copy a word you say. |
| **B** Early acquisition | Single words and short phrases ("cat", "my mummy", "I like red"). Understands simple questions with gesture or pictures. |
| **C** Developing competence | Short sentences about familiar things ("I play with my friend"), with errors. Understands most simple questions. |
| **D** Competent | Talks in sentences, tells you about the picture or a simple event, joins ideas with "and" or "because". |
| **E** Fluent | Talks as a fluent English-speaking child of 5 or 6 would. |

## 3. What to look for during the whole visit

Tick what you saw on the Interview sheet, and add a note.

- Separates from the parent without great distress, or settles within a few minutes
- Joins in the activities and stays with each one for a few minutes
- Follows a simple instruction (with gesture if needed)
- Holds a pencil or crayon with control; draws a person with several parts
- Takes turns, shares, waits
- Shows curiosity: looks, touches, asks, explores
- Copies new English words, or tries to
- Recovers when something is hard or when they do not understand
- Uses the toilet, coat and shoes independently (ask the parent)

## 4. Readiness

Rate each strand on the Interview sheet: **1** Concern, **2** Some concern, **3** Good, **4** Strong. Write the evidence: a short quotation or what you saw.

| Strand | What strong looks like at this age |
|---|---|
| Attitude to learning | Enjoys the activities, has a go, pleased with success |
| Curiosity and engagement | Interested, explores, asks questions |
| Social and emotional readiness | Settles with new adults, manages separation, plays alongside or with others |
| Independence | Manages simple tasks and self-care for age |
| Kindness and conduct | Gentle, polite, shares |
| Readiness for learning in English | Happy in an English-speaking setting, tries words, is not distressed by not understanding |

## 5. Anything to follow up

Tick any that apply on the Interview sheet, and add a note:

- Speech and language (for example very little speech in Chinese as well as English, unclear speech for age)
- Attention, hearing, vision, movement or fine motor skills
- Wellbeing (for example very anxious, very tired)
- Safeguarding (passed to the Designated Safeguarding Lead the same day)
- Medical or dietary

**Speech in Chinese.** Ask the parent how the child speaks in Chinese (Parent Conversation Record, Section 4). A child with little English but age-appropriate Chinese is a typical English learner. A child who also has very little speech in Chinese for their age may need a speech and language check: raise it with the parent gently, as a question rather than a conclusion.

**Receiving homeroom teacher (optional).** If the likely homeroom teacher meets the child, they note first impressions and what the child would need in the first weeks (for example a buddy, picture cards for routines, a familiar comfort object, a gradual start) on the Interview sheet.

**To finish.** On the Interview sheet, write your comment for the final mark sheet (strengths, concerns, support), and sign and date it.
