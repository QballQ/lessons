# Student interview and suitability record: Years 5 and 6

[[namebox:Candidate name,Year group applied for,Date,Interviewer,Others present (for example receiving homeroom teacher; Chinese-speaking colleague)]]

**About 12 minutes, 1:1.** The interview is held **in English**. It has three jobs: to hear the student's spoken English (the speaking band), to get to know the student (interests, learning, wellbeing, **future plans and aspirations**), and to judge their **suitability** for the international programme. It opens with the Graded Sentence Reading Screen (start Level 4 for Year 5, Level 5 for Year 6; record on `shared/Sentence_Reading_Record.md`).

**How to ask.** Smile, sit beside rather than opposite the child if you can, and start with something easy. Ask the main question first. If the child struggles, use the **easier** prompt (a yes or no, or a choice). If they answer easily, use the **stretch** prompt. Wait at least five seconds before rephrasing. Answers must be in English.

**Safeguarding.** Keep the door open or another adult present. If a child tells you something that worries you about their safety or wellbeing, do not promise to keep it secret; listen, write down their words, and pass it to the Designated Safeguarding Lead the same day.

## 3. Speaking band

Circle the band that fits best, for a child of this age.

| Band | What you hear |
|---|---|
| **A** New to English | Little or no spoken English, or single words and memorised phrases. Relies on Chinese, gesture or silence. Understands very little unless supported with pictures or gesture. |
| **B** Early acquisition | Short answers and simple memorised sentences about familiar things (family, school, likes). Frequent errors. Needs the easier prompts. Understands slow, simple questions. |
| **C** Developing competence | Answers most main questions with short connected sentences ("because", "and then", "but"). Errors are frequent but meaning is usually clear. Everyday vocabulary. |
| **D** Competent | Talks easily and at some length, explains and gives examples, manages most stretch prompts. Occasional errors that rarely affect meaning. |
| **E** Fluent | Speaks as fluently and accurately as a fluent English speaker of the same age. |
