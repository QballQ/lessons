# Interviewer guide: Years 3 and 4

**This guide is reusable.** Nothing is written on it: keep one in the room, in a folder or laminated. Ratings and notes for each student go on the **Interview sheet** (`forms/Interview_Sheet_Y3_Y4`).

**About 8 minutes in Year 3 and 12 minutes in Year 4, 1:1.** The interview is held **in English**, as a friendly chat. It has three jobs: to hear the child's spoken English, to get to know the child (likes, school, friends, **hopes for the future**), and to judge their **suitability** for the international programme. In Year 3 the sentence reading is done by the assessor, after the Early Reading Screen; in Year 4 the interview opens with the Graded Sentence Reading Screen (3 to 4 minutes; start Level 3; follow `shared/Sentence_Reading_Guide` and write the points on the Reading front sheet, `forms/Reading_Front_Sheet`).

**How to ask.** Sit beside the child. Have paper and crayons ready: some children talk more easily while drawing ("Draw something you love doing, and tell me about it"). Ask the main question first. If the child struggles, use the **easier** prompt (a yes or no, a choice, or pointing). If they answer easily, use the **stretch** prompt. Wait at least five seconds before rephrasing. Answers must be in English.

**Pace of speech.** Speak slowly and clearly: about 120 words a minute in Year 3 and about 130 in Year 4. Use the same pace for every candidate in the year group, and in Year 4 use it for the instructions and meaning questions of the sentence reading too. Do not rush: wait at least five seconds before rephrasing.

**Safeguarding.** Keep the door open or another adult present. If a child tells you something that worries you, do not promise to keep it secret; listen, write down their words, and pass it to the Designated Safeguarding Lead the same day.

## 1. Questions

Choose about two questions from each block. On the Interview sheet, write short notes under "Key things the student said": what they said, and how they said it.

| Block | Main question | Easier | Stretch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Warm-up** | Tell me about you. | What is your name? How old are you? | What makes you special? |
| | Who is in your family? | Do you have a brother or sister? A pet? | What do you like doing with your family? |
| **School** | Tell me about your school. | Do you like school? | What is the best thing about it? |
| | What is your favourite thing to learn? Why? | Do you like maths? Reading? Art? | What did you learn last week? |
| | What is hard for you at school? What do you do? | Is anything hard? | Who helps you? |
| | Do you read at home? Tell me about a book you like. | Do you like stories? | What happened in it? |
| **Things I like** | What do you like to do after school? | Do you like to play outside? Draw? Sport? | What are you best at? |
| | Do you have classes after school or at the weekend? | Do you go to classes on Saturday? | Do you like them? |
| **Friends and feelings** | Tell me about your friends. | Do you have a best friend? What is their name? | What do you play together? |
| | What makes you happy? What makes you sad? | Are you happy today? | What do you do when you are sad? |
| | How do you feel about a new school? | Happy, nervous, or both? | What would help you? |
| **When I grow up** | What do you want to be when you grow up? | Do you want to be a teacher? A doctor? Something else? | Why? |
| | What would you like to learn at our school? | Do you want to learn English? Sport? Music? | |
| **Kindness** | A child in your class is crying. What do you do? | Do you help them? | How can you help? |
| **Questions for us** | Do you want to ask me anything? | | |

## 2. Speaking band

Choose the band that fits best, for a child of this age, and tick it on the Interview sheet. Rate **listening in conversation** with the same letters. Pronunciation features of a speaker of Chinese do not lower the band if the child is easy to understand.

| Band | What you hear |
|---|---|
| **A** New to English | No English yet, or a few single words ("yes", "cat"). Uses Chinese, pointing or silence. Understands very little without pictures or gesture. |
| **B** Early acquisition | Single words, short phrases and some simple sentences ("I like football"). Needs the easier prompts. Understands slow, simple questions with support. |
| **C** Developing competence | Answers in short sentences and can say a little more with prompting ("I like football because it is fun"). Errors are common; meaning is usually clear. |
| **D** Competent | Talks easily, joins ideas, tells a little story or explains, with occasional errors. Understands nearly everything said at a normal pace. |
| **E** Fluent | Speaks as a fluent English-speaking child of the same age would. |

## 3. Suitability

Rate each strand on the Interview sheet: **1** Concern, **2** Some concern, **3** Good, **4** Strong. Write the evidence: a short quotation or what you saw.

| Strand | What strong looks like at this age |
|---|---|
| Attitude to learning | Has a go, enjoys success, tries again after a mistake |
| Curiosity and engagement | Interested in the activities and pictures, asks questions |
| Social and emotional readiness | Separates from the parent and settles with a new adult, talks about friends, manages nerves |
| Independence | Follows two-step instructions, manages pencils and paper, can work alone for a few minutes |
| Kindness and conduct | Polite, shares, gives a kind answer to the kindness question |
| Readiness for learning in English | Happy to try English words, copies new words, not upset by not understanding |

**Boarding readiness** (only if the child will board): independence with daily routines, sleep, homesickness. Rate it on the Interview sheet in the same way.

## 4. Anything to follow up

Tick any that apply on the Interview sheet, and add a note:

- Possible special educational need or disability (for example speech and language, attention, hearing, vision, fine motor skills, pencil grip)
- Wellbeing (for example very anxious, tired, very heavy after-school workload)
- Safeguarding (passed to the Designated Safeguarding Lead the same day)
- Medical or dietary

**Receiving homeroom teacher (optional).** If the likely homeroom teacher meets the child, they note first impressions and what the child would need in the first weeks (for example a buddy, picture cards for routines, a quiet space) on the Interview sheet.

**To finish.** On the Interview sheet, write your comment for the final mark sheet (strengths, concerns, support), and sign and date it.
