# Year 4 Admission Assessment: assessor booklet

[[namebox:candidate]]

**Who this is for.** Students applying for Year 4 are usually 8 years old on 1 September. A student from a Chinese mainstream school has usually **completed Grade 2** (二年级): numbers to 10 000, adding and subtracting, times tables to 9 × 9 and division facts, cm and m, g and kg, time to the minute, right angles and simple tables of data. Fractions, perimeter, area, millimetres and multiplying by a one-digit number usually come in Grade 3. English as a school subject often starts in Grade 3, so many students have only had English classes outside school; students from bilingual schools may be close to fluent.

**How it works.** About 57 minutes of student time, plus a welcome and a short break. The student writes in the **Student paper** (20 pages). Listening, reading, writing and maths can be taken in a small group with one invigilator (the listening is read to the group); the interview with sentence reading is always 1:1. Mark everything the same day and copy the score summary (Section 5) onto the Candidate Summary and Decision (`forms/Candidate_Summary_and_Decision.md`).

## 2. Before you start

**The room.** Quiet and bright, with space between desks. Each student has a pencil, a rubber and a ruler. Keep the door open or another adult present.

**Say:** "Hello, everyone. My name is [your name]. I am a teacher here. Today you will do some listening, reading, writing and maths, and then you will talk with a teacher. These activities help us get to know you. Some questions are easy and some are hard. That is fine. Do your best. It is fine if you cannot answer every question."

Help each student write their name and the date on page 1. Point to the "How to answer" box and say: "This shows you how to answer. Tick a box like this. Write in a box like this. Show your working like this."

**Access rules** (the same in every year group)

| | English sections: listening, reading, writing, sentence reading | Maths |
|---|---|---|
| Reading aloud | Say only what the script says. Do not explain words, translate, or read the reading texts or questions for the student. You may repeat an instruction in English. | Read any question aloud in English, as often as the student asks, and point to the picture. |
| Chinese | Not used. Note what the student could say in Chinese, because it shows understanding. | If a Chinese-speaking colleague is present and the student cannot follow the English after it has been read aloud, the colleague may say the question once in Chinese. Write **L** by the question. The mark still counts. |
| Answers in Chinese | Do not score. | Accepted in explanations; write **L**. |

**Codes.** **NT** not yet taught: the student says they have not learnt it; it scores 0 and is listed in the summary. **L** language support in maths. **C** an interview answer given in Chinese. Record extra time, a reader or any other access arrangement for a known need in the score summary.

**Stopping and keeping students at ease.** Any student may stop a section when they have done what they can: they turn over and wait quietly. Praise effort, not answers. If a student is upset, give them a break or stop, and record it. Never press a distressed child. Collect the paper at the end of each timed part only if a student is still working well past the time; otherwise let them finish the question they are on.

## 3. English

### 3.1 Listening (5 minutes, student paper pages 2 to 3)

**Say:** "Turn to page 2, Part 1: Listening. You are going to hear a phone call. Li Na is talking to her friend Amir. I will read it two times. First, look at the questions with me."

Read the eight questions aloud, once, without the answer choices: "Question 1. Where are they going on Saturday? Question 2. What time will they meet? Question 3. Where is the bus stop? Question 4. What must Amir bring? Tick two pictures. Question 5. What will Grandma make for lunch? Question 6. How old is Amir's brother? Question 7. Why does Amir's brother not need a ticket? Question 8. How does Amir feel about the trip? How do you know?"

**Say:** "Now listen. You can answer while you listen, and after."

**Read it twice at a natural, steady pace.** Change your voice a little for the two speakers, but do not act. Do not stress the answers. Leave about 20 seconds between the two readings, and about 1 minute after the second reading for students to finish.

[[keep]]
> **A phone call**
>
> **Li Na:** Hello, Amir. It's Li Na.
>
> **Amir:** Hi, Li Na!
>
> **Li Na:** Are you free on Saturday? My grandma is taking me to the zoo. Last time we went to the park, but this time we're going to the zoo. Can you come with us? [pause]
>
> **Amir:** Yes, please! I love the zoo. What time are you going?
>
> **Li Na:** Grandma wanted to go at eight o'clock, but that's very early. So we're going to meet at nine o'clock. [pause]
>
> **Amir:** Nine o'clock. OK. Where?
>
> **Li Na:** At the bus stop. Not the bus stop near our school. The bus stop next to the supermarket. [pause]
>
> **Amir:** OK, next to the supermarket. What do I need to bring?
>
> **Li Na:** Bring a hat, because it will be sunny. You don't need an umbrella. And bring some water. Don't bring any lunch. Grandma is making dumplings for everyone. [pause]
>
> **Amir:** Yum! I love dumplings. Can my little brother come too? He's five.
>
> **Li Na:** I'll ask Grandma. [pause] She says yes! Children under six don't need a ticket. [pause]
>
> **Amir:** Brilliant! I can't wait. See you on Saturday!
>
> **Li Na:** Bye, Amir!
[[/keep]]

**Answers** (1 mark each)

| Q | Answer | Also accept | Do not accept |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A (the zoo) | | B (the park: they went last time) |
| 2 | B (9 o'clock) | | A (8 o'clock: Grandma's first idea) |
| 3 | B (next to the supermarket) | | A (near school) |
| 4 | A and C (hat and water) | | Any other picture ticked, or only one ticked |
| 5 | dumplings | Any spelling that is clearly "dumplings": dumpling, damplins, jiaozi, jiaozis | noodles; an answer in Chinese characters |
| 6 | 5 | five, fiv | 6 |
| 7 | He is five / under six, and children under six do not need a ticket. | "He is under six." "He is five." "Because he is small and children under six are free." | "He is small" or "he is a child" with no age; "Grandma pays" |
| 8 | A positive feeling **and** a reason from the call: "He is excited because he says 'Brilliant' / 'I can't wait' / 'I love the zoo' / 'Yes, please'." | happy, glad, pleased, can't wait, excited; the reason may be in the student's own words ("he wants to go a lot") | A feeling with no reason; a negative feeling |
| | | | **Total / 8** |

**Band guide:** 0 to 2 **A**; 3 to 4 **B**; 5 to 6 **C**; 7 **D**; 8 **E**. &nbsp; Listening band: **A &nbsp; B &nbsp; C &nbsp; D &nbsp; E**

[[keep]]
**What to look for**

- Questions 1 to 3 each have a distractor that is also said (the park, eight o'clock, the school). Choosing the distractor means the student caught a familiar word but not "last time", "but" or "not": typical of band B.
- Questions 5 and 6 need one heard word. A clear but misspelt word still scores: it is a listening task.
- Questions 7 and 8 need the student to join two pieces of information or read Amir's feelings. Students who score these are following connected talk at a natural pace (bands D and E).
[[/keep]]

### 3.2 Reading paper (12 minutes, student paper pages 4 to 10)

**Say:** "Turn to page 4, Part 2: Reading. There are three texts: a menu, a story and a text about honeybees. Read each text and answer the questions. Do as much as you can. It is fine if you do not finish. You have 12 minutes." Do not read any text or question aloud. Tell the students when there are 2 minutes left.

The three parts get harder. **Part A** is a menu with pictures. **Part B** is a short story written about two years below age level. **Part C** is **non-fiction** (an information text about honeybees, with a labelled diagram) at the level of a fluent 8 year old reader, and it is meant to stretch.

**Band guide**

[[keep]]
**What to look for**

- **Strong readers** finish Part C, answer in their own words and give reasons ("because ...").
- **Decoding ahead of understanding:** a good sentence reading score but weak Part B and Part C answers, or answers copied from the wrong place in the text. Common for students who learnt phonics or the phonetic alphabet outside school.
- **An English barrier, not a reading difficulty:** correct answers that are hard to read ("so it not scard and sting") still score. Note answers the student could explain in Chinese to a colleague afterwards; they do not score, but they show understanding.
[[/keep]]

### 3.3 Writing (10 minutes, student paper pages 11 to 12)

**Say:** "Turn to page 11, Part 3: Writing. There are two tasks. Choose one task and tick the box. Task 1 is a story about a big egg. Task 2 is about your favourite day of the year. Use the plan box first if you want to. Then write on page 12. You have 10 minutes." You may read the two task boxes aloud once. Do not give words, spellings or ideas.

**The two tasks**

- **Task 1: The big egg** (story, with a picture). "Jun and Sofia find a very big egg in the park. Write a story. What is inside the egg? What happens next?"
- **Task 2: My favourite day of the year** (my favourite). "Write about your favourite day of the year. When is it? What do you do? Who is with you? Why do you like it?"

[[keep]]
**The four criteria.** Score each from 0 to 4; 1 and 3 fall between the descriptions.

| Criterion | 0 | 2 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Content and organisation | No English writing on the task, or only copied words. | A few connected sentences on the task, with a simple order (a beginning and an end, or a short list of things they do). | A complete piece that suits the task: a story with a beginning, an event or problem and an ending, or a description with several points and reasons. Ideas grouped in paragraphs or clear sections. |
| Vocabulary | No English words of the student's own. | Everyday words, often repeated (nice, good, big, go, play). | Varied, precise words for the age (cracked, peeped, wobbled, delicious, lanterns, excited) and some longer noun phrases ("a huge spotted egg"). |
| Grammar and sentences | No sentence. | Simple sentences with frequent errors in tense, plurals and "a" and "the"; ideas joined mostly with "and" and "then". | Mostly accurate sentences, a range of joining words (because, when, so, but, if, after), tense kept the same, some variety (a question, some speech). |
| Spelling and punctuation | Very few words can be read. | Common words mostly right; other words spelt as they sound; capital letters and full stops some of the time. | Most words right, including common Year 2 and Year 3 words; capital letters, full stops, question marks and commas in lists used correctly; perhaps speech marks or apostrophes. |

| Task chosen | Content and organisation | Vocabulary | Grammar and sentences | Spelling and punctuation | **Total** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 &nbsp; 2 | / 4 | / 4 | / 4 | / 4 | **/ 16** |
[[/keep]]

**Writing band (best fit).** Read the piece against the band descriptors in `00_Admissions_Guide.md`, Section 6, and choose the band that fits best, not the total alone. As a rough guide: about 0 to 3 **A**, 4 to 7 **B**, 8 to 10 **C**, 11 to 13 **D**, 14 to 16 **E**. &nbsp; Writing band: **A &nbsp; B &nbsp; C &nbsp; D &nbsp; E**

**What band E looks like at this age.** In 10 minutes, about 10 to 14 lines or more: a story with a clear beginning, middle and end, or an organised description with reasons; sentences joined with because, when, so and if; the tense kept the same; some well-chosen words and longer phrases; common words spelt correctly; capital letters, full stops and question marks used correctly, perhaps with speech marks. This is what a fluent English-speaking child at the end of UK Year 3 writes.

[[keep]]
**What to look for**

- Missing past tense and plural endings and missing "a" and "the" are normal for a learner of English. Judge how much the student can communicate and organise.
- A long, well-organised piece with many grammar errors (band C or D) shows a student who will make fast progress with support; a short, accurate piece may show a cautious writer: compare with the interview.
- Task 2 is easier to write from memory; Task 1 needs imagination. Note which task was chosen.
[[/keep]]

### 3.4 Interview with sentence reading (12 minutes, 1:1)

Use `forms/Interview_Record_Y3_Y4.md`. The interview opens with the **Graded Sentence Reading Screen** (`shared/Sentence_Reading_Card.md` and `shared/Sentence_Reading_Record.md`): **start at Level 3** (sentence 7) and follow the start, going back and stopping rules on the record (about 3 to 4 minutes). Then hold the interview: choose about two questions from each block, and give the **speaking band** on the form. The interview also gives a "listening in conversation" band: compare it with the listening task. To finish, thank the student and tell them one thing they did well. Do not give any results.

## 4. Maths (18 minutes, student paper pages 13 to 20)

**Say:** "Turn to page 13, Part 4: Maths. Section A is number problems. Section B is word problems and problems with pictures. I can read any question to you. Just put your hand up. You have 18 minutes. Do not use a calculator."

- **Equipment:** pencil, rubber, ruler. No calculator.
- **Reading aloud:** read any question in English as often as the student asks, and point to the picture. The word problems are Questions 10, 12, 14b and 17. In a group, go to the student and read quietly. The Chinese reading is once only, by a colleague, after the English: write **L**.
- **Time:** about 6 minutes for Section A and 12 for Section B. Tell the students when there are 5 minutes left. If a student says they have not learnt something, write **NT**.
- Questions 7 and 16b are Year 4 content, there to find the ceiling. A Year 4 entrant is not expected to get them.

**Mark scheme.** One mark for each answer unless the table says otherwise. Type: **N** number problem, **W** word problem, **P** problem with a picture, chart or diagram.

**Band guide** (provisional, to be reviewed after the first 20 to 30 candidates): 0 to 9 **1** Well below expected; 10 to 14 **2** Working towards; 15 to 21 **3** Expected; 22 to 25 **4** Greater depth. List the NT questions. If they explain a low score, judge whether the student would reach band 3 once taught.

**Number problems and word problems.** The word problems use the same skills as number problems in Section A: Question 10 (3 × 8) with Question 3, Question 12 (92 − 35) with Questions 1 and 2, Question 17 with Questions 3 and 1. A student who does well on number problems but poorly on word problems may have an English barrier rather than a maths difficulty: check by reading the word problem aloud (and, if a Chinese-speaking colleague is present, once in Chinese, marked L).

[[keep]]
**What to look for**

- **Strong:** accurate written methods (column addition and subtraction, a written method for 23 × 4), working shown, sensible use of the diagrams, and answers checked against the picture.
- **A maths difficulty:** errors in Section A on taught content (Questions 1, 3 and 4) as well as in Section B; counting in ones on the grid and the bar chart.
- **An English barrier:** a strong Section A but blank or wrong word problems, or many requests to read a question; answers that come quickly once the question has been read aloud or said in Chinese.
[[/keep]]

**Likely not yet taught** for a student who has completed Chinese Grade 2: Questions 5 and 7 (multiplying by a one-digit number), 6 and 15 (fractions), 11 (millimetres), 14b (time intervals across the hour) and 16 (perimeter and area). Question 2 (column methods with exchanges) and Question 9 (bar charts) may also be new. Students from bilingual schools, or who have started Grade 3, may have met some of these.
