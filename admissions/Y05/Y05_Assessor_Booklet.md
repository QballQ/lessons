# Year 5 Admission Assessment: assessor booklet

**For staff only.** Use with the Year 5 student paper (`Y05/Y05_Student_Paper`), the Graded Sentence Reading Screen (`shared/Sentence_Reading_Card` and `shared/Sentence_Reading_Record`) and the interview record for Years 5 and 6 (`forms/Interview_Record_Y5_Y6`). The whole process, the English proficiency bands and the decision guide are in `00_Admissions_Guide.md`.

**Who this is for.** Students joining UK Year 5 (age 9 to 10 in September). A student from a Chinese mainstream school has usually **completed Grade 3** (三年级); a student joining mid-year is partway through Grade 4. The maths tests UK Year 4 content, with a few easier and a few harder items. The science tests UK Year 4 science and working scientifically.

**Print for each student:** the student paper (21 pages, A4, printed at actual size: switch off "fit to page", because the diagrams and the type sizes depend on it); the sentence reading record; the interview record; the score summary (Section 6 of this booklet). **For each room:** a laminated sentence reading card, a clock or timer, spare pencils. The written papers can be taken in a small group with one invigilator; listening is read to the group; the interview is always 1:1.

**Marks at a glance:** listening / 8, reading / 24, writing / 16, maths / 25 (number problems / 10, word problems and pictures / 15, of which word problems / 5), science / 10.

## 2. Before you start

### 2.1 Set up

- A quiet, bright room. The student sits where they cannot see other students' papers.
- Write the student's name on the paper if they cannot (in English, with the Chinese name in brackets if the family uses one).
- Have this booklet open at 3.1 for the listening script, and a timer ready.

### 2.2 Welcome (say this)

> "Hello, I am ______. Welcome to our school. Today you will do some activities: listening, reading, writing, maths and science. Then we will have a chat. The activities help us get to know you and plan how to help you. Some parts are easy and some parts are hard. That is fine. Do your best. If you do not understand what to do, you can ask me to say it again."

Show the cover page. Point to the "How to answer" examples and say: "Look. You tick a box, write in a box, circle, join with a line, and show your working like this."

### 2.3 Access rules (the same in every year group)

| | English parts (listening, reading, writing, sentence reading) | Maths and science |
|---|---|---|
| Reading aloud | Read only what the script says. Do not explain words, translate, or read the reading texts or reading questions aloud. The student may ask for instructions to be repeated in English. | Read any question aloud in English, as often as the student asks, and point to the picture. |
| Chinese | Not used. Note anything the student could say in Chinese, because it shows understanding. | If a Chinese-speaking colleague is present and the student clearly cannot follow the English after it has been read aloud, the colleague may say the question **once** in Chinese. Write **L** in the margin. The mark still counts. |
| Answers in Chinese | Do not score. Note them. | Accepted in explanations; write **L**. |
| Not yet taught | | If the student says they have not learnt a topic, write **NT** beside the question. It scores 0, and the summary lists the NT topics. |

Extra time or a reader for a known need is allowed: record it in the score summary. The number of **L** questions is evidence about English and goes in the summary.

### 2.4 Stopping rules

- In every part, a student may stop when they have done what they can. Encourage once ("Have a look at the next one; some are easier"), then accept their decision.
- **Listening:** always read the whole script twice, even if the student writes nothing.
- **Reading:** let the student work for the full 12 minutes. Stop early only if the student has answered nothing in Part A after 4 minutes, or is upset; record "stopped after 4 minutes".
- **Writing:** if the student cannot start, say once: "You can write words, or draw and label your plan." Never write for them. A student who can write nothing in English may write two or three sentences in Chinese at the end so that a Chinese-speaking colleague can comment on their first-language literacy; note it, but it does not score.
- **Maths and science:** stop at the time. A student who is stuck may be told: "You can go on to the next question."
- A distressed child is never pressed: stop, take a break or end the session, and record it.

### 2.5 Keeping the student at ease

- Sit beside rather than opposite if you can. Smile, use the student's name, and praise effort ("Thank you, you are working hard"), not answers.
- Do not show marks or say whether an answer is right.
- Say how many parts are left. Offer water and the break after the writing.

## 3. English

### 3.1 Listening (5 minutes, / 8)

**Say:** "Turn to page 2. I am going to read a message from a teacher, Mr Chen, to his class. First I will read the questions to you. Then I will read the message two times. You can answer while I read. Answer on pages 2 and 3."

**Read the questions aloud once**, slowly, pointing to each number. Do not name or explain the pictures.

> "Question 1. How will the class travel to the museum? Tick one box. Question 2. What time does the bus leave school? Tick one box. Question 3. What must the children bring? Tick two boxes. Question 4. What will the class visit first? Tick one box. Question 5. How many children will be in each group? Question 6. How much does the trip cost? Question 7. Mr Chen says, 'Don't worry if it rains.' Why does he say this? Question 8. Will the children buy lunch at the museum? Tick yes or no. How do you know?"

**Say:** "Now listen. I will read the message two times."

**Read it twice at a natural, steady pace.** Pause for about 3 seconds at each [pause]. After the first reading, wait 20 seconds, then say "Now listen again" and read it again. After the second reading, give one minute to finish.

> **Mr Chen:** Good morning, everyone. I have some news about our class trip. [pause]
>
> Next Thursday, we are going to the City Science Museum. We will go there by bus. The bus leaves school at half past eight, so please be at school by a quarter past eight. [pause]
>
> Please wear your sports clothes and comfortable shoes, because we will walk a lot. Bring a water bottle and a packed lunch. You do not need to bring any money on the day. [pause]
>
> At the museum, we will visit the space room first. After that, we will go to the robot workshop. There, you will build a small robot in groups of four. [pause]
>
> The trip costs forty yuan. Please ask your parents to pay by Monday. [pause]
>
> We will be back at school at three o'clock. And don't worry if it rains. Everything at the museum is inside. [pause]
>
> I am sure it will be a great day.

**Answers.** 1 mark each. Spelling and grammar are not marked in listening; the answer must be in English and the meaning must be clear.

| Q | Answer | Also accept | Do not accept | What it tests |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A** bus | | | key word |
| 2 | **B** 8:30 | | A 8:15 (the time to arrive at school) | a time; "half past" |
| 3 | **A** water bottle and **C** lunch box | both ticked and nothing else | money (they were told not to bring it), umbrella | two details; a negative ("do not need") |
| 4 | **A** space | | B robots (that is second) | order words: "first", "after that" |
| 5 | **4** | four | | number |
| 6 | **40** | ¥40, 40 yuan, forty | 14 (fourteen and forty sound alike) | number |
| 7 | Everything at the museum is inside, so the rain does not matter | "inside"; "they are inside"; "they will not get wet" | "it rains"; "bring an umbrella" | inference: links two sentences |
| 8 | **No**, because they bring a packed lunch, or because they do not need money | No with either reason; a reason without the tick if the meaning is clear ("no, they bring lunch") | Yes; No with no reason or a wrong reason | inference |

**Band guide**

| Score | 0 to 2 | 3 to 4 | 5 to 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Listening band | **A** | **B** | **C** | **D** | **E** |

**What to look for**

- Questions 1 to 4 need key words and pictures only. A student who gets these but none of 5 to 8 usually knows familiar words but cannot yet follow connected speech (band B).
- The distractors in 2 (8:15) and 3 (money) show whether the student hears the words around a key word ("be at school by", "do not need").
- Questions 7 and 8 need two ideas linked: they separate D from E. If the student clearly understood but could not write it in English (for example, tells a colleague in Chinese), score 0 and note "understood, could not express in English": this is evidence about writing and speaking as well as listening.
- Compare with "listening in conversation" on the interview record.

### 3.2 Reading paper (12 minutes, / 24)

**Say:** "Turn to page 4. This is the reading. There are three parts: A, B and C. Read the texts and answer the questions. Part C is hard. Do as much as you can. It is fine if you do not finish. I cannot help you with the words, but I can say the instructions again. You have 12 minutes."

Start the timer. Say "Two minutes left" at 10 minutes and "Please stop" at 12 minutes. Do not read any text or question aloud and do not explain words.

**Marking.** Spelling is not marked unless the word cannot be recognised. For "find and copy" the word must be the right word from the text; a small copying slip is fine. Answers in Chinese score 0 (note them).

**Part A: the park notice** (questions 1 to 6, / 8)

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | 8:00 | | 18:00 is the closing time |
| 2 | 2 | swings, picnic, feed ducks | Score = correct ticks minus wrong ticks: 3 gives 2 marks, 2 gives 1 mark, 1 or less gives 0 | The pictures match the notice; the skill is "you can" against "please do not" |
| 3 | 1 | ¥2 | 2, 2 yuan | |
| 4 | 1 | at the café | cafe, the cafe, café shop | "the office", "the shop" (not in the text) |
| 5 | 2 | swim in the lake: **False**; open on Sundays: **True** | 1 mark each | "Sundays" needs the reader to use "every day" |
| 6 | 1 | the office by the main gate | office; main gate; gate office | the café |

**Part C: The Red Kite** (questions 15 to 22, / 8). Age-appropriate fiction for a fluent reader of 9.

| Q | Marks | Skill | Answer | Also accept | Do not accept |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 | 1 | retrieval | a fish | a red fish; a fish kite | "red" on its own |
| 16 | 1 | retrieval | on the hill behind the village | on a hill; the hill; behind the village | in the sky; in the village |

| 19 | 1 | figurative language | The kite was pulling hard in the wind. | | |

**Band guide**

| Result | Band |
|---|---|
| Part A under 4 | **A** New to English |
| Part A 4 or more, Part B under 4 | **B** Early acquisition |
| Part B 4 or more, Part C under 3 | **C** Developing competence |
| Part C 3 to 5 | **D** Competent |
| Part C 6 to 8 | **E** Fluent |

Use judgement when a result is uneven (for example, Part C higher than Part B), and check against the sentence reading screen.

**What to look for**

- **Picture support.** A student who scores only on questions 1, 2 and 5 may be using the pictures, not the words.
- **Copying.** In Parts B and C, a pattern of copying words that sit next to the question words (for example "small insects" for question 7) suggests word matching without understanding.
- **Inference.** Questions 20 to 22 separate D from E. A student at band C often gets the retrieval questions and leaves the "why" questions blank.
- **Speed.** A student who is accurate but does not reach Part C may decode slowly: check the fluency rating on the sentence reading record before giving a low band.

### 3.3 Writing (10 minutes, / 16)

**Say:** "Turn to page 10. This is the writing. Choose A or B, and tick the one you choose. You can plan in the box on page 11, then write on the lines. Write as much as you can. You have 10 minutes."

**The task.** A: a story about a child who finds a message in a bottle on a beach (picture and question prompts). B: "My favourite place", a description with question prompts. Both give a fair chance to show narrative or descriptive writing; do not give a higher mark for either choice.

**Criteria.** Score each criterion 0 to 4 (1 and 3 sit between the descriptions). Total / 16.

| Criterion | 0 | 2 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Content and organisation** | Nothing in English, or only words copied from the page | A few connected ideas on the topic in a simple order; may be short (3 to 5 sentences) or a list | A complete piece that fits the task: a story with an opening, a problem and an ending, or a description with several developed points; ideas link across sentences; paragraphs or clear sections |
| **Vocabulary** | No English words, or single words not on the topic | Everyday words that carry the meaning; a few topic words; much repetition (nice, good, big) | Varied, precise words for a 9 year old: chosen verbs, adjectives and adverbs; some ambitious words used correctly |
| **Grammar and sentences** | No sentences | Simple sentences with mostly correct word order, joined with and, then, because; frequent errors in tense, plurals and articles, but the meaning is clear | Different sentence types (with when, if, because, so, after); tense kept consistent; errors are rare and do not affect meaning |
| **Spelling and punctuation** | Spelling cannot be read; no punctuation | Common words mostly correct, others spelt as they sound; capital letters and full stops sometimes | Most words correct, including less common ones; full stops and capitals secure; commas, question marks and speech marks mostly correct |

**The writing band** is given by best fit with the descriptors in `00_Admissions_Guide.md`, Section 6, not by the total alone. As a rough guide only: totals of 0 to 3 usually fit **A**, 4 to 7 **B**, 8 to 10 **C**, 11 to 13 **D**, 14 to 16 **E**.

**Band E at this age.** A fluent writer of 9 to 10 usually writes about 100 to 180 words in 10 minutes. The piece has an opening and an ending, is in paragraphs or clear sections, uses some varied sentences ("When she opened the bottle, ...", "Suddenly, ..."), descriptive words, and in a story some speech with speech marks (not always correct). Common words are spelt correctly and harder words are spelt plausibly. The errors are the ones any 9 year old makes, not the features of a learner of English (missing "a" and "the", missing past tense -ed or plural -s, Chinese word order).

**What to look for**

- **Learner features** (missing -s, -ed, articles; "I very like") point to C or D even when the content is good.
- **Memorised chunks** ("My name is ... I am 9 years old. I like ...") with little else usually fit B.
- **A full plan and little writing** may mean slow writing in English rather than no ideas: say so in the notes.

### 3.4 Speaking: the interview (about 8 minutes of questions)

Use `forms/Interview_Record_Y5_Y6`. The interviewer records the speaking band and "listening in conversation" on that form; copy the speaking band into Section 6. If the listening task (3.1) and listening in conversation differ by more than one band, note why (for example, the student relies on gesture and context in conversation).

### 3.5 Reading aloud: the Graded Sentence Reading Screen (opens the interview, 3 to 4 minutes)

Use `shared/Sentence_Reading_Card` and `shared/Sentence_Reading_Record`. **Start at Level 4 (sentence 10)** for Year 5. If you already know the student is new to English, start at Level 1. Follow the record's rules for going back, stopping and the meaning checks. Copy the total points (/ 72), reading age, meaning checks and fluency rating into Section 6. Use the "Years 3 to 5" column of the record's table to turn the reading age into an indicative band, and use it to confirm the reading paper band.

## 4. Maths (18 minutes, / 25)

**Say:** "Turn to page 12. This is maths. Section A is number problems. Section B is word problems and pictures. You can use a pencil, a rubber and a ruler. If you want me to read a question to you, put up your hand. You have 18 minutes."

**What it tests.** UK Year 4 content, with easier floor items (Year 3) and ceiling items (Year 5). Section A, number problems: 10 marks. Section B, word problems and pictures: 15 marks, of which **word problems: 5 marks** (questions 10, 12, 13 and 19b). Every Section B question has a picture, chart, table or diagram.

**Marking.** Give the mark for a correct answer with or without working. Units are printed in the answer boxes, so they are not needed. Accept answers in a different but correct form unless the question asks for a particular form.

List the NT questions and judge whether the student would reach band 3 once taught.

**Topics that may not have been taught** to a student who has completed Chinese Grade 3: the 11 and 12 times tables (4), hundredths (6), equivalent fractions (7), rounding (8), bar charts with a scale (11), the names of triangles and quadrilaterals (16), coordinates (18) and negative numbers (19). Calculation (1 to 3, 5, 9), measures, money, area and perimeter (10, 12 to 14) are usually taught by the end of Grade 3. Textbooks differ (PEP, 苏教版, 北师大版), so always ask the student.

### 4.3 What to look for

- **Number problems against word problems.** A student who scores well on number problems (Section A) but poorly on the word problems (10, 12, 13, 19b) may have an English barrier rather than a maths difficulty. Check by reading the word problem aloud (and, if a Chinese-speaking colleague is present, once in Chinese, marked L). If the answer then comes quickly, record it as an English barrier.
- **Method.** Students from Chinese schools often calculate quickly and accurately in their heads or in neat columns. A correct answer without working is fine. Look at the working when an answer is wrong: one slip in a sound method is a different need from a wrong method.
- **Conventions.** Reading a scale in 5s, "Not drawn accurately", coordinates in brackets and the UK names of shapes may be new. A student who answers the maths correctly in Chinese words, or with a picture, knows the maths.

**Band guide (provisional)**

| Score / 10 | 0 to 3 | 4 to 5 | 6 to 8 | 9 to 10 |

**What to look for**

- **Working scientifically.** Question 7 shows scientific thinking separately from topic knowledge. A student who gets 7 right but marks other questions NT is likely to do well once taught.
- **An English barrier** looks like wrong answers that depend on a single English word (solid, liquid, gas in 1; beetle in 3; vibrates in 5) while the picture questions (4, 6) are right. Reading the question aloud, or once in Chinese (L), usually settles it.
- **Everyday ideas** (for example "the stick hits the rice") are a science misconception, not an English problem.
