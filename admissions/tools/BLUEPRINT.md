# Blueprint: what each year group's assessment contains

The design for the whole pack. Writers of each year group follow this exactly so that results are comparable from year to year. Read `STYLE_GUIDE.md` first.

## 1. Who the students are

- Students joining the **international programme** (English-medium, UK National Curriculum year groups, Years 1 to 10) from **Chinese mainstream schools** or from **other bilingual schools**. Most are learning English as an additional language. Some have almost no English; some from bilingual schools are close to fluent.
- **Age and grade.** Placement is by age. A student joining UK Year N in September is the same age as a student starting Chinese Grade N minus 1, so **they have usually completed Chinese Grade N minus 2**. Examples: a Year 2 entrant has completed kindergarten (幼儿园); a Year 5 entrant has completed Grade 3; a Year 8 entrant has completed Grade 6 (end of primary); a Year 10 entrant has completed Grade 8 (初二). A student joining mid-year is partway through the next grade.
- **What a Chinese mainstream student has typically learnt** (PEP 人教版 maths sequence; Jiangsu schools often use 苏教版 and other regions 北师大版, which differ a little; always ask the student):
  - Maths, by grade completed. G1: numbers to 100, add and subtract within 100, o'clock and half past, yuan and jiao, 2D and 3D shapes. G2: times tables to 9 × 9, division facts, numbers to 10 000, cm and m, g and kg, time to the minute, right angles, simple tables of data. G3: multiplying and dividing by one digit, two-digit × two-digit, perimeter, area (cm², m²), first fractions, first decimals, 24-hour time, compass directions. G4: large numbers, measuring angles with a protractor, three-digit × two-digit, dividing by two digits, parallel and perpendicular lines, bar charts, decimals (meaning, adding, subtracting), triangle types and angle sum 180°, the mean, symmetry and translation. G5: multiplying and dividing decimals, simple equations, area of parallelograms, triangles and trapeziums, factors, multiples and primes, cuboid and cube volume, adding and subtracting fractions, line graphs, simple likelihood. G6: multiplying and dividing fractions, ratio, circles (circumference and area), percentages, pie charts, negative numbers, cylinders and cones, proportion. G7 (初一): operations with negative numbers, algebraic expressions, linear equations, angles and parallel lines, square roots, coordinates, simultaneous linear equations, inequalities. G8 (初二): congruent triangles, reflection, expanding and factorising, algebraic fractions, surds, Pythagoras, parallelograms, linear functions, mean, median, mode and variance. G9 (初三): quadratic equations and functions, rotation, circle properties, probability, similarity, trigonometry.
  - Times tables beyond 9 × 9 (11 and 12 times) are often not learnt. Roman numerals and imperial units are not taught. UK-style statistics and "explain your reasoning" questions are less familiar. Calculation is usually strong and fast.
  - Science: primary 科学 from Grade 1 covers broad topics (plants and animals, materials, water and air, weather, sound in G4, simple circuits in G4, light and heat in G5, microscopes and cells in G6, simple machines, the Earth and space) and varies by textbook. Middle school splits into subjects: biology from 初一 (cells, plants, the human body, digestion, breathing), physics from 初二 (sound, light, speed, mass and density, force, pressure; electricity and magnetism in 初三), chemistry only in 初三. So a Year 8 entrant has done no secondary science, a Year 9 entrant has done some biology, and a Year 10 entrant has done biology and some physics but usually no chemistry. Mark NT rather than reading this as weak science.
  - English: public schools usually start English as a subject in Grade 3 (some cities in Grade 1), with a strong focus on vocabulary lists, grammar and textbook dialogues. Many students have learnt phonics (自然拼读) or the phonetic alphabet (音标) outside school. Extended writing, extended reading and spontaneous speaking are usually the weakest areas. Common features of English from Chinese speakers: missing plural -s, third person -s and past tense -ed; missing or extra articles (a, the); dropped or added final consonants ("bed" read as "be" or "bed-uh"); l and r, n and l, v and w, th as s or f; pinyin letter values carried over (for example x, q, zh, c, i, e, u). These are features of a learner's English, not errors in decoding or understanding, unless they change the word.

## 2. The four English skills and the proficiency scale

Every candidate gets a band for each of **Listening, Speaking, Reading and Writing** on the five-point proficiency scale used for students learning English as an additional language: **A New to English, B Early acquisition, C Developing competence, D Competent, E Fluent**. Band E means "comparable to a fluent English speaker of the same age". The descriptors are in `00_Admissions_Guide.md`, Section 6. Each year group's assessor booklet gives a band guide for its own listening and reading scores (Section 3 below) and a short version of the writing descriptors.

## 3. Standard structure of the English tasks

**Years 1 to 3**
- **Speaking: picture talk** (assessor-led, 3 to 4 minutes): a picture or a short picture story in the student booklet; prompts from easy (name things) to harder (tell the story, say why, say what might happen next). Record with the speaking descriptors.
- **Listening: follow instructions** (4 to 5 minutes): the assessor reads instructions of increasing difficulty and the student points, colours, draws or circles on a picture page. 10 items: one-step (point to the cat), then positions (under, next to, between), then two-step, then sequence or condition (before you ..., if ...). Score 1 per item, /10. Band guide: 0 to 2 A, 3 to 4 B, 5 to 6 C, 7 to 8 D, 9 to 10 E (Year 1: 8 to 10 is E).
- **Reading**: the shared **Early Reading Screen** (`shared/`), started at the point this year's booklet gives, and the **Graded Sentence Reading Screen** (`shared/`). Year booklets do not write their own reading tasks; they say where to start and how to record.
- **Writing**: Year 1: write their name, five dictated words, and a sentence about a picture if they can (5 minutes). Year 2: one dictated sentence, then 2 to 4 sentences about the picture story (6 minutes). Year 3: write the picture story or a short recount with a frame (8 minutes). Assessed with the writing descriptors and a short checklist.

**Years 4 to 10**
- **Listening** (5 minutes): the assessor reads a short text twice (a message, an announcement, a short talk, a conversation for two voices read by one person). The student answers 8 questions on the paper: picture choice and tick boxes first, then one-word or number answers, then one or two inference questions. /8. Band guide: 0 to 2 A, 3 to 4 B, 5 to 6 C, 7 D, 8 E.
- **Reading paper** (12 minutes): three parts in one paper, each harder than the last.
  - Part A: a short, highly accessible text with picture support (a notice, a message, a menu, a timetable, a very short story). 8 marks.
  - Part B: a text about two years below the student's age level. 8 marks.
  - Part C: an age-appropriate text, fiction in some years and non-fiction in others. 8 marks: retrieval, vocabulary in context, inference, and one "why do you think" or author's purpose question.
  - Total 24. Band guide: Part A under 4: A. Part A 4 or more, Part B under 4: B. Part B 4 or more, Part C under 3: C. Part C 3 to 5: D. Part C 6 to 8: E. Use judgement when a result is uneven, and check against the sentence reading screen.
  - Tell the student on the paper: "Do as much as you can. It is fine if you do not finish." Part C is meant to stretch.
- **Writing** (10 minutes): choose one of two prompts, a planning box, then lines. Years 4 to 6: one story or recount prompt with a picture, one description, letter or "my favourite" prompt. Years 7 to 10: one narrative or descriptive prompt, one opinion or persuasive prompt. Assessed with the writing descriptors and four criteria (content and organisation, vocabulary, grammar and sentences, spelling and punctuation), each scored 0 to 4, total /16, with the band given by best fit, not by the total alone.
- **Speaking**: assessed during the interview (shared form) with the speaking descriptors.
- **Reading aloud**: the Graded Sentence Reading Screen at the start of the interview (shared).

The parent conversation (about 15 minutes) and the previous homeroom teacher reference are extra and do not involve the student. Years 1 and 2 have a short movement break in the middle; Years 3 to 10 may have a 3 to 5 minute break after the written English.

**Order on the day.** Years 1 to 3: welcome and picture talk, listening, early reading screen and sentence reading, break, maths, writing, short interview and goodbye. Years 4 to 10: welcome (2 minutes), listening, reading paper, writing, break, maths, science, interview with sentence reading. The written papers from Year 4 can be done in a small group with one invigilator; listening is read to the group; the interview is 1:1.

## 5. Maths

- **What it tests.** The UK National Curriculum content for the year **before** the year applied for (what a student should know on entry), with a few easier items to find the floor and two or three items from the year applied for to find the ceiling. Years 1 and 2: the end of Reception (Early Years goals) and Year 1. Year 10: the end of Key Stage 3 and readiness for IGCSE.
- **Number problems and word problems.** Section A: **Number problems** (calculation and number facts, quick, no context, no calculator). Section B: **Word problems and pictures**: at least 3 short word problems in Years 1 to 3 (read aloud by the assessor; one-step; everyday contexts; a supporting picture is welcome) and at least 4 in Years 4 to 10 (one-step and two-step or multi-step; at least one with money in yuan and at least one with measures, time, speed or percentages), plus problems with diagrams, pictures, charts and tables. Word problems use plain, short sentences. Questions easy to hard within each section.
- **Word problems and English.** The assessor booklet reports the word-problem marks separately ("of which word problems / n"). A student who does well on number problems but poorly on word problems may have an English barrier rather than a maths difficulty: check by reading the word problem aloud (and, if a Chinese-speaking colleague is present, once in Chinese, marked L).
- **Marks and time.** Year 1: 15 marks, 12 minutes, 1:1 with picture pages and 20 counters. Year 2: 20 marks, 15 minutes, the assessor reads every question aloud and the student answers in the booklet. Year 3: 24 marks, 18 minutes. Years 4 to 6: 25 marks, 18 minutes. Years 7 to 10: 30 marks, 20 minutes.
- **Band guide** (provisional, review after the first 20 to 30 candidates): under 40% of the total: 1 Well below expected; 40 to 59%: 2 Working towards; 60 to 84%: 3 Expected; 85% or more: 4 Greater depth. NT questions are listed, and the assessor judges whether the student would reach the expected band once taught.
- **Equipment**: pencil, rubber; Years 1 and 2 counters; ruler from Year 3. No calculators. No protractor: angle questions use reasoning or a printed protractor in the diagram.
- **Content per year** (the year applied for: the content tested):
  - **Year 1** (Reception goals): count objects to 20; recognise numerals to 20; one more and one less; compare groups (more, fewer); subitise to 5 (dot patterns); number bonds to 5 and 10 with pictures; double small numbers; share equally; 2D shapes; repeating patterns; longer and shorter; positional words with pictures.
  - **Year 2** (Year 1): count, read and write numbers to 100; one more and one less; number bonds to 10 and 20; add and subtract within 20, missing numbers (7 = ? minus 9 style); count in 2s, 5s and 10s; simple multiplication and sharing with pictures and arrays; halves and quarters of shapes and small amounts; compare lengths and masses; o'clock and half past on clocks; days of the week; 2D and 3D shapes; half and quarter turns; position and direction.
  - **Year 3** (Year 2): place value in two-digit numbers (tens and ones, base 10 pictures); compare with <, > and =; add and subtract two-digit numbers; 2, 5 and 10 times tables, multiplication and division facts; fractions 1/3, 1/4, 2/4 and 3/4 of shapes and amounts; read scales (ruler in cm, thermometer, kitchen scale); time to five minutes; money in yuan; properties of 2D and 3D shapes (sides, vertices, faces); lines of symmetry; tally charts, pictograms and block graphs.
  - **Year 4** (Year 3): numbers to 1000, place value; add and subtract three-digit numbers (column methods); 3, 4 and 8 times tables; two-digit times one-digit; tenths; equivalent fractions with diagrams; adding fractions with the same denominator; mm, cm and m; perimeter; time (analogue and digital, minutes in an hour); right angles, horizontal and vertical lines; bar charts and tables.
  - **Year 5** (Year 4): numbers beyond 1000; rounding to 10, 100, 1000; negative numbers on a thermometer or number line; all times tables to 12 × 12; three-digit times one-digit; equivalent fractions; tenths and hundredths as decimals; area by counting squares and perimeter of rectilinear shapes; unit conversion (cm and m, g and kg); acute, obtuse and right angles; types of triangles and quadrilaterals; lines of symmetry; coordinates in the first quadrant and translation; bar charts and line graphs.
  - **Year 6** (Year 5): numbers to 1 000 000; negative numbers; multiples, factors, primes, square and cube numbers; long multiplication (four-digit by two-digit); short division with remainders; fractions (mixed numbers, improper fractions, add and subtract with related denominators); decimals to three places; percentages as hundredths; area and perimeter of rectangles and composite shapes; volume with cubes; angles on a straight line and around a point; reflection and translation; line graphs and timetables.
  - **Year 7** (Year 6): order of operations; long division; multiply and divide fractions; fraction, decimal and percentage equivalence; percentages of amounts; ratio and scale; simple formulae and missing numbers (algebra); the nth term of a simple sequence; area of triangles and parallelograms; volume of cuboids; angles in triangles and quadrilaterals, vertically opposite angles; parts of a circle; coordinates in four quadrants; pie charts; the mean.
  - **Year 8** (Year 7): operations with negative numbers; HCF and LCM; prime factorisation; fractions, decimals and percentages; percentage of an amount; sharing in a ratio; simplifying expressions and substitution; solving linear equations; sequences; angle rules (angles on a line, in a triangle, vertically opposite, parallel lines); area of compound shapes; perimeter and area with units; probability on a 0 to 1 scale; mean, median, mode and range; bar charts and pie charts.
  - **Year 9** (Year 8): index notation and powers; percentage increase and decrease; ratio and direct proportion; expanding single brackets and factorising; equations with brackets and unknowns on both sides; inequalities on a number line; straight-line graphs (plotting, gradient as steepness); circumference and area of circles; volume of prisms; angles in polygons and parallel lines; rotation and enlargement; probability of events; scatter graphs; mean from a frequency table.
  - **Year 10** (Year 9, IGCSE readiness): standard form; laws of indices; expanding double brackets and factorising simple quadratics; solving simultaneous equations; rearranging formulae; gradient and y-intercept (y = mx + c); Pythagoras' theorem; trigonometry in right-angled triangles; similar shapes and scale factors; circle area and circumference; volume of a cylinder; reverse percentages and compound interest; probability (tree or two-way table); averages from grouped data or a cumulative frequency idea.

## 6. Science (Years 5 to 10)

- **What it tests.** The UK National Curriculum science content for the year before the year applied for, plus **working scientifically** (fair tests, variables, reading tables and graphs, simple conclusions). About 10 marks in 8 minutes, 5 to 7 questions, every question with a diagram, picture, table or graph. Same band guide as maths.
- **Content per year**:
  - **Year 5** (Year 4): grouping and classification keys; teeth and digestion; food chains; solids, liquids and gases, melting, evaporation and condensation, the water cycle; sound (vibration, pitch, loudness); simple circuits, switches, conductors and insulators.
  - **Year 6** (Year 5): life cycles (mammal, amphibian, insect, bird); human growth; properties of materials, dissolving, separating mixtures (sieving, filtering, evaporating); reversible and irreversible changes; the Sun, Earth and Moon, day and night; gravity, air resistance, water resistance, friction; levers, pulleys and gears.
  - **Year 7** (Year 6): classification of living things; the heart, blood vessels and blood; diet, exercise and health; evolution and inheritance (fossils, adaptation); light travels in straight lines, shadows, reflection, how we see; circuit symbols, more cells and brighter bulbs.
  - **Year 8** (Year 7): animal and plant cells and the microscope; the particle model; acids and alkalis and the pH scale; separating mixtures; forces, balanced and unbalanced, speed = distance ÷ time; energy stores and transfers; sound and light as waves (simple); reproduction in plants.
  - **Year 9** (Year 8): elements, compounds and mixtures, the periodic table (metals and non-metals); chemical reactions, word equations, combustion, conservation of mass; digestion and nutrition; aerobic respiration and photosynthesis (word equations); current and voltage in series and parallel circuits; magnets and electromagnets; heating and cooling (conduction, convection, radiation); food webs.
  - **Year 10** (Year 9, IGCSE readiness): specialised cells and diffusion; atomic structure (protons, neutrons, electrons); groups in the periodic table; simple balanced symbol equations; rates of reaction; distance-time graphs and speed calculations; pressure; energy resources and efficiency; waves; DNA and inheritance basics; factors affecting photosynthesis; analysing data and evaluating a method.

## 7. What every assessor booklet must contain

See `STYLE_GUIDE.md`, Sections 6 and 7, for the order and the access rules. In addition:
- **The listening script** in full, with "[pause]" marks and the instruction "Read it twice at a natural, steady pace", and the answers.
- **Answers and mark schemes** for every question, with acceptable alternatives and partial-credit rules.
- **"Likely not yet taught" notes** for maths and science questions, based on Section 1, worded as "may not have been taught" (never as certain).
- **What to look for**: two or three bullets per section on what separates a strong response from a weak one, and what an English barrier (as opposed to a maths or science difficulty) looks like.
- **Writing**: the task, the four criteria with what 0, 2 and 4 look like at this age, and a short note on what band E writing looks like at this age.
- **Score summary table** at the end:

## 8. Student paper layout (Years 4 to 10)

1. **Cover**: title ("Year 5 Admission Assessment"), `[[namebox]]`, a short friendly welcome in simple English ("These activities help us get to know you. Do your best. It is fine if you cannot answer every question."), a "How to answer" example panel (tick a box, write in a box, show your working), and a list of the parts.
2. **Listening** (new page): the answer page for the listening questions. The script is in the assessor booklet only.
3. **Reading** (new page): Parts A, B and C with their texts and questions.
4. **Writing** (new page): the two prompts, a planning box, ruled lines (Years 4 to 6 about 14 lines; Years 7 to 10 about 20 lines).
5. **Maths** (new page): Section A, then Section B.
6. **Science** (new page, Years 5 to 10).
7. End: "Thank you. You have finished." with `[[stop:Thank you. You have finished. Please wait for your teacher.]]`.

Years 1 to 3 have a **student booklet** instead: the pages the assessor uses 1:1 (picture talk, listening picture, maths picture pages) and the writing page, in the order of the day. Pictures are large and clear; text on these pages is minimal because the assessor reads it.
