# Year 6 Admission Assessment v3.1: assessor instructions

**Version 3.1, October 2026.** Same content as version 1. Use with `Y06/Y06_Student_Paper_v3.1`; do not mix versions.

**Reusable: print once and keep in the assessor's folder. Do not write on these pages.** Record each candidate's marks on their own **grading sheet** (`Y06_Grading_Sheet_v3.1`), reading on the **Reading front sheet** and the interview on the **Interview sheet**.

**For staff only.** Use with the Year 6 student paper (`Y06/Y06_Student_Paper_v3.1`), the Graded Sentence Reading Screen (`shared/Sentence_Reading_Card` and `shared/Sentence_Reading_Guide`) and the Interviewer guide for Years 5 and 6 (`forms/Interviewer_Guide_Y5_Y6`). The whole process, the English proficiency bands and the decision guide are in `00_Admissions_Guide.md`.

**Who this is for.** Students joining UK Year 6 (age 10 to 11 in September). A student from a Chinese mainstream school has usually **completed Grade 4** (四年级); a student joining mid-year is partway through Grade 5. The maths tests UK Year 5 content, with a few easier and a few harder items. The science tests UK Year 5 science and working scientifically.

## 2. Before you start

### 2.1 Set up

- A quiet, bright room. The student sits where they cannot see other students' papers.
- Write the student's name on the paper if they cannot (in English, with the Chinese name in brackets if the family uses one).
- Have these instructions open at 3.1 for the listening script (if you are not playing the recording), and a timer ready.

**Pace of speech.** Year 6 students are still learning English: speak a little slower than normal speech, about 155 words a minute. Use the same pace for every candidate, so that the results can be compared. The listening recording is paced the same way.

### 2.2 Welcome (say this)

> "Hello, I am [your name]. Welcome to our school. Today you will do some activities: listening, reading, writing, maths and science. Then we will have a chat. The activities help us get to know you and plan how to help you. Some parts are easy and some parts are hard. That is fine. Do your best. If you do not understand what to do, you can ask me to say it again."

Show the cover page. Point to the "How to answer" examples and say: "Look. You tick a box, write in a box, circle, join with a line, and show your working like this."

### 2.3 Access rules (the same in every year group)

| | English parts (listening, reading, writing, sentence reading) | Maths and science |
|---|---|---|
| Reading aloud | Read only what the script says. Do not explain words, translate, or read the reading texts or reading questions aloud. The student may ask for instructions to be repeated in English. | Read any question aloud in English, at the pace in 2.1, as often as the student asks, and point to the picture. |
| Chinese | Not used by staff. | If a Chinese-speaking colleague is present and the student clearly cannot follow the English after it has been read aloud, the colleague may say the question **once** in Chinese. Write **L** in the margin and list it on the grading sheet. The mark still counts. |
| Answers | In English only. An answer in Chinese scores 0. | In English only: numbers, words, ticks, drawings and working all count. An answer or explanation in Chinese scores 0. |
| Not yet taught | | If the student says they have not learnt a topic, write **NT** beside the question. It scores 0, and the grading sheet lists the NT topics. |

Extra time or a reader for a known need is allowed: record it on the grading sheet. The number of **L** questions is evidence about English and goes on the grading sheet.

### 2.4 Stopping rules

- In every part, a student may stop when they have done what they can. Encourage once ("Have a look at the next one; some are easier"), then accept their decision.
- **Listening:** always play the whole recording, or read the whole script twice, even if the student writes nothing.
- **Reading:** let the student work for the full 12 minutes. Stop early only if the student has answered nothing in Part A after 4 minutes, or is upset; record "stopped after 4 minutes" on the Reading front sheet.
- **Writing:** if the student cannot start, say once: "You can write words, or draw and label your plan." Never write for them.
- **Maths and science:** stop at the time. A student who is stuck may be told: "You can go on to the next question."
- A distressed child is never pressed: stop, take a break or end the session, and record it on the grading sheet.

### 2.5 Keeping the student at ease

- Sit beside rather than opposite if you can. Smile, use the student's name, and praise effort ("Thank you, you are working hard"), not answers.
- Do not show marks or say whether an answer is right.
- Say how many parts are left. Offer water and the break after the writing.

## 3. English

### 3.1 Listening (about 7 minutes with the recording, / 8)

**Recording.** A recording of this test is in `audio/v3.1/Y06/` (see its `READ_ME_FIRST.txt` for the tracks and timings). Play it instead of reading the script: every candidate then hears the same voices, pace and pauses. It says the instructions below, reads the eight questions aloud once, plays the script twice (20 seconds apart) and gives one minute to finish, so you do not say or read anything yourself. The two speakers have different voices, so it does not say their names. The script below is its transcript, and the live reading option if no recording can be played. Read live at the recording's pace, one reading of the script takes about 2 minutes, with the pauses: use this to check your own pace. Do not pause the recording during a reading of the script.

**Say:** "Turn to page 2. I am going to read a phone conversation between two friends, Aisha and Jun. They are talking about a film night in the park at Mid-Autumn. First I will read the questions to you. Then I will read the conversation two times. You can answer while I read. Answer on pages 2 and 3."

**Read the questions aloud once**, slowly, pointing to each number. Do not name or explain the pictures.

> "Question 1. Where will Aisha and Jun meet? Tick one box. Question 2. What time will they meet? Tick one box. Question 3. What must Jun bring? Tick two boxes. Question 4. Who is going to the film night with Aisha? Tick one box. Question 5. How wide is the screen? Question 6. What can they buy in the park? Question 7. Why does Aisha change the place where they meet? Question 8. Jun's piano lesson finishes at six o'clock. Will he be late? Tick yes or no, and explain your answer."

**Say:** "Now listen. I will read the conversation two times."

**Read it twice at about 155 words a minute, a little slower than normal speech.** Read both parts yourself: say each speaker's name before their words, and change your voice a little. Pause for about 3 seconds at each [pause]. After the first reading, wait 20 seconds, then say "Now listen again" and read it again. After the second reading, give one minute to finish.

**What to look for**

- Questions 1 to 4 need key words and pictures. A student who gets these but none of 5 to 8 usually knows familiar words but cannot yet follow connected speech (band B).
- Questions 1 and 2 have distractors: the first meeting place and the other times in the conversation. Choosing them shows the student caught words but missed the change ("instead", "but").
- Question 8 needs three facts combined (six o'clock, ten minutes, a quarter past six): it separates D from E.
- Compare with "listening in conversation" on the Interview sheet.

**What to look for**

- **Picture support.** A student who scores mainly on questions 1 to 3 may be using the menu pictures and prices, not the words.
- **Copying.** A pattern of copying words that sit next to the question words suggests word matching without understanding (for example "the wind can carry them" for question 16).
- **Inference and purpose.** Questions 21 to 23 separate D from E. A student at band C often gets the retrieval questions and leaves the "why" and "explain" questions blank.
- **Speed.** A student who is accurate but does not reach Part C may decode slowly: check the fluency rating from the sentence reading screen (Reading front sheet) before giving a low band.

### 3.3 Writing (10 minutes, / 16)

**Say:** "Turn to page 10. This is the writing. Choose A or B, and tick the one you choose. You can plan in the box on page 11, then write on the lines. Write as much as you can. You have 10 minutes."

**The task.** A: a story that starts when a small robot with an envelope knocks at the door (picture and question prompts). B: a letter to a student in the new class about themselves (prompts for content). Do not give a higher mark for either choice. In B, the content tells you about the student too: pass anything useful to the interviewer.

**Criteria.** Score each criterion 0 to 4 (1 and 3 sit between the descriptions). Total: 16 marks.

| Criterion | 0 | 2 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Content and organisation** | Nothing in English, or only words copied from the page | A few connected ideas on the topic in a simple order; may be short (3 to 6 sentences) or a list; a letter may have no opening or ending | A complete piece that fits the task: a story with an opening, a problem and an ending, or a letter with a greeting, several developed points and a closing; ideas link across sentences; paragraphs |
| **Vocabulary** | No English words, or single words not on the topic | Everyday words that carry the meaning; a few topic words; much repetition (nice, good, like) | Varied, precise words for a 10 year old: chosen verbs, adjectives and adverbs; some ambitious words used correctly |
| **Grammar and sentences** | No sentences | Simple sentences with mostly correct word order, joined with and, then, because; frequent errors in tense, plurals and articles, but the meaning is clear | A range of sentences (with when, if, because, although, so that); tenses controlled; errors are rare and do not affect meaning |
| **Spelling and punctuation** | Spelling cannot be read; no punctuation | Common words mostly correct, others spelt as they sound; capital letters and full stops sometimes | Most words correct, including less common ones; sentence punctuation secure; commas, apostrophes and speech marks mostly correct |

**The writing band** is given by best fit with the descriptors in `00_Admissions_Guide.md`, Section 6, not by the total alone. As a rough guide only: totals of 0 to 3 usually fit **A**, 4 to 7 **B**, 8 to 10 **C**, 11 to 13 **D**, 14 to 16 **E**.

**Band E at this age.** A fluent writer of 10 to 11 usually writes about 120 to 200 words in 10 minutes, in paragraphs, with a clear opening and ending suited to the form (a story ending, or a letter's closing such as "I hope to meet you soon"). Sentences vary in length and type, vocabulary is chosen for effect, and dialogue in a story is mostly punctuated correctly. Common words are spelt correctly and harder words plausibly. The errors are the ones any 10 year old makes, not the features of a learner of English (missing "a" and "the", missing past tense -ed or plural -s, Chinese word order).

**What to look for**

- **Learner features** (missing -s, -ed, articles; "I very like"; "There have many ...") point to C or D even when the content is good.
- **Memorised chunks** in letter B ("My name is ... I am 10 years old. My hobby is ...") with little else usually fit B. Look for what the student adds beyond the textbook pattern.
- **A full plan and little writing** may mean slow writing in English rather than no ideas: say so in the notes on the grading sheet.

Textbooks differ (PEP, 苏教版, 北师大版), so always ask the student.

### 4.3 What to look for

- **Number problems against word problems.** A student who scores well on number problems (Section A) but poorly on the word problems (11, 12, 15, 18) may have an English barrier rather than a maths difficulty. Check by reading the word problem aloud (and, if a Chinese-speaking colleague is present, once in Chinese, marked L). If the answer then comes quickly, record it as an English barrier on the grading sheet.
- **Method.** Students from Chinese schools often calculate quickly and accurately. A correct answer without working is fine. When an answer is wrong, look at the working: one slip in a sound method is a different need from a wrong method.
- **Conventions.** "Remainder" in words, timetables, "Not drawn accurately", cm³ and the percentage sign may be new. A student who shows the maths correctly with a picture or working knows the maths, even if the English words are new.
