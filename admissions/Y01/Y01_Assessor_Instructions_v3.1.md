# Year 1 Admission Assessment v3.1: assessor instructions

**Version 3.1, October 2026.** Same content as version 1. Use with `Y01/Y01_Student_Booklet_v3.1`; do not mix versions.

**Reusable: print once and keep in the assessor's folder. Do not write on these pages.** Record each candidate's marks on their own **grading sheet** (`Y01_Grading_Sheet_v3.1`), reading on the **Reading front sheet** and the interview on the **Interview sheet**.

For a child joining **Year 1** (about 5 years old). Most have finished the middle year of a Chinese kindergarten (中班); some come from bilingual kindergartens. The visit takes **about 45 minutes, 1:1**, and should feel like play. The child uses the Year 1 student booklet; you write the maths marks in the mark boxes on the child's pages and record everything else on the child's grading sheet, Reading front sheet and Interview sheet.

## 2. Before you start

**The room.** Use a low table. Sit beside the child at a corner of the table, not opposite. Keep the counters and crayons out of sight until each task needs them. A young child is never alone with one adult behind a closed door: keep the door open or have a second adult in the room. Show the child where the parent will wait. If the child will not separate, the parent may sit quietly at the back without helping; note it on the grading sheet.

**Pace of speech.** Year 1 students are still learning English: speak slowly and clearly, in short phrases, with a pause of about a second after each phrase. Use the same pace for every candidate, so that the results can be compared (do not slow down further for a child who finds it hard, and repeat an instruction only where these instructions allow it). The listening recording is paced the same way: a clear, steady voice with a pause of about a second after each phrase.

**Welcome (1 minute).** Say:

> "Hello! My name is [your name]. What's your name?" (wait) "Hello, [name]. How old are you?" (wait) "Today we are going to look at some pictures, play some games, count and draw. Some things are easy and some things are tricky. That's OK. Just try your best."

Write the child's name on the cover of the student booklet yourself (the child writes their name later, in the writing task). Note how the child first responds (English, Chinese, gesture, silence): it is early evidence for speaking and listening.

#### Access rules for Year 1

| | English tasks: picture talk, listening, reading, writing | Maths |
|---|---|---|
| What you may say | Only the words in the script. Do not explain words, translate, point or show what to do. You may point to the picture and show with counters. |

| Answers | English words, pointing and doing count. An answer in Chinese scores 0. | Pointing, fingers, counters and number words in English all count. An answer in Chinese scores 0. |

Codes on the grading sheet: **R** instruction repeated once; **L** language support in maths; **NT** not yet taught. Extra time, a reader or extra breaks for a known need are allowed: record them on the grading sheet. Wherever these instructions say "note it", write the note in the comment space on the grading sheet.

#### Keeping the child at ease

- Praise effort, never correctness: "You are working hard!", "Thank you!", "Good listening!" Use the same warm tone after right and wrong answers. Never say "No" or "Wrong", and do not correct.
- A silent child is common: many young children new to English go through a silent period. Smile, wait, move on. Pointing tasks still work. Never pressure the child.
- If the child is tired, take a short extra break. If the child becomes upset, stop, comfort them and let them go to the parent. Try again later if they recover; otherwise end the visit and record what was done on the grading sheet.

#### Stopping rules

| Task | Stop when |
|---|---|
| Picture talk | the child has not answered three prompts in a row. Finish with an easy "What's this?" so the child ends with success. |
| Listening | three items in a row score 0. Score the rest 0. |
| Reading screens | as the Early Reading Screen guide and the Sentence Reading Guide say. |
| Maths | three items in a row score 0 in Section A: go on to Section B, which starts easy again. Three in a row score 0 in Section B: stop. |
| Writing | the child says they cannot do more after one encouragement ("Have a go. Write the sounds you can hear."). |

**Movement break (about 2 minutes, after reading).** Say: "Let's have a break! Stand up. Stretch up high. Touch your toes. Jump three times. Now sit down." Do the actions with the child. It is not scored. Offer water and the toilet.

## 3. English

### 3.1 Speaking: picture talk (4 minutes, student booklet page 2)

The picture shows a windy day in a park: the wind is blowing a girl's balloon away and she is sad because she cannot reach the string; a boy has thrown a ball and a dog is running after it; two ducks swim on a pond; the sun is out and leaves blow in the wind.

**How.** Open the booklet at page 2. Give the prompts in order, slowly and clearly (see **Pace of speech** in Section 2). Wait about 5 seconds after each one; if there is no answer, go on to the next prompt. You may repeat a prompt once. Do not name things for the child or finish their sentences. Record what the child says on the grading sheet, as close to their words as you can ("dog run", "she sad balloon fly").

[[keep]]

| Step | Say | Listen for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Open | "Look at this picture. What can you see?" | Any naming: tree, balloon, girl, boy, dog, ball, duck, pond, sun, cloud, bird, flower |
| 2 Naming | Point to each and ask "What's this?": the dog, the ball, the balloon, a duck | dog, ball, balloon, duck. Count how many the child names in English (out of 4) and record it |
| 3 Action | Point to the dog. "What is the dog doing?" | running, chasing the ball, playing |
| 4 Action | Point to the boy. "What is the boy doing?" | throwing the ball, playing with the dog |
| 5 Feeling | Point to the girl. "How does she feel?" | sad, unhappy, crying, upset, cross |
| 6 Explaining | "Why?" | her balloon is flying away; the wind blew it; she can't reach the string |
| 7 Telling | "Tell me what is happening in the picture." | joined ideas: "The girl's balloon is flying away and the dog is running after the ball." |
| 8 Predicting | "What do you think will happen next?" | the balloon will fly up to the clouds or get caught in the tree; the boy will try to catch it; her mum will buy a new one; the dog will catch the ball |
| 9 Personal | "Do you like the park? What do you do at the park?" | talks about their own life |

[[/keep]]

[[keep]]

#### Speaking band at age 5 (examples from this picture)

| Band | What it sounds like at age 5 | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **A** New to English | Silent, nods or points; perhaps one or two English words; may speak in Chinese | points to the dog; "狗"; "dog" |
| **B** Early acquisition | Names several things in English; single words and two-word phrases; little description | "dog", "ball", "a duck", "balloon fly" |
| **C** Developing competence | Short sentences with a verb, often with errors; can say what someone is doing | "The dog is run." "She sad." "Boy throw the ball." |
| **D** Competent | Connected sentences; explains with "because"; tells the picture as a little story; errors do not get in the way | "She is sad because her balloon fly away." "Maybe the boy help her." |
| **E** Fluent | Talks as a fluent English-speaking 5 year old does: full sentences, words such as blowing, chasing, windy, reach, and ideas of their own | "The wind blew her balloon away and she can't reach the string. I think it will go up to the clouds, and her mummy will buy her a new one." |

[[/keep]]

Give the speaking band from the picture talk on the grading sheet. The final speaking band is the best fit of the picture talk and the interview (Section 5), and is given on the Interview sheet.

#### What to look for

- A strong response needs few prompts, joins ideas (and, because, then) and predicts ("maybe", "I think"). A weak one needs every prompt and gives one word or repeats your question.
- Do not count against the child: missing -s, -ed, "a" and "the"; "he" and "she" mixed up; accent features (l and r, light final consonants). These are normal for learners.
- A child who names little in English but, as the parent describes, talks well in Chinese has an English barrier only. A child who says very little in either language may need a speech and language check: see the Interviewer guide and the Interview sheet.

### 3.2 Listening: follow instructions (about 4 minutes with the recording, page 3)

**Recording.** A recording of this test is in `audio/v3.1/Y01/` (see its `READ_ME_FIRST.txt` for the tracks and timings). Play it instead of reading the script: every candidate then hears the same voices, pace and pauses. The script below is its transcript, and the live reading option if no recording can be played. Do not pause the recording during a reading of the script.

**Set up.** Open the booklet at page 3 (the bedroom). Put the four crayons on the table: red, blue, yellow and green. Do not name the colours.

**Say:** "Let's play a listening game. Look at this picture. Listen carefully and do what I say. Ready?" Practice, not scored: "Put your finger on the picture." (Smile.) "Good!"

#### Rules

- Say each instruction **once**, slowly and clearly, with a pause after each phrase (see **Pace of speech** in Section 2). Look at the child, not at the picture: children follow your eyes. Do not point, nod or gesture.
- If the child asks, or clearly did not hear, you may say it once more: tick **R** on the grading sheet. A correct response after one repeat still scores.
- With the recording, if the child asks, or clearly did not hear, pause, go back to the start of the item (the start times are in the read-me) and play it once more: tick **R** on the grading sheet. Pause the recording while a child is still working on an item.
- For colouring, a small mark of the right colour on the right thing is enough. Say "Just a little bit of colour is fine" if the child starts colouring carefully.
- **Score 1** only when every part is right, and in the right order where the instruction says "then" or "before". Otherwise 0. No half marks.
- Stop after three 0s in a row.

[[keep]]

| No. | Say | Type | Correct response |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Point to the cat." | one step | the cat on the rug |
| 2 | "Point to the window." | one step | anywhere inside the window frame (the sun or cloud seen through it is fine) |
| 3 | "Point to the ball under the bed." | position: under | the ball under the bed, not the ball on the chair |
| 4 | "Point to the book next to the lamp." | position: next to | the book standing on the desk beside the lamp, not the books on the shelf |
| 5 | "Point to the toy between the car and the boat." | position: between | the toy duck on the top shelf |
| 6 | "Point to the teddy, then point to the shoes." | two steps | the teddy on the bed, then the shoes on the floor, in that order |
| 7 | "Colour the lamp yellow and colour the boat blue." | two steps | yellow on the lamp and blue on the boat (either order) |
| 8 | "Draw a circle round the clock, then colour the ball on the chair red." | two steps | a circle round the clock, then red on the ball on the chair (not the ball under the bed) |
| 9 | "Before you colour the car green, point to the bed." | sequence: before | points to the bed **first**, then colours the car green |
| 10 | "If there is a moon in the window, colour it yellow. If there is a sun in the window, draw a circle round it." | condition: if | draws a circle round the sun in the window and does not colour it (there is no moon) |

Total: 10 marks (1 for each item).

[[/keep]]

#### Band guide (Year 1)

| Score | 0 to 2 | 3 to 4 | 5 to 6 | 7 | 8 to 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Listening band | A | B | C | D | E |

#### What to look for

- Items 1 and 2 need everyday nouns; 3 to 5 need the position words under, next to and between; 6 to 8 need the child to hold a longer piece of English in mind; 9 and 10 need grammar ("before", "if").
- Right object, wrong colour: score 0, but note it. Colour words may be the only gap.
- A child who does only the first part of every two-step item is probably holding too much new English at once, not showing a memory difficulty. A child who answers quickly and correctly but watches your face closely may be using your eyes as clues: keep looking at the child.

### 3.3 Reading: Early Reading Screen and Graded Sentence Reading Screen (12 minutes)

Use the Early Reading Screen (`shared/Early_Reading_Screen_Cards` and the record sheets in `shared/Early_Reading_Screen_Record`) starting at the letter sounds (Card 1, then Card 2 if 15 or more of Card 1 are right) and then the Section 1 words (Year 1 start point in the guide, Section 2); and the Graded Sentence Reading Screen (`shared/Sentence_Reading_Card`, with the rules in `shared/Sentence_Reading_Guide` and the score grid on the Reading front sheet) starting at Level 1.

The full scripts and rules for the Early Reading Screen are in `shared/Early_Reading_Screen_Guide`. Follow the scripts, scoring and stopping rules in that guide and in the Sentence Reading Guide; these instructions have no reading tasks of their own. All answers are in English, including the answers to the questions about what the child has just read. Many children starting Year 1 read very little English, and many fluent English speakers score 0 to 6 points on the sentence screen at this age, so stop kindly and praise the child's effort. Record the reading results on the **Reading front sheet** (the Early Reading Screen summary and the sentence reading results) and give it to the interviewer (Sections 5 and 6). Then take the movement break.

### 3.4 Writing (5 minutes, pages 12 and 13; given after maths)

Give the child a pencil. Do not spell any word, sound out, or write for the child.

**Part 1: name (page 12).** Point to the line next to "Name". Say: "Can you write your name here?" Record which script the child uses (English letters, pinyin or Chinese characters) on the grading sheet.

**Part 2: five words (page 12).** Say: "Now I will say some words. Write each word here. Listen to the sounds in the word. Have a go. It is fine if it is not right." For each word, point to the number, then say the word, the sentence and the word again, slowly and clearly (see **Pace of speech** in Section 2; do not stretch the sounds). Allow about 30 seconds; if nothing is written, say "That's fine. Next one."

| No. | Say | Word | Spelling pattern |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "sun. The sun is hot. sun." | sun | CVC |
| 2 | "fan. The fan goes round and round. fan." | fan | CVC |
| 3 | "pen. I can write with a pen. pen." | pen | CVC |
| 4 | "ship. The ship is on the sea. ship." | ship | digraph sh |
| 5 | "chop. Mum can chop the carrots. chop." | chop | digraph ch |

None of the five words is on the reading cards, so the child has not just seen them written.

**Part 3: a sentence about a picture (page 13).** Point to the picture (a puppy in a box). Say: "Look at the picture. What can you see?" Let the child say it, and do not correct or improve their words. Then say: "Can you write about it? Write it here." If the child says they can't: "Have a go. Write the sounds you can hear." If the child writes one word, say once: "Can you write some more?" When they finish, say "Can you read it to me?" and write down what they say on the grading sheet: it helps you read their spelling.

**Writing checklist.** Record the name, handwriting and sentence checklist on the grading sheet, and for each of the five words whether it is correct, shows all the sounds ("fen", "shep", "chup"), some sounds ("sp", "c") or nothing.

[[keep]]

**Four criteria.** Score each 0 to 4 (1 and 3 lie between the descriptions). Total: 16 marks.

| Criterion | 0 | 2 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Content and organisation | Nothing written, or marks not linked to the picture | One or two words or a phrase about the picture ("dog box") | A complete idea about the picture, perhaps with a second idea ("The dog is in the box. He is happy.") |
| Vocabulary | No English words | Simple nouns (dog, box) | Words chosen for meaning: puppy, sitting, happy, big, peeping |
| Grammar and sentences | No phrase or sentence | A phrase, or a sentence with words missing ("dog in box") | A simple sentence with a verb ("The dog is in the box."); may join two ideas with "and" |
| Spelling and punctuation (the five words and the sentence) | Random letters, or letters not linked to sounds | Most dictated words show some sounds, usually the first and last ("sn", "fn"); one or two words correct | All or nearly all dictated words correct or with all sounds shown, including sh and ch; a capital letter or a full stop in the sentence |

[[/keep]]

#### Writing band (best fit with the descriptors; the totals are only a guide)

| Band | Writing at age 5 | Typical total |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Makes marks, copies, or writes their name only; may write in Chinese; dictated words not attempted or random letters | 0 to 3 |
| **B** | Some letters match sounds (often first sounds); one or two dictated words with most sounds; a word or label for the picture | 4 to 7 |
| **C** | Most dictated words show most sounds and some are correct; a phrase or short sentence that can be read with help | 8 to 10 |
| **D** | Most dictated words correct; a simple sentence that another adult can read, with spaces between words | 11 to 13 |
| **E** | Comparable to a fluent English-speaking 5 year old (below) | 14 to 16 |

**What band E looks like at age 5.** The child writes their name correctly (capital letter, then lower case), writes all five words correctly or with one small slip ("chopp"), and writes one or two simple sentences about the picture that another adult can read, with spaces between the words and usually a capital letter and a full stop. Most letters are correctly formed. This matches the end-of-Reception expectation (the Early Learning Goal for writing) for a fluent English speaker.

#### Spelling from children who speak Chinese

- Pinyin letter values often appear: "x" for sh ("xip" for ship), "q" or "c" for ch ("qop" for chop). Count these as "all sounds shown" and note them on the grading sheet: the child hears every sound but needs the English letters taught.
- Vowel swaps (e and a: "pan" for pen; u and a: "san" for sun) and missing final sounds ("pe", "cho") are features of a learner's English.
- Many Chinese kindergartens are asked not to teach formal writing, pinyin or characters before primary school, so letter writing **may not have been taught**. A blank page is not, on its own, a sign of a learning difficulty.

#### What to look for

- Strong: hears every sound in the CVC words, uses sh and ch, writes a sentence without help.
- English barrier: forms letters well and spells by sound, but writes the sentence in Chinese or pinyin. The child knows how to write, but not yet what to write in English.
- Note pencil grip and letter formation separately: fine motor control is not English.

## 4. Maths (12 minutes, student booklet pages 4 to 11)

**Content:** the end of Reception (Early Years goals). **Section A: Number problems** (items 1 to 8, 8 marks). **Section B: Word problems and pictures** (items 9 to 15, 7 marks, with 3 word problems: 11, 12 and 15). Total 15 marks.

#### How

- Put the pot of 20 counters on the table. Say: "Now let's play some number games. You can use these counters to help you."
- Read each question as written, slowly and clearly (see **Pace of speech** in Section 2). Then you may repeat it in English as often as needed, point to the picture and show with counters. A Chinese-speaking colleague may say it once in Chinese (mark **L**).
- The child answers by pointing, saying, showing fingers or moving counters. Number words must be in English; a child who cannot yet say the number may show it with fingers or counters, or point to it.
- Write 1 or 0 in the mark box on the child's page as you go, and NT or L beside the box (list the NT and L items on the grading sheet). Do not say whether an answer is right.
- Stop as in Section 2: three 0s in a row in Section A, go to Section B; three in a row in Section B, stop.

**Number problems and word problems.** Items 11, 12 and 15 use the same number ideas as Section A, but in English sentences. A child who does well on the number problems but poorly on the word problems may have an **English barrier rather than a maths difficulty**: check by reading the word problem aloud again, showing it with counters, and, if a Chinese-speaking colleague is present, having it said once in Chinese (mark L). Record the result on the grading sheet.

#### Likely not yet taught

[[keep]]

Chinese kindergartens are asked not to teach primary school content (the policy known as 去小学化), so what a child knows varies a lot; many children also go to extra classes. Ask the parent. A child who has finished the middle year (中班):

| Item | May not have been taught |
|---|---|
| 2 | Quick recognition of dot patterns as a game, although dice are familiar |
| 3, 4 | Reading teen numbers; many kindergartens count to 20 aloud but read numerals only to 10 |
| 6, 8 | Five and ten frames, and number bonds (数的分成, splitting 5 or 10 into two parts), which are usually taught in the top year (大班) |
| 7 | "Double" as a named idea |
| 11 | The English word "fewer" ("more" is usually known) |
| 13 | The English position words (the ideas are usually known in Chinese) |
| 14 | The English words "pattern" and "comes next" (repeating patterns, 找规律, are often met in kindergarten) |
| 15 | Sharing equally (平均分) as a named idea |

#### What to look for

- How the child counts: touching each object, counting on from a number, or knowing at a glance. Confidence with numbers beyond 10.
- An English barrier looks like this: the child is lost at first, then answers quickly once you show it with counters or a colleague says it in Chinese. A maths difficulty looks like this: the child cannot do it even when the meaning is clear (for example, cannot count 7 objects reliably).
- Strong children often answer before you finish the question and use counters only to check.

## 5. Play-based interview and goodbye (6 minutes, page 14)

The questions are in `forms/Interviewer_Guide_Y1_Y2` and the record is the Interview sheet (`forms/Interview_Sheet_Y1_Y2`). Sit on the floor or at the low table, open the student booklet at page 14 (Draw) and offer the crayons: the interview starts with a drawing ("Can you draw you, doing something you love?"). Play alongside, talk about the drawing and follow the child's lead. Give the speaking band on the Interview sheet, combining the interview with the picture talk (recorded on the grading sheet). The observation boxes on the Interview sheet cover the whole visit, so fill them in now.

**Goodbye.** Say: "Thank you, [name]! You worked really hard today. I liked [something specific, such as your drawing or your counting]. Let's go and find Mum (or Dad)." Do not give any results to the family on the day.

## 6. Recording the results

1. Mark the student booklet the same day (every maths question has a mark box).
2. Copy the totals onto the grading sheet and give the bands from the band guides above (the grading sheet repeats them at a glance).
3. Record the reading results and band on the Reading front sheet (the reading band comes from the Early Reading Screen, guide section 6.7).
4. Give the interviewer the Reading front sheet and the Interview sheet before the interview.
5. After the interview, put together the grading sheet, Reading front sheet, Interview sheet and parent conversation record, and complete the final mark sheet (`forms/Candidate_Summary_and_Decision`) within two working days.
6. File the marked student booklet with them (or shred it under the school's policy).
