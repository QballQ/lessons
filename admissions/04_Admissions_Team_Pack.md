# Admissions team pack: running an assessment visit

This is the simple pack for the admissions team and the assessors. It says what to print, what happens when, who does what, and where every mark goes. The full guide for staff and the evidence documents (in the Office folder) stay as the background; you do not need them on the day.

## 1. The visit in one page

**One folder for each year group.** The admissions office works from the delivery pack: open the student's year group folder, for example **Year 5**, and read its one-page **READ ME FIRST**. Everything the visit needs is inside, named by what to do with it and who gets it.

| In the year folder | What it holds | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **1 Print one per student** | The visit checklist, the student paper or booklet, the **student record** (one booklet: marks, reading, interview, decision; it goes from the assessor to the interviewer to admissions), the parent conversation record, the assessment result and report (Years 1 to 3 also the Early Reading Screen record; Years 11 and 12 also the cognitive screen booklet and the optional sciences). Each file is one sheet or booklet, numbered in the order it is used | Print one of each for every student, double-sided. Hand each to the person named in the file name |
| **2 Print once for the room** | The day sheet, the assessor instructions, the interviewer guide, the sentence reading guide and card (Years 1 to 3 also the Early Reading Screen guide and cards; Years 11 and 12 also the optional sciences mark scheme and the cognitive screen guide) | Print once, laminate the cards, keep in the room. Nobody writes on them |
| **3 Listening recording** | The MP3 for that year group (Years 7 to 12 also Part A and Part B separately) | Copy to the laptop or phone in the room; test the speaker |
| **4 Send before the visit** | The previous homeroom teacher reference (English and Chinese) | Send to the student's current school once the family has given consent |

**Who does what.**

| Role | Does |
|---|---|
| **Admissions officer** | Collects documents and consent, books the visit and the interview slot, prints folder 1 of the year group, welcomes the family, hands the interviewer the sheets, files everything, enters the results, communicates the decision |
| **Assessor** (a teacher) | Runs the written and spoken tasks (Section 3), marks the paper, completes Part A and the top of Part B of the **student record** and hands it over |
| **Interviewer** (usually the principal; the homeroom teacher or the assessor in Years 1 to 3) | Runs the sentence reading (Years 4 to 12) and the interview, completes Parts B and C of the **student record** |
| **Head of School or Deputy** | Reads Part D of the student record and confirms the recommendation. The Designated Safeguarding Lead gets anything worrying the same day |

**The visit, step by step.**

| Step | Who | What | Paper |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Admissions | Family arrives. Welcome, show where parents wait. Parent conversation starts with another member of staff (about 15 minutes) | Parent conversation record |
| 2 | Assessor | Runs the sections in Section 3 (the day sheet for the year group) | Student paper, student record |
| 3 | Assessor | Marks the paper, fills in Part A and the top of Part B of the student record | Student record |
| 4 | Admissions | Gives the interviewer the student record (Section 5) | |
| 5 | Interviewer | Sentence reading and interview, then completes Parts B and C and returns the record | Student record |
| 6 | Admissions, Head of School or Deputy | Thanks the family: **no results are given on the day**. Part D of the student record within two working days (Section 6) | Student record, Part D |
| 7 | Admissions | After the Head of School or Deputy confirms the decision: completes the **assessment result and report** and gives it with the marked papers (Section 6) | Assessment result and report |

## 2. Before the visit

**A week before**
- [[tick]] Application, passport or ID page, school reports for the last two years and signed consent are in. Homeroom teacher reference sent.
- [[tick]] Visit booked (mornings are best for young children). Family told how long it takes, to bring glasses or hearing aids, and asked about access needs.
- [[tick]] Assessor, interviewer (check the principal's diary) and a second adult for young children are booked.

**The day before**
- [[tick]] Folder **1 Print one per student** of the year group printed, double-sided, one of each file (Section 8 lists them). Years 1 to 3: print only the Early Reading Screen passage sheets you expect to use. Years 11 and 12: print only the optional sciences the student will take.
- [[tick]] Room set up: quiet and bright; a low table for Years 1 to 3; a clock; pencils, rubbers, a ruler from Year 3; counters (Years 1 and 2); crayons (Years 1 and 2) or coloured pencils (Year 3); a calculator for each student in Year 9 and 10 maths Section C.
- [[tick]] Folder **2 Print once for the room** is in the room (printed once, the cards laminated), and for Years 1 to 3 the levelled books.
- [[tick]] **Listening recording** copied to the laptop or phone and the speaker tested (Section 4).

**On the day**
- [[tick]] Candidate number written on every printed sheet (the student paper carries the name). The visit checklist is ticked as the day goes on.
- [[tick]] Door open or a second adult in the room for young children. Anything worrying goes to the Designated Safeguarding Lead the same day.

## 3. Day sheets

Each sheet is the timetable for one phase. Each step has a section in the **assessor instructions** for the year group: the exact words to say, the answers and the marks are there. **Pace of speech:** speak slowly and clearly, in short phrases, with the youngest (Years 1 to 3) and steadily and clearly with the older students, with a pause after each sentence that is longer the younger the student (the recordings are paced this way). Use the same pace for every candidate in a year group.

### Years 1 to 3 (about 45 to 54 minutes, 1:1, play-based)

| Step | Minutes Y1 / Y2 / Y3 | Section | The child | You | Record on |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 / 1 / 2 | Welcome | Says their name and age | Smile; sit beside the child; write the name on the booklet cover; show where the parent waits | |
| 2 | 4 / 4 / 4 | Picture talk | Looks at the picture, answers the prompts | Give the prompts in order, wait about 5 seconds, write down the child's own words. Stop after three prompts with no answer | Grading sheet, Section 1 |
| 3 | 4 / 5 / 4 | Listening game | Points, draws, colours | **Play the recording.** Watch the child, not the picture. Mark each item 1 or 0 as you go. If asked, go back and play the item once more (tick R). Stop after three 0s in a row | Grading sheet, Section 2 |
| 4 | 12 / 12 / 12 | Reading: Early Reading Screen, then sentence reading | Reads letter sounds, words and short stories aloud, then sentences | Start at the point for the year (Early Reading Screen guide). Then sentence reading from Level 1 (Years 1 and 2) or Level 2 (Year 3) with Sentence reading guide | Early Reading Screen record (Sheet 13); Reading front sheet, Part 2 |
| 5 | 5 / 6 / 8 | Writing | Writes their name, dictated words, a sentence or a picture story (by year) | Dictate clearly at the year's pace. Do not spell or write for the child | Grading sheet |
| 6 | 2 / 2 / 3 to 5 | Movement break | Stretches, has water | Do the actions with the child | |
| 7 | 12 / 15 / 18 | Maths | Counts, points, shows with counters, writes | Read every question as written; repeat or show with counters if asked. Write 1 or 0 in the mark box on the child's page; write NT or L beside it | Mark boxes in the booklet; grading sheet |
| 8 | 6 / 6 / 8 | Play-based interview and goodbye | Draws and talks | Interviewer (often you): Interviewer guide for Years 1 and 2 or for Years 3 and 4. Do not give results. Say something specific that went well | Interview sheet |
| | **About 45 / 51 / 54** | | | | Then: grading sheet, Reading front sheet Part 1 and 3, final mark sheet |

Years 1 to 3: the assessor runs the sentence reading as well as the Early Reading Screen, so the Reading front sheet stays with the assessor and Parts 1 to 3 are completed together; the interviewer only needs the Interview sheet. Year 2: listening, maths and writing may be done with two children together (assessor instructions, Section 1).

## 4. The listening recordings

Every year group has a recording made from the scripts, so that every candidate hears the same voices, pace and pauses. It replaces the assessor reading the script. Play it on a speaker (not headphones) in a quiet room. Do the volume check before the student arrives. The MP3 is in folder **3 Listening recording** of the year group; the year's READ ME FIRST page gives the tracks and, for Years 1 to 3, the start time of every item.

| 12 | Year 12 listening.mp3 | 17:25 | 185 | Part A and Part B as separate tracks (if you may stop after Part A) |

**How to play it**
1. Student paper (or booklet) open at the right page, pencil ready, grading sheet in front of you.
2. Press play. **Do not pause or repeat** any part of the listening, except for the repeat rule in Years 1 to 3 (go back to the start of that item, play it once, tick R).
3. Mark the paper after the recording. Years 1 to 3: mark each item 1 or 0 as you go.
4. If the recording cannot be played, read the script live from the assessor instructions, at the pace for the year group in Section 3. Never mix the two in one test.

**The pauses are longer for younger students.** The voice speed is the same for every year group (the voice's normal speed). The pause after each sentence is about a second in Years 1 to 3, about three quarters of a second in Years 4 to 6 and about half a second in Years 7 to 12. The voices are offline text-to-speech in British English. In two-person scripts the speakers have different voices, so the recording does not say their names.

## 5. The student record: one booklet, passed from person to person

Each student has one **student record** (in folder 1; 8 to 10 pages, two or three sheets stapled). It replaces the separate grading sheet, Reading front sheet, Interview sheet and final mark sheet, and the assessor instructions still use those names for its four parts:

| Part | Who fills it in | What it holds |
|---|---|---|
| **A Marks** (the grading sheet) | The assessor | Listening, writing, maths and science scores and bands; NT and L; access arrangements; the assessor's comment |
| **B Reading** (the Reading front sheet) | The assessor fills in 1 (the reading levels so far and where the sentence reading starts); the interviewer, or in Years 1 to 3 the assessor, fills in 2 and 3 | The sentence reading grid, the reading age and the reading band |
| **C Interview** (the Interview sheet) | The interviewer | Speaking and listening bands, what the student said, suitability ratings, follow-ups, the interviewer's comment |
| **D Decision** (the final mark sheet) | Admissions, confirmed by the Head of School or Deputy | The bands together, the recommendation, support, comments and feedback for the family, sign-off |

**How it goes.** 1. Admissions gives the student paper and the student record to the assessor. 2. The assessor marks the paper, completes Part A and the top of Part B, and hands the record to the interviewer before the interview (writing who gave it and when, in Part B). 3. The interviewer opens the interview with the sentence reading from the start level in Part B (Level 1 if "new to English" is ticked), completes Parts B and C while the memory is fresh, and returns the record to admissions the same day. 4. Admissions keeps the record with the marked paper and the parent conversation record.

The interviewer's own folder, kept from one candidate to the next (nothing to print again): the Interviewer guide for the phase (the questions and descriptors), the Sentence reading guide (the rules, the sentences and the meaning questions) and the laminated sentence reading card.

## 6. After the visit

**The same day:** Parts A to C of the student record complete and signed; any safeguarding concern told to the Designated Safeguarding Lead.

**Within two working days: Part D of the student record.** Copy the bands from Parts A to C, give the overall English band, tick one recommendation, write the support plan, and add the comments and the feedback for the family. The Head of School or Deputy confirms it. Admissions tells the family (start with the child's strengths).

**The assessment result and report** is the front cover for the student's marked papers: one sheet, double-sided, with the outcome, the English bands and scores, maths and science, strengths, what to work on, the support recommended and the next step, and the band key on the back. Admissions fills it in from Part D **after the Head of School or Deputy has confirmed the decision**, and gives it with the marked papers when the family is told. Keep a copy with the file.

**Entering it in the admissions tracker** (Admissions tracker (Office folder), one workbook for all year groups). Give the candidate one row on the sheet for their year group (Year 1 to Year 12) and fill it in as the process moves on, changing the **Status** at each stage: Applied, Visit booked, Assessed, Decision confirmed, Family told, Enrolled, Withdrawn. It refuses a score above the paper's maximum, and its **Data check** column says what is missing. The **Dashboard** sheet shows, for every year group, where candidates are in the process, their levels, the decisions and offers, and the support needed: never type on it. The sheet How to use has the instructions, and the DEMO copy shows it working with fictional candidates.

| Tracker columns | From |
|---|---|
| Candidate details, dates applied and assessed, assessor, paper version | Application; final mark sheet, top |
| English bands A to E (each skill and overall); listening, writing and reading paper scores, or the book band (Years 1 to 3) | Final mark sheet, Section 1; grading sheet |
| Sentence reading points out of 72 (the reading age is worked out) | Reading front sheet, Part 3 |
| Maths and science scores and bands, word problems score, English barrier | Grading sheet; final mark sheet, Section 2 |
| Lowest suitability rating; follow-ups as **Y** only | Interview sheet; final mark sheet, Section 3 |
| Recommendation, support level and review date; dates decided, family told, report given | Final mark sheet, Sections 4, 5 and 7 |

The tracker holds children's personal data: keep it in the secure admissions folder, share it only with the admissions team and Head of School or Deputy, and keep safeguarding and medical detail out of it.

**Filing.** Keep the grading sheet, Reading front sheet, Interview sheet, parent conversation record and the marked student paper together with the final mark sheet, securely, for the period the school's data policy sets. Papers are confidential: do not let families photograph them or take them home.

## 7. The rules on one page

| Rule | English sections (listening, reading, writing, sentence reading) | Maths and science |
|---|---|---|
| Reading aloud | Only what the script says. Do not read the reading texts, explain words or translate. An instruction may be repeated in English | Any question may be read aloud in English, as often as asked, and you may point to the picture |
| Chinese | Not used by staff | A Chinese-speaking colleague may say the question **once**. Mark **L**. The mark still counts |
| Answers | In English only. An answer in Chinese scores 0 | In English only: numbers, ticks, drawings and working count. A Chinese answer or explanation scores 0 |

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **NT** | Not yet taught: the student says they have not learnt it. Scores 0; list the topics on the grading sheet |
| **L** | A Chinese-speaking colleague said a maths or science question once in Chinese |
| **R** | A listening instruction was repeated once (Years 1 to 3) |

**Always:** the student may stop any section when they have done what they can. Never press a distressed child: break or stop, and write it on the grading sheet. Praise effort, never correctness. Give no results on the day. Use the same papers, timings and pace for every candidate in a year group.

**If something goes wrong.** The recording will not play: read the script live at the year's pace (Section 4). A student is silent: smile, wait, move on; pointing tasks still work. A student stops early: record where and why. A parent wants to stay with a young child: they may sit quietly at the back; note it. A sheet is lost: reprint it from the candidate pack PDF; the version is on every page.
