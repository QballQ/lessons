## 1. Purpose and principles

### Purpose statement (for principals, staff and parents)

**English**
Think Like a Scientist is a ten-week enrichment programme for Years 7 to 9 in the bilingual stream. It sits outside the national curriculum and has no exam focus. Students work on hands-on projects that build curiosity, scientific thinking and critical thinking, and grow their confidence in using scientific English. Each series ends with a project that students plan, carry out and present.

**中文**
“像科学家一样思考”是为双语部七至九年级学生开设的为期十周的科学拓展课程。本课程独立于国家课程体系，不以考试为目的。学生通过动手实践的项目式学习，培养好奇心、科学思维和批判性思维，并增强使用英语进行科学表达的信心。每个系列都以学生自主设计、实施并展示的项目作为结束。

*Chinese text to be checked by a native-speaking colleague before it is shared.*

### Design principles

| Principle | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| **Project-based arc** | Weeks 1 to 5 build thinking skills through short, fun tasks. Weeks 6 to 10 put those skills to work on one project that students plan and own. |
| **Low floor, high ceiling** | Every task has a way in for students with less English and a way up for students who are ready to go further. Scaffolds are supports, not limits. |
| **Bilingual by design** | Key vocabulary is always shown in English and Chinese. Instructions are in English with key words glossed. Safety instructions are given in full in both languages. |
| **Kitchen-cupboard kit** | Every practical uses cheap, everyday materials. Nothing depends on specialist equipment the prep room may not have. |
| **Same lesson shape every week** | Five phases, same order, same icons. Students know what comes next; a new or cover teacher can pick up any lesson. |
| **Winter thread** | All three series connect to the cold season: ice structures (Y7), a frozen-notebook mystery (Y8), and winter claims including "Does hot water freeze faster?" (Y9). |
| **Handover-ready** | Week 5 is a checkpoint lesson that produces data for the incoming teacher. Every lesson has a one-page plan, a kit card and an answer key. |

### Freezer: Plan A and Plan B

Freezer access is not yet confirmed, so the programme does not depend on it. Both plans are fully built, and the choice is made by **Week 4 (w/c 2 November)**, before any ice is needed. The current teacher records the decision in the handover note.

**What Plan A actually needs** (much less than a full freezer):

## 4. Year 7: Ice Engineers | 冰雪工程师

**Big question:** How do scientists find things out?
**Project question:** Can we make ice stronger?
**End product:** An investigation poster and a 2-minute talk at the Ice Engineers Expo.

**The story hook.** During the Second World War, steel was in short supply. The inventor Geoffrey Pyke proposed building giant ships out of ice. His team found that ice mixed with about 14% sawdust (later called "pykrete") was much stronger than plain ice and melted more slowly. A small prototype was built on a lake in Canada in 1943. Legend has it that when the material was shown to military leaders, a bullet fired at a block of it bounced off. Students in China can link this to the giant ice buildings at the Harbin Ice and Snow Festival, and to the traditional use of straw in mud bricks: fibres make a brittle material stronger.

### Week by week

| Wk | Title | Thinking focus | What students do | Kit | Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What do scientists do? 科学家做什么？ | Observation vs inference | **Footprint puzzle:** a printed picture of animal tracks is revealed in three stages; students write observations, then inferences, and change their story as new evidence appears. **Mystery cups:** sealed cups with an object inside; students shake, listen, weigh in the hand and infer the contents. Cups are not opened: scientists often cannot "open the box". | Printed puzzle cards; opaque cups, tape, small objects | Baseline skills check; interest survey; logbook started |
| 2 | Thought experiments 思想实验 | Testable questions; predictions with reasons | **Galileo's thought experiment:** if heavy things fall faster, what happens when a heavy and a light ball are tied together? Then a quick test: drop a book and a sheet of paper, then the paper resting on the book. **Question sort:** testable vs not testable. | 3 | Meet the variables 认识变量 | Independent, dependent and control variables; fair test | **Paper spinners:** make spinners from a printed template, change wing length, time the fall from a 2 m mark. | 6 | Ice cube rescue 冰块保卫战 | Planning a fair test with ice | **Concept cartoon:** "If we put a coat on a snowman, will it melt faster or slower?" ### Project detail: making and testing the ice samples

- **Moulds (pick one and use it for everything):** ice-lolly moulds, ice-cube trays, or 250 ml drink cartons cut in half lengthways to make a beam mould. The mould must be the same for every sample.
- **Fixed recipe:** same volume of water every time (e.g. 100 ml). Only the fibre changes.
- **Choice of independent variable:**
  - Type of fibre: none (plain ice), sawdust, tissue, cotton wool, shredded paper
  - Amount of sawdust: 0 g, 5 g, 10 g, 15 g per 100 ml of water
- **Choice of dependent variable:**
  - Strength: volume of water (ml) the beam holds before it snaps
  - Melting: time to melt fully, or volume of meltwater after a set time
- **Timing:** make samples in Week 7, freeze for at least 24 hours, test in Week 8. Freezer decision by Week 4 (see Section 2).
- **Repeats:** each group makes three samples of each type.

### Safety notes for Year 7

- Use untreated natural wood sawdust or pet-bedding wood shavings only. **Never MDF dust** (it contains resin glues). Keep sawdust damp to reduce dust.
- Gloves for handling ice for more than a minute.
- Catch trays under every strength test; feet clear of hanging loads.
- Mop spills straight away.
- No standing on chairs or tables when dropping spinners.

### If there is no freezer

Year 7 runs Plan B: fibre-reinforced dough bars in Weeks 7 to 8 and a "keep it warm" challenge in Week 6. Full details are in **Section 2: Freezer, Plan A and Plan B**.

## 5. Year 8: Science Detectives | 科学侦探 (confirmed)

**Big question:** How strong is the evidence?
**Project:** Solve the Frozen Notebook case, then design your own forensic test.
**End product:** A case file and an expert witness statement at the Court of Science.

**Why this option:** very low kit, very high engagement, and it teaches the core of scientific thinking: separating what we observe from what we infer, using reference samples as controls, and judging how strong evidence really is.

**The case: The Frozen Notebook | 冰封笔记本之谜**
The night before the Winter Science Fair, Professor Frost's research notebook disappears from the science lab. Left behind: a handwritten note, a fingerprint on a glass, a spilled white powder, a small puddle of liquid and a footprint. Four fictional suspects each have a pen, a reason to be in the lab, a shoe size and a link to one of the powders. Each week, one type of evidence rules suspects in or out. (All characters are fictional. No real staff or students are used.)

**History hook:** Song Ci (宋慈) wrote *Collected Cases of Injustice Rectified* (《洗冤集录》) in 1247, one of the world's earliest books on forensic science. Fingerprints and handprints were also used to sign documents in ancient China.

### Safety notes for Year 8

- No tasting or eating any powder or liquid. Check for food allergies (flour, etc.).
- Goggles for vinegar, indicator and iodine work.
- The teacher makes the cabbage indicator in advance with hot water; students do not handle boiling water.
- Iodine stains skin and clothes; optional only.

## 6. Year 9: Claim Busters | 真相调查员 (confirmed)

**Big question:** Should I believe it?
**Project:** Choose a real-world claim, test it, and reach an honest verdict.
**End product:** A fact-check report, peer reviewed by classmates, presented at the Claim Busters Conference.

**Why this option:** it is the strongest of the options for critical thinking, it gives students ownership (they choose their claim), and it works with almost any kit. It is also the most mature in tone, which suits Year 9.

**Verdict scale (used all series):** Supported 有证据支持 | Not supported 证据不支持 | Not enough evidence yet 证据尚不足. "Not enough evidence yet" is a respectable scientific answer, and students learn to say it.

**Hooks:** Tu Youyou (屠呦呦) and her team took an idea from a text more than 1,600 years old and tested it with careful experiments, leading to a malaria medicine and the 2015 Nobel Prize. Erasto Mpemba, a Tanzanian school student, noticed in 1963 that hot ice-cream mixture seemed to freeze faster than cold, and scientists are still arguing about it.

| 2 | Brains that fool us 会骗人的大脑 | Bias; perception; why we measure | **Optical illusions** checked with a ruler. **Anchoring:** half the class is asked "Is the Yangtze longer or shorter than 1,000 km?", the other half "than 10,000 km?", then everyone estimates; compare the class means. **2-4-6 rule game** (confirmation bias). | Printed sheets, rulers | Bias reflection |

### Claim bank (curated, safe, low kit)

1. Hot water freezes faster than cold water. (ice and salt bath)
2. Ice melts faster in salt water than in fresh water. (cups, ice, salt; the result surprises most people)
3. You lose most of your body heat through your head, so a hat keeps you warm. (warm-water bottles, fabric, thermometers)
4. Wet clothes make you lose heat faster in winter. (cups of warm water wrapped in wet vs dry cloth)
5. Putting salt on ice makes it melt faster. (why roads are salted in winter)
6. Crushed ice cools a drink faster than ice cubes. (thermometers)
7. Listening to music helps you remember things. (memory test with and without music)
8. Practice makes you faster at a puzzle. (timed puzzle, repeated trials)
9. Taller people have bigger feet / your arm span equals your height. (measurements)
10. A spoon in the neck of an open fizzy drink keeps it fizzy. (mass loss on a kitchen scale over time)
11. Brand X paper towel is the most absorbent. (brands anonymised)
12. You react faster in the morning than after lunch. (ruler drop at different times)

**Claims to avoid:** health, medical or diet claims; traditional medicine; anything that involves eating or drinking; claims about named people or groups.

### Safety notes for Year 9

- Ice and salt baths get cold enough to hurt skin: handle tubes, not the mixture, and use gloves.
- Warm water for the Mpemba test is prepared by the teacher (no hotter than about 60 °C for student handling).
- Human-participant claims: voluntary participation, no pressure, results anonymised.

### Year 9 alternatives considered (not chosen)

**Option B: Clean Water Engineers | 净水工程师**
- Big question: how can engineers make dirty water clean?
- Weeks 1 to 2: why clean water matters; mixtures, settling and filtering
- Weeks 3 to 4: test filter materials (sand, gravel, charcoal, cotton wool, cloth); measure clarity with a printed "disappearing cross" scale

**Option C: Campus Climate Watch | 校园气候观察**

- Weeks 1 to 2: weather vs climate; build simple instruments (bottle rain gauge, paper wind vane, shaded thermometer box)
- Weeks 3 to 5: map microclimates around campus; set up a daily data rota
- Weeks 6 to 9: analyse ten weeks of data; compare with published climate averages for Changzhou; test climate claims with data
- Week 10: data story presentation
- Depends on: outdoor access and a reliable daily data rota.

## 7. How every lesson runs

Every lesson in every year follows the same five phases in a 40-minute session, with the same icons on slides and worksheets.

| Phase | 中文 | Minutes | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Do Now | 课前任务 | 4 | Retrieval of last week's vocabulary, or a puzzle; students start as they arrive |
| Hook | 引入 | 3 | A question, demo, story or thought experiment that creates curiosity |
| Learn | 新知 | 7 | The key idea, with bilingual vocabulary |
| Do | 实践 | 18 | The practical or thinking task, with Core / Stretch / Challenge tiers |
| Think and Reflect | 思辨与反思 | 8 | A quick critical-thinking discussion, then exit ticket and logbook line |

### Making 40 minutes work

- **Kit ready before students arrive.** Each group's tray is set out using the kit card. Templates are pre-printed; anything slow (cabbage indicator, freezing mixtures, ice samples) is prepared in advance.
- **Fixed group roles and tables.** Groups sit together from the first minute, so no time is lost moving.
- **Assessment time is kept short.** Skills check 15 minutes (Weeks 1 and 10), checkpoint 10 minutes (Week 5), survey 3 minutes. In Week 1 the main activity is trimmed to fit.
- **Homework-free.** Nothing depends on work outside the session; the logbook is written in class.

**Pressure points and fixes**

| Lesson | Pressure | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| All Week 1s | Baseline check and survey take 18 minutes | One short activity only (Y7: footprint puzzle; mystery cups move to the Week 2 Do Now) |
| Y7 W3 to 4 | Making spinners eats into testing | Spinners pre-printed on card; students only cut and fold |
| Y7 W6 | Ice needs 30 minutes to melt | Ice cubes wrapped and started at the beginning of the lesson; measured in the last 8 minutes |
| Y7 W7 | Planning and making samples in one lesson | Planning frame started in the Week 6 Think phase; samples made in the last 10 minutes |
| Y7 W8 / Y8 W8 / Y9 W8 | Repeats take time | Groups split roles so tests run in parallel; 3 repeats is the target, 2 the minimum |
| Y8 W5 | Indicator plus checkpoint | Indicator ready in cups; checkpoint 10 minutes |
| Y9 W6 | Water takes time to freeze | Tubes go into the freezing mixture during Learn; readings taken during Do |
| All Week 10s | Talks, skills check and survey in one lesson | Skills check 15 minutes, talks 2 minutes per group (about 15 minutes), survey and certificates 5 minutes; posters/reports displayed for a gallery walk at break or a later session |

### Sample one-page lesson plan: Year 7, Week 3, Meet the variables

**Learning goals**
- I can name the independent, dependent and control variables in an experiment.
- I can explain why a fair test changes only one thing.

**Key words (6):** variable 变量 | independent variable 自变量 | dependent variable 因变量 | control variable 控制变量 | fair test 公平测试 | air resistance 空气阻力

| Phase | Min | Teacher | Students |
|---|---|---|---|
| Do Now | 4 | Shows a picture of two paper planes thrown by different people from different heights | "Is this a fair race?" Answer with the frame: "It is / is not fair because ___." |
| Hook | 3 | Drops a short-winged and a long-winged spinner together | Vote on which lands first, then watch |
| Learn | 7 | Teaches "I change / I measure / I keep the same" with hand gestures; bilingual vocab panel | Complete the cloze on the slide together |
| Do | 18 | Circulates; checks roles and timing | Groups cut pre-printed spinners, test three wing lengths from the 2 m mark, record times |
| Think and Reflect | 8 | Shows four test descriptions; collects exit tickets | Odd one out: which test is not fair? Pair talk in any language, share in English with a frame. Exit ticket: name the IV, DV and one control variable |

**Tiers:** Core: cloze with word bank and picture sort. Stretch: name two more control variables and explain why each must stay the same. Challenge: design a new test with paper type as the independent variable and predict the result using "air resistance".
**Kit card:** spinner template printed on card (1 per student), scissors, paper clips, stopwatch (1 per group), 2 m mark on the wall.
**Safety:** no standing on chairs or tables; care with scissors.
**Teacher notes:** common misconception, "heavier things always fall faster". Timing errors are expected and set up Week 4.
**If time runs short:** test two wing lengths instead of three.

## 8. Language access: how slides and worksheets work

### Bilingual approach

- **Vocabulary is bilingual; instructions are English with glossed key words.** Full translation of everything would let students read only the Chinese, so most text stays in English and key terms carry a Chinese gloss.
- **Safety instructions are given in full in both languages.**
- **8 to 12 key words per lesson, maximum.** Shown in a vocabulary panel on every slide and worksheet, recycled in the next Do Now.
- **"Think in any language, record key words in English."** Pair discussion in Chinese is welcome during Think phases. Reporting back uses English sentence frames.
- **Native-speaker check.** A Chinese-speaking colleague checks every glossary and safety translation before printing.

### Worksheet structure (same every week)

1. Header: name, class, date, week number, lesson title in English and Chinese
2. Vocabulary panel (English | 中文 | small icon)
3. **Core** (all students): cloze, gap fill, matching, labelling, sequencing
4. **Stretch**: sentence-frame explanations, short answers, completing a table or graph
5. **Challenge**: open questions, designing a test, predicting and justifying, critical-thinking prompts
6. **My thinking** box: any language allowed, marked with an icon
7. Fold-under word bank at the foot of the page, so students can choose whether to use it

Tiers are not ability groups. Every student starts with Core and moves up when ready. A student with strong science and limited English can reach Challenge using frames and the "My thinking" box.

### Sample worksheet extract: Year 7, Week 3

**Core**
Fill the gaps using the word bank.
In our experiment we change the ______ of the wings. This is the ______ variable. We measure the ______ it takes to fall. This is the ______ variable. We keep the drop height the ______. This is a ______ variable.
*Word bank (fold under): length | independent | time | dependent | same | control*

**Stretch**
Name two more things we must keep the same. Explain why.
"We must keep ______ the same because ______."

**Challenge**
You want to find out if the type of paper changes the fall time. Write your independent variable, dependent variable and two control variables. Predict what will happen and explain using the words *air resistance* (空气阻力).

**My thinking (any language)**
Why do scientists change only one thing at a time?

## 9. Assessment

### Components (same structure in all three years)

| Component | When | What it measures | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Final project product** (poster / case file / fact-check report) | Weeks 9 to 10 | Rubric strands 1 to 5 | Main judgement of scientific thinking |
| **Final presentation** (Expo talk / expert witness / conference talk) | Week 10 | Rubric strand 6, plus questioning | Communication and confidence |
| **Skills check** (same 15-minute paper in Week 1 and Week 10; 10-minute checkpoint in Week 5) | Weeks 1, 5, 10 | Core skills: variables, fair tests, tables, graphs, conclusions, evaluation | Shows growth over the ten weeks |
| **Scientist's Logbook** | Weekly | Engagement, reflection, vocabulary | Supporting evidence; handover record |
| **Interest survey** (same in Week 1 and Week 10) | Weeks 1, 10 | Interest and confidence | Shows programme impact for principals and parents |

### Rubric (six strands, four levels)

Levels: **Beginning 起步 | Developing 发展 | Secure 达标 | Excellent 优秀**

| Strand | Beginning | Developing | Secure | Excellent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Questioning and predicting** 提问与预测 | Asks a question with help; makes a guess without a reason | Asks a question that can be tested; prediction has a simple reason | Writes a clear testable question naming what changes and what is measured; prediction uses science ideas | Sharpens the question; prediction uses science and states what result would show it is wrong |
| **2. Planning** 计划设计 | Lists some equipment and steps with support | Names the independent and dependent variables; method can mostly be followed | Identifies IV, DV and key controls; method is clear and repeatable, with repeats and safety | Justifies choices (range, repeats, sample size); anticipates problems; risk assessment is specific |
| **3. Doing and recording** 实施与记录 | Carries out steps with support; records some results | Follows the method safely; records results in a table with headings | Works carefully; table has units; repeats done; anomalies noticed | Adjusts the method sensibly when problems arise; records precisely; explains how anomalies were handled |
| **4. Analysing and concluding** 分析与结论 | Describes what happened | Draws a suitable chart with help; states a simple pattern | Chooses and draws the right graph accurately; conclusion uses data as evidence | Explains the pattern with science ideas; notes the limits of what the data can show |
| **5. Evaluating and critical thinking** 评价与批判性思维 | Says whether the experiment worked | Identifies one problem and a simple improvement | Explains how errors affected results; suggests specific improvements; judges how confident to be | Weighs the strength of evidence; considers other explanations; links to how scientists check each other's work |
| **6. Communicating** 表达与交流 | Shares findings using some key words, with frames and support | Presents findings clearly using key English vocabulary | Clear, organised presentation; uses vocabulary accurately; answers questions | Confident and well structured; adapts to the audience; responds to challenge with evidence |

**Important:** communication is judged on the clarity of the science, not on English grammar. Students may explain their reasoning in Chinese in the "My thinking" box; key vocabulary is expected in English.

### Skills check (15 minutes, bilingual question stems)

Question types, with year-specific contexts:
1. Observation or inference? (sort statements)
2. Which question is testable?
3. Identify the IV, DV and one control in a short scenario
4. Spot what makes a test unfair
5. Complete a results table (headings and units)
6. Calculate a mean; spot the anomaly
7. Read a value from a graph
8. Write a conclusion from a graph using a frame
9. Suggest one improvement and say why
10. Year 8: rank evidence. Year 9: correlation or cause?

Scored out of 20. The Week 5 checkpoint uses questions 1 to 6 (10 minutes).

### Interest survey (5-point smiley scale, bilingual)

1. I enjoy science. 我喜欢科学。
2. I like asking questions about how things work. 我喜欢问“事物是怎样运作的”。
3. I can plan my own experiment. 我能设计自己的实验。
4. I feel confident using science words in English. 我有信心用英语表达科学词汇。
5. I check the evidence before I believe something. 我会先看证据再相信一件事。
6. I would like to do more science outside lessons. 我希望在课外做更多科学活动。

### Reporting

- **Student certificate** with the series title and an award level: Gold (mostly Excellent), Silver (mostly Secure), Bronze (mostly Developing), Certificate of Participation. A separate "Most Improved Scientist" award per class, based on skills-check growth.
- **One-page bilingual skills report** per student: rubric level for each strand, skills check Week 1 vs Week 10, one teacher comment.
- **Programme summary** for principals: class averages, skills-check growth, interest survey change, photos of project work.
