1 · Purpose and principles

Enrichment for its own sake

No national curriculum link and no exam focus. The aim is curiosity, thinking, and confidence with scientific English.

Purpose statement · English

Think Like a Scientist is a ten-week enrichment programme for Years 7 to 9 in the bilingual stream. It sits outside the national curriculum and has no exam focus. Students work on hands-on projects that build curiosity, scientific thinking and critical thinking, and grow their confidence in using scientific English. Each series ends with a project that students plan, carry out and present.

Purpose statement · 中文

“像科学家一样思考”是为双语部七至九年级学生开设的为期十周的科学拓展课程。本课程独立于国家课程体系，不以考试为目的。学生通过动手实践的项目式学习，培养好奇心、科学思维和批判性思维，并增强使用英语进行科学表达的信心。每个系列都以学生自主设计、实施并展示的项目作为结束。

To be checked by a native-speaking colleague before sharing.

Project-based arc

Weeks 1 to 5 build thinking skills through short, fun tasks. Weeks 6 to 10 put them to work on one project students plan and own.

Low floor, high ceiling

Every task has a way in for students with less English and a way up for those ready to go further. Scaffolds support; they never cap.

Bilingual by design

Key vocabulary always in English and Chinese. Instructions in English with key words glossed. Safety instructions in full in both languages.

Kitchen-cupboard kit

Every practical uses cheap, everyday materials. Nothing depends on specialist equipment.

Same lesson shape every week

Six phases, same order, same icons. Students know what comes next, and any teacher can pick up any lesson.

Winter thread

Ice structures (Y7), a frozen-notebook mystery (Y8), and winter claims such as "Does hot water freeze faster?" (Y9).

Handover-ready

Week 5 is a checkpoint that produces data for the incoming teacher. Every lesson has a one-page plan, kit card and answer key.

The programme does not depend on a freezer

Both plans are fully built. The choice is made by Week 4 (w/c 2 November), before any ice is needed, and recorded in the handover note.

What Plan A actually needs

  • Y7 Week 6: one or two trays of ice cubes (about 24).

4 · Year 7

Ice Engineers冰雪工程师

From thought experiments and vocabulary to a practical students design themselves, using the arrival of winter as the stage.

Big question
How do scientists find things out?
Project question
Can we make ice stronger?
End product
Investigation poster and 2-minute talk at the Ice Engineers Expo
The story hook

During the Second World War, steel was in short supply. The inventor Geoffrey Pyke proposed building giant ships out of ice. His team found that ice mixed with about 14% sawdust, later called "pykrete", was much stronger than plain ice and melted more slowly. A small prototype was built on a lake in Canada in 1943. Legend has it that when the material was shown to military leaders, a bullet fired at a block of it bounced off.

Local links: the giant ice buildings at the Harbin Ice and Snow Festival, and the traditional use of straw in mud bricks. In both cases, fibres make a brittle material stronger.

01

What do scientists do?科学家做什么？

Observation vs inference

Footprint puzzle: a printed picture of animal tracks is revealed in three stages; students write observations, then inferences, and change their story as new evidence appears. Mystery cups: sealed cups with an object inside; students shake, listen, weigh in the hand and infer the contents. The cups stay closed, because scientists often cannot "open the box".

Kit
Printed puzzle cards; opaque cups, tape, small objects
Output
Baseline skills check; interest survey; logbook started
02

Thought experiments思想实验

Testable questions; predictions with reasons

Galileo's thought experiment: if heavy things fall faster, what happens when a heavy and a light ball are tied together? Then a quick test: drop a book and a sheet of paper, then the paper resting on the book. Question sort: testable or not testable. Students write three testable questions about winter, such as "Does salt make ice melt faster?"

Kit
Paper, a book, printed question cards
Output
Question sort; three testable questions
03

Meet the variables认识变量

Independent, dependent and control variables; fair test

Paper spinners: make spinners from a printed template, change the wing length, time the fall from a 2 m mark. "I change / I measure / I keep the same."

Kit
Spinner template, scissors, paper clips, stopwatches, metre stick
Output
Variables table; first results

06

Ice cube rescue冰块保卫战

Planning a fair test with ice

Concept cartoon: "If we put a coat on a snowman, will it melt faster or slower?" Then the ice cube keeper challenge: wrap identical ice cubes in different materials and measure the meltwater after 30 minutes. It rehearses the exact skills the project needs.

07

The ice ship mission冰船计划

Planning their own investigation

Pykrete story hook. Groups choose an independent variable (type of fibre, or amount of sawdust) and a test (strength or melting). They complete a planning frame and risk assessment, then make samples in identical moulds and label them for the freezer.

Kit
Moulds, untreated sawdust, tissue, cotton wool, shredded paper, water, measuring jug, labels, freezer
Output
Plan sheet; labelled samples
08

Test day测试日

Carrying out a test carefully; recording

Strength test: rest the ice beam across two equal stacks of books with a fixed gap, hang a plastic bag from the middle, and pour in water 50 ml at a time until the beam snaps. 1 ml of water has a mass of about 1 g, so no masses are needed. Melting test: time how long each sample takes to melt, or measure the meltwater every 5 minutes.

Kit
Samples, books, string, plastic bags, measuring jug, catch trays, towels, gloves
Output
Raw results, at least 3 repeats per sample type
09

What does it mean?数据说明了什么？

Analysing and evaluating

Pool class data. Choose the right graph: a bar chart for fibre type, a line graph for amount of sawdust. Write a Claim-Evidence-Reasoning conclusion. Evaluate what went wrong and how to improve. Build the poster.

Kit
A3 poster template, printed results
Output
Draft poster
10

Ice Engineers Expo冰雪工程师成果展Assessment

Communicating

Gallery walk of posters. Each group gives a 2-minute talk and answers one question; peer feedback as "two stars and a wish". Final skills check and interest survey. Certificates.

Kit
Posters, sticky notes
Output
Poster and talk (rubric), final skills check, survey

Making and testing the ice samples

  • Moulds: ice-lolly moulds, ice-cube trays, or 250 ml drink cartons cut in half lengthways. Use the same mould for every sample.
  • Fixed recipe: the same volume of water each time (for example 100 ml). Only the fibre changes.
  • Independent variable, option 1: type of fibre: none, sawdust, tissue, cotton wool, shredded paper.
  • Independent variable, option 2: amount of sawdust: 0, 5, 10, 15 g per 100 ml of water.
  • Dependent variable: strength (ml of water held before snapping) or melting (time to melt, or meltwater after a set time).
  • Timing: make in Week 7, freeze for at least 24 hours, test in Week 8. Freezer decision by Week 4.
  • Repeats: three samples of each type per group.

Safety notes

  • Untreated natural wood sawdust or pet-bedding wood shavings only. Never MDF dust, which contains resin glues. Keep sawdust damp.
  • Gloves for handling ice for more than a minute.
  • Catch trays under every strength test; feet clear of hanging loads.
  • Mop spills straight away.
  • No standing on chairs or tables when dropping spinners.

If there is no freezer

Year 7 runs Plan B: fibre-reinforced dough bars in Weeks 7 to 8 and a "keep it warm" challenge in Week 6. See Freezer: Plan A and Plan B.

5 · Year 8

Science Detectives Confirmed科学侦探

Very low kit and very high engagement. It teaches the core of scientific thinking: separating what we see from what we infer, using reference samples as controls, and judging how strong evidence really is.

Big question
How strong is the evidence?
Project
Solve the Frozen Notebook case, then design your own forensic test
End product
Case file and expert witness statement at the Court of Science
The case: The Frozen Notebook 冰封笔记本之谜

The night before the Winter Science Fair, Professor Frost's research notebook disappears from the science lab. Left behind: a handwritten note, a fingerprint on a glass, a spilled white powder, a small puddle of liquid and a footprint. Four fictional suspects each have a pen, a reason to be in the lab, a shoe size and a link to one of the powders. Each week, one type of evidence rules suspects in or out. No real staff or students are used.

History hook: Song Ci (宋慈) wrote Collected Cases of Injustice Rectified (《洗冤集录》) in 1247, one of the world's earliest books on forensic science. Fingerprints and handprints were also used to sign documents in ancient China.

Hooks

Tu Youyou (屠呦呦) and her team took an idea from a text more than 1,600 years old and tested it with careful experiments, leading to a malaria medicine and the 2015 Nobel Prize. Erasto Mpemba, a Tanzanian school student, noticed in 1963 that hot ice-cream mixture seemed to freeze faster than cold, and scientists are still arguing about it.

Claim bank: curated, safe, low kit

  1. Hot water freezes faster than cold water. Ice and salt bath
  2. Ice melts faster in salt water than in fresh water. Cups, ice, salt; the result surprises most people
  3. You lose most of your body heat through your head, so a hat keeps you warm. Warm-water bottles, fabric, thermometers
  4. Wet clothes make you lose heat faster in winter. Warm water wrapped in wet vs dry cloth
  5. Putting salt on ice makes it melt faster. Why roads are salted in winter
  6. Crushed ice cools a drink faster than ice cubes. Thermometers
  7. Listening to music helps you remember things. Memory test with and without music
  8. Practice makes you faster at a puzzle. Timed puzzle, repeated trials
  9. Taller people have bigger feet, or your arm span equals your height. Measurements
  10. A spoon in the neck of an open fizzy drink keeps it fizzy. Mass loss on a kitchen scale
  11. Brand X paper towel is the most absorbent. Brands anonymised
  12. You react faster in the morning than after lunch. Ruler drop at different times

Claims to avoid: health, medical or diet claims; traditional medicine; anything that involves eating or drinking; claims about named people or groups.

Safety notes

  • Ice and salt baths get cold enough to hurt skin: handle the tubes, not the mixture, and wear gloves.
  • Warm water for the Mpemba test is prepared by the teacher, no hotter than about 60 °C for student handling.
  • Human-participant claims: voluntary participation, no pressure, results anonymised.

Sample one-page lesson plan · Year 7, Week 3, Meet the variables

Learning goals

  • I can name the independent, dependent and control variables in an experiment.
  • I can explain why a fair test changes only one thing.

Key words (6)

Tiers

  • Core: cloze with word bank and picture sort.
  • Stretch: name two more control variables and explain why each must stay the same.
  • Challenge: design a new test with paper type as the independent variable and predict the result using "air resistance".

Teacher notes

Common misconception: "heavier things always fall faster". Timing errors are expected and set up Week 4. If time runs short, test two wing lengths instead of three.

Skills check · 15 minutes, bilingual question stems

  1. Observation or inference? (sort statements)
  2. Which question is testable?
  3. Identify the IV, DV and one control in a scenario
  4. Spot what makes a test unfair
  5. Complete a results table (headings and units)
  6. Calculate a mean; spot the anomaly
  7. Read a value from a graph
  8. Write a conclusion from a graph using a frame
  9. Suggest one improvement and say why
  10. Year 8: rank evidence. Year 9: correlation or cause?

Scored out of 20. The Week 5 checkpoint uses questions 1 to 6 (10 minutes). Contexts change by year.
